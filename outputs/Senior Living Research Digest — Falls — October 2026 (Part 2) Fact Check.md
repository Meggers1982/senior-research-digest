# Fact-Check Report: Senior Living Research Digest — Falls — October 2026
**Checked:** 2026-10-23 | **Studies reviewed:** 23
**Primary audience:** older adults and seniors | **Secondary audience:** families, caregivers, and senior living professionals

### Study 1: Most Fractures in Men Over 85 Happen Indoors, During Ordinary Activity — and Often Without the Walker
**PMID:** 42897253 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

**Issue A. Indoor-only framing omits upper-limb exception** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "suggesting prevention efforts should target the living room and hallway rather than outdoor hazards"
- **Abstract says:** "all fracture types most often occurred indoors, except upper limb fractures that most often occurred outdoors (p = 0.001)"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports one clear exception — upper limb fractures most often happened outdoors — so advising that outdoor hazards be deprioritized overstates the finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a clause such as "though upper-limb fractures were the exception, most often occurring outdoors.""

### Study 3: In Knee Arthritis, Hearing, Vision and Mood Predicted First Falls Better Than the Knee Itself
**PMID:** 42759165 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Comparison with knee measures not stated in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The most influential predictors were not joint-specific measures but hearing and vision problems and depressive symptoms."
- **Abstract says:** "Sensory impairments (hearing and vision problems) and depressive symptoms were the most influential predictors of falls."
- **Problem:** The abstract identifies the top predictors but never says joint-specific variables ranked lower or were even among the 28 inputs; the explicit "not the knee" contrast is an inference. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "The most influential predictors were hearing and vision problems and depressive symptoms" and drop or soften the knee comparison in both headline and body."

### Study 4: A One-Page Checklist Transformed What Hospitals Record After a Patient Falls
**PMID:** 42877601 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Every clinical domain" overstates** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Documentation was dramatically more complete with the proforma across every clinical domain"
- **Abstract says:** "Documentation was significantly higher in the proforma group across examination domains... Scenario documentation was high in both groups (85.6% vs. 95.2%, p = 0.100)."
- **Problem:** Scenario documentation showed no significant difference and was numerically higher in the non-proforma group, so the gain was not universal across all 12 domains.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "across 11 of 12 assessment domains, with documentation of the fall scenario itself similarly high in both groups.""

**Issue B. Sampling description simplified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Australian clinicians reviewed 167 first falls among hospital inpatients over age 65 across three years"
- **Abstract says:** "A stratified random sample of 202 inpatient falls was reviewed, with 167 eligible for analysis."
- **Problem:** The 167 came from a stratified random sample of falls, not a review of all first falls over the period; readers may assume complete case capture.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that cases were drawn as a stratified random sample of inpatient falls, of which 167 were eligible."

**Issue C. "Dementia" substituted for cognitive impairment** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "watch for the signal that patients with dementia were less likely to receive it, the very group at highest risk"
- **Abstract says:** "preexisting cognitive impairment was associated with lower odds of proforma use, however this association did not reach statistical significance after adjustment (OR = 0.450, 95% CI 0.190-1.030, p = 0.062)"
- **Problem:** The abstract refers to preexisting cognitive impairment, not a dementia diagnosis, and the association was not statistically significant — presenting it as a "signal" to act on in a story angle outruns the data.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "cognitive impairment" and note the association fell short of significance."

### Study 5: Depression That Never Lets Up Speeds the Slide Into Frailty, Four Global Studies Agree
**PMID:** 42822543 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Claim about remitting symptoms not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "while symptoms that remitted were not associated with the same trajectory"
- **Abstract says:** "Five phenotypes were reproducibly identified... persistently healthy, remitting, late-onset, fluctuating, and persistent high burden... The fluctuating phenotype (P4) was associated with frailty mainly concurrently rather than temporally"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports results for the persistent high burden and fluctuating phenotypes but says nothing about outcomes in the remitting group. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Remove the remitting-group claim or state that the abstract reports results only for the persistent and fluctuating phenotypes."

**Issue B. Education direction inferred** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Lower education predicted membership in the worse trajectories across all four cohorts."
- **Abstract says:** "education was a directionally consistent predictor of phenotype membership across the four cohorts (OR 0.32-0.80)"
- **Problem:** The abstract states only that education was a directionally consistent predictor; the specific reading that lower education predicts worse trajectories is an inference from the OR range.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "Education was a consistent predictor of which trajectory people followed in all four cohorts.""

**Issue C. Headline verb implies causation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Depression That Never Lets Up Speeds the Slide Into Frailty"
- **Abstract says:** "Persistent high burden thus represents a reproducible risk marker of accelerated frailty accumulation"
- **Problem:** "Speeds" reads as causal for an observational cohort analysis; the authors describe a risk marker.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Depression That Never Lets Up Is a Marker of Faster Frailty, Four Global Studies Agree.""

### Study 7: Why Uneven Ground Trips Up Some Older Walkers More Than Others — a Brain Clue
**PMID:** 42679689 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Free-water association was with baseline variability** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "their stepping became far more erratic as the terrain got bumpier; their gait irregularity was also more strongly tied to a marker of fluid accumulation in brain white matter, especially in the corpus callosum"
- **Abstract says:** "A significant group-by-behavior interaction showed that higher FW was more strongly associated with greater baseline gait variability in lower-functioning than higher-functioning older adults, particularly in the body of corpus callosum."
- **Problem:** Placed immediately after the uneven-terrain sentence, this implies the free-water link applied to terrain-related instability; the abstract ties it specifically to baseline gait variability.
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify "their baseline step-timing variability was more strongly tied to a marker of free water in white matter, particularly the body of the corpus callosum.""

### Study 8: Visual Hallucinations From Sight Loss May Be an Overlooked Cause of Falls
**PMID:** 42833804 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Treatable" fall hazard not supported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "meaning a genuinely treatable, modifiable fall hazard goes unspoken in the exam room"
- **Abstract says:** "A falls prevention approach for CBS-related falls spanning primary, secondary, and community services is indicated to ensure this potential contributory factor is not overlooked."
- **Problem:** The abstract calls CBS a potential contributory factor and proposes an adapted falls prevention approach; it makes no claim that CBS or CBS-related fall risk is treatable. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "a potentially modifiable contributor to falls goes unmentioned" and drop "genuinely treatable.""

**Issue B. Unverifiable specifics about mechanisms and non-disclosure** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "hallucinated images such as patterns, figures, or distorted floors can cause trips, missteps, and startle reactions... Many people with CBS never mention their hallucinations because they fear being thought to have dementia"
- **Abstract says:** "recent work has highlighted that CBS may also lead to physical outcomes by causing and increasing susceptibility to falls. As awareness of CBS among sight loss and falls prevention specialists is low, its role in the sight loss and falls mechanism may be missed."
- **Problem:** These mechanistic examples and the stated reason patients stay silent do not appear in the abstract, though they are plausible content of the full review. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute these details explicitly to the full review text or soften to what the abstract supports."

### Study 10: Falls Prevention Works in Lower-Income Countries Too — But the Evidence Is Thin
**PMID:** 42895234 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline firmer than authors' conclusion** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Falls Prevention Works in Lower-Income Countries Too — But the Evidence Is Thin"
- **Abstract says:** "This integrative review finds that fall-prevention interventions may reduce falls, fall risk, and fear of falling among older adults in LMICs. However, due to the limited number of available studies and variation in interventions and outcomes measured, these findings should be interpreted with caution."
- **Problem:** "Works" asserts established effectiveness, while the authors say interventions "may reduce" falls and urge caution; the subheading only partly offsets this.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Falls Prevention May Work in Lower-Income Countries Too — But the Evidence Is Thin.""

**Issue B. "Why it matters" overstates transferability** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The same core ingredients — strength and balance training plus home hazard fixes — appear to work across very different settings, reinforcing that these are the fundamentals worth investing in regardless of resources."
- **Abstract says:** "evidence remains limited on their contextual adaptation, scalability, and long-term sustainability in LMIC settings"
- **Problem:** Fourteen heterogeneous studies with no pooled estimate do not support a conclusion that the ingredients work across settings regardless of resources.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the authors' caution, e.g. "early evidence points the same direction, but the studies are few and varied.""

### Study 11: Painkiller Prescribing After Hip Fracture Has Barely Budged — Even as Sedative Use Plummets
**PMID:** 42852585 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Opioid figure described as year-long continuation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "nearly half of hip fracture patients are still leaving the hospital on opioids a year out"
- **Abstract says:** "Our primary outcome was post-discharge opioids and BZDs dispensed, defined as ≥ 1 prescription dispensed within 1-365 days following discharge... opioids dispensing peaked in 2015 at 55.8% and decreased to 44.6% in 2024"
- **Problem:** The 44.6% figure means at least one opioid prescription dispensed at any point in the year after discharge, not that patients were still on opioids a year later. The wording implies persistent long-term use.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to "nearly half of hip fracture patients received at least one opioid prescription in the year after discharge.""

**Issue B. "Barely budged" overstates flatness** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Painkiller Prescribing After Hip Fracture Has Barely Budged"
- **Abstract says:** "opioids dispensing peaked in 2015 at 55.8% and decreased to 44.6% in 2024... opioids dispensing remained consistently high"
- **Problem:** Opioid dispensing did fall roughly 11 percentage points from its 2015 peak; the abstract's point is that it remained high, not that it was unchanged.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Has Stayed High" or "Has Fallen Far Less Than Sedative Use.""

### Study 16: A Nudge in the Doctor's Computer Cut Falls for 12,000 Older Patients
**PMID:** 42886484 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies all 12,000 received the intervention** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A Nudge in the Doctor's Computer Cut Falls for 12,000 Older Patients"
- **Abstract says:** "Among 11 945 participants... of whom 5681 (47.6%) were in the intervention group and 6264 (52.4%) were in the control group"
- **Problem:** Only about 5,700 patients were in intervention practices; the 11,945 figure includes the control arm, and the reduction was modest (RR 0.91).
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like "A Nudge in the Doctor's Computer Modestly Cut Falls in a 12,000-Patient Trial.""

**Issue B. "Proven to prevent" hype wording** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Exercise is proven to prevent falls, but it rarely makes it into a busy 15-minute primary care visit"
- **Abstract says:** "Although exercise reduces fall risk, implementation in primary care remains limited."
- **Problem:** "Proven to prevent" is stronger than the abstract's "reduces fall risk" and is the kind of absolute claim the digest otherwise avoids.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Exercise reduces fall risk, but it rarely makes it into a busy 15-minute visit.""

### Study 17: Day 3 and Day 7 in the Hospital: When Fall Risk Quietly Changes
**PMID:** 42842205 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Fall source described as incident reports** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Falls were taken from incident reports, which can be underreported."
- **Abstract says:** "This retrospective multicenter cohort study used electronic health record and administrative data from two tertiary hospitals in Japan... The outcome was the first documented in-hospital fall within 30 days after each landmark."
- **Problem:** The abstract says falls were "documented" in EHR/administrative data and does not specify incident reports. Unverifiable from abstract only.

### Study 18: A Taiwan Study of 245,000 Seniors Maps Which Combinations of Decline Predict ER Visits for Falls
**PMID:** 42575258 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Claim about size of highest-risk group** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Those residents are a minority but carry disproportionate emergency department risk."
- **Abstract says:** "LCA identified five IC patterns; the highest risk occurred in the physio-cognitive decline with sensory impairment (aRR = 1.87, 95% CI: 1.60-2.19)"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not report the proportion of participants in each latent class, so the claim that this group is a minority is unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the size claim or say "this combined-decline pattern carried the highest rate of fall-related ED visits.""

### Study 19: Meals Plus Exercise at Home: Homebound Seniors Say Pain and Fear of Falling Got in the Way
**PMID:** 42847733 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Omits parent trial's loneliness benefit** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The original trial found no improvement in physical measures, and these participant voices explain why"
- **Abstract says:** "a randomized controlled trial evaluating a nutrition and exercise intervention for homebound older adults that showed no differences in physical measures but improvements in loneliness for those in the exercise group"
- **Problem:** The digest repeatedly cites the null physical result but never mentions that the parent trial did improve loneliness, which is relevant given participants named social contact as a need.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "though the exercise group did report less loneliness" when describing the parent trial's results."

### Study 22: Could Imagining Movement Improve Balance? Researchers Say the Evidence Is Still Too Scattered to Tell
**PMID:** 42501905 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Follow-up duration overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "only one study looked beyond a month after the intervention"
- **Abstract says:** ""Only one study examined long-term effects on balance and gait performance (i.e., 1-month post-intervention).""
- **Problem:** The single long-term study followed participants to one month post-intervention, not beyond a month; as written it implies follow-up extended past one month, making the evidence base sound marginally stronger than it is.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "only one study followed participants as far as one month after the intervention.""

### Study 23: In a Study of Nearly a Million Diabetes Patients, One Drug Class Was Linked to Fewer Falls and Fractures Than Another
**PMID:** 42896077 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Frailer patients" subgroup not specified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Results held in most "vulnerable" subgroups, including frailer patients."
- **Abstract says:** ""These findings remained consistent in the per-protocol analysis, across most vulnerable subgroups and individual SGLT2i and DPP4i agents.""
- **Problem:** The abstract names "vulnerable subgroups" but does not say which ones; the specific mention of frailer patients cannot be confirmed from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "across most of the subgroups the authors classed as vulnerable (not itemised in the abstract)" or drop the frailty specifier."

**Issue B. Study population not restricted to older adults** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Using Taiwan's national health insurance database, researchers emulated a clinical trial comparing 224,510 people with type 2 diabetes who newly started an SGLT2 inhibitor against 698,012 who newly started a DPP-4 inhibitor"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract describes enrolling "participants with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM)" with no age restriction or reported mean age."
- **Problem:** The digest is written for an older-adult audience, but the abstract gives no indication the cohort was limited to or predominantly older adults, so the applicability to seniors is unverified.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a caveat noting the cohort was adults with type 2 diabetes of all ages, with no age-specific results reported in the abstract."
