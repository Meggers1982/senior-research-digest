### Study 5: Trouble Seeing Up Close Was Linked to Weaker Thinking Scores in a Study of 3,650 Older Adults
**PMID:** 42716809 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Effect size rounded down** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "was associated with roughly three times higher odds of poorer cognitive scores even after adjusting for the full set of demographic and health covariates"
- **Abstract says:** "Near VI was associated with a 2.21-fold higher likelihood of poorer MMSE scores following initial adjustment (p=0.025), and this association remained significant after full adjustment (OR= 3.44, p=0.028)."
- **Problem:** The fully adjusted odds ratio is 3.44; "roughly three times" understates it slightly and omits the fact that the partially adjusted estimate was 2.21.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "about 3.4 times higher odds after full adjustment (OR 3.44)" or cite both the initial and fully adjusted estimates."

**Issue B. "Uncorrected" not stated in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "yet they went uncorrected in the vast majority of this population"
- **Abstract says:** "The prevalence of distance VI and near VI was 27.53% and 91.99%, respectively... highlighting the potential role of correctable vision loss in cognitive health."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports prevalence of near visual impairment and describes the vision loss as "correctable," but does not state that the impairment was uncorrected (i.e. whether acuity was presenting or best-corrected).
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "near vision impairment was present in the vast majority of this population and is the type most often correctable," or note that the abstract does not specify correction status."

### Study 7: After Eye Injections Clear Up This Form of Macular Disease, Half of Patients Relapse Within Two Years
**PMID:** 42752539 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Recurrence denominator conflated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Of the 32 eyes whose abnormal vessels fully regressed by week 12 or 24, treatment was then given only as needed; half recurred within two years"
- **Abstract says:** "32 (40%) achieved regression at week 12 or 24. Of 26 eyes with regression, 13 (50%; 95% CI, 30%-70%) recurred within 2 years."
- **Problem:** The 50% recurrence figure applies to the 26 eyes analysed for recurrence, not to all 32 eyes that achieved regression. The Caveats mention the 26–32 range, but the body text attributes the figure to the 32-eye group.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "32 eyes achieved full regression; among the 26 analysed for recurrence, half (13) recurred within two years.""

**Issue B. Biomarker prediction overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Certain OCT scan features at baseline predicted who was more or less likely to relapse."
- **Abstract says:** "Post hoc exploratory analyses... baseline massive subretinal hemorrhage had lower recurrence (HR, 0.18...; P = .01). In contrast, leakage without subretinal hemorrhage may have had a higher hazard of recurrence (HR, 2.65; 95% CI, 0.89-7.94; P = .07)."
- **Problem:** One of the two features (leakage without subretinal haemorrhage) was not statistically significant, and both came from post hoc exploratory analysis; "predicted" implies firmer evidence than the data support. The Caveats note the post hoc nature but the body text does not hedge.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "exploratory post hoc analysis suggested certain baseline features may be associated with recurrence risk, one of them not statistically significant.""

### Study 11: A Chemotherapy Drug, Injected Into the Cornea, Shrinks Vision-Threatening Blood Vessels
**PMID:** 42215273 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

**Issue A. Eyes vs. procedures** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Vessel regression was seen in 59% of eyes by objective image analysis"
- **Abstract says:** "39 procedures were performed... NV regression was demonstrated in 59% by ImageJ analysis, 71% by independent grading and 81% by surgeon-reported outcomes."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports percentages without specifying whether the denominator is eyes, patients or procedures; the digest assigns them to "eyes."
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "in 59% of cases by objective image analysis" or note that the abstract does not specify the denominator."

**Issue B. Transplant rejection claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Corneal neovascularization is stubbornly hard to treat and raises the risk that a corneal transplant will be rejected"
- **Abstract says:** "Corneal neovascularisation (NV) is a refractory condition associated with lipid deposition, scarring and vision loss... may have an important role in stabilising high-risk corneas prior to keratoplasty."
- **Problem:** The rejection-risk statement is background context not stated in the abstract (the abstract only refers to "high-risk corneas"). Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the rejection-risk point to general background rather than to this study, or reword as "corneas the authors describe as high-risk for transplant.""

### Study 12: Women Carry a Heavier Burden of Blindness Worldwide — and the Gap Isn't Closing
**PMID:** 42521465 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Inference about untreated disease** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this analysis suggests older women are more likely to be living with untreated or under-treated eye disease"
- **Abstract says:** "females had higher YLD rates than males for blindness and vision loss (BVI) (300.9 vs 262.8 per 100 000; +14.5%), cataract (95.1 vs 75.0; +26.7%)... The strong association with GDI and GII indicates these disparities are modifiable and linked to structural factors."
- **Problem:** Higher years-lived-with-disability rates can reflect higher prevalence, longer survival or treatment gaps; the abstract does not measure treatment coverage, so "untreated or under-treated" is an inference.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "older women carry a larger share of the disability burden from cataract and AMD, which the authors link to structural gender inequality.""

### Study 13: Beyond the Needle: What's Coming Next for Macular Degeneration and Diabetic Eye Disease

### Study 14: Swollen Eyelids in Older Men May Signal a Rare Inflammatory Disease — and Occasionally Threaten Sight
**PMID:** 41535098 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Most patients" overstates 43%** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "note how fast this can move — most patients presented within two to seven days of symptoms starting"
- **Abstract says:** "Eye symptoms showed variable duration at presentation, most commonly 2-7 days (n=6, 43%)."
- **Problem:** 2–7 days was the most common category, but it covered only 43% of patients — fewer than half, not "most." The abstract also explicitly describes duration as variable.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "the most common symptom duration at presentation was 2–7 days (43% of patients), though duration varied.""

**Issue B. Progression speed not reported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "in a minority of cases progressed to sight-threatening emergencies within days"
- **Abstract says:** "Severe vision loss occurred unilaterally in two patients (14%), due to orbital compartment syndrome and periorbital necrotising fasciitis."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports symptom duration before presentation, not how quickly cases progressed to severe vision loss; the "within days" time course is not supported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "within days" or say "in 14% of cases progressed to sight-threatening complications.""

### Study 15: An Experimental Gel Injection Restored Vision in Eyes With Dangerously Low Pressure
**PMID:** 41521029 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Restored Vision" in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "An Experimental Gel Injection Restored Vision in Eyes With Dangerously Low Pressure"
- **Abstract says:** "Best corrected visual acuity (BCVA) logarithm of the minimum angle of resolution (LogMAR) improved in 87.5% of eyes, with one eye remaining stable... The treatment improved vision and restored ocular anatomy."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports improved acuity (e.g., 0.3–1.7 to 0.1–1.6 LogMAR) and restored anatomy, not restored vision; "restored" overstates the visual gains in an eight-eye pilot.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Improved Vision in Eyes With Dangerously Low Pressure" and keep the experimental framing."

**Issue B. Follow-up duration described as additional** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this protocol required injections every two to four weeks over months plus 12 months of follow-up"
- **Abstract says:** "They were followed up for 12 months after the first intravitreal injection."
- **Problem:** The 12 months of follow-up runs from the first injection and includes the treatment period, rather than being 12 months on top of months of injections.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "injections every two to four weeks, with follow-up for 12 months from the first injection.""

### Study 17: Diabetes-Related Vision Loss Is Rising Worldwide — and Wealthier Countries Saw the Biggest Jump
**PMID:** 42476825 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Regions" vs SDI groups** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "prevalence rose steadily with advancing age across all regions"
- **Abstract says:** "Increasing ASPR with advancing age was consistent across SDI groups though less pronounced for type 1 than type 2 diabetes."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports consistency across socio-demographic index groups, not geographic regions.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change "across all regions" to "across all development levels (SDI groups).""

**Issue B. Why it matters contradicts own caveat** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "meaning screening and blood sugar control are not keeping pace with the spread of the disease"
- **Abstract says:** "Substantially different trends were observed for ASPR, period and cohort effects in recent decades for type 1 versus type 2 diabetes across different SDI."
- **Problem:** The analysis cannot attribute rising prevalence to screening or glycemic control failures, and the digest's own caveat says rising prevalence may partly reflect better detection and longer survival.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "the burden of diabetes-related vision loss is still growing even in well-resourced systems, though the analysis cannot say why.""
