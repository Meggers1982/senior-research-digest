## Study 2: Smartphone App Shows Promise for Tracking Memory and Sleep in Those with Cognitive Concerns
**PMID:** 42275527 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Completion rate discrepancy** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Over 50% completed the full testing protocol"
- **Abstract says:** "The word-pairs task completion rate for all trials was over 50%. There was 57% (39/68) completion of both evening and morning delayed recall tests."
- **Problem:** The digest conflates two different metrics. The "over 50%" refers to completion of all trials, while 57% specifically completed both evening and morning delayed recall tests.
- **Suggested fix:** "The word-pairs task completion rate for all trials was over 50%, with 57% completing both evening and morning delayed recall tests."

**Issue B. Acceptability percentage imprecision** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "81% who participated found the app acceptable"
- **Abstract says:** "81.7% (98/120) perceived it as helpful for stress management"
- **Problem:** The abstract says 81.7% found it "helpful for stress management," not "acceptable." Acceptability is described more broadly: "User feedback indicated that the app was well-accepted and liked."
- **Suggested fix:** "User feedback indicated the app was well-accepted, and 81.7% perceived it as helpful."

## Study 9: Music Therapy Improves Cognition and Reduces Depression in Healthy Older Adults
**PMID:** 42161569 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Sleep quality not mentioned in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** Listed in parenthetical outcomes: "(general well-being, depression, sleep quality)"
- **Abstract says:** "Pre-post intervention measures assessed cognitive functions (memory, attention, executive function, verbal fluency) and well-being (general well-being, depression, sleep quality)."
- **Problem:** Sleep quality is mentioned in the abstract as an outcome measure, so this is accurate. However, the digest's "Why it matters" and story angles don't mention sleep, which is appropriate since the abstract doesn't report significant sleep findings—only cognitive and depression results.
- **No correction needed.**

## Study 14: Restricting Daytime Naps for a Year May Slow Memory Decline
**PMID:** 41973339 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Sample size clarification** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "58 healthy older habitual nappers (ages 59-82) in Belgium"
- **Abstract says:** "Fifty-eight habitual nappers were randomised either to a nap-control condition (n = 28), or to a nap-intervention condition (n = 30)"
- **Problem:** The digest states 58 nappers were randomized, which is accurate. However, stating "N=58 nappers" in caveats is slightly imprecise since there was also a non-napper reference group (n=29).
- **Suggested fix:** In caveats: "Small sample (N=58 nappers randomized; 29 non-nappers as reference)"

## Study 17: Lifestyle Factors Explain Some—But Not All—of the Link Between Mental Health and Memory Complaints
**PMID:** 42051031 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Sample size rounding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "over 5,000 dementia-free adults"
- **Abstract says:** "5,011 dementia-free users"
- **Problem:** Minor rounding; "over 5,000" is technically accurate but less precise.
- **Suggested fix:** "5,011 dementia-free adults"

**Issue B. Mediation percentages** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "lifestyle only partially explained the mental health-memory link"
- **Abstract says:** "Lifestyle explained 9% of the age-SMCs, 10% of depression-SMCs, and 6% of perceived stress-SMCs associations"
- **Problem:** The digest accurately characterizes this as "partially" but could be more specific.
- **No correction required** (digest is accurate if less detailed).
