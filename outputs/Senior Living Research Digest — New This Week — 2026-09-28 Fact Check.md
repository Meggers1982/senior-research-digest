### Study 1: Nine years of cycling later: these older athletes still outperform 99% of their peers — even as aging took its toll
**PMID:** 42802250 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline misreads percentile statistic** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "these older athletes still outperform 99% of their peers"
- **Abstract says:** "overall, 98.7% of those who completed the test were at or above the 90th percentile for V̇O2peak based on reference populations"
- **Problem:** 98.7% is the share of participants who reached the 90th percentile or better — not a percentile rank. The headline converts it into a claim that the cyclists outperform 99% of peers, which the data do not support (the study establishes top-10%, not top-1%, standing).
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like: "nearly all of these older athletes still rank in the top 10% for aerobic fitness"."

**Issue B. Descriptor of participants not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "re-tested 82 of 125 lifelong recreational cyclists"
- **Abstract says:** "Of an original 125 cyclists first assessed in 2012-2013, 82 (53 males, 29 females) returned 9 years later"
- **Problem:** "Lifelong" and "recreational" are not stated in the abstract. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the adjectives or attribute them to the earlier cohort description."

**Issue C. Comparison to younger people not supported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "staying active keeps you functioning at a level most people decades younger never reach"
- **Abstract says:** "98.7% of those who completed the test were at or above the 90th percentile for V̇O2peak based on reference populations"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports percentile standing against reference populations but says nothing about comparisons with people decades younger. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say they scored in the top 10% against reference populations, without the cross-generational comparison."

### Study 2: New Alzheimer's drugs slow decline — but a pooled analysis says the benefit may be too small for patients to notice
**PMID:** 42803728 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Pooled estimates presented as per-drug results** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "All three produced statistically significant but small improvements on cognitive and functional scales (CDR-SB difference of 0.41 points; MMSE 0.44 points)."
- **Abstract says:** "Seven trials were included. Treatment favored intervention for ADAS-Cog (SMD -0.15...), CDR-SB (MD -0.41...), and MMSE (MD 0.44...), although effects were small."
- **Problem:** The CDR-SB and MMSE figures are pooled across all seven trials, not drug-specific estimates; the abstract does not report separate per-drug effect sizes (though its conclusion does name all three drugs collectively).
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "Pooled across the trials, the antibodies produced statistically significant but small improvements (CDR-SB 0.41 points; MMSE 0.44 points).""

### Study 3: Across 1.2 million people, lithium users had about 37% less dementia — but no one has proven the drug is the reason
**PMID:** 42803710 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline pairs 1.2 million with the pooled hazard ratio** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Across 1.2 million people, lithium users had about 37% less dementia"
- **Abstract says:** "Twenty-three primary studies with original data (n > 1.2 million) were included in qualitative synthesis... Four cohort studies showed a pooled hazard ratio (HR) of 0.633 ... indicating ∼37% lower dementia risk with lithium."
- **Problem:** The 1.2 million figure covers the full qualitative synthesis; the 37% estimate is pooled from only four cohort studies. Pairing them in the headline overstates the base for the effect estimate.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Pooling four cohort studies, lithium users had about 37% less dementia" — with the 1.2 million total kept in the body."

**Issue B. Drinking-water framing not specified in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "two ecological studies of water lithium levels pointed the same direction"
- **Abstract says:** "Two ecological studies using incidence rate ratios (IRR 0.830, 95% CI: 0.810-0.850) were directionally consistent."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not state that the ecological studies concerned lithium in drinking water. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "two ecological studies" without specifying the exposure source, or attribute the detail to the full text."

### Study 4: Virtual reality "positive psychology" sessions eased stress for older Korean immigrants who faced discrimination
**PMID:** 42802583 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Attributing the effect to VR plus tailoring specifically** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "this is early evidence that immersive technology plus cultural tailoring — not one without the other — is what made the program land"
- **Abstract says:** "The results underscore the significance of advanced technologies with culturally tailored features to address mental health disparities and highlight the benefits of PPI."
- **Problem:** The two-arm trial compared the combined intervention against a wait-list; it did not dismantle the components, so it cannot show that either element was necessary.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the program combined immersive technology with cultural tailoring, and that the trial could not separate the contribution of each."

**Issue B. Feasibility/uptake claims beyond abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It is one of the first trials to show that adults in their 80s will readily use a VR headset"
- **Abstract says:** "Our study examines the feasibility and preliminary efficacy... We conducted descriptive statistics to assess the feasibility"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports that feasibility was assessed but gives no completion, adherence or acceptability figures, so "will readily use" and "older participants completed it" cannot be checked. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the authors assessed feasibility descriptively without citing specific uptake results."

### Study 7: Staying active still protects thinking skills in your 80s and 90s, 30-year Jerusalem study finds
**PMID:** 42803718 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Odds ratios assigned to wrong age intervals** — Severity: Major

- **As written:** "Being sedentary at age 78 roughly tripled the odds of cognitive impairment by 85 (OR 2.90), and being sedentary at 70 more than doubled the odds by 78 (OR 2.61)"
- **Abstract says:** "PA-S at baseline was found to have a statistically significant association with cognitive impairment at a later age between ages 78-85, 85-90: OR 2.61 (95%CI 1.38-4.82); OR 2.90 (95%CI 1.63-5.11), respectively."
- **Problem:** The abstract's OR 2.61 applies to the 78-to-85 interval and OR 2.90 to the 85-to-90 interval. The digest shifts both estimates one interval younger and reports a 70-to-78 finding the abstract never presents.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Being sedentary at 78 more than doubled the odds of cognitive impairment by 85 (OR 2.61), and being sedentary at 85 roughly tripled the odds by 90 (OR 2.90).""

**Issue B. Design described as a single followed cohort** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Researchers followed a community-dwelling cohort born in 1920-1921 in Jerusalem from 1990 to 2023, assessing participants at ages 70, 78, 85, 90, 95 and 100"
- **Abstract says:** "The Jerusalem Cross-sectional and Longitudinal Study (1990-2023) prospectively follows a representative community-dwelling cohort born 1920-1921, combining follow-up of the original cohort with repeated recruitment of same-birth cohort participants at each age."
- **Problem:** The design mixes longitudinal follow-up with fresh recruitment at each wave (which is why the n rises from 604 to 1,222), so the wave-by-wave activity percentages do not track the same people dropping out of exercise over time.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that new same-birth-cohort participants were recruited at each assessment age, and that the activity-rate figures are cross-sectional snapshots."

### Study 8: Only about 1 in 9 memory clinic patients qualified for the new Alzheimer's drug
**PMID:** 42803707 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Reasons for ineligibility not reported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "most people with memory complaints — including many diagnosed with Alzheimer's — do not qualify, often because they lack amyloid or have risk factors that make treatment unsafe"
- **Abstract says:** "Overall, 21 patients (11.7% of the total cohort and 45.6% of amyloid-positive individuals) fulfilled all AUR criteria for donanemab."
- **Problem:** The abstract gives eligibility proportions but does not break down which criteria patients failed, so the stated reasons are inference. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "State the eligibility proportions without specifying which criteria caused exclusion."

**Issue B. Diagnostic discordance stated at individual level** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this study found that clinical Alzheimer's diagnoses and amyloid test results disagreed often"
- **Abstract says:** "A clinical diagnosis of AD was made in 78 (43.6%), and 46 (25.7%) were amyloid-positive."
- **Problem:** The abstract gives only aggregate counts and does not cross-tabulate clinical diagnosis against amyloid status, so patient-level disagreement rates are not reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say that clinical AD diagnoses (44%) outnumbered amyloid-positive results (26%) in this cohort."

### Study 9: Frail stroke patients are far less likely to get clot-busting drugs — and it isn't explained by age alone
**PMID:** 42803571 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Non-significant mortality result framed as no association** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this study found no evidence that frailty consistently predicts worse outcomes in women"
- **Abstract says:** "High frailty was associated with an increased risk of 30-day mortality in males (2.09, 1.52-2.87), but not females (1.22, 0.90-1.66)."
- **Problem:** The female estimate is a point estimate of 1.22 with a confidence interval running up to 1.66 — imprecise rather than demonstrably null. Telling readers there is "no evidence" of worse outcomes overstates the certainty of a non-significant result.
- **Suggested fix:** ""In women, the link between frailty and 30-day death was not statistically significant, though the estimate still pointed upward.""

### Study 10: Mild cognitive trouble often reverses — and a handful of everyday advantages predict who bounces back
**PMID:** 42802990 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Intervention recommendation from observational data** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "the study supports treating depression screening, sleep hygiene and structured social programming as cognitive interventions, not just quality-of-life extras"
- **Abstract says:** "Resource advantage was associated with more favorable observed cognitive-domain transitions after localized impairment, but not clinically verified recovery."
- **Problem:** The study reports associations between a composite resource-count score and observed test-score transitions; it did not test any intervention, so it cannot support recommending these as cognitive interventions.
- **Suggested fix:** "Frame it as an association worth testing — e.g., "these modifiable factors travel with better cognitive trajectories and are plausible intervention targets for future trials.""

**Issue B. Frequency of recovery not quantified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "in this large cohort, people frequently returned to unimpaired status"
- **Abstract says:** "Origin-state-restricted logistic models estimated apparent recovery from localized impairment and progression to multidomain impairment... 15,611 intervals from 5,997 respondents"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports odds ratios for apparent recovery but never gives the rate or share of participants who recovered, so "frequently" is not supported by reported figures.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say that recovery transitions occurred and were modeled, without characterizing how common they were."

**Issue C. Cognitive-state labels inferred** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "(OR 1.40 from single-domain impairment, 1.32 from two-domain)"
- **Abstract says:** "higher odds of S1-to-S0 apparent recovery (OR = 1.40...) and S2-to-S0 apparent recovery (OR = 1.32...)"
- **Problem:** The abstract labels the states only as S0-S3 and does not define what S1 and S2 represent, so the single-domain/two-domain gloss cannot be verified. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the state definitions to the full text or describe them generically as lesser and greater degrees of localized impairment."

### Study 14: A tape measure and a two-minute walk test can flag which heart failure patients are at highest risk
**PMID:** 42800123 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. SPPB called a "two-minute walk test"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A tape measure and a two-minute walk test can flag which heart failure patients are at highest risk"
- **Abstract says:** "The Short Physical Performance Battery (SPPB) is a validated predictor of adverse outcomes in HF."
- **Problem:** The SPPB is a three-part battery (balance, short gait-speed walk, chair rises), not a two-minute walk test — a distinct, differently named instrument. The digest body describes it correctly, so only the headline misleads.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword the headline to "a tape measure and a short physical performance test" or "a brief balance-and-walking test.""

**Issue B. "Need" for treatment vs. need for assessment** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "can identify which older heart failure patients most need nutrition support and rehabilitation"
- **Abstract says:** "This bedside tool may help identify patients who could benefit from further nutritional and rehabilitation assessment."
- **Problem:** The abstract claims the tool flags candidates for further assessment; the digest upgrades this to identifying who needs the interventions themselves.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "which older heart failure patients most need further nutrition and rehabilitation assessment.""

### Study 15: A thicker brain in midlife doesn't guarantee sharper thinking after 80
**PMID:** 42784930 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies midlife-to-late-life tracking** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A thicker brain in midlife doesn't guarantee sharper thinking after 80"
- **Abstract says:** "We analyzed cross-sectional data from cognitively healthy adults in the Human Connectome Project-Aging/Aging Adult Brain Connectome, spanning middle (36-59 years), early older (60-79), and late older (80 +) adulthood."
- **Problem:** The data are cross-sectional — different people at different ages — so nobody's midlife cortical thickness was followed to age 80. The headline reads as a within-person prediction, which the digest's own caveat says the study cannot support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like "Cortical thickness tracks with thinking in midlife — but the link fades in the oldest adults.""

**Issue B. Extension to "90s"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It challenges the assumption that brain structure seen on a scan explains who stays sharp into their 80s and 90s"
- **Abstract says:** "spanning middle (36-59 years), early older (60-79), and late older (80 +) adulthood"
- **Problem:** The abstract defines the oldest group only as 80+, with no upper bound specified; the reference to the 90s goes slightly beyond what is stated.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "into their 80s and beyond" or simply "in late life.""

### Study 16: A routine blood count may hold early clues about who develops Parkinson's disease
**PMID:** 42778869 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Sex difference reported without null interaction test** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Baseline inflammation levels — not changes over time — carried the predictive signal, and estimates were stronger in men."
- **Abstract says:** "Effect estimates were larger in male participants, while female estimates were imprecise as a result of limited events; no statistically significant biomarker-by-sex interaction was detected."
- **Problem:** The digest presents the male-female difference as a finding without noting the authors found no statistically significant interaction by sex, which makes the difference uninterpretable as a real effect modification.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "though the difference between sexes was not statistically significant" after "stronger in men.""

**Issue B. Prodromal criteria not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "452 people with prodromal Parkinson's disease — early warning signs such as REM sleep behavior disorder or loss of smell, but no diagnosis"
- **Abstract says:** "We conducted a retrospective cohort analysis of 452 prodromal participants from the Parkinson's Progression Markers Initiative."
- **Problem:** The specific prodromal features (RBD, hyposmia) are plausible for PPMI but are not stated in the abstract. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the examples to the PPMI enrollment criteria generally, or drop the specifics."
