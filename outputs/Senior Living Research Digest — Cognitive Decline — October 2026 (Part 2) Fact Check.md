### Study 3: Longer Reproductive Years Linked to Slower Cognitive Decline in 14,000 Nurses
**PMID:** 41979543 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Race of cohort not stated in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Participants were almost entirely White female health professionals, limiting generalizability."
- **Abstract says:** "We assessed cognitive change (1995-2008) with four telephone interviews ... in 14,217 Nurses' Health Study participants (mean age 74.3 y)"
- **Problem:** The abstract confirms the participants are female health professionals but gives no racial or ethnic breakdown, so the 'almost entirely White' characterization is unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to 'Participants were female health professionals in a cohort with limited racial and ethnic diversity' or cite the full text for the demographic breakdown."

### Study 6: Frailty Drags Down Thinking Skills Even When Alzheimer's Markers Say Otherwise
**PMID:** 42816830 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal phrasing for cross-sectional data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "suggesting frailty itself can produce memory complaints without Alzheimer's pathology"
- **Abstract says:** "lower frailty levels are associated with a higher likelihood of an AD-compatible CSF profile, suggesting that frailty assessment may aid in identifying patients at greater risk"
- **Problem:** The study is cross-sectional and cannot show that frailty 'produces' cognitive symptoms; the digest's own caveat says direction cannot be established, so the body text contradicts it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'suggesting frailty may contribute to memory complaints in the absence of Alzheimer's pathology, though direction cannot be determined.'"

**Issue B. 'Lowest of all' comparison not tested** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "people who were both frail and amyloid-positive scored lowest of all"
- **Abstract says:** "Compared to robust (FI ≤ 0.11) amyloid-negative subjects, individuals with higher FI (>0.11) and CSF amyloid positivity had significantly lower MMSE scores."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports only the contrast against robust/amyloid-negative participants, not a ranking against every other subgroup.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'scored significantly lower than robust, amyloid-negative patients.'"

### Study 8: When Your Doctors Talk to Each Other, Your Brain May Benefit
**PMID:** 42875818 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies benefit from a baseline cross-sectional association** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "When Your Doctors Talk to Each Other, Your Brain May Benefit"
- **Abstract says:** "higher perceived CC (β = 0.25, p < 0.05) and technical support (β = 0.22, p < 0.05) were associated with higher baseline cognitive scores ... Perceived CC and technology use characterized cognitively robust older adults ... These associations are observational and do not establish causality."
- **Problem:** Perceived care coordination was linked only to higher baseline cognition, not to slower decline, and the authors frame it as a marker of already-robust cognition rather than a benefit.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as 'Better-coordinated care goes along with stronger thinking scores — though which comes first isn't clear.'"

### Study 10: Staying Active Still Protects Thinking Skills Well Into Your 90s
**PMID:** 42803718 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Follow-up interval misstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Being sedentary at baseline roughly tripled the odds of cognitive impairment eight years later between ages 78-85 (OR 2.61) and 85-90 (OR 2.90)"
- **Abstract says:** "PA-S at baseline was found to have a statistically significant association with cognitive impairment at a later age between ages 78-85, 85-90: OR 2.61 (95%CI 1.38-4.82); OR 2.90 (95%CI 1.63- 5.11), respectively."
- **Problem:** The intervals are roughly seven years (78→85) and five years (85→90), not eight; 'roughly tripled' also overstates an odds ratio of 2.61.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write 'roughly two-to-three times the odds of cognitive impairment five to seven years later (OR 2.61 from age 78 to 85; OR 2.90 from 85 to 90).'"

**Issue B. Headline claims protection and extends past significant results** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Staying Active Still Protects Thinking Skills Well Into Your 90s"
- **Abstract says:** "At ages 90-95 a similar not statistically significant effect was found. ... PA is associated with better cognitive aging and lower risk for cognitive impairment in advanced age."
- **Problem:** 'Protects' is causal language for an observational cohort, and the significant associations ended at age 90 — the 90–95 analysis was not statistically significant, so 'well into your 90s' goes beyond the data.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'Staying Active Is Still Linked to Better Thinking Skills in the Late 80s' and keep the non-significant 90–95 result visible."

### Study 11: Losing All Your Teeth Is Linked to Faster Progression From Mild Memory Problems to Dementia
**PMID:** 42803715 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Death risk transition not specified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Edentulism was associated with a 26% higher risk of moving from mild cognitive impairment to dementia and a 27% higher risk of death"
- **Abstract says:** "Edentulism significantly increased the risk of transition from baseline to death (HR = 1.27, 95% CI:1.13, 1.42) and from MCI to dementia (HR = 1.26, 95% CI:1.12, 1.40)."
- **Problem:** The 27% figure applies specifically to the transition from baseline (normal cognition) to death in the multi-state model, not to overall mortality risk across all states.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "a 27% higher risk of dying from the baseline cognitive state" or similar."

### Study 12: Four in Five Patients Stuck With Lecanemab for the Full 18 Months, Japanese Clinic Reports
**PMID:** 42664573 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Blood tau testing not studied** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "whether spinal-fluid or blood tau testing could help gauge your personal risk of ARIA before you commit to 18 months of infusions"
- **Abstract says:** "ARIA ... was significantly associated with ... elevated baseline cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)-phosphorylated tau (ptau) 181 (ptau181; ≥78.6 pg/ml)"
- **Problem:** Only CSF p-tau181 was evaluated; the abstract says nothing about a blood-based tau test predicting ARIA.
- **Suggested fix:** "Limit the suggestion to spinal-fluid p-tau181, which is what the study measured."

**Issue B. Infusion schedule not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "biweekly infusions plus surveillance MRIs over 18 months is a substantial transportation and scheduling commitment"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract describes an 18-month course and baseline MRI but does not state infusion frequency or a surveillance MRI schedule."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only (though consistent with standard lecanemab dosing).
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the dosing schedule to standard prescribing information rather than to this study."

### Study 13: Sleep Quality in Your 50s and 60s Tracks With How Your Thinking Holds Up a Decade Later
**PMID:** 42628340 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Direction of microstructure correlations** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "specific brain-wave features such as sleep spindles and slow waves tracked with better processing speed, attention and global cognition"
- **Abstract says:** "Fast spindle amplitude and frequency, slow-wave characteristics, and N2 and REM coherence correlated with processing speed, attention, and global cognition."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports correlations without specifying the direction, so "better" is an inference.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say these features "correlated with" processing speed, attention and global cognition, without asserting the direction."

**Issue B. N2 described as deep sleep** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "deeper sleep stages (N2 and N3)"
- **Abstract says:** "A higher proportion of N1 sleep was linked to poorer executive function, whereas N2 and N3 were positively associated with cognitive outcomes."
- **Problem:** N2 is conventionally light rather than deep sleep; grouping it as a "deeper sleep stage" is imprecise.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "more time in N2 and N3 sleep (as opposed to the lightest stage, N1)"."

### Study 14: A Treatable Cause of Walking, Bladder and Memory Trouble That Often Gets Mistaken for Dementia

### Study 15: Brief Brain Games on Your Phone Can Spot Memory Changes Years Before Standard Tests
**PMID:** 42954140 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline overstates comparison with standard tests** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Brief Brain Games on Your Phone Can Spot Memory Changes Years Before Standard Tests"
- **Abstract says:** "Our findings indicate that a digital multiday learning paradigm combined with computational modeling of response times sensitively detects age-related cognitive decline over short timescales (<3 years), which are difficult to capture in conventional study designs relying on infrequent assessments."
- **Problem:** The study detected normal age-related slowing in cognitively unimpaired volunteers; it did not run a head-to-head comparison against standard tests or show earlier detection of a disease process, so "years before standard tests" is unsupported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like "Short, repeated phone-based memory tasks pick up age-related cognitive change in under three years"."

**Issue B. Age range inferred** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "209 cognitively unimpaired older adults (mean age 73.6, range roughly mid-60s to 90s; 65% women)"
- **Abstract says:** "A total of 209 cognitively unimpaired older adults (mean age 73.6, SD 8.2 years; n=136, 65.1% female)"
- **Problem:** The abstract gives a mean and SD, not a range; the stated range is an estimate the abstract cannot confirm.
- **Suggested fix:** "Report "mean age 73.6 (SD 8.2)" and drop the invented range."

### Study 16: Disrupted Body Clocks, Poor Sleep and Low Mood Tied to More Memory Complaints — Especially in Men
**PMID:** 42567211 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Changes" implies longitudinal biomarker data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and with changes in plasma markers including GFAP, the amyloid Aβ42/40 ratio, and neurofilament light"
- **Abstract says:** "CircS was dose-responsively linked to GFAP and nonlinearly to Aβ42/40 (Pnonlinear = 0.029) and NfL (Pnonlinear = 0.030)."
- **Problem:** This was a cross-sectional study, so it measured differences in biomarker levels, not changes over time.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "with differences in plasma marker levels, including GFAP, the Aβ42/40 ratio and neurofilament light"."

### Study 18: Blood Vessel Responsiveness in the Brain May Flag Thinking Problems That Shrinkage Alone Doesn't
**PMID:** 42803706 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Lobe-specific result stated generally** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "older adults with greater decline in blood vessel responsiveness had poorer executive function regardless of whether their gray matter had shrunk"
- **Abstract says:** "significant CVR × GMV interaction effects on executive function were found in parietal and occipital lobes ... In the occipital lobe, compared with the "low CVR decline + low GMV loss" group, "high CVR decline + low GMV loss" (β=-0.47, p = 0.004) and "high CVR decline + high GMV loss" (β=-0.60, p < 0.001) ... were significantly associated with poorer executive function."
- **Problem:** The "regardless of gray matter" pattern was specific to the occipital lobe (interactions only in parietal and occipital), not a whole-brain finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "in the occipital lobe" to the first sentence, as the digest does in the following sentence."

### Study 21: A new blood test for tau predicted memory decline and brain shrinkage years ahead
**PMID:** 42906069 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unspecified prediction window in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A new blood test for tau predicted memory decline and brain shrinkage years ahead"
- **Abstract says:** "NT1 levels were negatively associated with hippocampal volume (B [SE] = -264.49 [125.99], p = 0.036) and predicted longitudinal atrophy (B [SE] = -42.00 [16.90], p = 0.013)."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports longitudinal associations but never states the follow-up duration, so "years ahead" is unverifiable from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "predicted later memory decline and brain shrinkage" or add "over follow-up (duration not reported in the abstract)"."

**Issue B. Mediation estimate presented without its uncertainty** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Accelerated hippocampal atrophy accounted for roughly 70% of the link between high baseline NT1 and later memory decline"
- **Abstract says:** "B [95% CI]proportion mediated = 70.1% [32.6%-155.3%], p = 0.004"
- **Problem:** The point estimate is correct, but the confidence interval spans 33% to over 100%, so "roughly 70%" conveys more precision than the data support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a qualifier, e.g. "accounted for an estimated 70% (a wide and imprecise range) of the link"."
