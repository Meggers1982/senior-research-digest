### Study 1: How you fill out a questionnaire may hint at dementia years before diagnosis
**PMID:** 42760255 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Lead time not stated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "How you fill out a questionnaire may hint at dementia years before diagnosis"
- **Abstract says:** "Associations with subsequent dementia risk were examined within each cohort using Cox proportional hazards models... Routine survey responses contain behavioral signals associated with emerging dementia risk."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports prediction of incident dementia but gives no time interval, so "years before diagnosis" cannot be verified from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "may hint at dementia risk before diagnosis" or add "in long-term follow-up" without specifying years."

**Issue B. Indicator described as "statistical outliers"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "five kinds of "atypical" answering behavior on ordinary questionnaires — skipped items, seemingly random answers, statistical outliers and logically inconsistent answer patterns"
- **Abstract says:** "item-nonresponse (overall hazard ratio [HR] per SD increase = 1.06), random response errors (HR = 1.25), multivariate outlier responses (HR = 1.31), and incompatible response patterns indexed by Guttman errors and person-fit statistics (HRs = 1.22-1.25). Extreme responding was not significantly associated"
- **Problem:** Only four indicator types are named in the list before extreme responding is mentioned separately; readers may miscount the "five kinds." Wording is otherwise faithful.
- **Suggested fix:** "Name all five explicitly, e.g. "...inconsistent answer patterns, and extreme responding.""

### Study 2: For patients in their 70s, some donor kidneys may offer no survival edge over staying on dialysis
**PMID:** 42759976 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Five European countries"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Using registry data from five European countries"
- **Abstract says:** "European Renal Association Registry data from five countries or regions: Catalonia (Spain), Denmark, France, Norway, and the UK."
- **Problem:** Catalonia is a region of Spain, not a country; the abstract deliberately says "countries or regions."
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "five European countries or regions (Catalonia, Denmark, France, Norway and the UK).""

**Issue B. Date range attached to transplantation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "39,244 of whom received a deceased donor kidney between 2000 and 2019"
- **Abstract says:** "Patients aged ≥20 years receiving dialysis and waitlisted for a first deceased donor kidney-only transplant between 2000 and 2019."
- **Problem:** The 2000-2019 window describes when patients were waitlisted, not when transplants occurred.
- **Suggested fix:** ""...waitlisted between 2000 and 2019, 39,244 of whom received a deceased donor kidney.""

**Issue C. Causal verb for an emulated-trial estimate** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Standard-criteria donor kidneys improved five-year survival at every age."
- **Abstract says:** "Standard criteria donor kidney transplantation was consistently associated with a survival benefit compared with continued dialysis."
- **Problem:** The abstract's own conclusion uses associational language for this observational target trial emulation; "improved" states causation.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Standard-criteria donor kidneys were associated with higher estimated five-year survival at every age.""

**Issue D. Waiting-list claim not from the study** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "knowing that declining one offer does not remove the person from the list"
- **Abstract says:** "(no statement on allocation or offer-refusal policy)"
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only, and offer-refusal rules vary by country and program.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute to general transplant practice or drop, e.g. "asking the transplant team what declining an offer would mean for their place on the list.""

### Study 3: Too little or too much sleep both track with weaker thinking in older couples — and your own sleep matters most
**PMID:** 42755049 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline age framing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Too little or too much sleep both track with weaker thinking in older couples"
- **Abstract says:** "The analytical sample comprised 4,260 couples (8,520 individuals) aged ≥45 years living a married, two-person, mixed-sex household."

### Study 4: Prism glasses plus tablet games beat pencil-and-paper cognitive therapy after stroke, trial finds
**PMID:** 42759357 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Single-center claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "should note the trial was small, single-center"
- **Abstract says:** "(the abstract describes a randomized controlled clinical trial of 60 participants but does not state the number of sites; authors are affiliated with Santa Lucia IRCCS in Rome and the University of Palermo)"
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only; the author affiliations span two cities, so the site count cannot be confirmed.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "single-center" or replace with "small, with no site count reported in the abstract.""

**Issue B. Hemineglect timing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The device group showed greater and lasting gains in hemineglect and language"
- **Abstract says:** "The experimental group showed significant advantages for hemineglect at 3 months and for language at all posttreatment time points."
- **Problem:** The hemineglect advantage was significant specifically at 3 months, whereas the language advantage held at all post-treatment points; "lasting" applied to both blurs that distinction.
- **Suggested fix:** ""...significant advantages for hemineglect at 3 months and for language at every post-treatment assessment.""

### Study 6: Three months of online exercise lifted mood in midlife — but memory didn't budge
**PMID:** 42754613 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Attendance claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "it proved feasible and well-attended"
- **Abstract says:** "a structured, instructor-led, live online exercise programme in midlife is feasible and may produce temporally staged adaptations"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports feasibility but says nothing about attendance or adherence rates.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "and well-attended" or say "the authors describe the programme as feasible.""

**Issue B. Direction of the cell-culture effect unstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the blood of exercisers measurably changed how hippocampal progenitor cells behaved in the dish"
- **Abstract says:** "significant intervention-by-time effects were detected... for the percentage of proliferative cells in vitro (β = -0.23, adjusted p = 0.02)"
- **Problem:** The effect on proliferation was negative; framing it only as "markers of new brain cell growth" changing could leave readers assuming growth increased.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the percentage of proliferating cells decreased, and that the authors interpret this as exercise remodelling the circulating milieu rather than as more neurogenesis."

### Study 7: Where older adults look when they walk — and why it may signal fall risk
**PMID:** 42753670 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Dropped "mixed" qualifier** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Differences in the timing of eye movements were most pronounced in older adults at high risk of falls."
- **Abstract says:** "Results regarding eye movement timing were mixed, but most pronounced in older adults who were at high risk of falls and certain patient populations."
- **Problem:** Omitting "mixed" makes an inconsistent body of findings sound settled.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Findings on eye-movement timing were mixed overall, though differences were most pronounced in older adults at high risk of falls.""

**Issue B. Fall-risk link transferred to floor-gazing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "If you notice you have to stare at the floor to walk safely, that's a common age-related shift — and one worth mentioning at your next checkup, because studies link it to higher fall risk."
- **Abstract says:** "more and longer fixations at immediately relevant locations and less time engaging in exploratory gaze behaviors... Results regarding eye movement timing were mixed, but most pronounced in older adults who were at high risk of falls"
- **Problem:** The abstract ties fall risk to eye-movement timing differences, not specifically to looking down at the ground; the digest also adds "the ground" where the abstract says "immediately relevant locations."
- **Suggested fix:** "Say that gaze timing differences were most pronounced in older adults already at high fall risk, and that the review does not show floor-gazing itself predicts falls."

### Study 8: Thirty years, 596 trials, and post-stroke cognitive rehab research still isn't getting better
**PMID:** 42759356 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Thirty years" time span** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Thirty years, 596 trials, and post-stroke cognitive rehab research still isn't getting better"
- **Abstract says:** "MEDLINE, Embase, CINAHL, PsycINFO, Scopus, and Web of Science were searched for English RCTs published up to December 31, 2024."
- **Problem:** The abstract gives only an end date, not a start year, so a 30-year span cannot be verified.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the figure or say "decades of trials.""

**Issue B. Omits the improvements reported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "post-stroke cognitive rehab research still isn't getting better"
- **Abstract says:** "The number of RCTs and sample sizes increased steadily over time. However, there was no improvement in methodological quality, measured by PEDro scores, and female recruitment declined over time."
- **Problem:** Trial volume and sample size did increase; only quality, sex representation and outcome standardization failed to improve. The headline generalizes beyond that.
- **Suggested fix:** ""...trial quality and sex representation still aren't improving.""

### Study 14: What makes 'age-friendly' hospital care actually work — and the one piece hospitals keep struggling with
**PMID:** 42760752 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Barrier ranked beyond what the abstract states** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The hardest part, even at high performers, was genuinely eliciting and acting on 'What Matters' to each patient during a hospital stay."
- **Abstract says:** "Barriers included: challenges meaningfully eliciting and integrating "What Matters" into inpatient care; difficulty identifying short-term patient impact; discipline-specific staffing issues; and lack of AFHS inpatient teams to consult about implementation."

**Issue B. "Most likely to be skipped" not supported** — Severity: Minor

### Study 21: Hearing loss marked a faster path to cognitive decline in 6,650 older Chinese adults
**PMID:** 42753934 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Interaction generalized beyond moderate decline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Notably, hearing loss weakened the usual protective link between a high starting cognitive score and staying stable — a strong baseline score offered less reassurance among those with hearing loss."
- **Abstract says:** "The MMSE-by-hearing interaction was significant for moderate decline (RRR 1.252, 95% CI 1.079-1.453), indicating a weaker inverse association between baseline MMSE and moderate-decline membership among participants with hearing loss."
- **Problem:** The digest presents the interaction as a general weakening of the baseline-cognition advantage, but the abstract reports it as significant only for the moderate-decline trajectory, not for rapid decline.
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify: "For the moderate-decline group only, hearing loss weakened the usual link between a higher baseline score and avoiding decline.""

**Issue B. "Untreated" hearing loss not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a person with good cognitive test scores but untreated hearing loss should not be considered low-risk"
- **Abstract says:** "Hearing loss was associated with higher relative likelihoods of moderate decline (RRR 1.262...) and rapid decline (RRR 1.551...)"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports baseline hearing loss without distinguishing treated from untreated; the digest adds a qualifier the data do not support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "untreated" and say "baseline hearing loss" or simply "hearing loss.""

### Study 22: A 10-minute tablet test could cut unnecessary Alzheimer's blood testing by two-thirds
**PMID:** 42757183 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Test format and duration not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A 10-minute tablet test could cut unnecessary Alzheimer's blood testing by two-thirds"
- **Abstract says:** "We evaluated the Altoida NeuroMarker Platform as a digital cognitive triage tool before plasma p-tau217 testing."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not describe the assessment's duration or the device used. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "a digital cognitive test" unless the 10-minute/tablet detail can be sourced from the full text."

**Issue B. "Unnecessary" overstates the rule-out** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the digital screen ruled out 64.4% of people with a 90.2% negative predictive value"
- **Abstract says:** "the Altoida NeuroMarker ruled out 64.4% of participants (negative predictive value 90.2%)"
- **Problem:** The body text is accurate, but the headline's framing of the 64.4% as "unnecessary" testing ignores that roughly 1 in 10 of those ruled out would have been positive — those blood tests would not have been unnecessary.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe the headline as "could reduce the number of people sent for Alzheimer's blood testing by about two-thirds" and note the ~10% false-negative rate implied by the 90.2% NPV."

### Study 23: Thousands of older Americans are sedated by paramedics each year — usually with the drug class geriatricians warn about
**PMID:** 42754548 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Usually" overstates 52.8%** — Severity: Minor

**Issue B. ALS restriction omitted** — Severity: Minor
