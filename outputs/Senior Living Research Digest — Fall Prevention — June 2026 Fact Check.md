## Study 1: Belly Fat Plus Weak Muscles: A Double Threat for Falls in Older Adults
**PMID:** 42223007 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Misrepresented risk increase for men with D/AO** — Severity: Major

- **As written:** "men with both conditions faced more than double the fall risk (126% increase)"
- **Abstract says:** "among males, the association between D/AO and fall risk was most pronounced (HR = 2.26, 95% CI: 1.58-3.23)"
- **Problem:** HR of 2.26 represents a 126% increase, which is correctly stated. However, the digest says "more than double" which implies >100% increase. HR 2.26 = 126% increase, so "more than double" is accurate. ✓ This is actually correct on review.

**Issue B. Mischaracterized women's risk pattern** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "women's risk was driven primarily by muscle weakness alone"
- **Abstract says:** "among females, the risk was primarily driven by D/NAO (HR = 1.32, 95% CI: 1.07-1.62)"
- **Problem:** D/NAO means "only dynapaenia" (muscle weakness without abdominal obesity). The digest accurately captures this finding.

No significant issues found on detailed review. Framing is consistent with the abstract.

**Verdict revised:** ✅ Accurate

## Study 7: Hip Strength and Diuretics: Overlooked Factors in Predicting Injurious Falls
**PMID:** 42296796 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Improvement characterization** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "improved prediction accuracy by 3-3.5 percentage points each"
- **Abstract says:** "Diuretic use (ΔC = 0.035, 95% CI: 0.008-0.061) and hip abductor strength (ΔC = 0.030, 95% CI: 0.002-0.057)"

## Study 9: Pelvic Floor Training Reduces Incontinence and May Help Prevent Falls
**PMID:** 42229075 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline/framing implies fall prevention evidence** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Pelvic Floor Training Reduces Incontinence and May Help Prevent Falls"
- **Abstract says:** "Physical therapy interventions, particularly PFMT and multimodal programs, are effective in reducing urinary incontinence severity... with multimodal approaches offering added value for enhancing functional independence and fall prevention"
- **Problem:** The abstract mentions fall prevention in the context of multimodal programs (which combine PFMT with mobility, strength, or fall-prevention training), not PFMT alone. The headline and body slightly overstate the direct fall prevention evidence for pelvic floor training specifically.

Study details verified: 20 RCTs, 2,002 women, ≥60 years, 13 countries.

## Study 11: High Exercise Adherence Key to Fall Prevention Success
**PMID:** 42176369 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Fall rate reduction at high adherence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "50% reduction in fall rates versus 22% for lower-adherence programs"
- **Abstract says:** "Programmes achieving adherence ≥75% were independently associated with greater reductions in fall incidence (IRR = 0.501)"
- **Problem:** IRR 0.501 = approximately 50% reduction. The abstract doesn't explicitly state "22% for lower-adherence programs" but the overall IRR of 0.776 (22% reduction) would apply to the pooled analysis. The comparison is somewhat imprecise but directionally correct.

Study details verified: 41 publications, 20,202 participants, 22% fall rate reduction (IRR 0.776), 9% risk reduction (RR 0.914), 25% injury reduction (IRR 0.754).
