### Study 6: Women Carry a Heavier Global Burden of Vision Loss — and the Gap Isn't Closing
**PMID:** 42521465 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unmeasured treatment-access explanation stated as fact** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "cataract in particular is treatable — meaning much of the excess burden among older women reflects care that never happened rather than disease that couldn't be helped"
- **Abstract says:** "In 2023, females had higher YLD rates than males for blindness and vision loss (BVI) (300.9 vs 262.8 per 100 000; +14.5%), cataract (95.1 vs 75.0; +26.7%)... Higher GII and lower GDI were significantly associated with larger sex disparities... The strong association with GDI and GII indicates these disparities are modifiable and linked to structural factors."
- **Problem:** The study analyzed modeled burden estimates and country-level gender indices; it did not measure cataract surgery uptake or unmet care, so attributing the excess to "care that never happened" is an inference presented as established.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "the authors link the gap to structural gender inequality, which may include unequal access to treatment" and note that treatment rates were not measured."

**Issue B. Age framing beyond the data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this analysis suggests women worldwide are disproportionately going without treatment"
- **Abstract says:** "we analysed sex-disaggregated age-standardised years lived with disability (YLD) rates and prevalence for major blinding diseases"
- **Problem:** Estimates are age-standardized across the whole population, not specific to older women, and no treatment measure was analyzed.
- **Suggested fix:** "State that the analysis covers all ages and reports burden rather than treatment receipt."

### Study 7: Childhood Hardship Reaches Late-Life Memory Partly Through Uncorrected Vision Problems
**PMID:** 42371784 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Omitted counterintuitive result** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Emotional abuse and community violence were directly linked to poorer cognition even after accounting for mediators. Physical neglect and parental divorce or death worked indirectly, operating through less education, depression, and untreated vision loss."
- **Abstract says:** "Emotional neglect and household violence showed unexpected positive direct associations."
- **Problem:** The digest reports only the harmful associations and leaves out the reported positive associations for two of the eleven adversities, which bear on how consistently adversity predicts cognition.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a sentence noting that emotional neglect and household violence showed unexpected positive direct associations."

**Issue B. Outcome named as memory** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Childhood Hardship Reaches Late-Life Memory Partly Through Uncorrected Vision Problems"
- **Abstract says:** "the direct paths from individual ACEs to a latent global cognition factor"
- **Problem:** The outcome was a latent global cognition factor, not memory specifically.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "late-life cognition" in the headline."

### Study 11: Diabetes-Related Blindness Is Rising Worldwide — and Type 2 Diabetes Is Driving It
**PMID:** 42476825 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported superlative** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The fastest-growing cause of preventable vision loss in older adults is tied to a condition many already have"
- **Abstract says:** "Globally, age-standardised prevalence (ASPR) of diabetes-related blindness and vision loss rose from 48.8 per 100 000 in 1990 to 67.3 per 100 000 in 2021"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports trends only for diabetic retinopathy; it makes no comparison with other causes of vision loss, so "fastest-growing cause" is unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "a rising cause of preventable vision loss in older adults" or note the comparison is not made in this paper."

**Issue B. Where the trend is worsening** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the trend is worsening precisely in high-income countries where screening should be strongest"
- **Abstract says:** "The highest relative increase was recorded in the high SDI group (45.8%)... High-middle SDI and middle SDI groups showed the most notable increase in period and cohort effect, whereas low-middle and low SDI groups experienced favourable period and cohort risks"
- **Problem:** The largest relative prevalence rise was in high SDI countries, but the worsening period and cohort risk was most notable in high-middle and middle SDI groups — so "the trend is worsening precisely in high-income countries" blurs two different findings.
- **Suggested fix:** "Distinguish the two: the steepest relative rise in prevalence was in high-SDI countries, while the worst period/cohort risk trends were in high-middle and middle-SDI countries."

**Issue C. SDI described as income** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Prevalence climbed steadily with advancing age across all income groups"
- **Abstract says:** "Increasing ASPR with advancing age was consistent across SDI groups"
- **Problem:** Socio-demographic index combines income, education and fertility; calling the strata "income groups" is a simplification.
- **Suggested fix:** "Refer to "socio-demographic development groups (SDI)" at first mention, then plain-language shorthand."

**Issue D. Screening recommendation not tested** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a dilated eye exam or retinal photo at the interval your doctor recommends is one of the few genuinely proven ways to catch retinopathy before it steals sight"
- **Abstract says:** "A secondary analysis of data from the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2021 study... Age-standardised prevalence rates, relative percentage changes, net drift and local drift were analysed."
- **Problem:** The study analyzed burden trends and did not evaluate screening; "genuinely proven" imports evidence from outside this paper.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the screening advice to existing guidelines rather than to this study's findings."

### Study 12: Gene Therapy Repairs the Retina in an Inherited Blinding Disease — A Preview of Where Eye Medicine Is Heading
**PMID:** 42269152 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Extrapolation to age-related disease** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "strengthening the case that gene- and cell-based therapies will eventually reach more common causes of age-related sight loss"
- **Abstract says:** "In this study of subretinal gene therapy with scAAV8-hRS1 in 12 patients with X-linked retinoschisis, there were no reports of adverse events of grade 3 or higher or ocular inflammation. Further clinical testing of scAAV8-hRS1 is warranted."
- **Problem:** The trial enrolled children and adolescents with a single-gene recessive disease and concluded only that further testing is warranted; it says nothing about age-related macular degeneration or other common causes of late-life sight loss.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "this is a proof of concept in a rare inherited disease; whether the approach transfers to common age-related conditions is untested.""

**Issue B. Headline claims retinal repair** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Gene Therapy Repairs the Retina in an Inherited Blinding Disease"
- **Abstract says:** "SS-OCT imaging showed closure of the macular schisis cavity by week 13... No clinically meaningful changes in the function of photoreceptor and bipolar cells or macular retinal sensitivity were observed in the treated eyes."
- **Problem:** Anatomical closure occurred but retinal function measures did not improve, so "repairs the retina" overstates; the body and caveats do note this, but the headline stands alone for skimmers.
- **Suggested fix:** "Consider "Gene Therapy Closes Retinal Cavities in an Inherited Blinding Disease — Structure Improved, Function Largely Unchanged.""

### Study 13: Half of people with mid-stage macular degeneration have vision problems standard eye charts miss
**PMID:** 42386395 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline: eyes vs people and overlapping proportions** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Half of people with mid-stage macular degeneration have vision problems standard eye charts miss"
- **Abstract says:** "Proportions iAMD eyes breaching reference limits: 50% for AULCSF, 36% for BCVA, 39% for LCVA"
- **Problem:** The 50% refers to intermediate AMD eyes, not people, and it is the total share failing contrast sensitivity limits — some of those eyes also failed the acuity chart, so 50% is not the proportion "missed" by standard charts.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "Half of intermediate AMD eyes fall outside normal contrast sensitivity limits — well above the 36% flagged by standard eye charts.""

**Issue B. Omitted LCVA null result** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Eyes with more structural risk markers on OCT scans showed worse contrast sensitivity at low spatial frequencies, but their standard acuity scores did not differ."
- **Abstract says:** "risk scores were significantly correlated with AULCSF (p<0.02) and CS at low frequencies (1-3 cpd, p<0.005) but not BCVA and LCVA (p>0.05)"
- **Problem:** Low-contrast visual acuity also failed to track with risk scores, which slightly weakens the digest's emphasis on LCVA testing in the story angle.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that risk scores tracked with contrast sensitivity but not with either standard or low-contrast acuity."

### Study 14: Newer targeted breast cancer drugs damaged the eye surface in 86% of patients — usually within a month
**PMID:** 42331364 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Timing overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "usually within a month"
- **Abstract says:** "Ocular surface toxicity occurred in 85.7% (18/21) of patients after HER2-ADC therapy, with a mean onset at 28.0±8.4 days"
- **Problem:** A mean onset of 28 days (SD 8.4) means roughly half of cases appeared after a month; "usually within a month" implies most did so.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "on average about four weeks after starting therapy.""

**Issue B. Population characteristics not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Findings come from a breast cancer population that skews younger and predominantly female, so they may not generalize to all older adults"
- **Abstract says:** "21 HER2-positive breast cancer patients receiving HER2-ADC therapy were enrolled."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports no age or sex distribution, so the description of the cohort's age and sex profile is unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "State that the abstract does not report participant ages, so applicability to older adults is unclear."
