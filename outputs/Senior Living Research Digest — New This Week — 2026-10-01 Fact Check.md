### Study 3: Berries and brain function: pooled trial data show a measurable cognitive benefit from anthocyanins
**PMID:** 41351717 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Cognitive domain labels simplified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "smaller but significant gains appeared for memory, attention, processing speed, verbal fluency, and working memory"
- **Abstract says:** "significant benefits for visuospatial processing/reasoning and attention (SMD = 0.37...), processing and psychomotor speed (SMD = 0.19...), verbal speed and fluency (SMD = 0.21...), episodic memory (SMD = 0.30...), and working memory (SMD = 0.24...)"
- **Problem:** The domain reported as 'attention' is actually a combined 'visuospatial processing/reasoning and attention' domain, and 'memory' was specifically episodic memory. The relabeling slightly misdescribes what was measured.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use the abstract's domain names, e.g. 'episodic memory, visuospatial processing/attention, processing speed, verbal fluency and working memory'."

**Issue B. Unverifiable comparative claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "one of the few dietary signals in cognition to survive meta-analysis"
- **Abstract says:** "Although these results are promising, further well-designed RCTs are needed to validate these outcomes and consolidate the current evidence base."
- **Problem:** The abstract makes no claim about how anthocyanins compare with other dietary interventions in meta-analyses; this is editorial and unverifiable from the abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the comparative flourish or attribute it clearly as context outside this paper, and note the authors' own call for further validation."

### Study 11: Two decades on, 9/11 responders show brain thinning that tracks with how much dust they breathed
**PMID:** 42805945 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Effect size characterized as "strong"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Greater exposure severity was strongly associated with thinner cerebral cortex (R = -0.38)"
- **Abstract says:** "Cortical thickness was associated with WTC-NESI (R = -0.38, p < 0.001) that survived multivariable adjustment."
- **Problem:** An R of -0.38 is conventionally a weak-to-moderate correlation; the abstract uses no intensity adjective. "Strongly" inflates the magnitude for readers.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Greater exposure severity was associated with thinner cerebral cortex (R = -0.38)"."

### Study 12: People with HIV on long-term treatment show less thymus shrinkage than their peers — and less inflammation with it
**PMID:** 42775653 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies PWH had less inflammation than controls** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "People with HIV on long-term treatment show less thymus shrinkage than their peers — and less inflammation with it"
- **Abstract says:** "PWH had higher odds of a higher thymus grade compared to population controls (adjusted-OR = 1.75...). Higher thymus grade was associated with greater sjTREC content, and among PWH with higher proportions of naïve CD4+ and CD8+ T cells, lower proportions of senescent T cells, and lower IL-6 and hs-CRP levels."
- **Problem:** The inflammation comparison was within people with HIV (thymus grade vs. IL-6/hs-CRP), not PWH versus the general-population controls. The headline reads as though PWH had lower inflammation than their peers.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "...less thymus shrinkage than their peers — and, among those with HIV, a preserved thymus tracked with lower inflammation.""

### Study 13: A self-guided app eased hot flashes as well as many non-drug options — in a trial run by the app's maker
**PMID:** 42641440 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported comparison with other non-drug treatments** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "A self-guided app eased hot flashes as well as many non-drug options"
- **Abstract says:** "Fully decentralized, open-label, parallel-group pilot randomized controlled trial... digital cognitive behavioral therapy produced clinically meaningful improvements in bothersome vasomotor symptoms and related menopausal symptoms over 12 weeks compared with usual care"
- **Problem:** The only comparator was usual care; no other non-drug option was tested, so no claim of equivalence to "many non-drug options" can be made from this trial.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change the headline to something like "A self-guided app eased bothersome hot flashes compared with usual care — in a trial run by the app's maker.""

**Issue B. Device-related adverse events omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and no serious adverse events"
- **Abstract says:** "No serious adverse events occurred; 5.9% reported device-related events."
- **Problem:** The digest reports the absence of serious events but omits that 5.9% reported device-related events, giving a slightly cleaner safety picture than the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "no serious adverse events, though 5.9% reported device-related events.""

### Study 18: Lighter weights built just as much muscle as heavier ones in older women — but roughly a third responded to neither
**PMID:** 42551773 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline and body give different non-responder rates** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "but roughly a third responded to neither ... About 61% of participants were classified as responders under at least one scheme, while 26% responded to neither"
- **Abstract says:** "Overall, 61.1% of participants were classified as responders in at least one condition, whereas 38.9% were classified as non-responders ... 25.9% as non-responders under both conditions."
- **Problem:** The headline's "roughly a third" reflects the 38.9% figure while the body uses the 25.9% (both-conditions) figure; the two statements describe different denominators and read as contradictory.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use one figure consistently, e.g. headline "about a quarter responded to neither," matching the 25.9% non-responders-under-both-schemes statistic cited in the body."

**Issue B. "Rarely changed" understates switching** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "switching load intensity rarely changed a person's responder status"
- **Abstract says:** "Only seven participants (25.9%) changed responsiveness status between interventions."
- **Problem:** Roughly one in four participants did change status; "rarely" is a stronger word than the abstract's "consistent across conditions for most participants."
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "switching load intensity changed responder status for only about a quarter of participants.""

### Study 19: Living with breathlessness: 33 studies reveal how much of the struggle is social, not just physical
**PMID:** 42372996 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Shame" not named in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this review shows that withdrawal, shame, and lost identity are a central part of the burden that inhalers and oxygen alone do not address"
- **Abstract says:** "Five interrelated themes were identified: overwhelming physical and sensory experience, psychological and emotional impact, coping strategies from self-management to healthcare and social support, social and relational impact, and contextual and environmental influences."

- **Suggested fix:** "Replace "shame" with the abstract's own language, e.g. "withdrawal, emotional distress, and threats to identity.""

### Study 20: Blood tests for Alzheimer's predict who will lose their sense of smell — 15 years ahead

**Issue A. Predictive framing for an association study** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Blood tests for Alzheimer's predict who will lose their sense of smell — 15 years ahead"
- **Abstract says:** "Blood-based biomarkers of AD were consistently associated with faster olfactory decline in older adults, particularly in the highest biomarker quartiles."

### Study 21: Nursing home residents with dementia are less likely to get hospice care at the end of life
**PMID:** 41176320 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal/mechanistic framing contradicts caveat** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "For families and facility leaders: this review points to referral patterns, not resident needs, as the likely bottleneck."
- **Abstract says:** "Hospice enrollment varied across studies but was consistently lower for NH decedents with cognitive disabilities compared to those without. Future research should ... clarify targets for intervention, and address barriers to care."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports an enrollment gap but does not identify referral patterns as the driver; the digest's own caveat concedes the review "cannot ... determine how much of the gap reflects unmet need versus appropriate decisions," so the body text overstates what the review establishes.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to: "this review documents a consistent enrollment gap, though it cannot tell us how much reflects referral practice versus appropriate individual decisions.""

**Issue B. Racial comparison narrowed beyond the data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and whether Black residents are being referred at the same rate as White residents"
- **Abstract says:** "Factors associated with increased likelihood of hospice enrollment at the time of death included ... White race (n = 2)"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports only that White race was associated with higher enrollment in two studies; it does not break out Black residents specifically, and the finding concerns enrollment rather than referral.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "whether residents of colour are enrolling in hospice at the same rate as White residents (a pattern seen in two of the included studies).""

**Issue C. Screening numbers compressed** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers screened 479 articles and reviewed 12 US studies"
- **Abstract says:** "We screened 479 titles and abstracts and reviewed 36 full-text articles. Twelve studies met the inclusion criteria."
- **Problem:** The digest skips the full-text review step; "reviewed 12" conflates inclusion with review. Meaning is largely preserved but the figures are slightly misdescribed.
- **Suggested fix:** ""screened 479 titles and abstracts, read 36 full texts, and included 12 US studies""

### Study 22: Fitness trackers alone barely move the needle for older adults — it's the human coaching that works
**PMID:** 42442508 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Stronger than the authors' tentative conclusion** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "it's the human coaching that works"
- **Abstract says:** "Tentatively, and based on a very limited evidence base, wearables may have greater impact when embedded within broader behavioral systems, incorporating feedback, coaching, or interactive components, rather than when used in isolation as passive monitoring tools."
- **Problem:** The authors frame the coaching conclusion as tentative and hypothesis-level; the headline (and the body line "the device is only useful as part of a package") presents it as established. The caveats do note the evidence is preliminary, but the headline does not hedge.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "— the evidence tentatively favours pairing them with human coaching" and change "is only useful as part of a package" to "may be more useful as part of a package.""

**Issue B. Predictive language for an associational observation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Comfort and whether you find the device genuinely useful also predicted whether people stuck with it."
- **Abstract says:** "Adherence and psychosocial outcomes appeared related to comfort and perceived usefulness among older adults, though larger and more robust trials are needed to confirm these patterns."
- **Problem:** "Predicted" implies a tested predictive relationship; the abstract only says adherence "appeared related to" comfort and perceived usefulness and calls for confirmation.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Comfort and whether you find the device genuinely useful also appeared related to whether people stuck with it.""

### Study 24: Walking programs cut blood sugar nearly as much as some medications — if you set a step target
**PMID:** 42456792 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Drug comparison not in the study** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Walking programs cut blood sugar nearly as much as some medications"
- **Abstract says:** "walking training significantly reduced glycated hemoglobin levels, with a combined effect of -0.48% ... Walking training can be used as an effective auxiliary treatment for the management of type 2 diabetes"
- **Problem:** The meta-analysis compared walking with control, not with any glucose-lowering drug; the headline introduces a benchmark the study never tested and could suggest to readers that walking substitutes for medication (the abstract frames walking as auxiliary treatment).
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "Walking programs lowered HbA1c by about half a point — and more when a step target was set.""

**Issue B. Clinical-meaningfulness claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A 0.48-point drop in HbA1c is clinically meaningful for diabetes management"
- **Abstract says:** "walking training significantly reduced glycated hemoglobin levels, with a combined effect of -0.48% (95% CI: -0.60 to -0.36, P < 0.00001)"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports statistical significance and calls walking an effective auxiliary treatment, but does not characterize the 0.48% reduction as clinically meaningful.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the judgement: "a drop of this size is generally regarded as clinically useful, though the authors describe walking as an auxiliary treatment rather than a substitute for medication.""
