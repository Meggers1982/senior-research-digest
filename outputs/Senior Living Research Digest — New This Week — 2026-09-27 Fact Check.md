### Study 3: Japan's first real-world look at lecanemab: 1 in 9 patients had brain imaging abnormalities, most without symptoms

- **As written:** "Japan's first real-world look at lecanemab"
- **Abstract says:** "Real-world evidence for lecanemab remains limited, particularly among Asian populations."

### Study 4: Dozens of tools exist to safely cut medications at the end of life — but almost none have been properly tested
**PMID:** 42799555 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline overstates number of tools** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Dozens of tools exist to safely cut medications at the end of life"
- **Abstract says:** "Of 4108 papers, 34 studies met inclusion criteria. Eighteen deprescribing tools were identified."

### Study 5: Nerve problems starting after 50 turn out to be inherited far more often than doctors assumed
**PMID:** 42799779 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Denominator described imprecisely** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "reviewed 10 years of genetic testing in 642 patients whose neuropathy symptoms began after age 50"
- **Abstract says:** "We retrospectively reviewed all patients who underwent next-generation sequencing (NGS) for suspected hereditary neuropathy between 2015 and 2025... Among 642 patients tested after age 50, 57 had genetically confirmed late-onset CMT (8.9%)."
- **Problem:** The 642 were patients tested after age 50 because hereditary neuropathy was already suspected, not simply everyone with symptom onset after 50; the distinction matters for how the 8.9% should be read (the Caveats do note preselection, but the body text does not).
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "642 patients over 50 who were referred for genetic testing because hereditary neuropathy was suspected.""

### Study 8: Adderall fails a 12-week test for cognitive problems in MS, contradicting earlier promising data
**PMID:** 42800227 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Direction of secondary outcomes assumed** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Some secondary measures of cognitive and psychosocial fatigue, and quality of life at the 20 mg dose, did favor treatment."
- **Abstract says:** "There was a significant difference over 12 weeks on the MFIS-cognitive (p = 0.018) and MFIS-psychosocial (p = 0.018), as well as on the SF-36 (20 mg MAS-XR group compared to the placebo group (p = 0.041))."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports significant differences but does not state which direction they went, so "did favor treatment" is an inference.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "differed significantly from placebo (direction not specified in the abstract)" or attribute the favorable direction explicitly as inferred."

### Study 9: Stepping over an obstacle takes real brainpower — and in older adults the extra effort often isn't enough
**PMID:** 42785654 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Safety claim beyond the evidence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "it's genuinely safer to stop talking, put down the phone and give the step your full attention"
- **Abstract says:** "Task characteristics, such as unanticipated obstacles and dual-task conditions, were associated with further increases in PFC activation."
- **Problem:** The review measured cortical activation and gait performance in the lab, not falls or injury, so "genuinely safer" asserts an outcome the studies did not test — and it sits against the digest's own caveat that no link to real-world fall rates was established.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "these findings suggest it may help to pause conversation and give the step your full attention.""

### Study 16: Behavioral symptoms in Alzheimer's don't cluster the way researchers thought — and the groupings shift as the disease progresses
**PMID:** 42775783 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Cluster shift attributed to disease progression** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Behavioral symptoms in Alzheimer's don't cluster the way researchers thought — and the groupings shift as the disease progresses"
- **Abstract says:** "Whether a previously identified NPS cluster pattern remains transportable after expansion of an amyloid PET-positive cohort is uncertain. ... The original four-cluster solution was only partly reproduced. ... Delusion formed a single-domain cluster and, together with aggression, aberrant motor behavior, and disinhibition, showed the largest stage-associated prevalence differences between Clinical Dementia Rating (CDR) 0.5 and CDR 1.0-2.0."
- **Problem:** The reorganization of clusters occurred when the analysis was rerun in an expanded cohort, not as patients progressed. The abstract reports only stage-associated prevalence differences for individual symptoms, not clusters that regroup with advancing disease — and the digest's own caveat says progression within individuals cannot be established.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "…and the groupings didn't hold up when the cohort was expanded," and in "Why it matters" say individual symptoms differ in frequency by dementia stage rather than that clusters change as disease advances."

**Issue B. Direction of stage differences asserted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "symptoms like delusions, aggression, disinhibition and restless motor behavior became markedly more common as dementia severity increased"
- **Abstract says:** "showed the largest stage-associated prevalence differences between Clinical Dementia Rating (CDR) 0.5 and CDR 1.0-2.0"
- **Problem:** The abstract states these symptoms had the largest prevalence differences between stages but does not specify the direction of those differences.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say these symptoms "differed most in frequency between very mild and moderate dementia stages.""

### Study 18: Air pollution linked to slightly higher Parkinson's risk in 5.3 million older adults
**PMID:** 42778501 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Exposure increment not specified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Each incremental rise in fine particulate matter (PM2.5) and ozone was associated with about a 2% higher rate of Parkinson's"
- **Abstract says:** "each IQR increase in PM2.5 (4.3 μg/m3) and O3 (7.3 ppb) was associated with a modestly increased PD incidence (PM2.5: HR = 1.018, 95% CI: 1.008-1.028; O3: HR = 1.019, 95% CI: 1.006-1.033)"
- **Problem:** "Each incremental rise" leaves readers unable to judge how large an exposure increase produces the 2% figure; the estimate is per interquartile-range increase.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "each interquartile-range increase (4.3 µg/m³ of PM2.5, 7.3 ppb of ozone) was associated with about a 2% higher rate.""

**Issue B. Authors' characterization of the NO2 result** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Nitrogen dioxide, by contrast, showed an inverse association once fully adjusted — a result the authors flag as inconsistent."
- **Abstract says:** "However, NO2 showed an inverse association after full adjustment. ... effect sizes were modest and should be interpreted with caution."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports the inverse NO2 association but does not attribute an "inconsistent" label to it; the word "inconsistent" appears only in describing the prior literature.
- **Suggested fix:** "State the inverse NO2 finding and note the authors urge cautious interpretation, without attributing a specific "inconsistent" flag."

### Study 20: Having a sense of purpose keeps boredom at bay — and that may protect memory eight years later
**PMID:** 42773782 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal phrasing in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Having a sense of purpose keeps boredom at bay — and that may protect memory eight years later"
- **Abstract says:** "Purpose in life was associated with less boredom measured concurrently (Median β=-0.31, p < 0.001) and prospectively (Median β=-0.24, p < 0.001) ... Boredom mediated the prospective association between purpose in life and better episodic memory measured 8 years later"
- **Problem:** "Keeps boredom at bay" states a causal effect for what is an observational association in a survey cohort; the second clause is hedged but the first is not.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "A sense of purpose goes with less boredom — and that may track with better memory eight years later.""

### Study 21: One in five brain cancer patients got aggressive treatment in their final three months — and the youngest were most affected
**PMID:** 42776059 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Study period described as years of death** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Dutch researchers reviewed medical records for 310 patients with glioblastoma (median age 66) who died between 2015 and 2026"
- **Abstract says:** "deceased patients diagnosed with glioblastoma, who survived at least 3 months after diagnosis and received routine care at a single tertiary hospital between January 2015 and February 2026"
- **Problem:** The 2015–2026 window describes when patients received care at the center, not necessarily when they died; the digest also omits the inclusion criterion that patients had to survive at least 3 months after diagnosis.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say patients 'who received care at a single Dutch tertiary hospital between January 2015 and February 2026 and survived at least three months after diagnosis.'"

**Issue B. Timing of palliative care conversations not measured** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "this study suggests those conversations tend to happen too late"
- **Abstract says:** "Identification of patients at risk may support targeted palliative care interventions."
- **Problem:** The study measured healthcare-utilization indicators in the last three months of life; it did not assess when or whether palliative care conversations occurred, so it cannot show they happen 'too late.'
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to: 'the authors suggest identifying at-risk patients could support earlier, targeted palliative care.'"

### Study 22: Cancer patients' biggest regrets trace back to how the decision was made, not just how treatment turned out
**PMID:** 42772893 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies ranking of contributors** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Cancer patients' biggest regrets trace back to how the decision was made, not just how treatment turned out"
- **Abstract says:** "DR in oncology is influenced by clinical outcomes and quality of the decision-making process."
- **Problem:** A descriptive scoping review did not quantify or rank which factors contribute most; the abstract presents outcomes and process as jointly influential.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as 'Regret in cancer care tracks with how the decision was made, not only how treatment turned out.'"

**Issue B. 'Consistently' overstates synthesis** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "were consistently higher among patients who experienced treatment side effects and distress"
- **Abstract says:** "Higher DR was associated with treatment-related morbidity, psychological distress, inadequate information, limited involvement in decision-making and discordance between expectations and outcomes."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports associations synthesized descriptively across heterogeneous studies; it does not claim consistency across all of them.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop 'consistently' or say 'were commonly higher among…'"

### Study 23: Fitbits and text messages get high marks from rural older adults with high blood pressure — but the trial was tiny
**PMID:** 42773923 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal explanation for retention** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "recruitment and retention were near-perfect because residents helped shape the program and it was delivered through health systems they already used"
- **Abstract says:** "Key pre-design themes included as follows: accessibility, intervention delivery through existing health systems, social isolation… Recruitment (90.9%), retention (100%), attendance (93%), adherence (90%), and intervention fidelity (98.5%) rates… were promising."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports design themes and feasibility rates separately; a single-arm feasibility study with 10 participants cannot attribute retention to the co-design process or delivery channel.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change 'because' to 'in a program that residents helped shape and that was delivered through health systems they already used.'"
