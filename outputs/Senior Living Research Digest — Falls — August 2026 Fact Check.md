# Fact-Check Report

### Study 1: Falls Accelerate Frailty Progression and Raise Mortality Risk in Older Adults
**PMID:** 42361731 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. Framing is consistent with the abstract. The digest accurately reports the sample size (12,501 participants), study design (longitudinal, multi-state survival framework), time period (2011-2020), and the key statistical findings (18% higher rate robust to pre-frail, 51% higher rate pre-frail to frail). The "sentinel events" language is drawn directly from the abstract. Caveats appropriately note the observational design and self-reported measures.

### Study 3: Exergaming May Reduce Falls in Older Adults, Offering an Engaging Alternative
**PMID:** 42536979 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Fall rate reduction figure** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "exergaming was associated with about a 47% lower overall fall rate"
- **Abstract says:** "incidence rate ratio 0.53, 95% CI 0.41-0.68"
- **Problem:** An IRR of 0.53 represents a 47% lower rate, which is mathematically correct. However, this pooled analysis combined all control groups and had substantial heterogeneity (I²=76%), which the digest does note. No correction needed.

No factual errors found upon closer review. The digest accurately reports 9 RCTs, N=1,385, risk ratio 0.75 for fallers at 12 months, and appropriately notes substantial heterogeneity. The caveats are comprehensive.

### Study 5: Low Vitamin D Linked to Depression, Falls, and Slower Recovery After Stroke
**PMID:** 42542873 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Effect size for functional recovery** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Each 10 ng/mL decrease in vitamin D was associated with 17% worse odds of functional recovery"
- **Abstract says:** "Each 10 ng/mL lower 25(OH)D was associated with worse 3-month mRS shift (common OR 1.17, 95% CI 1.05-1.31)"
- **Problem:** The mRS shift is a measure of disability distribution, not "functional recovery" per se. An OR of 1.17 for worse mRS shift means 17% higher odds of a worse outcome, which the digest reasonably paraphrases. This is acceptable shorthand.

**Issue B. Fall risk effect size** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a 29% higher fall risk"
- **Abstract says:** "low 25(OH)D predicted first fall (HR 1.29, 95% CI 1.07-1.55)"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports HR 1.29 per 10 ng/mL decrease, and the digest correctly states this as 29% higher risk. Accurate.

No significant errors. Framing is appropriate for observational data.

### Study 10: Social Isolation Raises Fall Risk, With Depression and Cognition as Pathways
**PMID:** 42249766 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal language** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "loneliness and cognitive decline appear to be part of the causal chain"
- **Abstract says:** "depressive symptoms and cognitive function serve as distinct mediators"
- **Problem:** While mediation analysis can suggest pathways, this observational study cannot establish causation. The phrase "causal chain" overstates what the data support.
- **Suggested fix:** "loneliness and cognitive decline appear to be part of the pathway linking isolation and falls"
