## Study 2: Zumba plus brain games improve cognition in older Malaysian adults—especially with regular attendance
**PMID:** 42398018 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Overstated specificity of combined intervention effect** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Those who attended at least half of the combined sessions gained roughly 2 points on the MoCA, a clinically meaningful improvement."
- **Abstract says:** "median Montreal Cognitive Assessment scores increasing by approximately 2 points in both adherent groups" (cognitive AND combined groups)
- **Problem:** The digest implies the 2-point gain was specific to the combined group, but the abstract states both the cognitive-only and combined adherent groups showed ~2-point gains.
- **Suggested fix:** "Those who attended at least half of the cognitive or combined sessions gained roughly 2 points on the MoCA, a clinically meaningful improvement."

**Issue B. Missing key finding about group equivalence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "This study suggests that combining physical activity with cognitive games may be more beneficial than either alone"
- **Abstract says:** "All intervention types were associated with improvements in cognitive performance over time, with comparable gains across groups" and "time × group interaction: F3,92=0.57, P=.64" (non-significant)
- **Problem:** The "Why it matters" statement suggests the combined approach is superior, but the abstract explicitly states gains were "comparable across groups" with no significant group interaction.
- **Suggested fix:** "This study suggests that physical activity, cognitive training, or a combination can all improve cognition—and that showing up regularly is key."

## Study 9: Frailty and advanced age strongly predict both cognitive decline and death in Brazilian cohort
**PMID:** 42268499 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Slightly imprecise odds ratio for frailty and cognitive decline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Frailty tripled the odds of declining cognition"
- **Abstract says:** "Frailty increased the odds of both cognitive deficit (OR = 3.1; 95% CI 1.86-5.18)"
- **Problem:** "Tripled" is slightly imprecise for OR = 3.1, though this is a very minor rounding/language difference.
- **Suggested fix:** "Frailty was associated with about three times higher odds of declining cognition"

## Study 10: Combined antioxidant diet and normal sleep duration associated with lower cognitive impairment risk
**PMID:** 42241923 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Imprecise characterization of OBS association** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Those with the highest OBS (reflecting more antioxidants and healthier behaviors) had 22% lower odds of impairment."
- **Abstract says:** "OR = 0.78, 95% CI: 0.63-0.96" which equals 22% lower odds
- **Problem:** None—this is accurate. However, the abstract notes "the association for the moderate OBS tertile was not statistically significant," suggesting a threshold rather than dose-response relationship that the digest doesn't capture.
- **Suggested fix:** No revision necessary; the caveat could note the non-linear relationship if desired.

## Study 13: GLP-1 diabetes medications associated with higher repair cells and better cognition in older adults with diabetes
**PMID:** 42177747 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Sample size detail** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Italian researchers compared 70 older adults with type 2 diabetes"
- **Abstract says:** "A cross-sectional study was conducted on 154 T2DM patients aged 60-80 years"
- **Problem:** The total sample was 154; the 70 (35 per group) were the matched subset used for the comparative analysis.
- **Suggested fix:** "Italian researchers studied 154 older adults with type 2 diabetes (ages 60–80) and, after matching for age, weight, and blood sugar control, compared 35 taking GLP-1 receptor agonists plus metformin to 35 taking metformin alone."

## Study 15: Higher pulse pressure associated with faster cognitive decline—but only in those with elevated blood pressure
**PMID:** 42178211 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Sample size discrepancy** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Using data from 2,787 older adults (mean age 74)"
- **Abstract says:** "N = 2,802 adults" in methods, "Among 2,787 older adults (mean age 73.65" in results
- **Problem:** The digest uses the analytic sample (2,787) which is appropriate, but the mean age should be 73.65, not 74.
- **Suggested fix:** "Using data from 2,787 older adults (mean age 74)"—this is acceptable rounding, or use "mean age 73.7" for precision.

## Study 17: Remote sleep-and-memory app feasible for older adults with cognitive concerns
**PMID:** 42275527 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Incomplete characterization of willingness predictors** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Higher education and anxiety levels predicted better task completion, while lower global cognition was associated with less willingness to participate."
- **Abstract says:** "Lower willingness to participate was associated with lower global cognitive scores, poorer sleep behavior, and clinical factors. Higher task completion was associated with greater education and greater anxiety levels."
- **Problem:** The digest conflates willingness to participate (initial consent) with task completion (completing both sessions). These are distinct outcomes with different predictors.
- **Suggested fix:** "Lower willingness to initially participate was associated with lower cognition and poorer sleep. Among those who did participate, higher education and anxiety predicted better task completion."
