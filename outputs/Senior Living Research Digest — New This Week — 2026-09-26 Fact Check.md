### Study 1: An old local anesthetic matched ketamine for severe cancer pain — with a third of the side effects
**PMID:** 42791055 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unreported adverse-effect specifics** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a lignocaine-first approach could reduce the agitation, hallucinations and monitoring burden that often accompany ketamine in frail patients"
- **Abstract says:** "Adverse effects were significantly more frequent with ketamine than lignocaine (30.3% vs 9.1%; p=0.03)."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports only the overall adverse-effect rate; it does not break out agitation, hallucinations or monitoring burden, so the specific harms named here are not verifiable from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'could reduce the adverse effects reported with ketamine (30.3% vs 9.1%)' without naming specific symptoms, or attribute the symptom list to general ketamine pharmacology rather than to this trial."

### Study 5: Cancer history didn't hurt short-term results of a common heart valve procedure — but long-term survival was lower
**PMID:** 42786380 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mechanism stated more confidently than the evidence** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "with longer-term survival differences likely reflecting the cancer and overall frailty rather than the repair"
- **Abstract says:** "the available evidence does not establish the factors responsible for this association, which could be mediated by frailty, CV comorbidity, treatment-related comorbidity mitral regurgitation (MR) characteristics, or other residual confounders"
- **Problem:** The authors explicitly say the drivers of the excess mortality are not established; the digest converts a list of possible mediators into a 'likely' explanation.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as 'with the reasons for the longer-term survival gap unestablished — the authors list frailty, cardiovascular and treatment-related comorbidity and residual confounding as possibilities.'"

**Issue B. Contextual claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Older adults with both a leaky mitral valve and a cancer history are often steered away from valve procedures"
- **Abstract says:** "Their impact in patients with cancer has recently gained attention in observational studies but remains poorly characterized."
- **Problem:** The claim that such patients are routinely denied procedures is not stated in the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute this to general clinical background or soften to 'cancer history can complicate decisions about valve procedures.'"

### Study 9: Anxiety in people with mild cognitive impairment is linked to faster progression to dementia
**PMID:** 42789462 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. 'Faster progression' vs higher risk of conversion** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Anxiety in people with mild cognitive impairment is linked to faster progression to dementia"
- **Abstract says:** "Anxiety was significantly associated with an increased risk of all cause dementia, HR = 1.25 ... and Alzheimer's disease, HR = 1.32"
- **Problem:** The pooled estimates describe an increased risk of converting to dementia, not a measured rate or speed of progression.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'is linked to a higher risk of converting to dementia.'"

**Issue B. Effect measures described as study designs** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A separate pooled analysis using odds ratios did not reach significance, so the picture is not uniform across study designs."
- **Abstract says:** "while pooled odds ratios did not show a significant association OR = 1.15; 95% CI, 0.84-1.57; p = 0.39"
- **Problem:** All included studies were cohort studies; the discrepancy is between effect measures (HR vs OR), not between different study designs.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change 'across study designs' to 'across effect measures.'"

### Study 10: Losing an adult child measurably damages older parents' health, sleep and memory
**PMID:** 42791223 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for an observational study** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Losing an adult child measurably damages older parents' health, sleep and memory"
- **Abstract says:** "Adjusted fixed-effects analyses showed that the onset of child death was associated with increased depressive symptoms, poorer sleep quality, more PTSD symptoms, poorer self-rated health, reduced cognitive functioning, and marginally lower life satisfaction."
- **Problem:** 'Damages' asserts causation, which the abstract states only as association and which the digest's own caveat says is 'inferred rather than proven.'
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as 'Losing a child tracks with worse health, sleep and memory in older parents.'"

**Issue B. Causal framing in reader advice** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "the changes you may notice in your sleep, memory, and physical health are documented consequences of that loss"
- **Abstract says:** "the onset of child death was associated with worsening psychological, sleep, physical health, and cognitive outcomes"
- **Problem:** 'Documented consequences' states cause and effect; the within-person design strengthens inference but the authors report association only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'are changes researchers have documented among bereaved parents.'"

**Issue C. 'Adult child' not specified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Using three waves of the HAALSI study ... including 432 who experienced the death of a child during follow-up"
- **Abstract says:** "Child death was modelled as a time-varying indicator capturing the onset of child death between survey waves."
- **Problem:** The abstract refers to child death generally; it does not state that the children who died were adults, as the headline asserts.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop 'adult' from the headline, or note that the age of the deceased children is not specified in the abstract."

### Study 13: A simple memory test predicted who would decline fastest among adults with Down syndrome
**PMID:** 42786385 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Imprecise description of the p-tau217 threshold** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a baseline p-tau217 level above the group median was linked to a 3.6-fold higher risk of progressing to a more advanced clinical stage"
- **Abstract says:** "individuals with values one unit above the sample median showed a 3.56-fold increased risk of progression to a more advanced clinical stage"
- **Problem:** The hazard applies to values one unit above the sample median, not simply to anything above the median, so the digest slightly broadens who the 3.6-fold risk applies to.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write 'a baseline p-tau217 level one unit above the sample median was linked to a 3.6-fold higher risk'."

**Issue B. Single-center claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Small N (57), single-center, observational design cannot establish causation"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract reports 57 adults with DS assessed at baseline and 24 months; it does not state the number of recruiting sites (ethics approval from La Princesa University Hospital)."
- **Problem:** Single-center status is plausible but not stated in the abstract. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop 'single-center' or soften to 'recruited through a single hospital-based programme (site count not stated in the abstract)'."

### Study 14: AI tools that predict who will survive a broken hip aren't ready for the bedside yet
**PMID:** 42771090 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Design label omits meta-analysis** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Orthopedic researchers in the UK systematically reviewed 40 studies published between 2010 and 2026"
- **Abstract says:** "Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning for Outcome Prediction After Osteoporotic Hip Fracture: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis of Prediction Model Performance"
- **Problem:** The paper is titled a systematic review and meta-analysis; the digest describes it only as a systematic review, and the caveat states no pooled outcome is reported (the abstract text indeed reports no pooled estimates, but the design label is incomplete).
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'systematic review and meta-analysis of prediction model performance' and note that the abstract does not report pooled estimates."

**Issue B. Overstated conclusion wording** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "mean none are ready for routine clinical use"
- **Abstract says:** "Current AI/ML models show promise for outcome prediction after hip fracture, but inconsistent external validation, limited calibration reporting, incomplete uncertainty estimates and variable methodological quality restrict clinical implementation."
- **Problem:** The authors say these problems 'restrict clinical implementation' and that models 'show promise'; 'none are ready' is a firmer blanket statement than the abstract makes.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'currently restrict clinical implementation' or 'are not yet ready for routine clinical use, in the authors' assessment'."

**Issue C. Unverifiable claim about current hospital practice** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "hospitals are increasingly using algorithmic risk scores to guide surgical decisions and discharge planning"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract describes model development and validation literature and states that methodological problems restrict clinical implementation; it makes no claim about uptake in hospitals."
- **Problem:** This assertion about routine hospital use is not supported by the abstract and sits awkwardly beside the review's finding that implementation is restricted. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to 'as such tools move toward hospital use' or attribute the uptake claim elsewhere."

### Study 15: Depression at 85 doesn't predict dementia — but getting outdoors might protect against it
**PMID:** 42791168 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Effect-size ranking not supported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the ability to get outside on your own at least weekly was one of the strongest markers of staying dementia-free"
- **Abstract says:** "Factors associated with the development of dementia were: having been outside independently ≥ 1 per week at baseline (inversely associated), stroke between baseline and follow-up, and MMSE at baseline."
- **Problem:** The abstract lists three factors without reporting effect sizes or ranking them, so calling outdoor activity 'one of the strongest markers' (and later 'the standout protective factor') implies a comparison the abstract does not report.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say it was the only factor inversely (protectively) associated with dementia in the adjusted model, without ranking strength."

**Issue B. Direction of MMSE association inferred** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "What did independently predict dementia: lower baseline MMSE scores"
- **Abstract says:** "Factors associated with the development of dementia were ... and MMSE at baseline."
- **Problem:** The abstract names MMSE at baseline as an associated factor but does not state the direction; the digest supplies 'lower', which is plausible but not verifiable from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write 'baseline MMSE score' or add 'presumably lower scores' with a hedge."

### Study 19: More muscle mass, fewer spine problems — a half-million-person analysis points to lean mass, not grip strength
**PMID:** 42790802 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Intervention leap from Mendelian randomization** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "shifts attention toward resistance training and protein adequacy rather than grip dynamometry"
- **Abstract says:** "both WBLM and ALM exhibited protective causal effects against lumbar slipped discs and spinal stenosis, whereas no significant causal effect of grip strength on SDDs was detected ... systemic muscle status, particularly muscle mass, may play a crucial role in SDDs progression"
- **Problem:** The study examined genetically predicted lean mass, not exercise or protein intake; it does not show that increasing muscle mass through training or diet changes spinal disease risk.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as 'suggests muscle mass rather than grip strength is the more relevant marker for spinal outcomes', and note the study did not test any exercise or nutrition intervention."

### Study 21: Weak thigh muscles and stiff tendons predicted knee arthritis two years before pain began
**PMID:** 42784800 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Direction of effect not stated in abstract** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Weak thigh muscles and stiff tendons predicted knee arthritis two years before pain began"
- **Abstract says:** "SHAP analysis identified KESt as the dominant predictor (mean /SHAP/ 0.314, 95% CI 0.251-0.388), with PTS as the second most important predictor (0.166, 95% CI 0.128-0.200)."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports only that knee extensor strength and patellar tendon stiffness were important predictors; it does not report whether lower strength or higher stiffness was the risk direction. The headline and the story angle ("weaker thigh muscles precede knee arthritis by years") assert a direction that cannot be verified from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to "Thigh-muscle strength and tendon stiffness predicted who developed knee arthritis within two years" and drop the directional claim from the reader angle, or note that the abstract does not specify direction."

**Issue B. Measurement method unverifiable** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "measuring knee extensor (quadriceps) strength and patellar tendon stiffness with ultrasound-based tools"
- **Abstract says:** "This 2-year prospective cohort study enrolled 116 community-dwelling older adults (220 knees) free of knee pain at baseline... Predictor importance was quantified using Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP) values"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not describe how patellar tendon stiffness or extensor strength were measured, so "ultrasound-based tools" is unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "with ultrasound-based tools" or attribute it to the full text."

### Study 23: JAMA tracks what older Americans are actually taking — prescriptions, drugstore pills and supplements together
**PMID:** 42783349 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Omitted industry-related disclosure** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "authors report consulting and grant relationships including NIH, Arnold Ventures and the National Community Pharmacists Association"
- **Abstract says:** "Dr Alexander reported being past chair of the US Food and Drug Administration's (FDA) Peripheral and Central Nervous System Advisory Committee and being a co–founding principal and equity holder in Stage Analytics."

**Issue B. Design details not in the record** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "cross-sectional survey data relying on self-reported and observed medication use"
- **Abstract says:** "Plain Language Summary: This study uses a nationally representative sample to estimate changes in the use and concurrent use of prescription and over-the-counter (OTC) medications and dietary supplements among older adults in the US between 2015-2016 and 2021-2023"
- **Problem:** The posted record gives no methods beyond "nationally representative sample"; the cross-sectional design and self-reported/observed medication capture cannot be confirmed from it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Label these design details as unverifiable from the posted record, or attribute them to the survey the study likely draws on with a hedge."
