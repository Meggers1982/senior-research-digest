# Fact-Check Report

### Study 1: Higher resting heart rate linked to faster cognitive decline in older adults
**PMID:** 42329536 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. Framing is consistent with the abstract. The sample size (19,114), population characteristics, study design (secondary analysis of ASPREE), and statistical findings (9% higher risk at baseline RHR ≥80 bpm, 16% higher risk for 5-year averaged RHR ≥80 bpm) all match the abstract. The caveat appropriately notes observational design and lack of significant dementia association.

### Study 2: Unstable sleep patterns in middle age tied to higher stroke and heart disease risk
**PMID:** 42208063 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Stroke risk statistic context** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a striking 110% higher stroke risk compared to those with steady, adequate sleep"
- **Abstract says:** "the inverted U-shaped sleep trajectory showed a 110% increased stroke risk during nocturnal periods compared to the steady high group (nocturnal sleep: HR=2.10, 95% CI 1.08-4.05)"
- **Problem:** The digest omits that the 110% stroke risk figure specifically applies to *nocturnal* sleep trajectories, not total sleep trajectories.
- **Suggested fix:** "People with an 'inverted U-shaped' sleep pattern—sleeping more during middle years then less later—had a 34% higher risk of developing hypertension. For nocturnal sleep specifically, this pattern was associated with a striking 110% higher stroke risk compared to those with steady, adequate sleep."

### Study 3: Childhood trauma linked to heart disease and accelerated biological aging decades later
**PMID:** 42208056 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Biological aging estimate precision** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "accelerated biological aging equivalent to about 1.5 additional years, with an even stronger effect in women (nearly 2.7 years)"
- **Abstract says:** "Forced childhood migration was also associated with age acceleration (∆KDMAge β =1.52), with a stronger association in women (∆KDMAge β =2.67)"
- **Problem:** The digest rounds 2.67 to "nearly 2.7 years" which is accurate, but presents the beta coefficient as direct years. This is technically correct for the KDM biological age measure, but the phrasing "about 1.5" and "nearly 2.7" appropriately captures the precision.
- **Suggested fix:** None needed; this is acceptable rounding.

No significant issues found. All other facts verified.

### Study 6: Blood lipid patterns across midlife predict heart disease risk decades later
**PMID:** 42387180 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Protective lipid risk reduction figure** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Some protective lipids were linked to 79% lower coronary heart disease risk"
- **Abstract says:** "baseline levels of specific glycerophospholipids and fatty acids associated with diastolic function (E/A ratio and deceleration time) were linked to a reduced risk of CHD (HR range: 0.21-0.86)"
- **Problem:** The 79% reduction (HR 0.21) represents the extreme end of a range (0.21-0.86). The digest presents it as representative rather than the maximum observed effect.
- **Suggested fix:** "Some protective lipids were linked to substantially lower coronary heart disease risk (hazard ratios ranging from 0.21 to 0.86), while others predicted increased risk."

### Study 8: "Healthy agers" may hold clues to preventing atrial fibrillation
**PMID:** 42476318 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. The abstract confirms the paradoxical reduction of AF in centenarians compared to octogenarians/nonagenarians, and the protective mechanisms (reduced inflammation, oxidative stress, improved mitochondrial function, reduced fibrosis) are accurately represented. Caveat appropriately notes narrative review status and centenarian population selectivity.

### Study 9: Diet-driven inflammation linked to cardiovascular disease and other aging conditions
**PMID:** 42425421 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. The digest accurately represents the review's dual focus on pro-inflammatory components (AGEs, oxidized fats, trans fats) and anti-inflammatory compounds (polyphenols, omega-3s, carotenoids, vitamins). The caveat appropriately notes that human evidence is limited to biomarker changes rather than lifespan extension, which matches the abstract's statement that "clinical evidence in humans remains largely restricted to biomarker and healthspan-related endpoints."

### Study 10: Statins do not appear to harm cognition in older adults—and may help
**PMID:** 42010230 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. The abstract confirms: most RCTs found no harmful effect, some observational studies showed possible protective associations, Mendelian randomization studies (including >100,000 individuals) found no causal link. The ongoing PREVENTABLE and STAREE trials are correctly mentioned. Caveats appropriately note narrative review status and underrepresentation of older adults.

### Study 12: Machine learning approaches show potential for predicting frailty and outcomes in heart disease
**PMID:** 41941969 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. The study count (31 studies), population focus (71% heart failure), data sources (EHR and clinical assessments), performance ranges (frailty AUC 0.80-0.96, mortality AUC 0.70-0.93), and validation limitations (only 3 external validations, few calibration reports) all match the abstract.

### Study 13: Mouse study maps heart aging trajectories, revealing critical "frailty transition"
**PMID:** 42406280 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. The sample size (58 male mice), study design (quarterly echocardiography from 6 months until natural death), frailty transition timing (18-21 months), and key finding about divergent trajectories despite genetic uniformity all match the abstract. Caveats appropriately note animal model limitations and male-only subjects.

### Study 14: Socioeconomic disadvantage casts lifelong shadow on cardiovascular and aging outcomes
**PMID:** 41864545 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

Caveat appropriately notes the preclinical/early clinical evidence base.
