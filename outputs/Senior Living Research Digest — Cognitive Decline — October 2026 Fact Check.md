### Study 2: Romance and sexual satisfaction tracked with slower memory decline in early dementia
**PMID:** 42648931 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Outcome described as "memory"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Romance and sexual satisfaction tracked with slower memory decline in early dementia"
- **Abstract says:** "Cognitive function was assessed using the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) at baseline and follow-up."
- **Problem:** The MMSE is a global cognitive screen, not a memory test; the headline narrows the outcome to memory.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "slower cognitive decline" or "slower decline on a global thinking test.""

**Issue B. Population labelled "early dementia"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "slower memory decline in early dementia"
- **Abstract says:** "56 patients with mild NCD and 57 patients with mild-stage major NCD, diagnosed according to DSM-5 criteria"
- **Problem:** Half the sample had mild neurocognitive disorder, which is the DSM-5 equivalent of mild cognitive impairment, not dementia. The body text gets this right but the headline generalizes to dementia.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "in early-stage cognitive disorders" or "in mild cognitive impairment and early dementia.""

### Study 4: Weak grip plus excess weight linked to worse memory and attention in older adults
**PMID:** 42825653 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Overlap of the two phenotypes not disclosed** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "researchers compared people with sarcopenic obesity (low muscle mass plus low grip strength plus obesity) and dynapenic obesity (low grip strength plus obesity) against everyone else"
- **Abstract says:** "All 16 participants with dynapenic obesity also met the criteria for sarcopenic obesity."
- **Problem:** The two phenotypes are presented as distinct comparison groups, when in fact every dynapenic-obesity participant was also sarcopenic-obese, so the two sets of results are not independent.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that all 16 dynapenic obesity participants also met sarcopenic obesity criteria, so the two estimates largely reflect the same people."

**Issue B. "Most evident" subgroup phrasing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Associations were most evident in women and in people who did not carry the APOE ε4 gene."
- **Abstract says:** "The associations of sarcopenic obesity and dynapenic obesity with global cognition and attention performance remained evident among women and apolipoprotein E (APOE) ε4 non-carriers."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports that associations persisted in these subgroups, not that they were strongest there; no formal comparison across subgroups is reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "Associations persisted among women and among people who did not carry APOE ε4.""

### Study 5: Longer reproductive years linked to better long-term cognition in nurses study
**PMID:** 41979543 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Hormone therapy timing wording** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Hormone therapy started within 10 years of menopause was associated with slightly faster decline, while use beginning 11 or more years after menopause showed no association."
- **Abstract says:** "MHT use 0-10 years postmenopause was associated with faster decline ...; use during 11+ years postmenopause was not associated."
- **Problem:** The abstract analyzes duration of use during each post-menopausal window, not the age at which therapy was started; "started within" and "beginning 11 or more years after" misstate the exposure.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "hormone therapy used during the first 10 years after menopause" and "use during the period 11 or more years after menopause.""

### Study 8: For Parkinson's Patients With a GBA1 Gene Variant, Timing of Brain Surgery May Shape Memory Decline
**PMID:** 42817906 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Assertive framing in care-partner angle** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Care partners weighing DBS should know the decision has a clock attached: the calculus may differ for genetically defined subgroups, and 'wait and see' is not a neutral choice."
- **Abstract says:** "In GBA-PD, STN-DBS treatment at later stages is associated with greater risk of cognitive decline."

### Study 10: Living Under a Leafy Canopy Tracks With Fewer Depression Symptoms in Older Age
**PMID:** 42442669 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Omitted counterintuitive tree canopy result** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Counterintuitive findings — more parkland and falling nitrogen dioxide linked to more symptoms — turned out to be driven almost entirely by participants who had moved house during follow-up."
- **Abstract says:** "Unexpectedly, higher average proportion of parkland, within-person increases in tree canopy cover and decreases in NO₂ exposure were associated with increased depressive symptoms."
- **Problem:** The digest lists only two of the three unexpected findings and drops the one involving tree canopy — the very exposure the headline is built on — making the canopy result look cleaner than the abstract reports.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that within-person increases in tree canopy were also unexpectedly linked to more symptoms, and that this too was confined to participants who relocated."

### Study 11: Where You Live When You Retire May Shape How Fast Your Memory Changes
**PMID:** 42826499 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Acceleration 'in every setting'** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Decline was already underway before retirement but accelerated afterward in every setting."
- **Abstract says:** "Cognitive decline was observed before and after retirement, with faster decline following retirement. Post-retirement decline was steepest in rural areas (-0.194 points/year), followed by suburban (-0.184) and urban (-0.164) settings."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports the pre-to-post-retirement acceleration overall and gives post-retirement slopes by setting; it does not report that decline accelerated after retirement within each setting separately. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Decline was already underway before retirement and sped up afterward, with the steepest post-retirement decline in rural areas."

### Study 12: Fitness, Not Grip Strength, Tracks Most Closely With Thinking Skills in Frail Older Adults
**PMID:** 42821209 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Grip strength association overstated as null** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "while the grip strength association largely faded once fitness was accounted for"
- **Abstract says:** "The association with MS was attenuated after adjusting for CRF, with Q3 (OR = 0.65, 95% CI 0.45-0.94) and Q4 (OR = 0.66, 95% CI 0.45-0.97) statistically significant."
- **Problem:** Muscle strength was attenuated but remained statistically significant in the top two quartiles; 'largely faded' implies the association essentially disappeared.
- **Suggested fix:** "while the grip strength association weakened once fitness was accounted for, though the top two strength quartiles remained significantly associated"

**Issue B. Omits that fitness plus strength was best** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "these data suggest aerobic capacity may be the higher-yield target when choices must be made"
- **Abstract says:** "Joint analysis revealed that high CRF with high MS had lowest odds of cognitive impairment (OR = 0.38, 95% CI 0.26-0.56) compared to low CRF with low MS, and high CRF alone conferred 56% lower odds than high MS alone."
- **Problem:** The digest's either/or framing leaves out the abstract's finding that the lowest odds of cognitive impairment were in people with both high fitness and high strength.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that people with both high fitness and high strength had the lowest odds of impairment, so strength work still matters where it is feasible."

### Study 17: Worsening Sleep in Later Life Tripled Alzheimer's Risk in an 8-Year Study
**PMID:** 42542107 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline conflates trajectories and implies causation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Worsening Sleep in Later Life Tripled Alzheimer's Risk in an 8-Year Study"
- **Abstract says:** "a significantly elevated risk of incident AD was associated with "High-peaked" (hazard ratio [HR] = 3.28; 95% confidence interval [CI] = 1.89-5.70; p < 0.001) and "Moderate-increasing" trajectories (HR = 2.13; 95% CI = 1.28-3.53; p = 0.003)"
- **Problem:** The roughly tripled risk belonged to the 'high-peaked' (sharply spiking) pattern; the steadily worsening group — which the headline phrase 'worsening sleep' most naturally describes — had about double the risk. 'Tripled' also states an observational association as cause and effect, though the body text and caveats are careful.
- **Suggested fix:** "Sharply Spiking Sleep Problems Linked to Triple the Alzheimer's Risk in an 8-Year Study"

### Study 19: Traumatic Events After Age 50 Linked to Faster Memory Decline — Regardless of Childhood History
**PMID:** 42606294 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Null interaction framed as reassurance** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A difficult childhood does not appear to make you more vulnerable to this effect, which is genuinely reassuring news."
- **Abstract says:** "Childhood adversity did not modify this association."
- **Problem:** A single non-significant interaction test is absence of evidence rather than evidence of absence; calling it 'genuinely reassuring' overstates what a null modification result can tell an individual reader.
- **Suggested fix:** "This study found no sign that childhood adversity amplified the effect, though one cohort cannot rule it out."

### Study 20: Epilepsy Follows Alzheimer's in Down Syndrome — and Shortens Survival
**PMID:** 42692040 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for an observational association** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Epilepsy Follows Alzheimer's in Down Syndrome — and Shortens Survival"
- **Abstract says:** "LOMEDS was associated with increased mortality (hazard ratio 2·10 [95% CI 1·57-2·82]; p<0·0001), and faster cognitive decline."
- **Problem:** 'Shortens Survival' asserts causation from an observational cohort, and the mortality finding applies specifically to late-onset myoclonic epilepsy rather than epilepsy in general.
- **Suggested fix:** "Epilepsy Follows Alzheimer's in Down Syndrome — and Myoclonic Seizures Are Linked to Shorter Survival"

**Issue B. Dementia vs symptomatic Alzheimer's as the starting point** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "More than half of people with Down syndrome who develop Alzheimer's dementia will go on to develop epilepsy within a decade"
- **Abstract says:** "Cumulative incidence of epilepsy increased linearly after symptomatic Alzheimer's disease, from 9·5% (95% CI 7·8-11·3) at diagnosis to 56·9% (51·7-61·7) after 9 years."
- **Problem:** The cumulative incidence clock starts at symptomatic Alzheimer's disease — a category that in this study includes prodromal disease as well as dementia — and the interval is nine years, not a decade.
- **Suggested fix:** "About 57% of people with Down syndrome developed epilepsy within nine years of a symptomatic Alzheimer's diagnosis."

**PMID:** 42606290 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Subgroup wording** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the fat findings were strongest in women"
- **Abstract says:** "this association was significant in women with MCI and AD"

### Study 24: A Routine Blood Test Ratio May Flag Who Declines Faster After a Memory Diagnosis
**PMID:** 42818734 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Population in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "May Flag Who Declines Faster After a Memory Diagnosis"
- **Abstract says:** "1,190 non-people living with dementia older adults ... comprising 405 cognitively normal participants and 785 participants with mild cognitive impairment (MCI)"
- **Problem:** About a third of the cohort had no cognitive diagnosis at all, so framing the finding as applying to people 'after a memory diagnosis' narrows and mislabels the sample.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like 'May Flag Who Declines Faster — Even Before a Memory Diagnosis' or 'in Older Adults With and Without Mild Cognitive Impairment'."

### Study 25: What Actually Helps People With Memory Concerns Change Their Habits: A Review of 23 Trials
**PMID:** 42586247 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the answer is that one-off advice rarely works while sustained, social, well-equipped programs do"
- **Abstract says:** "Moderate-certainty evidence indicated that interventions providing a socially supportive environment and combining nutritional education and counselling for 6 + months improved diet ... Interventions simultaneously targeting capability, opportunity and motivation showed greater overall effectiveness."
- **Problem:** The review did not test or report on 'one-off advice' as a comparator; the contrast is the digest's inference rather than a reported finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as 'the trials that changed behaviour were longer, socially supportive and gave participants concrete tools'."

### Study 26: Latina Women Report More Memory and Language Worries Than Men — and Heart Health May Be Part of Why
**PMID:** 42424748 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Wrong outcome domain in headline** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Latina Women Report More Memory and Language Worries Than Men"
- **Abstract says:** "Overall, females reported more SCD in language and visuospatial domains."
- **Problem:** The sex difference was found in the language and visuospatial domains, not memory; the headline attributes a finding to a domain the abstract does not report as significant, and it contradicts the digest's own body text.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'Latina Women Report More Language and Visuospatial Worries Than Men'."

**Issue B. Mechanistic framing of an effect modifier** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and Heart Health May Be Part of Why"
- **Abstract says:** "This was less pronounced for 1) language SCD at lower levels of CVD risk"
- **Problem:** CVD risk was tested as a modifier of the sex difference in a cross-sectional analysis; 'part of why' implies an explanatory/causal pathway the design cannot support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'and the Gap Is Narrower in Those With Lower Cardiovascular Risk'."

### Study 27: A Brain Growth Factor May Determine How Hard the Alzheimer's Risk Gene Hits
**PMID:** 42599691 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal verb in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A Brain Growth Factor May Determine How Hard the Alzheimer's Risk Gene Hits"
- **Abstract says:** "APOE ε4 appears to interact with CSF GDNF levels to affect longitudinal cognitive decline among older adults without dementia."
- **Problem:** 'Determine' implies a causal, deterministic role from an observational interaction analysis; the digest's own caveats state causation cannot be established.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to 'May Be Linked to How Hard the Alzheimer's Risk Gene Hits'."
