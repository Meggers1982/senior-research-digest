### Study 3: Which older adults keep returning to the emergency department — and what makes the visit go badly
**PMID:** 42762629 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal language for observational associations** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "shows that communication, not just wait times, determines whether the experience feels good or bad"
- **Abstract says:** "negative ED experience was associated with long waits (≥60 minutes; OR 1.70...), complications (OR 1.63...), and poor communication or involvement in decisions, with insufficient treatment information showing the strongest association (OR 5.17...)"
- **Problem:** A retrospective cohort plus patient-experience survey can show associations only; 'determines' implies a causal driver of experience.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'shows that communication, not just wait times, was the factor most strongly associated with a negative experience.'"

**Issue B. Index presentations described as visits** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "linked hospital and mortality records for 336,283 emergency department visits by adults aged 65 and older in New South Wales in 2018, following them for five years"
- **Abstract says:** "There were 336,283 index presentations in 2018; Individuals with an index ED presentation in 2018 who survived the ascertainment period were followed for five years"
- **Problem:** The 336,283 figure counts index presentations (one per individual) rather than all ED visits, so the digest slightly misdescribes the denominator.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say '336,283 older adults with an index emergency department visit in 2018'."

### Study 4: The Mediterranean diet and your bones: what the evidence actually shows
**PMID:** 42641790 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Interpretive leap about eating pattern vs single nutrients** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this suggests the overall eating pattern may matter more for fractures than any single nutrient"
- **Abstract says:** "These components exert synergistic effects by reducing oxidative stress and chronic inflammation ... Evidence from observational studies consistently suggests an inverse association between adherence to the Mediterranean diet and fracture risk"
- **Problem:** The review discusses synergy among components but does not compare whole-pattern effects against single nutrients, so the comparative claim is not supported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as 'the review emphasises the combined effect of the whole dietary pattern rather than any single nutrient.'"

**Issue B. Unsupported practical claims in story angle** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Mediterranean-style meals are affordable, familiar, and support bone health alongside the cardiovascular benefits already well established in older populations"
- **Abstract says:** "the Mediterranean diet represents a promising and feasible non-pharmacological approach for osteoporosis prevention, although further research is needed"
- **Problem:** Affordability, familiarity and established cardiovascular benefit are not addressed in the abstract, and 'support bone health' is firmer than the review's 'promising'.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute only feasibility to the review and label cost/familiarity and cardiovascular benefit as outside this paper."

### Study 6: Big vitamin D doses didn't prevent broken bones in a 5-year trial of older adults
**PMID:** 42704968 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Follow-up period misdescribed** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "or placebo for five years, then tracked fractures through national health registries for an average of 7.3 years"
- **Abstract says:** "During the mean 4.1-year supplementation period ... During the extended 7.3-year follow-up"
- **Problem:** The 7.3 years is the total extended follow-up, not an additional 7.3 years after the 5-year supplementation period, so the digest implies roughly 12 years of observation.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'for five years (mean 4.1 years of supplementation), with fracture tracking through registries over a total follow-up of 7.3 years.'"

**Issue B. Baseline vitamin D level was from a sub-cohort** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with a mean baseline blood level of about 75 nmol/L, and supplementation pushed levels to roughly 100 and 120 nmol/L"
- **Abstract says:** "In the sub-cohort of 550 participants, the mean ± SD baseline serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D concentration was 74.8 ± 18.2 nmol/L"
- **Problem:** Blood levels were measured in a 550-person sub-cohort, not in all 2,495 participants; the digest reads as if everyone was tested.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add 'in a 550-participant sub-cohort who had blood tests'."

### Study 7: One in six adults over 40 had a spinal fracture they didn't know about
**PMID:** 42624233 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies all fractures were undetected** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "One in six adults over 40 had a spinal fracture they didn't know about"
- **Abstract says:** "244 MVFs were identified in 171 participants (15.9%) ... After review, 52 participants (4.8%) had CFVFs, 19 traumatic fractures, and 100 morphometric fractures"
- **Problem:** Of the 171 with fractures, 52 had clinical fragility fractures and 19 traumatic fractures, so not all were silent or unknown; only the morphometric-only group clearly fits 'didn't know about'.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'One in six adults over 40 had a spinal fracture on imaging — most of them silent'."

### Study 9: 162 Trials, One Message: Exercise Builds Bone — But the Payoff Depends on Where You Measure
**PMID:** 42575297 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Modality claim stronger than the evidence** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "chair-based or purely aerobic classes are unlikely to shift bone density, even if they help with balance and mood"
- **Abstract says:** "NMA suggested site-specific ranking patterns, but credible intervals were often wide and NMA certainty was commonly limited by imprecision, heterogeneity, and sparse direct evidence ... Modality rankings ... should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than definitive prescriptions"
- **Problem:** The network comparisons between exercise types were too uncertain to conclude that specific modalities do not work; the digest turns low-certainty rankings into a definitive negative statement.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to 'the rankings hinted that resistance and weight-bearing loading may matter most, though modality comparisons were too uncertain to rule any approach in or out.'"

**Issue B. Superlative and 'confirming' language** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "This is the largest synthesis to date confirming that structured exercise measurably improves bone density in adults"
- **Abstract says:** "Exercise is associated with small-to-moderate, site-specific improvements in BMD across adulthood"
- **Problem:** The abstract makes no claim to being the largest synthesis (unverifiable from abstract only), and 'confirming' overstates effects the authors describe as small-to-moderate with variable certainty.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'A large pooled analysis finds exercise produces small-to-moderate improvements in bone density'."

### Study 10: Broken Wrist, Missed Diagnosis: Nationwide Data Show Osteoporosis Goes Unrecorded After Many Fragility Fractures
**PMID:** 42448012 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Coding gap presented as treatment gap** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this analysis suggests those signals are routinely not acted on — leaving people untreated until a hip fracture happens"
- **Abstract says:** "Osteoporosis is not systematically coded across all fracture types in routine hospital data ... These patterns suggest under-recognition of osteoporosis at the time of fracture"
- **Problem:** The study examined diagnostic coding, not prescriptions or clinical follow-up, so it cannot show that patients went untreated or that hip fracture followed; this goes further than the digest's own caveat about coding practice.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as 'osteoporosis often goes unrecorded after these fractures, which may mean the warning signal is not followed up.'"

### Study 11: When Your Spine Scan and Your MRI Disagree: Metabolic Clues to Hidden Bone Weakness
**PMID:** 42492717 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Single-center claim not stated in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Retrospective, single-center, observational design in surgical patients"
- **Abstract says:** "We retrospectively included 266 consecutive adults with preoperative lumbar MRI and DXA performed within 6 months before surgery. (Author affiliations list the Affiliated Hospital of Nantong University plus Jianhu County Second People's Hospital and Qidong People's Hospital.)"
- **Problem:** The abstract never says the cohort came from a single institution, and multiple hospitals appear in the affiliations. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "retrospective, observational design in spine surgery patients at one Chinese hospital system (number of centres not specified in the abstract)"."

**Issue B. Glycated albumin described as "blood sugar control"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Metabolic markers like triglycerides and blood sugar control came up in this analysis"
- **Abstract says:** "triglycerides (β = 0.397, p = 0.003), very low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (β = 1.325, p = 0.002), glycated albumin (β = 0.184, p = 0.003), and conjugated bilirubin (β = 0.162, p = 0.004) were independent determinants of VBQ"
- **Problem:** Glycated albumin is a glycation-burden marker; translating it as "blood sugar control" is a reasonable but loose paraphrase that drops conjugated bilirubin and VLDL from the lay summary.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "markers of blood fats and glycation (triglycerides, VLDL cholesterol, glycated albumin)"."

### Study 19: Belly Fat, Not Body Weight, Tracks With Weaker Bones — and the Pattern Differs by Sex
**PMID:** 42641825 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline asserts an untested body-weight contrast** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Belly Fat, Not Body Weight, Tracks With Weaker Bones — and the Pattern Differs by Sex"
- **Abstract says:** "BMI cannot distinguish fat distribution and may mask the true effects of different fat depots on bone mineral density (BMD)."
- **Problem:** The abstract offers no analysis showing body weight or BMI is unrelated to BMD; the "not body weight" framing goes beyond the reported results and the rationale statement in the background.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "Where Fat Sits, Not Just How Much: Visceral Fat Tracks With Lower Spine Bone Density"."

**Issue B. "Mainly in men" versus "only in males"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a higher proportion of visceral fat was linked to greater odds of low bone mass mainly in men (odds ratio 1.30)"
- **Abstract says:** "VAT% was associated with greater odds of low bone mass only in males (OR 1.30, P = 0.03)."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports the association exclusively in males; "mainly" implies a weaker effect was also seen in women.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change "mainly in men" to "only in men"."

**Issue C. "Challenges the assumption that weight protects bones"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It challenges the common assumption that carrying extra weight protects bones — where the fat sits may matter more than what the scale or BMI says."
- **Abstract says:** "These cross-sectional findings highlight sex-specific associations between fat distribution and BMD."
- **Problem:** The study measured fat depots and BMD cross-sectionally; it did not evaluate whether overall body weight protects bone, so this inference is not supported by the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "it suggests fat distribution, not just total body size, is associated with bone density — though this snapshot design cannot establish cause"."

### Study 20: Men at High Risk of Osteoporosis Almost Never Get Bone Density Tests, U.S. Data Show
**PMID:** 42493193 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Background claim about men's fracture burden** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Men account for a substantial share of hip fractures and have higher post-fracture mortality than women"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract states only that Endocrine Society guidelines recommend screening in high-risk men and that DXA ordering appears markedly low among eligible men, "representing a missed opportunity for early detection and fracture prevention.""
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only — the mortality and hip-fracture-share comparison comes from outside this paper and is presented as if it were part of the study's basis.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute it ("other research has found…") or drop it, and lean on the abstract's own "missed opportunity for early detection and fracture prevention" wording."

### Study 21: Dental Work Right Before Starting Bone Drugs Linked to Higher Jaw Complication Risk
**PMID:** 42372806 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Clinical advice beyond what was tested** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Before you start a bisphosphonate or denosumab, ask your doctor and dentist to coordinate: get a dental check first, finish any needed extractions or other invasive work, and allow healing time rather than starting the medication right on the heels of a procedure."
- **Abstract says:** "Oral risk treatment within 1 month before therapy initiation was associated with MRONJ in both groups (low-dose: SHR 2.99 [95% CI 1.47-6.07]); high-dose: 4.28 [2.47-7.42])... suggesting that oral risk conditions near initiation may be important for risk stratification."
- **Problem:** The study observed an association between invasive dental work in the month before initiation and later MRONJ; it did not test whether delaying therapy to allow healing lowers risk, and the authors frame the finding as useful for risk stratification rather than as a prevention protocol.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to something like: 'this study found jaw problems were more common in people who had invasive dental work in the month before starting, so it is worth asking your doctor and dentist to coordinate timing — though the study could not test whether waiting for healing lowers the risk.'"

### Study 23: After a Hip Fracture, Fewer Than a Third of Patients Start Bone Medication — But the Number Is Rising
**PMID:** 42288172 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Omitted disclosed industry relationship** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "One author has a collaborative research agreement with Amgen; the study received Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare support."
- **Abstract says:** "Akira Okada is a member of the Department of Prevention of Diabetes and Lifestyle-related Diseases, a cooperative program between the University of Tokyo and the Asahi Mutual Life Insurance Company. Takeyuki Tanaka has a collaborative research agreement with Amgen, Inc."
- **Problem:** The digest's caveat lists the Amgen agreement and ministry support but omits the second author's departmental funding relationship with Asahi Mutual Life Insurance Company, which is also a commercial tie disclosed in the competing-interest statement.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add: 'another author's department is funded through a cooperative program with Asahi Mutual Life Insurance Company.'"

**Issue B. Rounding of non-initiation rate** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "roughly two-thirds of hip fracture patients still leave the system without starting bone-protective treatment"
- **Abstract says:** "The proportion of patients initiating anti-osteoporosis medication within 3 months of a hip fracture increased starting in 2022, reaching 29.5% in 2023."
- **Problem:** If 29.5% initiated, about 70.5% did not — closer to seven in ten than two-thirds, so the phrasing mildly understates the treatment gap.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'roughly seven in ten hip fracture patients' or 'more than 70%.'"
