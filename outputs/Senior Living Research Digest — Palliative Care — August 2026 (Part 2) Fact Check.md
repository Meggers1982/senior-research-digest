## Fact-Check Report

### Study 1: More Than Half of Hospitalized Older Adults and Nearly Half of Nursing Home Residents Need Palliative Care — Most Don't Have Cancer

**PMID:** 42366281 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. The digest accurately reports the sample size (5,389), settings (235 facilities), median ages (81 and 86), prevalence rates (58.3% hospital, 48.1% LTCF), and the finding that substantial palliative care needs exist among non-cancer patients (50.8% hospital, 46.8% LTCF). Symptoms listed (functional decline, pain, delirium, dysphagia) match the abstract. Framing is appropriately observational.

### Study 4: Families, Nurses, and Doctors See Dying Differently — Especially When It Comes to Physical Symptoms

**PMID:** 42530751 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Subscale terminology** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with the biggest gap around dying symptoms (breathing, restlessness)"
- **Abstract says:** "with largest difference on the dying symptoms subscale"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not specify "breathing, restlessness" as the components of the dying symptoms subscale. While this may be accurate based on the EOLD-CAD scale, it cannot be verified from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "with the biggest gap on the dying symptoms subscale"

**Issue B. Direction of change over time** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** The digest does not mention that relatives' scores *increased* over time while healthcare providers' scores increased less.
- **Abstract says:** "EOLD-CAD-scores increased over time for relatives (+ 1.2 per 24 h, p = 0.001). Compared to relatives, doctors' and nurses' scores increased 0.7 less (p = 0.09) and 1.1 less (p = 0.004) per 24 h, respectively."
- **Problem:** This nuance about temporal changes is omitted but doesn't contradict the digest's main message.
- **Suggested fix:** No revision required; the core finding about perception differences is accurately conveyed.

### Study 9: Specialist Palliative Care Referrals for the "Oldest-Old" (90+) Come Late — and Often Only Near Death

**PMID:** 42580255 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Prior palliative care percentage** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "no prior palliative care involvement (93%)"
- **Abstract says:** "only 7% had prior palliative care involvement"
- **Problem:** The digest's phrasing is mathematically equivalent (93% = 100% - 7%) but slightly less direct than the abstract's framing.
- **Suggested fix:** No revision required; the information is accurate.

**Issue B. Death/discharge outcome** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Nearly two-thirds died in hospital or were transferred to a palliative unit for end-of-life care"
- **Abstract says:** "64.6% of patients died in the acute care hospital or were transferred to a palliative care unit for end-of-life care"
- **Problem:** "Nearly two-thirds" accurately represents 64.6%.
- **Suggested fix:** None needed.

### Study 18: Polypharmacy and Potentially Inappropriate Medications Remain Common in Older Adults Needing Palliative Care

**PMID:** 41950773 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Sample size and screening numbers** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "294 hospitalized and nursing home patients aged 65+ with identified palliative care needs"
- **Abstract says:** "Of 558 patients initially screened, 294 were administered the NECPAL tool, of whom 254 (45.5% of the sample screened) had PC needs"
- **Problem:** The digest says 294 had palliative care needs, but the abstract clarifies that 294 were assessed with NECPAL and 254 of those had PC needs.
- **Suggested fix:** "An Italian multicenter study assessed prescribing patterns among hospitalized and nursing home patients aged 65+, with 254 having identified palliative care needs out of 294 assessed."

**Issue B. Polypharmacy percentage** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Polypharmacy (5+ medications) was present in 94%"
- **Abstract says:** "Polypharmacy was reported in 93.9% (276/294)"
- **Problem:** 94% is an acceptable rounding of 93.9%.
- **Suggested fix:** None required.

**Issue C. Italian context correctly noted** — Verified accurate (Genoa, Liguria, Italy).
