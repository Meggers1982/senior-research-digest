### Study 3: A History of Depression Tracks With Higher Heart and Metabolic Disease Risk 15 Years Later
**PMID:** 42826766 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Symptom-profile nuance flattened** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Notably, no particular depression symptom pattern stood out: fatigue- and appetite-related 'atypical' symptoms, anhedonia, and classic symptoms all carried broadly similar risk."
- **Abstract says:** "In continuous analyses, only AES and depression severity were significantly associated with CMD incidence, though effect sizes were comparable across symptom profiles... All profiles were associated with cardiovascular disease incidence, but only AES was associated with diabetes incidence."
- **Problem:** The abstract does report one distinguishing result — atypical energy-related symptoms were the only profile significantly associated in continuous analyses and the only one linked to incident diabetes — which the digest omits.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a clause such as 'although atypical, fatigue-related symptoms were the only profile linked specifically to new diabetes.'"

### Study 4: Financial Strain and Discrimination Show Up in the Blood of Older Black Adults With Heart Disease
**PMID:** 42843435 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Size of the heart-disease subgroup not reported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "but among participants who already had heart disease, financial burden and discrimination were associated with altered levels of 11 to 20 inflammatory proteins"
- **Abstract says:** "Cardiovascular burden included BMI (mean = 30.6, SD = 6.4 kg/m2), hypertension (80.1%), type 2 diabetes (25.1%), and heart disease (6.8%)."
- **Problem:** Only about 6.8% of the 642 participants (roughly 44 people) had heart disease, so the headline interaction is based on a very small subgroup — context a reader needs to judge the strength of the finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that heart disease was present in just 6.8% of participants, making that subgroup small."

### Study 5: Depression Raises Heart Disease and Death Risk in People With Type 2 Diabetes — and Blood Sugar Isn't the Reason
**PMID:** 42624218 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal verb in headline** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Depression Raises Heart Disease and Death Risk in People With Type 2 Diabetes — and Blood Sugar Isn't the Reason"
- **Abstract says:** "Depression is associated with greater risks for incident cardiovascular disease and all-cause death in people with type 2 diabetes."
- **Problem:** 'Raises' asserts causation from an observational cohort; the body text and Caveats correctly say 'associated' and note causation cannot be proven, so the headline overstates relative to the rest of the item.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'Depression Is Linked to Higher Heart Disease and Death Risk in People With Type 2 Diabetes.'"

**Issue B. Null kidney outcome omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "had a 21% higher risk of developing cardiovascular disease and a 30% higher risk of dying from any cause, plus more hospital admissions and longer stays"
- **Abstract says:** "but not ESKD (HR 0.95 [0.89, 1.01])"
- **Problem:** The digest reports only the positive outcomes; the abstract also reports no association with end-stage kidney disease, which bounds the finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that depression was not associated with end-stage kidney disease."

### Study 9: Which Combinations of Chronic Conditions Carry the Highest Risk? A 16,000-Person Study Maps Them Out
**PMID:** 42821655 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal phrasing for cluster mortality** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the cluster combining frailty, depressive symptoms and osteoporosis raised death risk by roughly 56% in men and 68% in women"
- **Abstract says:** "The frailty, depressive symptoms and osteoporosis cluster was associated with higher mortality in both sexes (adjusted hazard ratio [aHR] 1.56 ... in males; 1.68 ... in females)."
- **Problem:** 'Raised death risk' implies causation in a secondary observational analysis; the abstract uses 'associated with'. The story angle does add a disclaimer, but the body text states it causally.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'was associated with roughly 56% higher mortality in men and 68% in women.'"

**Issue B. Two distinct clusters merged** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "clusters involving gout, kidney disease and anemia raised risk by 63% in men and 96% in women"
- **Abstract says:** "Higher mortality was also observed for the metabolic, gout and kidney disease cluster in males (aHR 1.63 [1.47-1.81]) and the gout, anemia and kidney disease cluster in females (aHR 1.96 [1.74-2.21])."
- **Problem:** The male and female clusters were differently composed (metabolic/gout/kidney in men; gout/anemia/kidney in women); the digest's single combined description blurs that distinction.
- **Suggested fix:** "Name the two clusters separately, as the abstract does."

### Study 15: Women's heart disease in Europe looks different from men's — and pregnancy history is a bigger part of the story than assumed
**PMID:** 42912409 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. 25-fold disparity not sex-specified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "country-of-origin matters given up to 25-fold differences in premature cardiovascular mortality across Europe"
- **Abstract says:** "even larger disparities in mortality and premature mortality (up to 25-fold in women)"
- **Problem:** The abstract attributes the up-to-25-fold premature mortality spread specifically to women; the digest presents it as a general cross-country figure, dropping the sex qualifier in a piece whose whole point is sex differences.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write 'up to 25-fold differences in premature cardiovascular mortality among women across Europe'."

### Study 16: Menopause before 45 flagged higher death risk after a heart attack in a five-year registry
**PMID:** 42837120 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. 'Five-year registry' and 'heart attack' shorthand** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Menopause before 45 flagged higher death risk after a heart attack in a five-year registry"
- **Abstract says:** "We analyzed a multicenter registry of women hospitalized with ACS from February 1, 2020 through February 28, 2025... median follow-up was 3.52 years"
- **Problem:** 'Five-year' refers to the enrolment window, not follow-up (median 3.52 years), and 'heart attack' narrows acute coronary syndrome, which also includes unstable angina. The body text is correct, but the headline could be misread.
- **Suggested fix:** "Consider 'in a multicentre registry of women with acute coronary syndrome followed about 3.5 years'."

### Study 17: People over 80 are dying less — and living longer with disability
**PMID:** 42594758 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Overstates the shift away from fatal burden** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The dominant health challenge in the oldest age group is shifting away from fatal heart disease and toward conditions that erode function and independence"
- **Abstract says:** "health loss at ages ≥80 years shifted toward a larger disabling share while fatal burden remained dominant... no country reached a YLD-over-YLL crossover. Mean cardiovascular-disease share decreased from 42.3% to 28.6%"
- **Problem:** The abstract stresses that fatal burden remained dominant and that no country crossed over to disability-dominated health loss; cardiovascular disease still holds the largest cause share. The digest frames the shift as having already changed which challenge dominates.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the disabling share of health loss is growing while fatal burden still dominates, and note cardiovascular disease remains the largest single cause share at 28.6%."

**Issue B. 'Living longer with disability' is an individual-level claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "People over 80 are dying less — and living longer with disability"
- **Abstract says:** "The findings... do not by themselves establish individual-level expansion or compression of morbidity"
- **Problem:** The headline states individual-level morbidity expansion, which the authors explicitly say their population-rate data cannot establish; the Caveats note this, so the headline contradicts the digest's own caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as 'Disability makes up a growing share of health loss after 80' to stay at the population level."

### Study 19: More Than a Third of Hispanic/Latino Adults With Diabetes Had Retinopathy — and Blood Pressure Was a Key Culprit
**PMID:** 42854854 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Heritage-group range is men-only** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "ranged widely by heritage group — from about 14% to 55%"
- **Abstract says:** "Any DR ranged across Hispanic/Latino backgrounds from 13.9%... to 55.4%... in men and 15.3%... to 39.2%... in women"
- **Problem:** The 14%-55% span is the range among men; among women the range was narrower (15.3%-39.2%). Presenting it without the sex qualifier overstates the spread for the sample as a whole.
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify 'from about 14% to 55% among men (15% to 39% among women)'."

**Issue B. Preventability claim not from this study** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Vision loss from diabetes is largely preventable with timely screening and blood pressure control"
- **Abstract says:** "Cross-sectional analysis... Hypertension was associated with DR outcomes, particularly VTDR... support longitudinal evaluation of cardiometabolic risk and DR outcomes"
- **Problem:** The cross-sectional design shows association only; the claim that blood pressure control prevents vision loss is outside background knowledge drawn from this paper and reads as a finding of it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the preventability point to existing guidelines, e.g. 'screening and blood pressure control are standard prevention advice, and these numbers show how common the problem is'."

**Issue C. Population described as 'older' adults** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "how unevenly distributed — the problem is among older Hispanic/Latino adults"
- **Abstract says:** "Adults aged ≥40 years... mean age, 62.7 years"
- **Problem:** The sample is adults 40 and older, not specifically older adults; framing the prevalence as an older-adult statistic slightly overgeneralizes to the digest's primary audience.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'among Hispanic/Latino adults aged 40 and over'."

### Study 21: The ECG Recorded During Your Sleep Study May Predict Heart Trouble a Decade Out
**PMID:** 42852996 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Model input described incompletely** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers trained a deep learning model on single-lead electrocardiogram traces — a signal routinely recorded during overnight sleep studies but usually ignored — from 15,809 patients at Massachusetts General Hospital"
- **Abstract says:** "We applied a deep residual neural network with attention to single-lead ECGs from PSG, combined with sleep stage data, to predict 10-year risk... The network, trained for arrhythmia detection, was finetuned on 15 809 Massachusetts General Hospital patients."
- **Problem:** The digest presents the model as using the ECG trace alone, when the published model also incorporated sleep stage data; it also omits that the network was pre-trained for arrhythmia detection and then fine-tuned on the MGH sample.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that the model combined the overnight ECG with sleep-stage information, and note the network was pre-trained for arrhythmia detection before being fine-tuned on the MGH cohort."

**Issue B. "Free screen" overstates current applicability** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this suggests that data already being collected could double as a free cardiovascular risk screen without any extra test, visit, or cost"
- **Abstract says:** "Deep learning applied to nocturnal ECGs with sleep stage information captures cardiac abnormalities, offering a tool for cardiovascular risk stratification. Integrating such prediction into PSG could enhance detection and support personalized clinical decision-making."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes a research tool that could potentially be integrated into polysomnography; calling it a free screen with no extra cost implies an available service and glosses over the processing, validation and implementation the authors frame as future work.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as "could one day be used for cardiovascular risk stratification using data already collected, if the tool is validated and implemented in practice.""
