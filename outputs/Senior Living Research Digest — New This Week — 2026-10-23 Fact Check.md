### Study 1: Lecanemab in the real world: brain swelling and bleeding risk hinges on genes and pre-existing microbleeds
**PMID:** 42907679 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline causal strength** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "brain swelling and bleeding risk hinges on genes and pre-existing microbleeds"
- **Abstract says:** "Safety outcomes particularly ARIA may be influenced by APOE ε4 genotype and baseline microbleeds."
- **Problem:** "Hinges on" implies a deterministic, established dependency; the authors use the hedged "may be influenced by" for subgroup comparisons within a pooled observational meta-analysis.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "brain swelling and bleeding risk appears to vary with genes and pre-existing microbleeds.""

**Issue B. "Three-fold" applied to both risk factors** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Those two answers shifted ARIA risk roughly three-fold in real-world practice"
- **Abstract says:** "APOE ε4 homozygotes showed the highest rates across all three subtypes (any ARIA 34.1%...) ... non-carriers (11.0%...). Baseline microhemorrhage was associated with higher any ARIA (23.4% vs 11.5%)"
- **Problem:** The ~3-fold figure fits the APOE comparison (34.1% vs 11.0%) but the microbleed contrast is about 2-fold (23.4% vs 11.5%).
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the two answers shifted ARIA risk roughly two- to three-fold, depending on which factor."

**Issue C. Follow-up duration caveat** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "follow-up largely 12 months or less"
- **Abstract says:** "Amyloid PET decreased at 6 months ... and 12 months ... Except for MoCA at ≤ 3 months ... no statistically significant changes were observed in cognitive outcomes."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports outcomes at specific timepoints but never characterizes overall follow-up length across the 37 studies.
- **Suggested fix:** "Unverifiable from abstract only — either drop or attribute to reported outcome timepoints (6 and 12 months)."

### Study 2: Restless legs syndrome travels with neurological disease far more often than chance — a map of 119 studies
**PMID:** 42906263 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Borderline prospective Parkinson's signal** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "particularly the prospective Parkinson's signal, where RLS preceded diagnosis"
- **Abstract says:** "Parkinson's disease (case-control OR 3.22, 95% CI 2.35-4.41; cohort RR 1.74, 95% CI 1.02-2.95)"
- **Problem:** The cohort estimate the digest leans on has a confidence interval whose lower bound is 1.02 — only just excluding no effect — in a synthesis the authors themselves call exploratory.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the prospective estimate was modest and only marginally significant (RR 1.74, 95% CI 1.02–2.95)."

### Study 4: Phone-based lifestyle coaching for people over 60 changed habits — but personalizing it added little
**PMID:** 42895589 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Analyzed vs randomized N** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "441 adults aged 60 and older were assigned to either general or personalized lifestyle counseling"
- **Abstract says:** "A total of 441 participants were analyzed (214 group A; 227 group B)."
- **Problem:** 441 is the number analyzed in the modified intention-to-treat population, not necessarily the number randomized.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "441 adults aged 60 and older were included in the analysis.""

**Issue B. Absence of difference read as equivalence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the evidence here says the simple version is likely to help about as much as the elaborate one"
- **Abstract says:** "The personalized intervention was not associated with a significant reduction in all-cause mortality ... the additional benefit of personalization was limited"
- **Problem:** A non-significant between-arm difference in an open-label trial is not the same as demonstrated equivalence, and the trial was not designed as an equivalence study.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the trial found no clear added benefit from personalization, rather than that the two approaches help equally."

### Study 5: A new blood marker tracks brain shrinkage and memory loss years before dementia
**PMID:** 42906069 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Pre-dementia prediction claim** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "A new blood marker tracks brain shrinkage and memory loss years before dementia"
- **Abstract says:** "Multivariable-adjusted Cox regression was performed to evaluate the association between baseline NT1 levels and risk of clinically diagnosed mild cognitive impairment (MCI) or AD. [No result reported]; Plasma NT1 levels were elevated in MCI/AD individuals ... and were cross-sectionally associated with worse memory performance"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports cross-sectional and longitudinal associations in a cohort that already includes MCI/AD participants; the incident-diagnosis (Cox) result that would support "years before dementia" is not reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "tracks brain shrinkage and memory decline," or note the pre-dementia prediction is not reported in the abstract."

**Issue B. Mediation estimate precision** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Accelerated hippocampal atrophy explained roughly 70% of the link between higher NT1 and memory decline"
- **Abstract says:** "B [95% CI]proportion mediated = 70.1% [32.6%-155.3%]"
- **Problem:** The point estimate is quoted without the very wide confidence interval, which runs from about one third to well over 100%.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that the estimate was imprecise (95% CI roughly 33%–155%)."

### Study 6: Common infections, including herpes viruses, showed no link to Alzheimer's brain changes in a 1946 birth cohort
**PMID:** 42886878 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Omitted suggestive amyloid-PET associations** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "None of the infection measures showed a meaningful association with Alzheimer's neuropathology."
- **Abstract says:** "Odds of Aβ-PET positivity may have been higher for individuals seropositive for Epstein-Barr virus (odds ratio [OR] 2.84; 95% CI 0.95-8.52) and Toxoplasma gondii (OR 1.69; 95% CI 0.94-3.05)."
- **Problem:** The abstract flags two non-significant but substantial point estimates for amyloid positivity; presenting the study as a clean null — and advising readers it "should ease that worry" — goes slightly beyond what the authors reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that EBV and Toxoplasma showed imprecise, non-significant signals for amyloid positivity that the authors note."

**Issue B. Mean age attributed to the PET subgroup** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "plasma p-tau217 in 1,356 people and amyloid PET scans in 424 (average age 69.9)"
- **Abstract says:** "A total of 1,356 and 424 individuals had complete data for p-tau217 and Aβ-PET analyses, respectively. Mean age at p-tau217 was 69.9 years (SD 0.7)"
- **Problem:** The 69.9-year mean is the age at p-tau217 measurement, not specifically the PET subgroup's age; the sentence structure attaches it to the 424.
- **Suggested fix:** "Move the age so it refers to assessment at ages 69–71 overall, e.g. "measured at ages 69–71 (mean 69.9 at the p-tau217 assessment).""

### Study 7: Even past age 90, the Alzheimer's risk gene still takes a toll — on one specific thinking skill

### Study 9: Before heart or lung surgery, weak breathing muscles double the odds of complications
**PMID:** 42906881 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Upper abdominal surgery described as a stated aim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "no upper abdominal surgery cohorts despite that being a stated aim"
- **Abstract says:** "This systematic review and meta-analysis aimed to assess whether low preoperative respiratory muscle mass, reduced strength, or their coexistence as RS or probable RS predicts PPCs after thoracic and cardiac surgery ... all involved thoracic or cardiac surgery, and none included an upper abdominal cohort."
- **Problem:** Upper abdominal surgery is mentioned only as background context for where PPCs occur; the stated aim was thoracic and cardiac surgery, so the absence was not a shortfall against the aim.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "upper abdominal surgery is a common PPC setting but no such cohorts were found.""

**Issue B. Older-adult framing not supported by reported population** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A simple, inexpensive breathing test before surgery may flag older patients who most need prehabilitation"
- **Abstract says:** "Sixteen studies were included; all involved thoracic or cardiac surgery ... [no participant ages reported]"
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no age data for the pooled studies, so the specific applicability to older surgical patients is an extrapolation.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the pooled studies' age distribution is not reported, so relevance to the oldest patients is inferred."

### Study 10: Having many ways to cope — not one good one — protects mood during stressful stretches of later life

### Study 12: Pool workouts beat the gym for lowering blood pressure, a 284-trial analysis finds
**PMID:** 42887271 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline asserts head-to-head superiority** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Pool workouts beat the gym for lowering blood pressure, a 284-trial analysis finds"
- **Abstract says:** "A total of 284 randomized controlled trials... Aquatic exercise achieved the greatest reduction in systolic blood pressure, with a mean difference of -10.28 mmHg... Ten exercise modalities were associated with reductions in blood pressure"
- **Problem:** Rankings in a Bayesian network meta-analysis are modelled estimates, mostly from indirect comparisons; 'beat the gym' implies aquatic exercise was directly tested against gym-based training and won.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to something like 'Pool workouts ranked top for lowering blood pressure in a 284-trial network analysis'."

### Study 13: A small dose of steroid placed next to the nerve quadrupled pain-free time after knee replacement in patients over 65
**PMID:** 42895857 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. 'Pain-free time' overstates the endpoint** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "quadrupled pain-free time after knee replacement in patients over 65"
- **Abstract says:** "The primary outcome was time to first rescue opioid analgesia within 48 h."
- **Problem:** Time to first rescue opioid is not the same as time pain-free; patients can have pain without requesting rescue medication. Also, the trial enrolled patients aged 65-100, i.e., 65 and older rather than 'over 65'.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'quadrupled the time before patients needed rescue pain medication' and say 'patients aged 65 and older'."

**Issue B. Single-centre and diabetes generalisability claims** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Small single-center trial (N=90, 30 per arm) from one Polish hospital... Results may not generalize to patients with diabetes or to different block techniques."
- **Abstract says:** "In this prospective, randomised, quadruple-blind, parallel-group trial, 90 patients aged 65-100 years were allocated equally..."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not state that the trial was single-centre, nor does it describe diabetes exclusion. Unverifiable from abstract only (author affiliations are all Poznan University of Medical Sciences).
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the single-centre descriptor to author affiliations or drop it; drop or soften the diabetes generalisability statement."

### Study 14: Helping people rebuild a sense of meaning may reduce suicide risk more than treating symptoms directly
**PMID:** 42895137 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies comparative effectiveness** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Helping people rebuild a sense of meaning may reduce suicide risk more than treating symptoms directly"
- **Abstract says:** "Meaning-based interventions may reduce suicide-related vulnerability primarily by enhancing existential resources rather than directly targeting symptoms."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes a proposed mechanism (benefit flows through existential resources), not a comparison showing meaning-based interventions outperform symptom-targeted treatment; none of the ten studies made that comparison. Also, outcomes were hopelessness, demoralisation and suicidal ideation, not suicide itself.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to 'Meaning-focused programmes may ease hopelessness and suicidal thinking by rebuilding existential resources rather than by targeting symptoms'."

### Study 17: Incontinence predicts falls — but not enough to screen for it, two big studies find
**PMID:** 42904834 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Age attenuation was an ELSA-specific finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "But the signal faded with age — in those 75 and older it essentially disappeared"
- **Abstract says:** "ELSA showed sub-multiplicative interaction with mobility limitation (ratio 0.80, 95% CI 0.70-0.92) and age attenuation; at ≥ 75 years, the urinary-only RR was 1.03 (95% CI 0.88-1.21)."

### Study 18: Shielding one side of the brain during radiation protected memory as well as shielding both
**PMID:** 42886534 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. 'First randomized evidence' claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is the first randomized evidence that protecting just the left side may preserve verbal memory just as well"
- **Abstract says:** "We investigated whether unilateral (left) HA-WBRT is equivalent to bilateral HA-WBRT for neurocognitive function... These findings warrant validation, particularly when bilateral avoidance is challenging."
- **Problem:** The abstract makes no priority claim. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop 'first' or attribute it ('the authors frame this as the first randomized test of...')."

**Issue B. Single-centre descriptor** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Small N (73 patients) at a single center in Taiwan"
- **Abstract says:** "Among 73 patients (unilateral, n = 36; bilateral, n = 37; median follow-up, 13.2 months)"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not state the trial was conducted at a single centre; this is inferred from author affiliations (National Taiwan University Hospital/Cancer Center).
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'conducted in Taiwan (author affiliations indicate a single academic centre)' or omit the single-centre claim."

### Study 19: A blood test changed the diagnosis for nearly half of memory clinic patients

### Study 21: One in five patients on an anti-amyloid drug had brain swelling — and genotype predicted who
**PMID:** 42908770 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Study reported edema/effusion, not bleeding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is among the first real-world looks at who actually develops brain swelling and bleeding"
- **Abstract says:** "Among 164 patients, 33 (20.1%) developed ARIA-E… ARIA with edema or effusion (ARIA-E) lesions were manually segmented"
- **Problem:** The reported results concern ARIA-E (edema or effusion) only; the abstract gives no incidence or risk-factor data for ARIA-H (microhemorrhage/siderosis), so 'and bleeding' goes beyond what is shown.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'who actually develops ARIA-E — brain swelling or fluid accumulation.'"

**Issue B. Severity range described as 'mild to moderate dementia'** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "retrospectively reviewed 168 patients with mild to moderate dementia (Clinical Dementia Rating 0.5 to 2)"
- **Abstract says:** "168 patients with a Clinical Dementia Rating (CDR) of 0.5 to 2"
- **Problem:** CDR 0.5 denotes very mild impairment (often MCI), so 'mild to moderate dementia' slightly overstates disease stage at the lower end of the range.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write 'patients with very mild to moderate impairment (CDR 0.5 to 2)'."

### Study 22: Helping others feels draining in the moment — but older adults reap the biggest rewards over time
**PMID:** 42902779 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Comparison group overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "it was larger for older adults than for anyone younger"
- **Abstract says:** "This association was significantly stronger among older adults than middle-aged adults in Refresher 1."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports the monthly moderation only as older vs. middle-aged adults (and only in one of the two samples); 'anyone younger' implies a tested comparison with young adults as well.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'larger for older adults than for middle-aged adults, in one of the two samples.'"

**Issue B. Headline implies uniform daily cost** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Helping others feels draining in the moment"
- **Abstract says:** "adults experienced mixed emotional consequences on days when they spent more time helping others, reporting both higher negative and positive affect. Age moderation showed young and middle-aged adults experienced more negative affect."
- **Problem:** Daily helping was tied to both higher negative and higher positive affect, and the extra negative affect was concentrated in younger and middle-aged adults — the headline's 'draining' reads as a general effect.
- **Suggested fix:** "Headline could read 'Helping others carries a same-day emotional cost for younger adults — but older adults reap the longer-term rewards.'"

### Study 23: Interpreters catch dementia clues doctors miss — but the system keeps them at arm's length
**PMID:** 42908888 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Background claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Dementia is routinely misdiagnosed — in both directions — in people assessed in a second language"
- **Abstract says:** "Training clinicians to work effectively with interpreters may improve diagnostic accuracy and care for multilingual patients with dementia."
- **Problem:** The abstract offers no data on misdiagnosis rates or direction; this is background context the study did not measure. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the misdiagnosis claim to prior literature or soften to 'assessment in a second language can compromise diagnostic accuracy.'"

**Issue B. Family-interpreter comparison not studied** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "something a family member is unlikely to do and may inadvertently cover up"
- **Abstract says:** "Clinicians valued interpreters' expertise beyond language translation, including identifying language cues (e.g., pronunciation or word-finding difficulties) and mediating across cultures."
- **Problem:** The study interviewed clinicians and professional interpreters; it did not compare professional interpreters with family members, so this contrast is inference, not finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "Frame as the authors' rationale or general practice guidance rather than a study result."

**Issue C. 'Assessment quality' vs 'communication quality'** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "repeatedly undermined assessment quality"
- **Abstract says:** "booking processes, interpreting modes (e.g., telephone interpreting), unclear role boundaries, and time constraints… limited communication quality"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports limits to clinician–interpreter communication quality; assessment quality is a related but broader claim.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'limited the quality of clinician–interpreter communication'."

### Study 24: Half of people with Long COVID fatigue have undiagnosed sleep apnea — but it isn't causing their symptoms
**PMID:** 42897409 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal phrasing from cross-sectional data** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "but it isn't causing their symptoms"
- **Abstract says:** "it was not significantly associated with Long COVID symptoms, alteration of QoL, or objective cognitive dysfunction"
- **Problem:** A null cross-sectional association does not establish absence of causation; the headline states a causal conclusion the design cannot support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'but apnea severity didn't track with their symptoms'."

**Issue B. Treatment prediction beyond the data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "treating sleep apnea likely won't resolve Long COVID fatigue or brain fog"
- **Abstract says:** "neither AHI nor mean desaturation nadir were significant predictors of QoL or cognitive function"
- **Problem:** No treatment was tested; this prediction also sits in tension with the digest's own caveat that the design 'cannot rule out that treating OSA would help over time.'
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'apnea severity didn't explain symptom burden in this cohort, so treating it may not be enough to resolve Long COVID fatigue — this study did not test treatment.'"

**Issue C. Headline generalizes a referral-clinic sample** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Half of people with Long COVID fatigue have undiagnosed sleep apnea"
- **Abstract says:** "We performed home sleep testing in 100 consecutive patients with Long COVID and no prior history of OSA who reported fatigue… in the population of a Neuro-COVID-19 clinic"
- **Problem:** The 53% figure comes from 100 consecutive patients at one specialty Neuro-COVID clinic, not from people with Long COVID fatigue generally; the caveat notes this but the headline does not.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the setting: 'Half of Long COVID fatigue patients at one neurology clinic had undiagnosed sleep apnea'."
