### Study 2: Volunteering and video calls ease loneliness in older adults — scrolling social media does the opposite
**PMID:** 41934642 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Cross-sectional finding presented as longitudinal** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "older adults who kept volunteering, who worked, and who newly took up video calls with family reported less loneliness two years later, while time spent on social networking sites went the other way"
- **Abstract says:** "Work and volunteering were cross-sectionally associated with lower loneliness at T1, whereas social network site visits were associated with higher loneliness. Continued volunteering over two years and newly initiated video calls with family/friends were associated with lower loneliness at T2"
- **Problem:** Only continued volunteering and newly initiated video calls were associated with loneliness two years later. Work and social-network-site use were cross-sectional (baseline) associations only, so attaching them to the 'two years later' result overstates the longitudinal evidence.
- **Suggested fix:** "Separate the two: 'at baseline, working and volunteering went with less loneliness and social media use with more; over two years, only continued volunteering and newly started video calls predicted lower loneliness.'"

**Issue B. Causal verb in headline for observational data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Volunteering and video calls ease loneliness in older adults — scrolling social media does the opposite"
- **Abstract says:** "Cross-Sectional and Longitudinal Associations … were associated with lower loneliness at T2, controlling for T1 loneliness and other covariates."
- **Problem:** 'Ease' and 'does the opposite' assert causation from a regression analysis of observational survey waves; the digest's own caveats note reverse causation is plausible.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to 'linked to less loneliness' / 'tracked with more loneliness'."

### Study 4: When grown children fall short of expectations, older parents pay a mental health price
**PMID:** 42029190 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Advice not supported by the review** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "naming expectations out loud, rather than waiting for them to be met, is where researchers see the relationship quality that protects mood"
- **Abstract says:** "Relational reciprocity and emotionally meaningful intergenerational bonds are of utmost importance."
- **Problem:** The review reports that relationship quality matters more than proximity; it does not test or recommend explicitly voicing expectations, so this is presented as a research finding when it is the digest's own inference.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the suggestion to the digest rather than the researchers, e.g. 'the review points to relational warmth and reciprocity, not distance, as what matters most.'"

**Issue B. Causal headline for a narrative review** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "When grown children fall short of expectations, older parents pay a mental health price"
- **Abstract says:** "Filial discrepancies and their psychological interpretations appear to be associated with depressive symptoms and loneliness among older adults."
- **Problem:** 'Pay a mental health price' reads as cause and effect from a review of heterogeneous observational studies that the authors describe only in associational terms.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'is linked to more depression and loneliness in older parents.'"

### Study 5: Phone calls and visits from adult children eased depression in older Icelanders over five years
**PMID:** 41201818 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mean ages misattributed** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "5,764 community-dwelling older adults at baseline (2002–2006) and 3,316 at follow-up (2007–2011), with mean ages of 77 and 78"
- **Abstract says:** "Among low depressive symptoms (mean age 77) … Among high depressive symptoms (mean age 78)"
- **Problem:** The two mean ages describe the low- and high-depressive-symptom subgroups, not the baseline and follow-up waves.
- **Suggested fix:** "State that the low-symptom group had a mean age of 77 and the high-symptom group 78."

**Issue B. Causal verb in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Phone calls and visits from adult children eased depression in older Icelanders over five years"
- **Abstract says:** "Longitudinal linear regression assessed associations between social questions and GDS score at follow-up … decrease in depressive symptoms was when telephoning children (β: -4.679, p = .002)"
- **Problem:** 'Eased depression' asserts causation from an observational cohort; the digest's caveats concede reverse causation is plausible.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'were linked to lower depression scores' in the headline."

**Issue C. Equivalence claim overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "while for those doing well, friendships matter just as much as family"
- **Abstract says:** "meeting with children (β: -0.439, p = .007); friends (β: -0.288, p = .002); feeling close to family (β: -0.232, p ≤ .001) and friends (β: -0.217, p = .009)"
- **Problem:** Both family and friend contacts were significant, but the coefficients for children/family were larger; the study did not test whether the two were statistically equivalent.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'contact with friends mattered too, alongside family' rather than 'just as much.'"

**Issue D. Regression coefficient described as a point drop** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "telephone contact with children was associated with a GDS drop of nearly five points"
- **Abstract says:** "decrease in depressive symptoms was when telephoning children (β: -4.679, p = .002)"
- **Problem:** β = -4.679 is a regression coefficient whose scaling (per unit of the contact variable) is not described in the abstract; presenting it as a straightforward five-point GDS fall may overstate the magnitude for readers.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that this is a regression coefficient of -4.7 GDS points per unit of telephone contact, with the units unspecified in the abstract."

### Study 8: One in three cerebral stroke-clot survivors has lasting cognitive problems — even those who look fully recovered
**PMID:** 41416684 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Prevalence stated at the upper bound** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "One in three cerebral stroke-clot survivors has lasting cognitive problems"
- **Abstract says:** "Cognitive impairment affects roughly one-fifth to one-third of CVT survivors (19-29%)"
- **Problem:** The pooled estimate is a range from 19.25% (low-risk-of-bias studies) to 29.20% (overall analysis); stating 'one in three' presents the highest figure as the finding and rounds 29% up.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'one in five to one in three' or 'about a quarter to a third' in the headline and the 'Why it matters' line."

### Study 9: Vitamin E for brain blood flow: decades of research, and not a single human trial
**PMID:** 41964896 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Absolute 'never conducted' claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with no human trial ever conducted"
- **Abstract says:** "Six databases … were searched in 2024 … Of the 335 records returned, no clinical studies were identified."
- **Problem:** The review found no clinical studies meeting its inclusion criteria in a 2024 search of six databases; that is not the same as no human trial ever having been conducted on vitamin E and cerebrovascular function.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write 'the review found no human clinical studies meeting its criteria.'"

**Issue B. Safety advice drawn from animal data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "because vitamin E affects platelet clumping, it's worth telling your doctor or pharmacist you take it, especially if you're also on a blood thinner or aspirin"
- **Abstract says:** "studies examining platelet aggregation reported delayed thrombus formation, likely mediated by reduced oxidative stress and improved endothelial function"
- **Problem:** The platelet-aggregation finding in this review comes from preclinical animal studies; the digest presents it as an established human pharmacological effect underpinning a clinical caution.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the platelet effect was observed in animal models and that interaction concerns come from broader clinical guidance, not this review."

### Study 10: Insurance gets you the blood test — but it doesn't close the diabetes care gap
**PMID:** 42380557 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Gender disparity attributed to the wrong outcome** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Racial, ethnic and gender disparities in blood sugar control persisted even among people who were insured"
- **Abstract says:** "Racial and ethnic disparities in glycemic control persisted among insured individuals … and gender disparities were also observed, particularly in lipid control."
- **Problem:** The abstract locates the persisting glycemic-control disparities in race and ethnicity; gender disparities were observed particularly in lipid control, not blood sugar control.
- **Suggested fix:** "Split the sentence: racial and ethnic disparities persisted in blood sugar control, while gender disparities showed up particularly in lipid control."

### Study 11: Hospital-level care delivered at home worked as well as a hospital bed — and patients liked it better
**PMID:** 42816724 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Equivalence claimed from a non-significant pilot result** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Hospital-level care delivered at home worked as well as a hospital bed — and patients liked it better"
- **Abstract says:** "Remission/complete treatment was achieved in most patients in both groups (86.7% vs. 95.1%; p = 0.153)... This pilot trial... provides preliminary evidence supporting the feasibility and acceptability of community-initiated hospital-level acute care at home (ACAH) for frail older adults."
- **Problem:** A non-significant difference in a small, non-randomized pilot (recovery 8.4 percentage points lower at home) is not evidence of equivalence; the authors claim only feasibility and acceptability, not comparable effectiveness.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like "Hospital-level care at home looked feasible and was preferred by patients — but this pilot was too small to prove it matches a hospital bed.""

**Issue B. Safety overstated in "Why it matters"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is early evidence that intravenous antibiotics, point-of-care testing and remote monitoring can be delivered safely at home without the trade-offs families fear"
- **Abstract says:** "The HaH group received interprofessional visits, intravenous pharmacotherapy, point-of-care diagnostics, and remote patient monitoring... Mortality during the treatment episode was low (2.4% vs. 3.3%; p = 1.000). Nine HaH patients (10.8%) required clinical escalation."
- **Problem:** "Without the trade-offs families fear" goes beyond a pilot that was not powered for safety outcomes; the abstract also says "intravenous pharmacotherapy," not specifically antibiotics.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "early, preliminary evidence that IV therapy, point-of-care testing and remote monitoring can be delivered at home, with escalation to hospital needed in about 1 in 10 cases.""
