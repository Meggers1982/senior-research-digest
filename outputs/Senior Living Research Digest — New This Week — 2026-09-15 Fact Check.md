### Study 1: Handing an older adult a tablet — and teaching them to use it — measurably helps their thinking
**PMID:** 42734971 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "First" claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the first meta-analytic evidence that the relationship may actually run from technology use to better cognition rather than the reverse"
- **Abstract says:** "A recent meta-analysis of cross-sectional and prospective cohort studies has shown strong evidence... Given the observational nature of the reviewed studies, it is unclear whether the observed associations reflect causal effects. We conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis to examine the causal relation..."
- **Problem:** The abstract never claims priority or that this is the first such meta-analysis of experimental studies; it only notes prior meta-analyses were observational.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "pooled evidence from experimental rather than observational studies, suggesting the relationship may run from technology use to better cognition"."

**Issue B. Follow-up length unverifiable** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "follow-up periods in the underlying trials are generally short"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract reports 14 included experimental studies and the pooled effect but says nothing about follow-up duration in the included trials."
- **Problem:** A specific limitation is attributed to the included studies that cannot be checked from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the claim or mark it as not reported in the abstract."

### Study 4: Where you live may keep depression from lifting — even after accounting for your own circumstances
**PMID:** 42107653 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Green space association not statistically significant** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "living in a neighborhood with lower socioeconomic position, lower safety scores and less green space was associated with lower odds of recovery"
- **Abstract says:** "green space (OR [95%CI] = 0.87 [0.76-1.03])... social security beneficiaries [95%CI] = 1.14 [1.00-1.30]"
- **Problem:** The green-space odds ratio's confidence interval includes 1 (and the social-security estimate is borderline), so those two associations are not clearly significant, yet the digest lists green space alongside the significant findings and then recommends building green-space access into a routine.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the green-space (and social-security) estimates were borderline/non-significant, e.g. "less green space showed a similar but statistically borderline association"."

### Study 6: Common medications may be raising fracture and ER risk in Parkinson's patients
**PMID:** 42736699 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Odds ratios described as risk increases** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Higher burden was clearly linked to a 53% greater risk of fracture and a 25% greater risk of emergency department visits."
- **Abstract says:** "significantly associated with higher risks of fracture (OR: 1.53; 95% CI: 1.28 to 1.84) and emergency department visits (OR: 1.25; 95% CI: 1.15 to 1.36)"
- **Problem:** The pooled estimates are odds ratios, which are not identical to relative risk; converting them directly to "53% greater risk" slightly overstates precision, and "clearly linked" adds emphasis not in the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "53% higher odds of fracture and 25% higher odds of an emergency department visit"."

### Study 9: One in six memory-clinic patients got a blood test result that didn't match their brain scan
**PMID:** 42287769 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline comparator oversimplified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "One in six memory-clinic patients got a blood test result that didn't match their brain scan"
- **Abstract says:** "discordant plasma p-tau217 results and amyloid status determined by the Aβ42/p-tau181 CSF ratio or Aβ-PET biomarkers"
- **Problem:** The reference standard was cerebrospinal fluid analysis or amyloid PET, not only a brain scan; the headline could mislead readers about what was compared, although the body text is correct.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "...didn't match their spinal fluid or PET result"."

**Issue B. Country attribution inferred from affiliations** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers at memory clinics in Denmark and Sweden"
- **Abstract says:** "an ethnically diverse mixed memory clinic cohort; author affiliations are in Copenhagen, Denmark and Lund/Malmö, Sweden"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not state where patients were recruited; the two-country setting is inferred from author affiliations. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute more cautiously, e.g. "Danish and Swedish researchers ... in a mixed memory clinic cohort"."

### Study 16: Social disadvantage doubled dementia risk — and for women with type 2 diabetes, it multiplied it fivefold
**PMID:** 42730922 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal phrasing in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Social disadvantage doubled dementia risk — and for women with type 2 diabetes, it multiplied it fivefold"
- **Abstract says:** "Adverse SDOH was associated with higher dementia risk, with stronger T2D-related risk among medium/unfavorable SDOH groups, especially in women with T2D."
- **Problem:** 'Doubled' and 'multiplied it fivefold' state an effect rather than an association for observational cohort data; the body text and caveats correctly hedge, so the headline is out of step.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'Social disadvantage tracked with double the dementia risk — and fivefold odds for women with type 2 diabetes'."

**Issue B. SDOH components not specified in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "combined social determinants of health — things like income, education, housing, and social support — into a weighted score"
- **Abstract says:** "SDOH indicators were weighted using elastic net and categorized as favorable, medium, or unfavorable."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not list which indicators went into the score; the examples given are unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write 'combined a set of social determinants of health into a weighted score' or attribute the examples to the full text."

### Study 17: Screening older adults for a treatable brain condition turned up something else: widespread undiagnosed hearing loss
**PMID:** 42314794 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

### Study 21: Days spent with grandkids came with more happiness — and more steps
**PMID:** 42720933 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Bedtime emotion effect attributed to older participants only** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "On days when older participants spent more time with younger relatives, they reported more high-energy positive emotion (happiness, enthusiasm, excitement) during the day and more calm contentment before bed."
- **Abstract says:** "On days older adult participants spent more time with younger family members, they reported greater high-arousal positive emotion (happiness, enthusiasm, excitement) during the day. Across the age span of the sample, on days participants spent more time with younger family members, they reported greater low-arousal positive emotion (contentment, relaxation, calm) before bed."
- **Problem:** The low-arousal (calm contentment) finding was reported across the full 23–92 age span, not specifically for older participants; the digest folds both results into the older-adult sentence.
- **Suggested fix:** "Separate the two: "…older participants reported more high-energy positive emotion during the day, while across the whole sample, high-engagement days brought more calm contentment before bed.""

**Issue B. "Grandkids" in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Days spent with grandkids came with more happiness — and more steps"
- **Abstract says:** "Intergenerational engagement-here referring to time spent with younger family members"
- **Problem:** The exposure was time with younger family members generally; grandchildren are not specified as the relationship measured.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "time with younger family members" in the headline, or say "grandkids and other younger relatives.""

**Issue C. Slightly firmer than the authors' wording** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "time with grandchildren and younger relatives isn't just emotionally pleasant but functions as a naturally occurring form of exercise"
- **Abstract says:** "intergenerational engagement may represent a naturally occurring pathway through which emotionally meaningful social ties support both emotional well-being and opportunities for everyday movement"
- **Problem:** "Functions as" asserts more than the authors' hedged "may represent a naturally occurring pathway" for a within-person diary association.
- **Suggested fix:** ""…may act as a naturally occurring opportunity for everyday movement.""

### Study 22: Three simple measurements together flag stroke risk better than any one alone
**PMID:** 42722214 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Unsupported comparative claim in headline** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Three simple measurements together flag stroke risk better than any one alone"
- **Abstract says:** "to test whether a rule-based vascular-muscle-adiposity adverse-count phenotype was associated with incident stroke… This three-component phenotype may offer a reproducible way to summarise vascular, muscular and adiposity-related susceptibility to incident stroke"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports associations for the combined count only; it does not compare the composite's predictive performance against each component individually, so "better than any one alone" is not established.
- **Suggested fix:** ""A three-part score of blood pressure, grip strength and waist size tracks with stroke risk across three countries.""

**Issue B. Same overreach repeated in "Why it matters"** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "this suggests looking at them as a package conveys more about stroke risk than treating them as separate concerns"
- **Abstract says:** "Blood pressure, muscle function and central adiposity are routinely measurable but are usually examined as separate risk domains… may offer a reproducible way to summarise… susceptibility"
- **Problem:** Again asserts incremental value over the individual measures, which the reported analysis does not quantify.
- **Suggested fix:** ""…suggests a simple count of these three measures summarises stroke susceptibility in a reproducible way across cohorts.""

**Issue C. Causal verb for observational finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Each additional adverse measure raised stroke risk by about 30%"
- **Abstract says:** "Adjusted hazard ratios per 1-step increase were 1.32… 1.44… 1.22… The pooled hazard ratio per 1-step increase was 1.30 (95% CI 1.20-1.41)"
- **Problem:** "Raised" implies causation from pooled observational cohort data.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Each additional adverse measure was associated with about 30% higher stroke risk.""

**Issue D. Stroke ascertainment detail not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "stroke events in these cohorts are largely self-reported or interview-based"
- **Abstract says:** "it should not be interpreted as a clinical threshold without adjudicated outcome validation"
- **Problem:** The abstract notes the lack of adjudicated outcomes but does not state that events were self-reported or interview-based.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "outcomes were not formally adjudicated, as the authors note" — otherwise mark as unverifiable from abstract only."

### Study 23: Trouble cooking a hot meal or managing pills predicted dementia eight years later
**PMID:** 42720743 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Comparing effect sizes on different scales** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with daily-functioning problems more strongly associated than cognitive test scores"
- **Abstract says:** "Both decreased cognitive function [OR: 0.95 (95% CI: 0.92-0.99)] and daily functioning [OR: 0.70 (95% CI: 0.64-0.77)] at baseline were associated with increased risk of dementia."
- **Problem:** The two odds ratios are per-unit estimates on different measurement scales, so the abstract does not license a direct ranking of which domain is "more strongly associated."
- **Suggested fix:** ""Both baseline cognition and daily functioning predicted dementia, with three specific daily tasks and episodic memory emerging as the leading nodes in the network analysis.""

### Study 24: A body-composition reading tracked with measured hearing loss in adults over 40

- **Problem:** The digest presents an explicitly exploratory subgroup contrast as a firm finding.
