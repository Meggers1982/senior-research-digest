### Study 1: Depressive Symptoms Over Decades May Be the Bridge Between Early-Life Stress and Poor Health in Older Age
**PMID:** 42633642 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Cognitive health claim not supported by abstract** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "the mental health consequences of early adversity don't simply fade—they can accumulate and shape physical and cognitive health well into older age."
- **Abstract says:** "Early stressful life experiences predicted higher depressive symptoms, which in turn were associated with poorer physical health, greater limitation in physical functioning, and greater perceived health decline in later adulthood"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not mention cognitive health as an outcome. The outcomes listed are physical health, physical functioning limitations, and perceived health decline. The digest introduces "cognitive health" which is not supported by the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "the mental health consequences of early adversity don't simply fade—they can accumulate and shape physical health and functioning well into older age."

No other factual errors found. Study facts (N=561, LSOG, time points, outcomes) and framing are otherwise consistent with the abstract.

### Study 3: Insulin Resistance Marker Linked to Higher Risk of Enlarged Prostate in Older Men
**PMID:** 42640724 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Imprecise characterization of study design** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "cross-sectional data" (in Caveats)
- **Abstract says:** "Data from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS) and the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) were examined."
- **Problem:** CHARLS is a longitudinal study. The abstract does not specify whether the analysis was cross-sectional or longitudinal. This caveat may be accurate but cannot be verified from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** Flag as "Unverifiable from abstract only" or remove specific design claim if uncertain.

No other issues found. Sample sizes (4,382 CHARLS, 3,463 NHANES), inverse relationship between eGDR and BPH, and the note about eGDR being estimated are accurate.

### Study 9: Prediction Tool May Help Identify Older Men at Risk for Delirium After Prostate Surgery
**PMID:** 42634783 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Slight imprecision in delirium incidence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "about 20% developed delirium in the recovery room"
- **Abstract says:** "The incidence of RRD was 19.5% in the training set and 20% in the validation set"
- **Problem:** The digest's "about 20%" is reasonable rounding but slightly imprecise since the abstract gives specific figures for different cohorts. This is a very minor issue.
- **Suggested fix:** "About 20% developed delirium in the recovery room (19.5% in the training set, 20% in the validation set)" or keep as is given acceptable rounding.

No other issues found. Sample size (N=1,166), risk factors (age, ASA, albumin, hypothermia, hypotension), and single-center retrospective design caveat are accurate.

### Study 10: Elevated Inflammation Marker Linked to Longer Hospital Stays for Older Adults with Appendicitis
**PMID:** 42634733 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mediation percentage differs** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Complicated appendicitis partially explained the link between CAR and length of stay"
- **Abstract says:** "complicated appendicitis exerted a significant partial mediating effect between CAR and LOS, accounting for 57.8% of the total effect"
- **Problem:** The digest accurately states "partially explained" but omits the specific mediation percentage (57.8%), which is notable since it represents more than half the effect. Not an error, but a missed opportunity for precision.
- **Suggested fix:** "Complicated appendicitis partially explained (57.8%) the link between CAR and length of stay."

No other factual errors found. Sample size (N=327), age (≥65), outcomes, and study design are accurate.

### Study 12: Malnutrition and Multiple Chronic Conditions Together Worsen Outcomes for Cancer Patients

- **As written:** "82% were diagnosed with malnutrition"
- **Abstract says:** "10,673 (82.4%) patients were diagnosed with malnutrition"
- **Problem:** Minor rounding difference (82% vs 82.4%). Acceptable rounding.
- **Suggested fix:** No change needed; acceptable rounding.

No other issues found. Sample size (N=12,947), outcomes, risk ratios (>4 times for 30-day mortality), and observational design are accurately represented.
