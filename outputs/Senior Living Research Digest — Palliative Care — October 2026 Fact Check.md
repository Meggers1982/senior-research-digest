### Study 2: Older Cancer Patients Get Lower Opioid Doses Than Younger Ones — and Still Report the Same Pain Relief
**PMID:** 42853544 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal phrasing in Why it matters** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "in a specialist palliative care setting, lower doses tailored to age-related changes in how the body handles drugs produced equally good pain control"
- **Abstract says:** "Despite age-related differences in opioid prescribing, comparable pain control was achieved across all age groups ... supporting individualized age-tailored opioid titration in specialist palliative care."
- **Problem:** "Produced" asserts that the lower doses caused the equivalent pain control, and "tailored to age-related changes in how the body handles drugs" attributes a prescribing rationale the study only observed as an association. The digest's own Caveats say causation cannot be established, so the body text contradicts the caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "older patients received lower doses yet reported comparable pain control, which the authors say supports individualized, age-tailored titration.""

### Study 3: How Older Chinese Immigrants Come to Accept Hospice: A Slow Reconciliation of Filial Piety and American Medicine
**PMID:** 42833695 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Overstated ranking of information source** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The most influential information channel in this study was other family members and friends"
- **Abstract says:** "Familial and social networks served as key sources of new information; encounters with healthcare and other institutions reshaped care preferences and decision-making"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes familial and social networks as "key sources" of new information, not as the single most influential channel; a qualitative thematic study does not rank channels by influence.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Family members and friends were a key source of new information in this study, which argues for peer-led, language-matched education sessions.""

### Study 4: A Nursing Home Palliative Care Program Cut Hospitalizations Dramatically and Let 98% of Residents Die Where They Lived
**PMID:** 42580497 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for uncontrolled QI project** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A Nursing Home Palliative Care Program Cut Hospitalizations Dramatically"
- **Abstract says:** "An organization-wide palliative care pathway in LTCFs appears feasible and may support end-of-life care in facilities, although findings require cautious interpretation."
- **Problem:** The headline asserts the program caused the drop in hospitalizations, while this is a before-and-after quality improvement report with no control group — a limitation the digest's own Caveats acknowledge.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "Hospitalizations Fell Sharply After a Nursing Home Palliative Care Program Was Introduced.""

**Issue B. Denominator for the 98% figure** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Let 98% of Residents Die Where They Lived"
- **Abstract says:** "Among 250 deaths, 245 (98.0%) occurred in LTCFs."
- **Problem:** The 98% applies to the 250 residents who died, not to all 405 enrolled residents; the headline alone implies the larger denominator.
- **Suggested fix:** ""98% of Residents Who Died Did So in the Facility.""

### Study 8: "Delays, Bills and Confusion": What Pancreatic Cancer Patients and Their Caregivers Say Is Missing From Their Care

### Study 10: When Nurses Are Empowered to Flag Patients, Palliative Referrals Rise
**PMID:** 42853191 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Knowledge scores described as self-assessed** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "knowledge scores self-assessed via testing of 20 nurses"
- **Abstract says:** "Nursing knowledge scores improved substantially following education."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not say whether the knowledge measure was self-assessed or objectively tested — unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "nursing knowledge measured by pre/post scores in 20 nurses; the abstract does not describe the instrument.""

**Issue B. Overgeneralized conclusion from a tiny QI project** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the message is that screening works when a named person is empowered to act on it"
- **Abstract says:** "These findings suggest that structured nurse-driven screening can strengthen nursing advocacy, support earlier identification of unmet palliative needs, and promote more consistent access to palliative care consultation in acute care settings."
- **Problem:** A single-unit, uncontrolled project with 5 additional referrals cannot establish that screening "works"; the authors use "suggest" and "can," and no patient outcomes were measured.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "the two studies together hint that screening is more likely to lead to referral when someone is explicitly empowered to act on a positive result.""

### Study 11: Advance Care Plans Cut Unwanted Hospital Trips at the End of Life — But Only When They're Specific
**PMID:** 42714187 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal verb in headline for a review of mostly observational studies** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Advance Care Plans Cut Unwanted Hospital Trips at the End of Life — But Only When They're Specific"
- **Abstract says:** "Advance care plans or advance care directives that included instructions to not resuscitate or not hospitalize resulted in fewer hospital transfers than those that did not."
- **Problem:** "Cut" asserts causation from a scoping review of 23 studies that were mostly observational (the digest's own caveats acknowledge causation cannot be established). The abstract's own phrasing is loose, but the body text correctly says "were linked with," so the headline is out of step.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like "Specific Advance Care Plans Are Linked With Fewer Hospital Trips at the End of Life.""

**Issue B. "Only when they're specific" / "vague paperwork does little"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this review suggests that vague paperwork does little to prevent it while specific, actionable instructions do"
- **Abstract says:** "Advance care plans or advance care directives that included instructions to not resuscitate or not hospitalize resulted in fewer hospital transfers than those that did not."
- **Problem:** The review reports a relative difference between plans with and without explicit instructions; it does not establish that plans lacking such instructions have no effect at all.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say plans with explicit instructions were associated with fewer transfers than plans without them, rather than implying non-specific plans do nothing."

**Issue C. "Family pressure" as a driver** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with transfers driven by acuity, perceived vulnerability, age, and family pressure"
- **Abstract says:** "Reasons for hospital transfer were classified as age, acuity, vulnerability, and family-related."
- **Problem:** The abstract lists "family-related" reasons without characterizing them as pressure; "perceived vulnerability" likewise adds an interpretation.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use the abstract's categories: "age, acuity, vulnerability, and family-related factors.""

### Study 13: Psilocybin Therapy for Depression Worked Just as Well in Cancer Patients Still Receiving Chemotherapy
**PMID:** 42546985 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Chemotherapy" substituted for systemic anticancer treatment** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Psilocybin Therapy for Depression Worked Just as Well in Cancer Patients Still Receiving Chemotherapy"
- **Abstract says:** "Active systemic anticancer treatment was defined as receipt of systemic therapy within 30 days of psilocybin dosing (systemic treatment, n = 17; no systemic treatment, n = 13)."
- **Problem:** "Systemic anticancer treatment" covers immunotherapy, targeted and hormonal agents as well as cytotoxic chemotherapy; narrowing it to "chemotherapy" (repeated as "mid-chemotherapy" in Why it matters) misstates the exposure group.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "active cancer treatment" or "systemic anticancer therapy" in the headline and body."

**Issue B. "Worked just as well" from a non-significant difference in a 30-person trial** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Depression scores dropped substantially in both groups with no significant differences at weeks 1, 3, or 8"
- **Abstract says:** "MADRS scores decreased substantially in both groups, with no significant between-group differences at week 1 (P = .88), week 3 (P = .93), or week 8 (P = .97)."
- **Problem:** The body text is faithful, but the headline's "worked just as well" reads as demonstrated equivalence, which a 30-participant open-label single-arm secondary analysis cannot establish. The digest's caveats do flag the small N.
- **Suggested fix:** "Frame as "no detectable difference" rather than equivalence in the headline."

### Study 15: Heart Disease in the ICU: Experts Lay Out Ten Things Comfort-Focused Care Should Address
**PMID:** 42844232 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Frequently face" overstates hedged abstract wording** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "survivors frequently face lasting functional impairment and psychological distress"
- **Abstract says:** "those who survive may subsequently face persistent functional impairment and psychosocial distress"
- **Problem:** The abstract uses "may" without any frequency estimate; "frequently" adds a prevalence claim the abstract does not support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "survivors may face lasting functional impairment and psychosocial distress.""

**Issue B. Specific topics attributed to the guidance** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The guidance covers symptom burden, decisions about life-sustaining technologies such as mechanical circulatory support, prognostic uncertainty, and caregiver needs."
- **Abstract says:** "patients often endure prognostic uncertainty and face complex decisions regarding life-sustaining therapies alongside refractory symptoms... to support PC clinicians caring for CICU patients and their caregivers"
- **Problem:** Mechanical circulatory support is not named in the abstract; the specific content of the ten tips is unverifiable from the abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the "such as mechanical circulatory support" example or attribute it to the full text."

### Study 16: Nearly a Third of People Are Lonely in Their Final Year of Life
**PMID:** 42492599 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Risk factors described as "strongest"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The strongest risk factors in fully adjusted models were having a likely mental health condition, being unmarried, and living with three or more functional impairments."
- **Abstract says:** "Risk factors included having a likely mental health condition, being unmarried and having three or more functional impairments in fully adjusted analyses."
- **Problem:** The abstract lists these as risk factors retained in fully adjusted analyses but does not rank them by strength.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change "The strongest risk factors" to "Risk factors identified in fully adjusted models.""

**Issue B. Attribution of authorship** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers at Marie Curie and University College London pooled five waves of the UK Household Longitudinal Study"
- **Abstract says:** "Authors are affiliated with Marie Curie/UCL, the University of Sheffield, Queen's University Belfast, and Marie Curie Research Voices."
- **Problem:** The team was broader than the two named institutions, though the lead and senior authors are from Marie Curie/UCL.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "and colleagues at Sheffield and Queen's University Belfast" or say "UK researchers led by Marie Curie and UCL.""

### Study 17: Commercially Insured Patients Got More Aggressive — Not Better — End-of-Life Care Than Medicaid Patients
**PMID:** 42492600 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Extension to Medicare-age patients** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "it shows how rarely people with blood cancers get hospice or palliative care at all — a gap that persists into Medicare age"
- **Abstract says:** "adults enrolled in the Merative MarketScan Commercial and Medicaid Research Databases with hematologic malignancies (18-64 years) who died between 2016 and 2021"
- **Problem:** The cohort was entirely under 65; the study provides no data on patients of Medicare age, and the digest's own caveat says findings "extend only indirectly to Medicare-age seniors."
- **Suggested fix:** "Delete "a gap that persists into Medicare age" or rephrase as "a gap documented here only in adults under 65.""

**Issue B. Transfusion/hospice eligibility claim not in the study** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "ask what your insurance plan requires before hospice can begin, since transfusion and treatment rules often create the barrier"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract reports utilization rates and odds ratios by insurance type; it does not examine plan rules, transfusion policies, or hospice eligibility criteria."
- **Problem:** Presents a specific mechanism as if established by this study. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Flag it as general background about hematologic malignancies rather than a finding of this analysis."

### Study 18: Stopping Pills Can Be as Important as Starting Them in Hospice Care
**PMID:** 42519959 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Under-recognized" nursing role** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with nurses playing a central but under-recognized role in reconciliation and shared decision-making"
- **Abstract says:** "Nurses play a central role in medication reconciliation, identifying potentially inappropriate medications, facilitating shared decision-making, and monitoring patient response after medication withdrawal."
- **Problem:** "Under-recognized" is an editorial addition; the abstract describes the role as central but does not characterize it as unrecognized.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "but under-recognized" or attribute it to the authors' discussion."

**Issue B. Unsupported pill-count figure** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Many people in hospice or with advanced illness are still swallowing a dozen or more pills a day"
- **Abstract says:** "Adults with serious illnesses and limited life expectancy often continue medications with little meaningful benefit, contributing to polypharmacy, adverse drug events, patient distress, and caregiver burden."
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no medication counts; "a dozen or more pills a day" is a specific figure not supported by it. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use the abstract's language about polypharmacy and medication burden without a specific pill count."

### Study 19: When Cancer Blocks the Bowel: Chemotherapy Helped One in Three, but at Steep Cost
**PMID:** 42843988 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Chemotherapy" for systemic anticancer therapy** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Chemotherapy Helped One in Three, but at Steep Cost"
- **Abstract says:** "Eligible patients had advanced cancer with inoperable MBO refractory to medical management and treated by systemic anticancer therapy."
- **Problem:** Systemic anticancer therapy is broader than chemotherapy; the body text correctly says "systemic anticancer therapy," so the headline is narrower than the study.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "Anticancer Drug Therapy" or "Systemic Treatment" in the headline."

**Issue B. Causal "helped" in an uncontrolled retrospective cohort** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Chemotherapy Helped One in Three"
- **Abstract says:** "Obstruction resolution occurred in 33% of patients."
- **Problem:** With no comparison group (as the digest's caveats note), resolution in 33% cannot be attributed to the therapy; the headline implies it can.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as "Obstruction Cleared in One in Three Patients Given Anticancer Therapy.""
