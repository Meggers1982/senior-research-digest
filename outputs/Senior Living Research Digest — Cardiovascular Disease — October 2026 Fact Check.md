### Study 1: Three in Four Rural Medicare Patients With High Blood Pressure Aren't at Goal
**PMID:** 42720604 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

**Issue A. Specific mortality types vs. general CVD mortality** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "More than one in five U.S. older adults lives in a rural area where stroke and heart disease death rates run higher"
- **Abstract says:** "More than one-in-five older adults live in the rural U.S. Adults living in rural U.S. experience significantly higher hypertension prevalence and cardiovascular disease-related mortality compared to their urban counterparts."
- **Problem:** The abstract refers broadly to cardiovascular disease-related mortality; the digest narrows this to "stroke and heart disease" specifically.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "where cardiovascular death rates run higher.""

### Study 4: Long-Term Lithium Users With Bipolar Disorder Showed Less Artery Calcification a Decade Later
**PMID:** 42674302 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Long-term" lithium exposure not specified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Long-Term Lithium Users With Bipolar Disorder Showed Less Artery Calcification a Decade Later"
- **Abstract says:** "177 patients with BD who had available baseline and approximately 10-year follow-up chest radiographs were classified according to their lithium treatment status"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes classification by lithium treatment status over a roughly 10-year observation window but does not report duration of lithium therapy, so "long-term users" is an inference.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "Lithium-Treated Patients With Bipolar Disorder Showed Less Artery Calcification a Decade Later.""

**Issue B. Single health system claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "single health system population may not generalize"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract reports a retrospective cohort of 177 patients but does not describe the number of sites or health systems."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "single retrospective cohort; setting and recruitment source may limit generalizability.""

### Study 5: Depression Raises Heart Disease and Death Risk in People With Diabetes — and Blood Sugar Isn't the Reason
**PMID:** 42624218 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for observational data** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Depression Raises Heart Disease and Death Risk in People With Diabetes — and Blood Sugar Isn't the Reason"
- **Abstract says:** "Depression is associated with greater risks for incident cardiovascular disease and all-cause death in people with type 2 diabetes."
- **Problem:** "Raises" asserts causation from a population-based observational cohort; the abstract consistently uses associational language, and the body text and Caveats of the digest correctly hedge.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Depression Is Linked to Higher Heart Disease and Death Risk in People With Diabetes.""

**Issue B. Mean age/sex attribution** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "571,129 Chinese adults with type 2 diabetes (mean age 60.4 years, 66% women)"
- **Abstract says:** "Among 571,129 people with type 2 diabetes, 7.5% had depression (mean age 60.4 years, 66.1% women, mean HbA1c 7.2%)"
- **Problem:** In the abstract the mean age and sex figures sit inside the parentheses describing the depression subgroup, so attributing them to the full cohort may be incorrect.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the demographics to the depression group, or note the ambiguity ("the group with depression had a mean age of 60.4 years and was 66% women")."

**Issue C. Named mediators not specified in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "conventional risk factors like HbA1c, blood pressure and cholesterol explained very little of the excess risk"
- **Abstract says:** "We examined the mediating effects of eight cardiometabolic risk factors in these risk associations... Mediation analyses revealed minor contributions from metabolic risk factors on these associations"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not name which eight cardiometabolic factors were tested; the specific examples are an inference.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "eight conventional cardiometabolic risk factors explained very little of the excess risk.""

### Study 8: Optimism Is Linked to Less Depression in Older Adults — But It Doesn't Cushion Life's Blows
**PMID:** 42567210 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

**Issue A. Mild causal phrasing for associations** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "higher optimism was associated with lower risk of developing depression, while pessimism and stressful life events each raised it"
- **Abstract says:** "Pessimism (HR, 1.07; 95% CI 1.06-1.08) and stressful life events (HR, 1.17; 95% CI 1.14-1.21) were associated with a higher depression risk."

### Study 9: Big Statin Trial in Healthy 70-Somethings: Fewer Heart Attacks and Strokes, but No Extra Years of Independent Living
**PMID:** 42670961 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "First large randomized trial" claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "This is the first large randomized trial designed specifically to answer whether healthy older adults with no heart disease should start a statin"
- **Abstract says:** "The effectiveness and safety of statins for the primary prevention of cardiovascular events and the extension of disability-free survival among older adults remain uncertain."
- **Problem:** The abstract makes no priority claim; "first" is unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "This is a large randomized trial designed specifically to test statin initiation in healthy older adults.""

**Issue B. Rounding of effect size** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a statin's roughly one-third reduction in heart attack and stroke risk"
- **Abstract says:** "hazard ratio, 0.70; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.61 to 0.82"
- **Problem:** A hazard ratio of 0.70 is a 30% relative reduction; "roughly one-third" rounds upward, and the composite endpoint also included cardiovascular death and coronary revascularization, not just heart attack and stroke.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "about a 30% reduction in major cardiovascular events (heart attack, stroke, cardiovascular death or revascularization).""

### Study 14: First Oral GLP-1 Pill Clears Its Heart Safety Test in People With Type 2 Diabetes
**PMID:** 42815506 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline novelty claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "First Oral GLP-1 Pill Clears Its Heart Safety Test in People With Type 2 Diabetes"
- **Abstract says:** "Orforglipron is an oral, non-peptide GLP-1 receptor agonist. While some peptide GLP-1 receptor agonists have established cardiovascular benefit, the cardiovascular safety of non-peptide GLP-1 receptor agonists has not been studied."
- **Problem:** The abstract frames the novelty as the first cardiovascular safety assessment of a non-peptide GLP-1 agonist, not of the first oral GLP-1 pill — oral peptide GLP-1 agonists already exist and have been studied. The body text gets this right, but the headline does not.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "First Non-Peptide Oral GLP-1 Pill Clears Its Heart Safety Test" or "New Oral GLP-1 Pill Clears Its Heart Safety Test.""

### Study 15: Which Combinations of Chronic Conditions Predict Death? A 16,000-Person Study Maps Them Out — and They Differ by Sex
**PMID:** 42821655 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Hypertension–cholesterol cluster risk not reported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The common hypertension-and-high-cholesterol cluster, affecting over half the group, was not among the deadliest."
- **Abstract says:** "Five clusters were identified overall: hypertension & dyslipidemia (52.1%)... Higher mortality was also observed for the metabolic, gout & kidney disease cluster in males (aHR 1.63 [1.47-1.81]) and the gout, anemia & kidney disease cluster in females (aHR 1.96 [1.74-2.21])."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports no mortality estimate for the hypertension & dyslipidemia cluster (it appears to serve as the comparator), so its relative deadliness cannot be confirmed from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the hypertension-and-high-cholesterol cluster — the largest, at 52% — was not among the clusters flagged for elevated mortality in the reported results, or note this is unverifiable from the abstract."

### Study 17: One in Three Hispanic/Latino Adults With Diabetes Has Eye Damage — and Blood Pressure Is a Big Part of Why
**PMID:** 42854854 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Sex difference stated as established** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Age-standardized prevalence of any diabetic retinopathy was 34.7%, higher in men (41.8%) than women (27.2%)"
- **Abstract says:** "Among men, any DR and VTDR prevalence was 41.8% (95% CI, 33.5-50.0) and 13.4%... versus 27.2% (95% CI, 19.5-34.9) and 9.4%... among women."
- **Problem:** The confidence intervals for men and women overlap and the abstract reports no test of the sex difference, so presenting it as a definite difference slightly overstates the evidence.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add hedging: "estimates were higher in men (41.8%) than women (27.2%), though the confidence intervals overlap.""

**Issue B. Background-group range conflates sexes** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "ranged from about 14% to 55% across different Hispanic/Latino background groups"
- **Abstract says:** "Any DR ranged across Hispanic/Latino backgrounds from 13.9% ... to 55.4% ... in men and 15.3% ... to 39.2% ... in women"
- **Problem:** The 14%–55% range is the men-only range; the women's range was narrower (15%–39%). The digest presents it as a single overall range, and the story angle repeats it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify that the range was 13.9%–55.4% among men and 15.3%–39.2% among women."

**Issue C. Screening underuse claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "supports pushing hard on annual retinal screening, which remains underused in this population"
- **Abstract says:** "No statement about screening uptake or underuse."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only — the abstract reports prevalence and risk-factor associations, not screening rates.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "which remains underused in this population" or attribute it to a separate source."

### Study 19: Staying functionally strong in your 50s and 60s tracks with far lower heart disease risk, three-continent study finds
**PMID:** 42697165 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Three-continent" descriptor** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "three-continent study finds"
- **Abstract says:** "the Survey of Health, Ageing and Retirement in Europe (SHARE), the English Longitudinal Study of Ageing (ELSA), and the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS)"
- **Problem:** The three cohorts span two continents (Europe — including England — and Asia), not three.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "three-cohort study across Europe and China" or "two-continent, three-cohort study.""

**Issue B. "Pooled" overstates the analysis** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers pooled three major aging cohorts"
- **Abstract says:** "This study was based on three large longitudinal ageing cohorts... Compared with the low trajectory group... (SHARE, HR = 0.89...; ELSA, HR = 0.66...; CHARLS, HR = 0.67...)"
- **Problem:** Results are reported separately for each cohort, indicating parallel cohort-specific analyses rather than a pooled dataset.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say researchers "analysed three major ageing cohorts in parallel" or "replicated the analysis across three cohorts.""

**Issue C. Middle trajectory not significant in SHARE** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with the middle group falling in between"
- **Abstract says:** "participants in the middle trajectory group were associated with lower risks of CVD (SHARE, HR = 0.89, 95% CI: 0.77-1.02; ELSA, HR = 0.66, 95% CI: 0.53-0.82; CHARLS, HR = 0.67, 95% CI: 0.58-0.76)"
- **Problem:** In SHARE the middle-trajectory estimate crossed 1.0 and was not statistically significant; the digest implies a consistent intermediate benefit.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that the middle-group advantage was clear in ELSA and CHARLS but not statistically significant in SHARE."

### Study 20: Heart doctors issue practical playbook for surviving extreme heat with cardiovascular disease
**PMID:** 42843319 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Attributed author acknowledgment** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the authors acknowledge the advice, while grounded in thermal physiology, has not been tested in randomized trials of patient outcomes"
- **Abstract says:** "The advice, while novel, relates to best practice for multidisciplinary care before, during, and after heat extremes... this statement aims to bridge the evidence-to-practice gap"
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only — the abstract calls the advice "evidence informed" and "novel" but contains no acknowledgment about randomised trial testing.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as the editor's own caveat: "As an expert consensus statement, the advice has not itself been tested in randomised trials of patient outcomes.""

**Issue B. Claim about current cardiology care plans** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "most cardiology care plans still say nothing about heat"
- **Abstract says:** "There is an urgent need to provide scientific evidence to advise patients and healthcare professionals to manage cardiovascular health under these circumstances... aims to bridge the evidence-to-practice gap"
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only — the abstract describes an evidence-to-practice gap but gives no data on how many care plans address heat.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "heat planning is rarely part of routine cardiology care, a gap the statement is designed to close.""

### Study 21: Cardiology's new message: treating an older heart means assessing the brain and the body's reserves too
**PMID:** 42663983 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Rationale not in the source record** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The statement reflects a growing recognition that standard cardiology decision-making — built largely on evidence from younger, healthier trial populations — can misfire in older patients whose cognitive status and physical reserve shape both treatment tolerance and treatment goals."
- **Abstract says:** "Plain Language Summary: This Medical News article discusses a new scientific statement from the American College of Cardiology about caring for older adults with cardiovascular disease."
- **Problem:** The indexed record consists only of a title and a one-sentence plain-language summary; the stated rationale about trial populations and treatment tolerance cannot be confirmed. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the explanatory framing explicitly to the ACC statement or the news article text, e.g. "according to the article, the statement responds to concerns that...", or trim to what the record supports."

**Issue B. Specific expectation set for readers** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "You can ask directly whether your frailty level and thinking skills have been considered in deciding on a procedure, a new medication, or an intensive regimen"
- **Abstract says:** "The record describes only that the article discusses a new ACC scientific statement about caring for older adults with cardiovascular disease."
- **Problem:** The specific content of the recommendations (procedures, medications, intensive regimens) is not documented in the available record, so the advice is extrapolated rather than sourced.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to a general prompt to ask whether cognition and frailty were part of the assessment, which is what the title supports."

### Study 22: Muscle signals during a 10-meter walk flag frailty in older heart patients
**PMID:** 42830734 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Action step drawn from a baseline characteristic** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A medication review may be one of the more actionable levers available for frail older heart patients, independent of any new technology."
- **Abstract says:** "Frail participants were older (76.04 ± 7.57 vs. 68.55 ± 6.09 years), more frequently exposed to polypharmacy (93.33% vs 70.67%)... [polypharmacy was not among the factors independently associated with frailty in multivariable analysis]"
- **Problem:** Polypharmacy appears only as an unadjusted group difference in a case-control sample and was not retained in the multivariable model; recommending medication review as a "lever" implies a modifiable relationship the design cannot support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that frail patients were more often on multiple medications and that this was a descriptive difference, without implying medication review would alter frailty."
