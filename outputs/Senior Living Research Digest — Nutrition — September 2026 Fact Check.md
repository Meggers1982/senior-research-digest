### Study 1: Hearing loss sharply raises the death risk that comes with multiple health conditions
**PMID:** 42728658 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies causation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Hearing loss sharply raises the death risk that comes with multiple health conditions"
- **Abstract says:** "audiometric hearing loss modified the association between comorbidity and mortality. Additional research is needed to investigate whether treating age-related hearing loss could improve survival"
- **Problem:** "Raises" reads as causal for what the authors describe as effect modification in an observational cohort; the body text and caveats are appropriately hedged, but the headline is not.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like "Hearing loss marks a sharply higher death risk in older adults with multiple conditions.""

**Issue B. Borderline precision of the hearing-loss estimate** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Hearing loss alone was linked to about a 40% higher death risk"
- **Abstract says:** "Risk of mortality increased independently with hearing loss (aHR = 1.4 95% CI = 1.0-2.0"
- **Problem:** The confidence interval touches 1.0, so this association is at the edge of statistical significance; the caveats flag imprecision only for the comorbidity estimate.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "(a borderline result, 95% CI 1.0-2.0)" or note the imprecision alongside the comorbidity caveat."

**Issue C. Speculative mechanism stated as likely** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "likely because so much of chronic disease care depends on hearing and understanding instructions, medication changes, and follow-up plans"
- **Abstract says:** "Hearing loss impacts health care communication, utilization, and outcomes may influence this association"
- **Problem:** The abstract offers communication as a hypothesis, not a tested or supported explanation; "likely because" presents it as established.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "one proposed explanation is that chronic disease care depends heavily on hearing and understanding instructions.""

### Study 3: Insulin resistance tracks with enlarged prostate in older men across two countries
**PMID:** 42640724 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mediation finding generalized to both cohorts** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Monocyte counts, a measure of inflammation, partially explained the relationship."
- **Abstract says:** "The mediation effect model in the NHANES population showed that the mediating effect of monocyte was significant at -15.14% (p < 0.0001)."
- **Problem:** The mediation analysis is reported only for the US (NHANES) sample, not for CHARLS, but the digest presents it as a general finding across both populations.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "in the US sample" — e.g., "In the US cohort, monocyte counts accounted for about 15% of the relationship.""

**Issue B. Caveat not verifiable from abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "BPH definitions differ between the two national surveys."
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract does not describe how BPH was ascertained in CHARLS or NHANES."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only; the claim may be correct but cannot be checked against the published abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Either cite the full text for this point or soften to "BPH ascertainment in large surveys relies on indirect measures.""

### Study 8: Higher Vitamin D Levels Linked to Longer, Less Broken Sleep in Older Adults
**PMID:** 42696426 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Population narrowed in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Higher Vitamin D Levels Linked to Longer, Less Broken Sleep in Older Adults"
- **Abstract says:** "We studied 460 participants aged 40 and older [70.9±12.0(sd)years...] in the Baltimore Longitudinal Study of Aging... Higher vitamin D levels are associated with longer and less disturbed sleep in middle-aged and older adults."
- **Problem:** The cohort was middle-aged and older adults (40+); the headline implies an older-adults-only sample, though the body text correctly states "aged 40 and older."
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "in middle-aged and older adults" in the headline."

**Issue B. Regression coefficient read as a flat difference** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Higher vitamin D was associated with about 24 more minutes of total sleep per night"
- **Abstract says:** "Higher vitamin D concentration was associated with longer total sleep time (B = 23.63 minutes, 95% CI: 9.62, 37.64)"
- **Problem:** The 23.63 minutes is a regression coefficient tied to a unit increase in vitamin D, not a difference between "high" and "normal" people; the phrasing invites readers to expect 24 extra minutes from any increase.
- **Suggested fix:** "Phrase as "each step up in vitamin D level corresponded to roughly 24 more minutes of sleep in the statistical model.""

### Study 9: A 12-Week Group Exercise Class Improved Both Strength and Appetite in Older Adults With Sarcopenia
**PMID:** 42716495 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Within-group gains presented as superiority over controls** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "The exercise group improved significantly in ultrasound-measured muscle thickness, grip strength, balance, and walking-based physical performance. Notably, they also scored significantly better on the Mini Nutritional Assessment than controls"
- **Abstract says:** "When the exercise and control groups were compared, the exercise group demonstrated significantly greater improvements in SPPB score, Berg Balance Scale score, and MNA score (p < .05)."
- **Problem:** Muscle thickness and grip strength improved only within the exercise group pre-to-post; they were not significantly better than controls. Placing them in the same sentence as the MNA between-group result implies all four outcomes beat usual care.
- **Suggested fix:** "Distinguish the two: "The exercise group improved from baseline on muscle thickness, grip strength, balance and walking performance; compared with controls, the significant advantages were in physical performance (SPPB), balance, and nutritional status (MNA).""

**Issue B. Control group described as unchanged on every measure** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "who showed no significant change on any measure"
- **Abstract says:** "In the control group, muscle thickness, Timed Up and Go, SPPB, Berg Balance Scale, and MNA scores showed no significant changes postintervention (p > .05)."
- **Problem:** The abstract's list of unchanged control outcomes omits handgrip strength, so "any measure" goes beyond what is reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "who showed no significant change on the outcomes reported.""

**Issue C. Appetite/eating claim from a screening score** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "participants in this trial also ate and scored better nutritionally"
- **Abstract says:** "nutritional status was assessed with the Mini Nutritional Assessment (MNA) test"
- **Problem:** No dietary intake or appetite measure was used; only the MNA screening score improved. The body text contradicts the digest's own caveat about the MNA being a screening questionnaire. The headline's "Appetite" has the same problem.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "participants also scored better on a nutritional screening tool," and drop "Appetite" from the headline in favor of "nutritional status.""

### Study 12: Two Big National Studies Link Smoking to Sharper Cognitive Decline — With Inflammation as a Partial Culprit
**PMID:** 42709495 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies measured decline and causation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Two Big National Studies Link Smoking to Sharper Cognitive Decline — With Inflammation as a Partial Culprit"
- **Abstract says:** "Higher cotinine was associated with lower cognitive scores in NHANES (β = -0.219, p < 0.001). In CHARLS, current smokers exhibited lower cognitive scores than never-smokers... Mediation analysis revealed that neutrophil and leukocyte partially mediated 7.39% and 9.75% of the smoke-cognitive decline association."
- **Problem:** What was measured was lower cognitive test scores at a single assessment, not a faster rate of decline over time, and 'culprit' implies a causal mechanism the design cannot establish (the digest's own caveat calls the analyses cross-sectional).
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like 'Link Smoking to Lower Cognitive Scores — With Inflammation as a Possible Partial Pathway'."

**Issue B. Secondhand-smoke claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and note that secondhand exposure showed up in the objective blood measures used here"
- **Abstract says:** "Serum cotinine was mainly used to assess smoke exposure in NHANES, while self-reported smoking status was used in CHARLS."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports cotinine as a general measure of smoke exposure; it does not report any separate finding on secondhand (passive) exposure. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say that cotinine captures tobacco smoke exposure generally, without asserting a specific secondhand-exposure finding."

### Study 13: Older Women Found a Talking Smart Speaker a Welcome Coach for Bone Health
**PMID:** 42442524 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Implementation outcomes slightly mischaracterized** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Support was weaker around cost, fidelity, and long-term sustainability."
- **Abstract says:** "Evidence weighting indicated strong support for the intervention's appropriateness and acceptability, moderate support for its adoption, fidelity, feasibility and sustainability, and limited support for costs."
- **Problem:** Fidelity and sustainability had 'moderate' support, and the authors' conclusion describes the platform as 'feasible, acceptable and sustainable'; grouping them with cost (the only 'limited' category) understates them.
- **Suggested fix:** "Distinguish the tiers: strong for acceptability/appropriateness, moderate for adoption, fidelity, feasibility and sustainability, and limited only for cost."

**Issue B. Participant age not reported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Older Women Found a Talking Smart Speaker a Welcome Coach for Bone Health"
- **Abstract says:** "a qualitative analysis of interviews with postmenopausal women from the intervention arm (DVA group) of the RCT"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes participants only as postmenopausal women; no ages are given, so calling them 'older women' is an inference. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'postmenopausal women' in the headline and body, or note that ages were not reported."

### Study 14: 'Minimal Comfort Feeding' for Advanced Dementia: Hospice Professionals Say They're On Board
**PMID:** 42331265 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Survey timing and denominator** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Of 51 attendees, 45 took part in live polling and 22 completed a follow-up survey (mean age 50, 82% women)."
- **Abstract says:** "During a presentation on MCF's definition and ethical basis, attendees engaged in audience response polling and an optional electronic survey... Forty-five attendees (88%) participated in audience polling, and 22 (43%) participated in both polling and the survey... Thirty-five of 42 respondents (83%) agreed or strongly agreed..."
- **Problem:** The electronic survey was offered during the same session, not as a later follow-up, and the reported percentages use a denominator of 42 respondents rather than the 22 the digest cites, which a reader could misread.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say '22 also completed an optional electronic survey during the session' and note that the agreement percentages are based on 42 respondents."

**Issue B. 'Advocating' presentation is an interpretation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "recruited during a presentation advocating the approach"
- **Abstract says:** "During a presentation on MCF's definition and ethical basis, attendees engaged in audience response polling and an optional electronic survey."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes the presentation as covering MCF's definition and ethical basis; characterizing it as advocacy goes beyond what is stated (though it errs on the conservative side).
- **Suggested fix:** "Describe it as 'a presentation on MCF's definition and ethical basis,' which still supports the selection-bias point."

### Study 15: Hidden Potassium: More Than a Quarter of Packaged Foods Carry Potassium Additives That Don't Show Up on the Nutrition Panel
**PMID:** 42692364 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Ultra-processed foods generalized to 'packaged foods'** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "More Than a Quarter of Packaged Foods Carry Potassium Additives That Don't Show Up on the Nutrition Panel"
- **Abstract says:** "After analysing 5884 UPFs, 27.8% of the products contained at least one potassium-based additive... UPFs were identified according to the NOVA classification system."
- **Problem:** The 27.8% figure applies only to ultra-processed products, a subset of packaged foods; the headline implies all packaged goods.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'More Than a Quarter of Ultra-Processed Products...' (the body text already gets this right)."

**Issue B. Absorption claim not from this study** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "but additive potassium is absorbed far more completely than the potassium in whole foods, and it is often invisible on the nutrition facts panel"
- **Abstract says:** "Potassium additives are widespread in UPFs and can be a hidden source of potassium... These findings underline the importance of dietary guidance for individuals with CKD at risk of hyperkalaemia."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes additives as a 'hidden source' but reports nothing about bioavailability or absorption rates. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the absorption point to background literature rather than to this study, or drop the 'far more completely' comparison."

### Study 16: Anticholinergic Drugs After Stroke: Modest Link to Nutrition Risk, and a Clearer Signal for Psychiatric Medications and Thinking
**PMID:** 42728245 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Cognition omitted from the list of null results for total burden** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Overall drug burden was not linked to discharge scores for daily-living independence, swallowing ability, or grip strength."
- **Abstract says:** "Total JARS was not associated with discharge FIM-motor score (B = 0.032, 95% CI -0.799 to 0.862), FIM-cognitive score, FILS, or handgrip strength."
- **Problem:** Total anticholinergic burden was also unrelated to cognitive scores; leaving cognition off the null list, then reporting a psychotropic-specific cognitive association, could lead readers to think overall burden tracked with thinking.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add cognition to the list of outcomes total burden was not linked to, before contrasting it with the psychotropic-specific finding."

**Issue B. 'Appetite' overreads the nutrition outcome** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "to ask whether any drug on the list could be dulling thinking or appetite during recovery"
- **Abstract says:** "Secondary outcomes were FIM-cognitive, Food Intake Level Scale, Geriatric Nutritional Risk Index (GNRI), and handgrip strength at discharge... higher total JARS was negatively associated with GNRI at discharge"
- **Problem:** The nutrition signal was the Geriatric Nutritional Risk Index (a composite based on weight and albumin), not appetite or food intake — and the Food Intake Level Scale showed no association.
- **Suggested fix:** "Refer to 'nutritional status' rather than appetite."

### Study 20: Diabetes doubles the odds of weak grip strength in patients awaiting a liver transplant
**PMID:** 42692360 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline understates the effect size** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Diabetes doubles the odds of weak grip strength in patients awaiting a liver transplant"
- **Abstract says:** "Regression analysis identified DM as a predictor of reduced handgrip strength (OR 2.72, 95% CI 1.15-6.43; p = 0.022), independently from age."
- **Problem:** An odds ratio of 2.72 is closer to a near-tripling than a doubling, and the wide confidence interval (1.15-6.43) means the estimate is imprecise — worth signalling rather than fixing a single multiplier.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'nearly triples the odds' or 'roughly two-to-three-fold higher odds,' and mention the wide confidence interval in the caveats."

### Study 21: Incontinence plus depression together mark a higher-risk group of older adults, two national studies find
**PMID:** 42706488 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Additive vs. multiplicative interaction** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The two conditions did not appear to multiply each other's effects"
- **Abstract says:** "Fixed-horizon risk-ratio analyses provided no statistical evidence of additive interaction. … Additive interaction measures were estimated from fixed-horizon risk ratios, not hazard ratios."
- **Problem:** The authors tested and found no additive interaction; the digest describes it as no multiplicative ("multiply each other's") interaction, which is a different statistical question the abstract does not report.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "There was no statistical evidence that the two conditions added to each other's risk beyond their separate contributions.""

**Issue B. Follow-up period attribution** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "tracking deaths over roughly seven years"
- **Abstract says:** "NHANES used survey-weighted Cox models, whereas CHARLS used ordinary unweighted Cox models in a unified fixed 7-year cohort."
- **Problem:** The 7-year fixed horizon is specified for the CHARLS analysis; the abstract does not state a mean follow-up for the 2005-2010 NHANES mortality-linked sample, so applying "roughly seven years" to both cohorts is unverifiable from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "tracking deaths within a fixed seven-year window in the Chinese cohort and through linked national death records in the US cohort.""

### Study 23: Traditional Navajo Meals Cut Hospital Visits for Heart Failure Patients in Half-Sized Trial
**PMID:** 42507456 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Omitted eligibility criterion (high-risk enrichment)** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers randomized 206 adults with heart failure (mean age 65.8) receiving care at two Indian Health Service sites in the rural Navajo Nation"
- **Abstract says:** "Eligible patients were adults with heart failure who were receiving care at the study sites and had a hospitalization or emergency department visit during the last 12 months."
- **Problem:** Participants were an enriched, already high-utilization group, which shapes the absolute event rates and how much the results transfer to stable heart failure patients; the digest and caveats don't mention it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "…all of whom had been hospitalized or seen in the emergency department in the previous 12 months" to the study description or caveats."

**Issue B. Ambiguous headline phrasing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Traditional Navajo Meals Cut Hospital Visits for Heart Failure Patients in Half-Sized Trial"
- **Abstract says:** "The primary outcome was significantly less frequent in the intervention arm (43 [40.6%] vs 57 [57.0%]; relative risk, 0.72; 95% CI, 0.54-0.96; P = .02)"
- **Problem:** The phrase "Cut Hospital Visits … in Half" can be read as a 50% reduction in the primary endpoint, which fell by about 28% in relative terms (hospitalizations alone were roughly halved).
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite to avoid the split phrase, e.g., "Traditional Navajo Meals Reduced Hospital and ER Visits in a Heart Failure Trial.""

### Study 24: For Frailty, Exercise Works Better When It's Paired With Something Else
**PMID:** 42547949 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unverifiable claim about trial populations** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "most included trials were conducted in Chinese and other populations that may not reflect all settings"
- **Abstract says:** "A systematic electronic literature search was performed in the PubMed, EMBASE, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, CINAHL, Chinese National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), Wanfang, VIP and Sinomed databases up to July 1, 2025."
- **Problem:** The abstract lists Chinese-language databases among the sources but never reports the countries or proportion of included trials, so "most" cannot be confirmed. Unverifiable from abstract only.

### Study 25: Hospital Nurses Rate Their Own Nutrition Knowledge Lowest in Caring for Older Patients
**PMID:** 42705819 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Self-rated vs. measured knowledge** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this survey suggests it is precisely the area frontline staff feel least equipped to handle"
- **Abstract says:** "General knowledge about older adults was moderate (mean 6.88, SD 1.33 out of 10). Highest knowledge areas included falls, pain, and incontinence; lowest were nutrition, ADL assessments, and skin issues."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports knowledge scores without specifying whether the GIAP items tested knowledge or captured perceived confidence; the headline and rationale assume the latter. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use neutral wording such as "nutrition scored lowest among the geriatric knowledge domains assessed" and note that the abstract does not specify whether items were tested or self-rated."

### Study 26: Standardized ICU Feeding Protocols Get Nutrition in Faster — But Don't Change Survival
**PMID:** 42674351 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Certainty and risk-of-bias characterization** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "trials varied in protocol design and risk of bias; certainty of evidence was limited by the authors' own GRADE assessment"
- **Abstract says:** "We used the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 and the GRADE approach to assess the quality and certainty of the evidence. … The protocols likely resulted in little to no difference in adverse events"
- **Problem:** The abstract states that RoB 2 and GRADE were applied but does not report the resulting certainty ratings or describe variation in protocol design and bias across trials. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "the review applied Cochrane risk-of-bias and GRADE assessments; specific certainty ratings are not given in the abstract.""
