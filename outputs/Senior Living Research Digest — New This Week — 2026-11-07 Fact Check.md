### Study 2: Standard sleep apnea questionnaires largely stop working after age 65, hospital study finds
**PMID:** 42993419 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Age vs questionnaire comparison overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Chronological age by itself discriminated better than the questionnaires did, while BMI, neck circumference and symptom questions performed poorly."
- **Abstract says:** "In younger patients, questionnaire AUCs ranged 0.61-0.78. In the older subgroup, AUCs were 0.12-0.36 lower; BOAH had the highest AUC (0.59)... Chronological age yielded an AUC of 0.70 (95% CI, 0.61-0.80), whereas BMI, neck circumference, and symptom-based items showed poor discrimination."
- **Problem:** Age's AUC of 0.70 was calculated in the whole cohort, and in younger patients several questionnaires reached AUCs up to 0.78 — age only clearly beat the questionnaires in the 65-plus subgroup.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a qualifier: 'In the older group, chronological age alone (AUC 0.70, measured across the whole cohort) discriminated better than any questionnaire did.'"

### Study 3: One in three advanced Parkinson's patients stops infusion therapy — and living in a care facility was the strongest predictor
**PMID:** 42993214 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Treatments vs patients** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "One in three advanced Parkinson's patients stops infusion therapy"
- **Abstract says:** "Among 565 patients receiving 591 DATs, 170 (28.8%) treatments were discontinued... Among infusion therapies, discontinuation was 33.6%"
- **Problem:** The 33.6% figure is the share of infusion treatments discontinued, not the share of patients (some patients received more than one device-aided therapy).
- **Suggested fix:** "'One in three infusion therapies for advanced Parkinson's is discontinued'"

**Issue B. 'Strongest predictor' not stated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and living in a care facility was the strongest predictor"
- **Abstract says:** "Discontinuation was associated with institutional caregiving (HR 5.162, 95% CI 1.441-18.490), higher ON-state Hoehn and Yahr stage (HR 1.814...), and longer daily OFF time (HR 1.093...)"
- **Problem:** The abstract lists associated factors without ranking them; institutional caregiving has the largest point estimate but a very wide confidence interval, so calling it 'the strongest predictor' goes slightly beyond what is reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "'and living in a care facility carried the largest associated risk' or 'was among the factors most strongly associated with stopping'"

### Study 4: 50,000 dogs are now part of the largest study ever of how companions age — and owners are the researchers
**PMID:** 42980899 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Invitation to enroll not supported by abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "If you share your home with a dog, you can enroll it and become part of the research yourself: the project is an open cohort that recruits ordinary owners, not laboratory animals, and participation is survey-based from home."
- **Abstract says:** "The Dog Aging Project (DAP)'s prospective, longitudinal open cohort study was begun in 2019... As of December 31, 2024, the study has enrolled 50,188 companion dogs... This manuscript describes the recruitment and retention of this study population"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes an open cohort design and owner-reported surveys but does not state that new enrollment is currently open to readers. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to 'the project is designed as an open cohort of owner-volunteers rather than laboratory animals' and point readers to the project website to check whether enrollment is open."

**Issue B. 'All data' openly available** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with all data made openly available to outside scientists"
- **Abstract says:** "the means by which DAP Open Data are shared with the research community... Interested researchers and the general public can apply for data access"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes a defined 'Open Data' release available on application, not blanket release of all study data.
- **Suggested fix:** "'with an Open Data resource that outside researchers can apply to access'"

**Issue C. 'Largest study ever' claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "50,000 dogs are now part of the largest study ever of how companions age"
- **Abstract says:** "the study has enrolled 50,188 companion dogs across all 50 US states and the District of Columbia"
- **Problem:** The abstract never claims this is the largest such study. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "'50,000 dogs are now part of a major long-term study of how companion animals age'"

### Study 5: AI promises to predict who with mild cognitive impairment will get Alzheimer's — a review of 60 studies says it isn't ready

- **Abstract says:** "Deep learning models hold considerable potential for predicting MCI-to-AD conversion, yet substantial barriers remain to their translation into routine clinical practice."

### Study 6: Nearly 1 in 4 older adults with hard-to-treat depression also has dementia — and heart health may be the link
**PMID:** 42995052 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Definition of difficult-to-treat depression simplified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "whose first recorded depressive episode (2008-2023) did not respond to at least two antidepressants"
- **Abstract says:** "LL-DTD was defined as ≥ 2 antidepressants prescribed or a natural language processing algorithm indicating treatment resistance."
- **Problem:** The study's definition was two or more antidepressants prescribed (or an NLP flag), which is a proxy for — not confirmation of — non-response.
- **Suggested fix:** "'who were prescribed at least two antidepressants or otherwise flagged as treatment-resistant in their records'"

**Issue B. Headline implies a mechanism** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and heart health may be the link"
- **Abstract says:** "Somatic multimorbidity, cardiovascular and cerebrovascular conditions were also associated with higher odds... In our analysis, somatic multimorbidity, cardiovascular conditions and anxiety were associated with comorbid dementia in LL-DTD"
- **Problem:** A cross-sectional record study can show co-occurrence only; calling cardiovascular disease 'the link' suggests a causal pathway and singles out one of several associated factors.
- **Suggested fix:** "'— and physical illness, including heart and vascular disease, often travels with it'"

### Study 7: Back surgery works just as well after 70 — but nerve recovery is slower and less complete
**PMID:** 42993959 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Prevalence of six-week weakness not reported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "persistent foot or leg weakness at six weeks is common and does not mean the operation failed"
- **Abstract says:** "Younger patients (age < 70 years) demonstrated significantly faster and more complete recovery of both motor and sensory deficits at all follow-up intervals starting from 6 weeks."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a between-group difference in recovery from 6 weeks onward but gives no figures showing residual weakness is 'common'. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "'incomplete recovery of strength at six weeks was more frequent in the older group and does not by itself mean the operation failed'"

### Study 8: Scans of 1,000 Alzheimer's patients find hidden signs of a treatable brain condition
**PMID:** 42988576 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Cohort misdescribed in headline** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Scans of 1,000 Alzheimer's patients find hidden signs of a treatable brain condition"
- **Abstract says:** "We included N=30 amyloid-negative reference iNPH patients and N=1003 ADNI participants with structural T1-weighted MRI and CSF biomarkers... iNPH-like morphometry increased with cognitive impairment, with mean differences of 0.03 between cognitively normal and mild cognitive impairment (p=0.06)"
- **Problem:** The 1,003 ADNI participants span cognitively normal, MCI and dementia and include biologically non-Alzheimer's cases — they are not all 'Alzheimer's patients'. The study also quantified iNPH-like morphometric patterns rather than identifying confirmed cases of a treatable condition.
- **Suggested fix:** "'Scans of 1,000 people in a major Alzheimer's study show brain patterns resembling a potentially treatable condition'"

**Issue B. Non-significant comparison not flagged** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Brain shapes resembling iNPH — a fluid-buildup condition that can be treated with a shunt — became more common as cognitive impairment worsened"
- **Abstract says:** "mean differences of 0.03 between cognitively normal and mild cognitive impairment (p=0.06), 0.06 between mild cognitive impairment and dementia (p<0.001), and 0.09 between cognitively normal and dementia groups (p<0.001)"
- **Problem:** The cognitively normal versus MCI step did not reach statistical significance, and the differences are in similarity-score units rather than counts of people, so 'became more common' overstates a graded score shift.
- **Suggested fix:** "'iNPH-like similarity scores rose across cognitive stages, significantly so from MCI to dementia'"

**Issue C. Missing subgroup qualifier** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and were higher among people with biologically confirmed Alzheimer's than those without"
- **Abstract says:** "Among cognitively impaired participants, scores were higher in biologically-defined Alzheimer's disease patients than biologically-defined non-Alzheimer's disease patients (mean difference=0.08, p<0.001)."
- **Problem:** The comparison applies only to cognitively impaired participants, not to the whole cohort.
- **Suggested fix:** "'and, among cognitively impaired participants, were higher in those with biologically confirmed Alzheimer's'"

### Study 10: Your 'good' hand loses its edge with age — but the brain's movement planning stays intact
**PMID:** 42983147 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Extension to clinical rehabilitation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the finding suggests older clients may adapt to using the non-dominant limb better than expected, which could inform how tasks are retrained after stroke, fracture, or surgery on the dominant side"
- **Abstract says:** "Forty-one healthy young adults (24.3 ± 3 years) and forty-two healthy older adults (72.0 ± 6 years) performed single-degree-of-freedom upward and downward arm movements... These findings suggest that healthy aging is associated with a reduced lateralized expression of gravity-related motor optimization rather than with a global decline in motor planning."
- **Problem:** The study tested healthy volunteers on a single laboratory arm movement and reports no rehabilitation outcomes; the digest's own caveat says findings may not generalize to neurologically impaired older adults, which this angle partly contradicts.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a hedge: 'in healthy older adults; whether this carries over to rehabilitation after stroke or fracture was not tested.'"

### Study 11: Repairing a fractured spine may be linked to lower dementia risk later on
**PMID:** 42982275 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Landmark analysis purpose described imprecisely** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The association held in a landmark analysis designed to limit bias from early deaths and treatment timing."
- **Abstract says:** "A six-month landmark analysis was performed to limit immortal-time bias."
- **Problem:** The abstract names a specific methodological problem (immortal-time bias, i.e. the guaranteed event-free period before treatment is received); "bias from early deaths and treatment timing" is a loose rendering that could confuse readers about what was corrected. The digest also omits the landmark figures (6.2% vs 7.5%, OR 0.81).
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the result held in a six-month landmark analysis intended to limit immortal-time bias (6.2% vs 7.5%; OR 0.81)."

### Study 12: People who eat more magnesium report fewer depressive symptoms — and the link was strongest in those over 65
**PMID:** 42982236 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Subgroup comparison presented as a graded finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The association was strongest in participants aged 65 and older, where the highest-intake group had 45% lower odds."
- **Abstract says:** "In sensitivity analyses confined to those under 65 years of age, those aged 65 years and older, men, and women, the fully multivariate-adjusted ORs between extreme quartiles ... were 0.65 (0.53-0.80, < 0.0001), 0.55 (0.40-0.76, < 0.0001), 0.74 (0.56-0.98, 0.02), and 0.60 (0.48-0.75, < 0.0001)"
- **Problem:** The 65+ estimate (OR 0.55) is numerically the lowest, but the abstract reports these as sensitivity analyses with overlapping confidence intervals and no formal test of interaction, so describing the link as "strongest" in older adults overstates what was shown.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the estimate was similar across age groups (OR 0.65 under 65, 0.55 at 65+) and that no formal test of difference between age groups is reported."

**Issue B. Headline age cut-off** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the link was strongest in those over 65"
- **Abstract says:** "those aged 65 years and older"
- **Problem:** The subgroup is 65 and older, not over 65.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "those 65 and older.""

### Study 13: Feeling boxed in fuels loneliness — and loneliness erodes your sense of agency, a 8,400-person study finds
**PMID:** 42974910 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal language in headline** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Feeling boxed in fuels loneliness — and loneliness erodes your sense of agency, a 8,400-person study finds"
- **Abstract says:** "Higher perceived constraints were associated with higher subsequent loneliness, and higher loneliness was associated with higher subsequent perceived constraints. Higher loneliness was associated with lower subsequent personal mastery"
- **Problem:** "Fuels" and "erodes" assert causation from an observational cross-lagged panel analysis. The body text and caveats are appropriately hedged, so the headline overstates relative to the rest of the item.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to association language, e.g. "Feeling boxed in is linked to later loneliness — and loneliness to a later drop in sense of agency.""

**Issue B. Intervention inference in "Why it matters"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "removing real and perceived barriers may do more to break a loneliness cycle than pep talks about self-efficacy"
- **Abstract says:** "psychosocial care may need to address constraint-related appraisals, while the loneliness-to-mastery pathway highlights the relevance of supporting agency among older adults experiencing loneliness"
- **Problem:** The study compared no interventions; the abstract suggests addressing constraint appraisals but also supporting agency, so ranking barrier-removal above self-efficacy work goes slightly beyond the data.
- **Suggested fix:** "Frame as a hypothesis the study raises rather than a comparison of approaches, and note the abstract also flags supporting agency in lonely older adults."

### Study 14: Older Chinese adults don't automatically want to live with their children — circumstances, not tradition, drive the preference
**PMID:** 42974821 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unverified example of enabling factors** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "'Enabling' factors — resources and circumstances such as support availability — predicted coresidence preference more robustly than fixed personal traits"
- **Abstract says:** "Regressions showed enabling factors played a more robust role in shaping coresidence preference than time-invariant personal characteristics."
- **Problem:** The abstract never specifies which enabling factors were measured, so "support availability" is unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Either drop the example or mark it as not specified in the abstract."
