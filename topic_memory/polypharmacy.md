# Topic Memory: polypharmacy
_Last updated: 2026-11-03_

## Established findings
- Specific drug classes — anticholinergics, benzodiazepines/psycholeptics, antipsychotics, opioids, antihistamines, muscle relaxants, anticonvulsants, diuretics, NSAIDs — predict harm more reliably than raw pill counts (PMIDs 42584792, 42285234 Aug 2026; 42594508, 42503320 Oct 2026; 42931882, 42308749, 42296796 Nov 2026)
- Pharmacist-led medication review reduces inappropriate prescribing across hospital, pharmacy and oncology settings, with an economic case (~€181 net/patient) and no subgroup that clearly benefits more (PMIDs 42472621, 42570168, 42731364, 42467355; Aug–Oct 2026)
- Deprescribing is achievable but outcome evidence stays thin — reviews report prescribing metrics, not falls, readmissions, symptoms or quality of life (PMIDs 42607278, 42799555, 42313336, 42888381; Aug–Nov 2026)
- Medication burden rises steeply with frailty and over time, with CNS/psychotherapeutic drugs climbing fastest in the frailest (PMIDs 42669283, 42870717; Oct–Dec 2026)
- Raw medication count still works as a review trigger even where it predicts poorly: tied to inappropriate prescribing (+55%/drug), medication-related readmission (+7%/drug), ED admission and severe fear of falling (PMIDs 42934542, 42842153, 42701177, 42628444)
- Standard polypharmacy counts undercount reality — patches, creams, drops, OTC products and supplements sit outside the record (PMIDs 42536289, 42783349; Oct 2026)

## Emerging threads
- The five-drug threshold is losing explanatory power: no independent link to cognition after disease burden (42842789), negligible value in fall prediction (42296796), outperformed by anticholinergic/PIM measures (42931882) and by disease-matched intensity scores (42885111)
- End-of-life deprescribing shifts from "it never happens" to "it happens reflexively": near-total hospice discontinuation without symptom tracking (42897830) and abrupt stopping of drugs that need tapering (42731737)
- AI in geriatric care and medication review — AGS position statement, documented ageist bias and long-record failures, and patients/carers endorsing AI only as a supervised backstage tool (42478489, 42786110, 42969554; Oct–Dec 2026)
- Caregivers as the de facto medication monitors: information gaps outrank pill count as their burden (42897099), and resident medication circumstances — not caregiver demographics — predict openness to deprescribing (42850595)
- Nobody formally owns the medication list: assisted living accountability gap (42645013) now joined by workforce survey evidence that even pharmacists rate polypharmacy knowledge modestly and a third see it as outside their role (42969553)
