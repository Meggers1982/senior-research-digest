# Fact-Check Report

### Study 1: Plant-Based Ketogenic Diet Plus Fasting Shows Promise for Stabilizing Cognition in Early Alzheimer's
**PMID:** 42497811 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. RCI stability percentage interpretation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Ninety-three percent of those following the dietary intervention remained cognitively stable or improved."
- **Abstract says:** "RCI analysis indicated stability in 93% of intervention and 70% of control participants, with cognitive improvement in 7% and 3.3%, respectively."
- **Problem:** The digest combines "stable" and "improved" categories for the intervention group (93% + 7% = 100%), but the abstract indicates 93% were stable and 7% improved as separate categories. The 93% figure refers to stability alone, not "stable or improved."
- **Suggested fix:** "Ninety-three percent of those following the dietary intervention remained cognitively stable, with an additional 7% showing improvement."

### Study 9: Real-World "Precision Medicine" Approach Stabilized Cognition in 85% of Older Adults With Memory Concerns
**PMID:** 42393905 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Favorable trajectory breakdown** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "85% showed favorable cognitive trajectories—stable, improved, or only minor decline without diagnostic progression."
- **Abstract says:** "Favorable trajectories included 83.5% stable, improved, or minor decline without diagnostic progression and 1.7% slower-than-expected decline on benchmarked instruments"
- **Problem:** The digest rounds 85.2% to 85%, which is acceptable, but describes the categories slightly differently. The abstract specifies 83.5% for one category and 1.7% for another totaling 85.2%.
- **Suggested fix:** Minor issue; current phrasing is acceptable as a summary.

No significant issues found. Caveats appropriately flag the retrospective, nonrandomized design.

### Study 13: Being Active and Maintaining a Healthy Weight Together May Reduce Functional Difficulties in People With Memory Concerns
**PMID:** 42590846 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Risk estimate interpretation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "those who were inactive and had obesity were about 19% more likely to report difficulties with daily activities than those who were highly active and at a normal weight"
- **Abstract says:** "APRs ranged from 1.10, 95% CI: 1.10-1.11 (Nonactive, Normal Weight) to 1.19, 95% CI: 1.18-1.19 (Nonactive, Obesity)"
- **Problem:** The APR of 1.19 represents a 19% higher prevalence, which the digest correctly states. However, the comparison is against "Highly Active, Normal Weight" as reference, which the digest accurately conveys. No significant error.

No significant issues found. Cross-sectional design appropriately flagged.

### Study 20: Metabolomic "Clock" Predicts Mortality, Frailty, and Mild Cognitive Impairment
**PMID:** 42481860 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Sample size and age range** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "nearly 2,300 adults aged 20–89 from UK and Irish cohorts"
- **Abstract says:** "3,686 plasma samples in 2,295 participants, aged 20-89"
- **Problem:** The digest says "nearly 2,300" which is accurate (2,295 participants). Correct.

**Issue B. Risk estimates** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Each five-year acceleration beyond one's actual age was linked to 43% higher mortality risk, 27% higher risk of mild cognitive impairment, and 10% higher frailty risk."
- **Abstract says:** "Each standard deviation increase in metabolomic age acceleration (~ 5 years) was associated with 43% higher mortality risk, 27% higher risk of mild cognitive impairment, and 10% increased risk of a higher frailty score"
- **Problem:** The digest accurately captures this, though "10% higher frailty risk" slightly simplifies "10% increased risk of a higher frailty score."

No significant issues found.
