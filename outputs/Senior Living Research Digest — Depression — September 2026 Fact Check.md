### Study 5: Depression Doubled the Odds of Probable Dementia in a National Survey of Older Americans
**PMID:** 42731172 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Disparity extended to Black elders** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the findings argue that a one-size-fits-all cognitive screening or prevention program misses the mark for Hispanic and Black elders"
- **Abstract says:** "Hispanic adults aged 85 and older faced a 60.2% predicted probability of Prb-D, compared to 35.1% for Non-Hispanic (NH) White adults... Among hearing aid users, NH White and NH Black adults showed lower of Prb-D probability than non-users, whereas Hispanic users showed a higher probability."
- **Problem:** The reported disparities in the abstract are specific to Hispanic adults; the only NH Black result mentioned patterns like NH White adults. Naming Black elders as equally poorly served by standardized programs goes beyond the reported results.
- **Suggested fix:** "Limit the claim to Hispanic older adults, or say the study reported disparities "most clearly for Hispanic older adults.""

### Study 9: In the oldest old, loneliness drags down life satisfaction — and depression is part of the reason
**PMID:** 42678139 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for cross-sectional association** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "In the oldest old, loneliness drags down life satisfaction — and depression is part of the reason"
- **Abstract says:** "To examine the concurrent association between loneliness and life satisfaction across survey waves and assess whether depressive symptoms statistically account for this association... Loneliness is strongly associated with lower life satisfaction, with depressive symptoms partially and modestly accounting for this association."
- **Problem:** "Drags down" and "part of the reason" imply causation and mediation, while the analysis is a concurrent, repeated-measures association in which depressive symptoms only statistically account for part of the link.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Loneliness tracks with lower life satisfaction in the oldest old — and depressive symptoms explain part of the link.""

### Study 10: Dance came out on top when researchers compared exercise types for depression in people with mild cognitive impairment
**PMID:** 42709503 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Only modality" claim not stated in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Dance was the only modality to show a statistically significant improvement over control conditions... Other exercise types showed benefit trends but did not clearly separate from control."
- **Abstract says:** "Compared with the control group, dance exercise demonstrated a statistically significant improvement in depressive symptoms (SMD = -0.51, 95% credible intervals: -0.84 to -0.16), representing a moderate effect size, and showed a relatively high-ranking probability (SUCRA = 80.71%)."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports dance as significant but never states that it was the only significant modality or characterises the other eight as non-significant trends. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say dance was the modality the authors highlighted as showing a statistically significant benefit versus control, without asserting the others were all non-significant."

**Issue B. Comparative claim over solo exercise** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "points to a low-cost, social, non-drug option that may work better than exercise done alone"
- **Abstract says:** "Exercise interventions may contribute to the alleviation of depressive symptoms in patients with MCI, with dance exercise showing relatively greater therapeutic potential. However, these findings should be interpreted with caution in light of evidence inconsistency."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a ranking, not a significant head-to-head comparison between dance and solo exercise forms, so the implied superiority over "exercise done alone" is not established.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Dance ranked highest among the exercise types compared, though the network did not establish it as superior to other forms head to head.""

### Study 14: Nearly 1 in 5 people with Parkinson's report suicidal thoughts — and it's the non-movement symptoms that flag risk
**PMID:** 42727509 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Relative strength of predictors overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and — most strongly and consistently — for those with a heavier burden of non-motor symptoms and poorer quality of life"
- **Abstract says:** "female sex (OR 1.89...), non-iPD parkinsonism (OR 2.70...), living in rented accommodation (OR 4.48; 95%CI 2.24-8.94; p < 0.001), higher non-motor symptom (NMS) burden (OR per 1 unit change 1.15...) and poorer quality of life (OR per 1 unit change 1.04...)"
- **Problem:** The largest odds ratio in the abstract belongs to rented accommodation; NMS and quality-of-life ORs are per-unit-change and are not directly comparable, so ranking them as the strongest signals is not supported by the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "and for those with a heavier burden of non-motor symptoms and poorer quality of life," dropping the "most strongly and consistently" ranking, or note that rented accommodation carried the largest odds ratio."

### Study 22: Deep Brain Stimulation Improved Parkinson's Quality of Life — But Some Patients Felt Less Hopeful About the Future
**PMID:** 42731544 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Overstated predictor claim** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "and that no one could predict in advance who those patients would be"
- **Abstract says:** "a secondary exploratory aim was to assess the role of anxiety and depression as predictors of psychological and quality-of-life outcomes after STN-DBS... anxiety and depression did not emerge as significant predictors"
- **Problem:** The study tested only pre-surgery anxiety and depression, and did so as a secondary, exploratory aim. Saying no one could predict who would fare worse implies a broad predictive analysis that was not performed.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to "and that pre-surgery anxiety and depression — the only predictors tested, in an exploratory secondary analysis — did not identify who those patients would be.""

**Issue B. Single-center claim not supported by abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Small sample (N=64), single center, no control group (pre-post design), Italian specialty referral population may not generalize"
- **Abstract says:** "Author affiliations centre on Fondazione Policlinico Universitario Agostino Gemelli IRCCS, Rome, with a large collaborator list credited as the "Lazio DBS Study Group""
- **Problem:** The abstract does not state that recruitment was from a single center; the named study group suggests possible multi-site involvement. The claim cannot be verified from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "single center" or soften to "conducted within an Italian DBS study group led from a single Rome hospital.""
