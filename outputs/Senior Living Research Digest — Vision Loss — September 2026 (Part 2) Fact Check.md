### Study 2: Low-Contrast Obstacles Make Older Walkers Cut It Close — Even Those With Normal Vision
**PMID:** 42501620 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Recommendation goes beyond measured outcome** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is evidence that contrast marking on obstacles and level changes should be a standard part of fall-risk environmental audits"
- **Abstract says:** "Adults in both vision groups adopted riskier foot clearance when stepping over low-contrast obstacles as compared to high-contrast... Optimizing obstacle appearance may reduce risk of falls."
- **Problem:** The study measured foot clearance in a lab, not falls; the authors describe a possible benefit ('may reduce risk of falls'), while the digest turns it into a policy standard for audits.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to 'supports considering contrast marking of obstacles and level changes in fall-risk environmental reviews, though falls themselves were not measured.'"

### Study 3: Audiobooks Aren't an Automatic Replacement for Reading After Vision Loss — Listeners Have to Learn the Skill
**PMID:** 42500929 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Sample description not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Qualitative interview study with a small purposive sample"
- **Abstract says:** "In-depth qualitative interviews were conducted with adults aged 60 years and older living with vision loss."
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no sample size or sampling strategy, so 'small purposive sample' is unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'Qualitative interview study; sample size and recruitment approach are not reported in the abstract.'"

### Study 4: Half of Patients With a Common Cause of Vision Loss Relapse Within Two Years After Injections Are Paused
**PMID:** 42752539 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. 'Most people stay stable' conflicts with the 50% recurrence finding** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "most people stay stable, but a meaningful minority lose significant vision"
- **Abstract says:** "Of 26 eyes with regression, 13 (50%; 95% CI, 30%-70%) recurred within 2 years... Fluid-free probability after regression was ... 53% (95% CI, 33%-73%) at week 84."
- **Problem:** Exactly half the eyes recurred, so 'most people stay stable' misrepresents the headline finding (it is only true if restricted to major vision loss, which the sentence does not make clear) and contradicts the digest's own headline.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as 'about half remain fluid-free, while half recur — and among recurrences roughly 1 in 5 lost 10 or more letters.'"

### Study 5: Dementia Risk Looks Different in American Indian Communities — Depression Is the Dominant Factor
**PMID:** 42635108 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

**Issue A. 'Mid-life' attached to the depression PAF** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Mid-life depression carried by far the largest population attributable fraction at 60%"
- **Abstract says:** "Mid-life depression, head injury, smoking, hypertension, obesity, and diabetes were more common... Significant risk factors for dementia with PAF percentage were as follows: education (19%), hypertension (11%), isolation (13%), and depression (60%)."
- **Problem:** The abstract labels depression as 'mid-life' only in the prevalence comparison; the 60% PAF is reported for 'depression' without that qualifier, so the timing attached to the PAF is not verifiable from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write 'depression carried by far the largest population attributable fraction at 60%' and note separately that mid-life depression was more prevalent in this cohort."

### Study 10: The era of monthly eye injections may be ending: a review of what's coming next for macular degeneration and diabetic eye disease
**PMID:** 41076606 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline overstates imminence of change** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The era of monthly eye injections may be ending"
- **Abstract says:** "describes various novel therapies, some of which could potentially eliminate the need for intravitreal injections of anti-VEGF in the future... We conclude that gene therapy products, especially TKIs, have the potential to become inhibitors of ocular neovascularization in the upcoming years."
- **Problem:** The review describes potential future alternatives, most still experimental; declaring an era 'may be ending' reads as near-term hype for a narrative review.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as 'Researchers review experimental alternatives that could eventually reduce the need for repeated eye injections.'"

**Issue B. Unsupported claim in patient advice** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Keep your appointments in the meantime: skipping injections remains the biggest avoidable cause of vision loss in these conditions."
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract discusses efficacy of anti-VEGF treatment and injection-related adverse events, but makes no statement about missed injections being a leading avoidable cause of vision loss."
- **Problem:** A specific quantitative-sounding claim is presented to older readers as established fact; it is unverifiable from the abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the superlative and say 'staying on your current treatment schedule matters while these alternatives are still being studied.'"

**Issue C. 'Two leading causes' vs 'major causes'** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the two leading causes of severe vision loss in aging populations"
- **Abstract says:** "Neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD) and proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR) are major causes of severe vision loss."
- **Problem:** The abstract says 'major causes,' not that they are the top two causes.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'two major causes of severe vision loss in aging populations.'"

### Study 11: An off-label chemotherapy drug is shrinking the abnormal corneal blood vessels that cloud vision
**PMID:** 42215273 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Off-label status not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "An off-label chemotherapy drug is shrinking the abnormal corneal blood vessels that cloud vision"
- **Abstract says:** "Mitomycin C (MMC) intravascular chemoembolisation (MICE) has shown early promise but real-world data remain limited. This study evaluates the efficacy and safety of MICE and MMC intrastromal injection (MIST)."
- **Problem:** The abstract never states that mitomycin C use for corneal neovascularisation is off-label; this is background knowledge the abstract cannot confirm. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Either drop "off-label" from the headline or attribute it to general prescribing practice rather than to this study."

**Issue B. Etiologies of corneal neovascularisation not reported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "For people whose corneas have been damaged by infection, contact lens wear, injury, or prior transplant rejection"
- **Abstract says:** "Corneal neovascularisation (NV) is a refractory condition associated with lipid deposition, scarring and vision loss."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not describe the underlying causes or the case mix of the treated patients, so the specific list of causes cannot be checked.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "people with corneal neovascularisation, which can follow infection, injury or transplant rejection" and note these causes are general background, not study data."

**Issue C. Recommendation drawn from uncontrolled retrospective series** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "it's worth asking a corneal specialist whether mitomycin C injection is an option for you, particularly if a corneal transplant is being considered"
- **Abstract says:** "Retrospective case series ... 39 procedures were performed ... may have an important role in stabilising high-risk corneas prior to keratoplasty."
- **Problem:** A retrospective, single-centre series with no control group supports hypothesis-generating discussion rather than a patient-directed treatment inquiry; the caveats note this but the angle does not hedge.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a clause such as "bearing in mind this is early, uncontrolled evidence from one specialist centre.""

### Study 14: Women Carry a Heavier Burden of Blindness Worldwide — and the Gap Isn't Closing
**PMID:** 42521465 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Access-gap explanation not tested in the study** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a persistent female excess suggests many older women are not reaching surgery they would benefit from — a fixable access problem, not an inevitability of biology"
- **Abstract says:** "Higher GII and lower GDI were significantly associated with larger sex disparities ... The strong association with GDI and GII indicates these disparities are modifiable and linked to structural factors."
- **Problem:** The analysis uses modelled, country-level YLD rates; it reports an association with gender-inequality indices but contains no data on cataract surgical uptake by sex, so the surgery-access mechanism is inference rather than finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "the authors link the gap to structural gender inequality, which may include unequal access to cataract surgery, though surgical uptake was not measured.""

**Issue B. Individual-level advice from ecological data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the evidence suggests women are more likely to go without it — ask directly for a referral and a surgical assessment rather than waiting to be offered one"
- **Abstract says:** "In this repeated ecological analysis of Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2023 data, we analysed sex-disaggregated age-standardised years lived with disability (YLD) rates and prevalence for major blinding diseases."
- **Problem:** Country-level modelled estimates cannot speak to whether an individual woman is likely to miss out on surgery; the caveats note the ecological design but the angle states it as established.
- **Suggested fix:** "Frame as a population-level pattern: "globally, women shoulder more cataract-related disability, so it is worth asking directly about referral.""

### Study 15: Swollen Eyelids in Older Men With a Rare Inflammatory Disease Can Signal a Sight-Threatening Emergency
**PMID:** 41535098 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Permanent" not stated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "two patients (14%) suffered severe permanent vision loss in one eye"
- **Abstract says:** "Overall, visual acuity was normal and did not change. Severe vision loss occurred unilaterally in two patients (14%), due to orbital compartment syndrome and periorbital necrotising fasciitis."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports severe unilateral vision loss but does not say whether it was permanent.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "permanent" or write "severe vision loss in one eye (the abstract does not report whether it recovered).""

**Issue B. Country list inferred from affiliations** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Ophthalmologists across Australia, New Zealand, the UK, US, Denmark and Spain pooled 14 cases"
- **Abstract says:** "This is a case series of VEXAS patients submitted by ophthalmologists from relevant specialty organisations, including the Australian and New Zealand Society of Ophthalmic Plastic Surgeons, British Oculoplastic Surgical Society and the Orbital Society."
- **Problem:** The contributing countries are not given in the abstract text; the list appears drawn from author affiliations, which also include Saudi Arabia. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "ophthalmologists from specialist oculoplastic and orbital societies in Australasia, the UK and elsewhere.""

**Issue C. Epidemiology of VEXAS not from this study** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "VEXAS is increasingly diagnosed in men over 50 with unexplained inflammation and blood abnormalities"
- **Abstract says:** "Vacuoles, E1-ligase, X-linked Auto-inflammatory, Somatic (VEXAS) syndrome is a recently described multisystem inflammatory disorder. Ocular features are described, though not well known; we aimed to describe them."
- **Problem:** Statements about who is increasingly diagnosed and about haematological features are background not contained in this abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the background description to general literature rather than to this case series."

### Study 16: An Experimental Gel Injection Restored Vision in Eyes With Dangerously Low Pressure
**PMID:** 41521029 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Restored Vision" overstates modest gains** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "An Experimental Gel Injection Restored Vision in Eyes With Dangerously Low Pressure"
- **Abstract says:** "Best corrected visual acuity (BCVA) logarithm of the minimum angle of resolution (LogMAR) improved in 87.5% of eyes, with one eye remaining stable. The initial BCVA range was 0.3-1.7 LogMAR, and the final range was 0.1-1.6 LogMAR."
- **Problem:** Improvement was incremental (e.g. worst eye moved only from 1.7 to 1.6 LogMAR); "restored vision" suggests recovery of normal sight in an eight-eye uncontrolled pilot.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "improved vision in seven of eight eyes" in the headline."

**Issue B. Adverse-event tally assumes distinct eyes** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "adverse events occurred in four of the eight eyes"
- **Abstract says:** "Two adverse events occurred: uveitis flare-ups leading to loss of HPMC clarity in two eyes, managed with topical and intravitreal steroids, and two cases of temporary vision loss post-injection, resolved after paracentesis."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not say whether the two uveitis eyes and the two temporary vision-loss cases were four different eyes; the digest's total may double-count.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "uveitis in two eyes and temporary post-injection vision loss in two cases (overlap not specified).""

### Study 17: Diabetes-Related Vision Loss Is Rising Fastest in Wealthy Countries — and Type 2 Is Driving It
**PMID:** 42476825 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. SDI equated with income** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the steepest relative increase (45.8%) in the highest-income group of countries"
- **Abstract says:** "The highest relative increase was recorded in the high SDI group (45.8%)."
- **Problem:** The socio-demographic index combines income per capita with education and fertility; calling it "highest-income" is a simplification that slightly misnames the exposure variable.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "in the high socio-demographic index (SDI) group — broadly the wealthiest, most developed countries.""

**Issue B. Screening-gap explanation not supported by the analysis** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the trend is worsening in exactly the countries where eye care is most available — pointing to a gap between available screening and screening actually received"
- **Abstract says:** "Unfavourable period and cohort effects were observed for type 2 but not type 1-related blindness and vision loss risk ... Substantially different trends were observed for ASPR, period and cohort effects in recent decades for type 1 versus type 2 diabetes across different SDI."
- **Problem:** The study reports trends only; rising prevalence in high SDI settings could equally reflect rising type 2 diabetes prevalence, longer survival or better case ascertainment. The screening-uptake explanation is the digest's own inference.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the authors do not identify a cause, and list rising diabetes prevalence and longer survival alongside screening gaps as possible explanations."
