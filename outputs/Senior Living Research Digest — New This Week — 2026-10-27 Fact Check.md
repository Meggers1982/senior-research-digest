### Study 2: Popular diabetes drugs look safe in people over 75 — and were linked to fewer hospitalizations
**PMID:** 42937517 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Enrollment window described as follow-up** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "short follow-up window (2021-2023)"
- **Abstract says:** "We included individuals aged ≥75 years with type 2 diabetes initiating SGLT2is or DPP-4is between 1 January 2021 and 31 December 2023."
- **Problem:** 2021–2023 is the period during which patients started the drugs, not the length of follow-up; the abstract does not state follow-up duration.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "patients entered the cohort between 2021 and 2023, and the abstract does not report how long they were followed"."

**Issue B. Novelty claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is among the first real-world safety assessments focused squarely on people 75 and older"
- **Abstract says:** "SGLT2 inhibitors ... have demonstrated cardiorenal benefits in individuals with type 2 diabetes, but safety data in older adults remain limited."
- **Problem:** The abstract notes limited data but makes no priority claim. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "the authors note that safety data in this age group remain limited"."

### Study 3: Brain stimulation trial shows cognitive gains for stroke survivors — and the benefits held a month later
**PMID:** 42933772 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Painless" descriptor unsupported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "high-intensity transcranial alternating current stimulation (Hi-tACS) — a painless, non-invasive current applied through scalp electrodes"
- **Abstract says:** "Hi-tACS improved global cognition and working memory in patients with PSCI and was generally well tolerated."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports only that the intervention was "generally well tolerated"; it says nothing about whether 15 mA stimulation is painless, and a reader could be misled about the sensation involved.
- **Suggested fix:** "Replace "painless" with "non-invasive and reported as generally well tolerated"."

### Study 4: Adding probiotics to iron pills did nothing extra for anemia in older adults, trial finds
**PMID:** 42933603 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Inflammation-marker result not reported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "There were no meaningful between-group differences in hepcidin, inflammation markers, or most measures of gut microbiome diversity."
- **Abstract says:** "Hepcidin increased in both groups, with no significant between-group difference (p=0.550) ... there were no significant between-group differences in hepcidin or most gut microbiota alpha-diversity measures at endline."
- **Problem:** CRP was measured but no between-group result for inflammation is given in the abstract, so the inflammation claim is unverifiable from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "inflammation markers" or note that CRP was measured but results were not reported in the abstract."

### Study 6: Hospitals that screen every new patient's swallowing cut aspiration pneumonia by more than half
**PMID:** 42934351 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for retrospective observational data** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Hospitals that screen every new patient's swallowing cut aspiration pneumonia by more than half"
- **Abstract says:** "Adherence to an admission dysphagia screening protocol was associated with a significantly lower risk of hospital-acquired aspiration pneumonia in general wards ... DESIGN: Retrospective cohort study."
- **Problem:** "Cut" asserts causation from a retrospective cohort, and the comparison was between protocol-adherent and non-adherent patients within a single hospital, not between hospitals that screen and those that do not.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "Patients who got a swallowing screen on admission had less than half the rate of hospital-acquired aspiration pneumonia"."

### Study 7: It's not just how many pills — it's which ones: anticholinergic drugs tied to hip fracture and dementia in nationwide data
**PMID:** 42931882 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Single "best metric" recommendation beyond abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "flags anticholinergic load as the metric most worth tracking for fall and cognition risk"
- **Abstract says:** "Overall, the strengths of the associations for the number of anticholinergic drugs and total anticholinergic burden were broadly similar ... comprehensive exposure assessments that account for disease characteristics and temporal factors are crucial."
- **Problem:** The abstract found anticholinergic drug count and total burden to be broadly similar and concluded in favor of comprehensive, outcome-specific assessment rather than endorsing one metric.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as "supports tracking anticholinergic exposure, by count or burden, alongside pill count rather than instead of a broader review"."

### Study 9: Multiple chronic conditions already affect over a third of older Africans — and in urban South Africa, 62%
**PMID:** 42933453 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Site description slightly overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "across rural and urban sites in The Gambia, South Africa and Zimbabwe"
- **Abstract says:** "we recruited adults aged at least 40 years in rural and urban The Gambia, rural and urban South Africa, and urban Zimbabwe"
- **Problem:** Zimbabwe contributed an urban site only; the phrasing implies rural and urban sampling in all three countries.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "rural and urban sites in The Gambia and South Africa and an urban site in Zimbabwe"."

**Issue B. "First" claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It provides the first robust, comparable population-level picture of multimorbidity in older Africans"
- **Abstract says:** "the gap in robust, comparable, population-level evidence on multimorbidity in Africa remains substantial"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes a substantial evidence gap but does not claim this is the first such study. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "It helps fill a substantial gap in comparable population-level data on multimorbidity in Africa"."

### Study 10: Denmark's 84,000-person study: older adults with intellectual disability get less cancer — but it's caught later
**PMID:** 42934045 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Age-stratified stage claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Regardless of age, people with intellectual disability were significantly more likely to be diagnosed at an advanced stage (OR 1.28) or with an unknown stage (OR 1.57)."
- **Abstract says:** "Individuals with intellectual disability were more likely to be diagnosed with advanced (Odds Ratio [OR]: 1.28, 95% CI: 1.20-1.36) or unknown cancer stage (OR: 1.57, 95% CI: 1.46-1.69)."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports overall stage odds ratios only; it does not show that the pattern held within every age group, so "regardless of age" is unverifiable.
- **Suggested fix:** "Delete "Regardless of age" and report the stage figures as an overall finding."

**Issue B. Novelty claim unverifiable** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is among the first nationwide looks at their cancer risk across the lifespan"
- **Abstract says:** "People with intellectual disability have lower screening participation and higher cancer-related mortality, yet few studies have investigated cancer incidence in this group."
- **Problem:** The abstract notes that few studies exist but makes no claim about being among the first nationwide lifespan analyses.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "few previous studies have examined cancer incidence in this group, according to the authors"."

### Study 11: A simple nutrition score flagged which dialysis patients lost toes — and who died first
**PMID:** 42933604 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Follow-up window for foot events omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "low scores also tracked with more hospital admissions, more minor amputations and significantly worse survival over up to six years"
- **Abstract says:** "DF-related events (hospitalisations and amputations) were recorded over a 12-month follow-up period, whereas all-cause mortality was tracked throughout the entire study observation period (up to 6 years)."
- **Problem:** The 'up to six years' applies only to mortality; hospitalizations and amputations were counted over 12 months. As written, readers may assume all outcomes were tracked for six years.
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify that hospitalizations and amputations were recorded over a 12-month follow-up, while mortality was tracked for up to six years."

**Issue B. Predictive framing of a concurrent association** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A falling score may be a cue to escalate foot surveillance before an ulcer appears."
- **Abstract says:** "Multivariate binary logistic regression revealed that baseline GNRI was independently and inversely associated with the presence of DF (OR=0.803...)"
- **Problem:** The GNRI–diabetic foot relationship was an association with foot disease already present, not a demonstrated prediction of future ulcers; the 'early warning' and 'before an ulcer appears' framing goes beyond the design, which the caveat itself acknowledges can run in reverse.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to note that lower nutrition scores accompanied existing foot disease and that whether a falling score predicts new ulcers remains untested."

**Issue C. 'Diabetic foot ulcers' vs diabetic foot disease** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Diabetic foot ulcers were present in 41.5% of patients"
- **Abstract says:** "The prevalence of DF [diabetic foot] was 41.5% (n=22)."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports prevalence of diabetic foot (a broader category than ulceration), and the digest switches to 'diabetic foot disease' later in the same paragraph.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'diabetic foot disease' consistently, matching the abstract's terminology."

- **As written:** "with consistent results in a second independent set of 184 samples"
- **Abstract says:** "In an independent cohort, the combined model showed consistent directional effects and achieved the highest accuracy (AUC = 0.796)."

- **Abstract says:** "The Neuro-Lupus Program evaluated 50 patients with SLE referred for any neurocognitive concern over a 12-month period."
