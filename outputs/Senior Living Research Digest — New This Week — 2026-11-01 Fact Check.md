### Study 2: Tai chi and other mind-body exercise improved memory and thinking in older adults with mild cognitive impairment — but didn't move the biology marker
**PMID:** 42951347 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Subgroup result generalized** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Tai chi-based programs and durations longer than 12 weeks showed the largest gains."
- **Abstract says:** "Subgroup analyses suggested greater benefits for Tai Chi-based interventions and for verbal fluency when intervention duration exceeded 12 weeks."
- **Problem:** The duration benefit in the abstract is reported specifically for verbal fluency, not for cognitive outcomes generally.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say that Tai Chi-based programs showed greater benefit overall and that longer-than-12-week programs showed greater gains specifically in verbal fluency."

**Issue B. Programming advice beyond subgroup data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the subgroup findings argue for prioritizing tai chi-style movement over one-off mindfulness sessions, and for building programs that run beyond 12 weeks rather than short blocks"
- **Abstract says:** "Subgroup analyses suggested greater benefits for Tai Chi-based interventions and for verbal fluency when intervention duration exceeded 12 weeks."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports no head-to-head comparison with mindfulness sessions; the ranking of modalities is inferred rather than tested.
- **Suggested fix:** "Frame as 'subgroup analyses hinted that tai chi-based programs fared better' and drop the explicit comparison with mindfulness sessions."

### Study 3: The dementia therapy that works almost everywhere — when stigma and caregiver burden don't block the door
**PMID:** 41512238 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported comparative framing** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "which makes community attitudes, not clinical capacity, the real bottleneck"
- **Abstract says:** "Although Cognitive Stimulation Therapy provision is expanding, its access remains limited, particularly in low- and middle-income countries... Stigma, caregiver burden, and lack of awareness about dementia were significant barriers"
- **Problem:** The review lists barriers but does not rank attitudinal barriers above capacity or resource constraints; the comparison is the digest's own conclusion.
- **Suggested fix:** "State that stigma, caregiver burden and low awareness were among the barriers identified, without declaring them the dominant one relative to clinical capacity."

**Issue B. 'Not program content' claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "budget effort for dementia-awareness outreach and caregiver relief up front, because the review found those — not program content — are what sink implementation"
- **Abstract says:** "Stigma, caregiver burden, and lack of awareness about dementia were significant barriers, highlighting the need for culturally sensitive strategies."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not compare these barriers with program content as a cause of failed implementation.
- **Suggested fix:** "Remove '— not program content —' or recast as 'the review highlighted these contextual barriers rather than examining program content'."

**Issue C. Retention claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "groups that build in social connection and respect participants' spiritual or cultural lives are the ones people actually stay with"
- **Abstract says:** "Social connection, emotional support, and spirituality were key facilitators."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes these as facilitators of adaptation and implementation, not as drivers of participant retention.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say these were identified as factors that helped programs take root, rather than implying a measured retention effect."

### Study 5: Cocoa flavanols nudge blood pressure down — but the researchers work for a chocolate company
**PMID:** 42679502 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Certainty of evidence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Certainty of evidence was rated moderate for systolic pressure and limited for the vascular measures."
- **Abstract says:** "Certainty was moderate for SBP; other outcomes were limited."
- **Problem:** Diastolic blood pressure was also graded 'limited', not just the vascular measures, so the digest slightly overstates confidence in the DBP result.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write 'moderate for systolic pressure and limited for all other outcomes, including diastolic pressure and the vascular measures.'"

**Issue B. Trial characteristics not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "pooled trials were small and heterogeneous in dose and duration; ... trials were not restricted to older adults"
- **Abstract says:** "Twenty-five studies (n = 1,167) included hypertensive and normotensive participants; daily cocoa flavanol doses ranged from 10.6 to 1,680 mg."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not characterize trial size, duration heterogeneity or participant age limits. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute these caveats more cautiously or note that trial duration and participant age were not specified in the abstract."

### Study 6: Noisy breathing at the end of life distresses families, not the dying person — and there's almost no evidence on how to treat it
**PMID:** 41169186 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Effectiveness attributed to non-drug measures** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "the review suggests the main effective intervention is caregiver education and repositioning, not medication"
- **Abstract says:** "Treatment included anticholinergics for death rattle/noisy breathing, showing limited efficacy, and nonpharmacological approaches such as caregiver education and patient positioning."
- **Problem:** The abstract lists non-pharmacological approaches as treatments described in the literature but does not report evidence that they are effective — it concludes there is a 'dearth of evidence'.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the review found limited efficacy for anticholinergics and that caregiver education and positioning were the non-drug approaches described, without labeling them 'effective'."

**Issue B. Prevalence overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Most people who die at home with hospice will have one of these breathing patterns"
- **Abstract says:** "End-of-life respiratory changes-agonal breathing, Cheyne-Stokes breathing, and death rattle/noisy breathing-are common in home hospice settings"
- **Problem:** 'Common' is not the same as 'most people'; no prevalence figure appears in the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'These breathing patterns are common in home hospice.'"

### Study 7: Time-restricted eating proved doable for older cancer patients — but didn't move fatigue or frailty
**PMID:** 42570448 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Randomized vs enrolled count** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "30 adults aged 55 to 81 (average 69, 93% male) ... received dietitian counseling and were randomized to a self-selected 10-hour daytime eating window or usual eating"
- **Abstract says:** "Participants (n = 30, 93% male) were 68.8 ± 6.4 years old (range 57.4-81.0 y); 24 were randomized and 20 completed the intervention (83.3% retention)."
- **Problem:** Only 24 of the 30 enrolled participants were randomized; the body text implies all 30 were, although the Caveats state the correct figure.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write '30 adults enrolled, of whom 24 were randomized...' in the body text."

**Issue B. Age range** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "30 adults aged 55 to 81"
- **Abstract says:** "eligible if they were ≥ 55 years old ... range 57.4-81.0 y"
- **Problem:** 55 was the eligibility floor; the actual enrolled age range started at 57.4.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'aged 57 to 81 (eligibility was 55 and older)'."

**Issue C. Retention figure attribution** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "83% completed the study"
- **Abstract says:** "24 were randomized and 20 completed the intervention (83.3% retention)"
- **Problem:** The 83.3% is of those randomized (20/24), not of all 30 enrolled, which the sentence could be read to imply.
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify '83% of those randomized completed the intervention'."

### Study 9: Brain-training on a tablet, tailored to your life story, sharpened thinking in healthy 65-plus adults
**PMID:** 42400967 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Active ingredient not tested** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the active ingredient here was personalization to each resident's education level, work history and interests, paired with upfront device training"
- **Abstract says:** "The intervention comprised digital literacy sessions followed by 8 weeks of home-based computerized cognitive stimulation tailored to participants' cognitive reserve profiles and life history. Controls received a single group-based health education session..."
- **Problem:** The trial compared the whole tailored package against a single education session; it did not compare tailored versus untailored stimulation, so personalization cannot be identified as the active ingredient.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the program that was tested included personalization and upfront device training, and note that the trial did not isolate which component drove the benefit."

**Issue B. Delivery platform** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Brain-training on a tablet"
- **Abstract says:** "8 weeks of home-based computerized cognitive stimulation ... the Mobile Device Proficiency Questionnaire-16"
- **Problem:** The abstract says 'computerized' and does not specify tablets as the delivery device. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'computer-based' or 'on a screen' in the headline."

### Study 10: An online self-help program for dementia caregivers works — and researchers now know why
**PMID:** 41925437 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Depressive symptom threshold** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "274 caregivers from 43 countries who provided at least 10 hours of care weekly and had meaningful depressive symptoms"
- **Abstract says:** "274 caregivers who provided ≥10 weekly care hours to relatives with dementia, scored ≥5 on Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9)"
- **Problem:** PHQ-9 ≥5 is the threshold for mild symptoms; 'meaningful depressive symptoms' overstates the entry criterion.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'scored 5 or higher on the PHQ-9, indicating at least mild depressive symptoms'."

**Issue B. Cost claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "it can be delivered free, around the clock, with no therapist"
- **Abstract says:** "SETTING: Web-based platform accessible 24/7 globally. INTERVENTION: A self-guided automated intervention..."
- **Problem:** The abstract confirms self-guided 24/7 web delivery but says nothing about cost. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop 'free' or attribute it to a source outside the abstract."

**Issue C. Headline certainty** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "An online self-help program for dementia caregivers works — and researchers now know why"
- **Abstract says:** "PDC30 ... was found to improve depression, burden, and positive gains in dementia family caregivers, compared with a 1-month waitlist control. However, the therapeutic mechanism is unclear. This study investigated whether caregiving self-efficacy and positive reappraisal coping mediated the intervention effects."
- **Problem:** 'Works' rests on an earlier one-month waitlist-controlled comparison, and a mediation analysis identifies statistical mediators rather than definitively establishing mechanism.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to 'An online self-help program for dementia caregivers helped — and a new analysis points to why'."

### Study 11: A phone-based support program eased depression in family caregivers — and the frail relatives they cared for got less frail
**PMID:** 42288393 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Timepoints attributed to all outcomes** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Caregivers in the program showed greater improvement in depressive symptoms, anxiety, happiness and caregiver burden both right after the program and a month later"
- **Abstract says:** "the intervention group showed greater improvement in depressive symptoms (βT1 = -1.593... βT2 = -2.020...), anxiety symptoms, happiness, caregiver burden, and care recipients' frailty compared to usual care (all p < 0.05)"
- **Problem:** Only the depression result is reported with separate estimates at both T1 and T2 in the abstract; the other outcomes are reported as significant overall without timepoint-specific figures, so the 'both right after and a month later' framing goes slightly beyond what is shown.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write: 'Caregivers in the program showed greater improvement in depressive symptoms — both immediately after the program and a month later — as well as in anxiety, happiness and caregiver burden.'"

### Study 15: Seizures affect roughly 1 in 14 people with frontotemporal dementia — and may be badly underrecognized
**PMID:** 42664548 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Subtype prevalence precision** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with variation by subtype — about 3% in behavioral-variant FTD and 9% in primary progressive aphasia"
- **Abstract says:** "including 3% (95% CI, 1%-11%) in behavioral-variant FTD and 9% (95% CI, 0%-69%) in primary progressive aphasia"
- **Problem:** The 9% figure for primary progressive aphasia carries a confidence interval of 0%-69%, meaning it is essentially uninformative; presenting it as a point estimate alongside the bvFTD figure implies a reliable subtype difference.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the uncertainty: 'about 3% in behavioural-variant FTD and 9% in primary progressive aphasia, though the latter estimate was extremely imprecise (95% CI 0%-69%).'"

### Study 16: A $0 approach beat a high-tech device for overactive bladder in a rigorous sham-controlled trial
**PMID:** 42403115 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline overstates a non-superiority finding** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "A $0 approach beat a high-tech device for overactive bladder in a rigorous sham-controlled trial"
- **Abstract says:** "Both groups showed significant improvement in OAB symptoms... However, no superiority over sham treatment was observed, reinforcing the value of BT as the, current first-line treatment recommendation for patients with OAB."
- **Problem:** The trial compared radiofrequency plus behavioural therapy against sham plus behavioural therapy and found no difference — behavioural therapy alone was never tested as a separate arm and did not 'beat' anything. The headline also contradicts the digest's own body text ('no advantage for radiofrequency over sham').
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to something like 'High-tech vaginal radiofrequency added nothing to behavioural therapy for overactive bladder — but doubled side effects.'"

**Issue B. Omits within-group pelvic floor findings** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "At six months, both groups improved significantly on bladder symptoms and quality of life — with no advantage for radiofrequency over sham."
- **Abstract says:** "In the RF group, pelvic floor tone normalized and pain on palpation decreased (p = 0.026 and p = 0.035), repetitions of sustained and rapid contractions improved (p = 0.039, p = 0.015)"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports several pelvic floor measures that improved in the radiofrequency group; omitting them makes the 'no advantage' summary slightly flatter than the published results, though the primary outcome did show no superiority.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a clause noting that the radiofrequency group did show some pelvic floor changes (tone, pain on palpation, contraction repetitions), but no advantage on the primary bladder-symptom outcome."

### Study 19: Depression in later life follows nine different paths — and widowhood and shrinking family ties shape which one you're on
**PMID:** 42362063 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Socioeconomic finding omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Notably, chronological age itself was not reliably linked to depression — what mattered were age-related events: losing a spouse, weakening family connections, and declining physical health."
- **Abstract says:** "Better socioeconomic conditions were associated with decreased risks of entering depressive trajectories... While long-term efforts should target socioeconomic inequalities, promoting social connections offers a modifiable pathway"
- **Problem:** The abstract gives socioeconomic conditions equal or greater prominence as a predictor and as the main long-term policy target; the digest's framing attributes everything to life events and social ties.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a sentence noting that better socioeconomic conditions were also associated with lower risk of entering a depressive trajectory, and that the author flags socioeconomic inequality as a long-term target."

**Issue B. Rounding of the symptom-free group** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this study found nearly half of people sailed through nine years with no depressive symptoms at all"
- **Abstract says:** "43% of respondents did not report depressive symptoms over the study period."
- **Problem:** 43% is described accurately in the body as 'forty-three percent' but is inflated to 'nearly half' in the story angle.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'more than four in ten' rather than 'nearly half'."

### Study 20: A two-marker blood test sharpens Alzheimer's detection — and catches people a single test would have called low risk
**PMID:** 42601653 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. 'Negative to positive' versus 'non-positive to positive'** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "roughly 70-76% of people who flipped from negative to positive on the combined test turned out to be amyloid-PET positive"
- **Abstract says:** "Critically, 69.7 to 75.8% of Non-positive to Positive cases defined by p-tau217/Aβ42 were Aβ-PET positive."
- **Problem:** The abstract's category is 'Non-positive' — which includes both negative and intermediate baseline results — not strictly negative, so the digest narrows the group described.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write 'people who moved from a negative or intermediate result to a positive one on the combined test'."

**Issue B. Longitudinal subsample attribution** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with repeat data over time for 285 people"
- **Abstract says:** "Longitudinal data were available for 285 Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative participants."
- **Problem:** The 285 with repeat data were all from the ADNI cohort, not a mix of both cohorts as the phrasing implies.
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify 'with repeat data over time for 285 of the ADNI participants'."

### Study 21: Older transgender adults carry far higher rates of PTSD, depression and anxiety — in some cases eleven times higher
**PMID:** 42362064 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Comparison group for "two to three times" not specified** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Transgender adults had roughly two to three times the prevalence of diagnosed anxiety and depression"
- **Abstract says:** "aPRs (95% CI) comparing TF to CM were 2.16 (2.08, 2.23) for anxiety, 2.77 (2.66, 2.89) for depression... corresponding TF versus CW estimates were 1.38 (1.34, 1.42), 1.65 (1.59, 1.71)... Among TM adults, aPRs versus CM were 2.36 ... 3.18 ... corresponding estimates versus CW were 1.49 ... 1.80"
- **Problem:** The preceding sentence says the comparison was with "cisgender men and women," but the 2–3x figure applies only to comparisons with cisgender men; against cisgender women the ratios are roughly 1.4 to 1.8, so the stated gap is overstated for half the comparison group.
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify the referent: "roughly two to three times the prevalence of diagnosed anxiety and depression compared with cisgender men, and about 1.4 to 1.8 times compared with cisgender women.""

**Issue B. Causal explanation for observed disparities** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "If you're a transgender older adult, these numbers reflect a lifetime of discrimination and trauma, not something inherent to being trans"
- **Abstract says:** "This analysis used electronic health record data... A cross-sectional analysis within the STRONG cohort... Anxiety, depression, and PTSD prevalence were substantially higher among TF and TM adults than among matched cisgender referents."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports prevalence differences only; it offers no data on discrimination, trauma exposure or any other cause. Stating what the numbers "reflect" asserts a causal mechanism the cross-sectional design cannot establish, and conflicts with the digest's own caveat that causation and direction cannot be determined.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as interpretation, e.g. "researchers and advocates attribute gaps like these to cumulative discrimination and trauma rather than to being transgender, though this study measured only diagnosis rates.""

**Issue C. Unsupported claim about family support** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a group that will increasingly show up in geriatric clinics, home care and senior housing, often without family support networks"
- **Abstract says:** "Little is known about anxiety, depression, and post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) diagnoses among midlife and older transgender adults (aged 45 years and older)."
- **Problem:** The abstract contains no data on family support, social networks or living arrangements.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the clause or attribute it to outside literature rather than this study. Unverifiable from abstract only."
