# Fact-Check Report

### Study 1: Loneliness, not social isolation, drives the link between social connections and mental health in midlife and older adults
**PMID:** 42667132 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. Framing is consistent with the abstract. The sample size (N=4,963), age range (40–65), follow-up period (2004–2014), six dimensions of social connectedness, and the finding that loneliness (rather than social isolation) served as the pathway linking social connectedness to mental health outcomes are all accurately reported. The note about effects being particularly pronounced for family support and among women matches the abstract.

### Study 2: Engaging in a variety of activities—not just doing more of the same—may help protect against dementia
**PMID:** 42658656 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Effect size description slightly imprecise** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Higher variety of activities—not how frequently people did them—was associated with a 6% lower risk of dementia per additional activity type, equivalent to roughly a 6-month delay in diagnosis."
- **Abstract says:** "higher activity variety was associated with lower risk of incident dementia (HR=.94, 95% CI: 0.89-0.99), equivalent to about a 6-month delay in diagnosis per additional activity."
- **Problem:** The digest accurately reports HR=0.94 as "6% lower risk" and the 6-month delay. However, the digest omits that this finding was for the 15-year follow-up only (not the 5-year), which slightly overstates the consistency of the finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "For the 15-year follow-up period, higher variety of activities—not how frequently people did them—was associated with a 6% lower risk of dementia per additional activity type, equivalent to roughly a 6-month delay in diagnosis."

### Study 3: Psychological health at age 13 predicts memory complaints nearly 60 years later
**PMID:** 42658651 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Sample size location** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "researchers assessed 625 individuals at ages 10 and 13, then again at age 67"
- **Abstract says:** "subjective memory, anxiety, and depressive symptoms were measured with the Prospective and Retrospective Memory Questionnaire (PRMQ) and Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS) at age 67 (n = 625)"
- **Problem:** The abstract indicates n=625 at age 67, but doesn't specify how many were assessed at ages 10 and 13. The digest implies 625 were assessed at all three timepoints, which may overstate the longitudinal sample size.
- **Suggested fix:** "In a remarkable six-decade longitudinal study from Sweden, researchers assessed individuals at ages 10 and 13, then again at age 67 (n=625 at follow-up)."

### Study 10: Pilot study suggests blueberries may affect gut bacteria linked to mood regulation
**PMID:** 42628247 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Funding source description incomplete** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "industry-funded research (USDA and one author affiliated with a nutrition company)"
- **Abstract says:** "Courtney Millar reports financial support was provided by US Department of Agriculture. Courtney Millar reports financial support was provided by Boston Roybal Center for Active Lifestyle Interventions... Douglas P. Kiel reports a relationship with Solarea Bio that includes: board membership and funding grants."
- **Problem:** USDA is a government agency, not industry. The relevant industry conflict is that Douglas P. Kiel has a relationship with Solarea Bio (a company) including board membership and funding grants, and with Johnson & Johnson. The digest conflates government funding with industry funding.
- **Suggested fix:** "industry conflicts (one author has board membership and receives funding from biotech/pharmaceutical companies); USDA-funded."

### Study 19: Three-month exercise program produces clinically meaningful benefits for most older adults, primarily in physical function
**PMID:** 42545663 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Sample size discrepancy** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "118 older adults (75+) with mild cognitive impairment"
- **Abstract says:** "188 older patients (> 75 years) with mild cognitive impairment were randomly assigned... A total of 72 and 46 participants were included in the analyses for the control and intervention group, respectively."
- **Problem:** The digest says 118, but the abstract indicates 188 were randomized and 118 (72+46) were included in the final analysis. The digest should clarify this is the analyzed sample, not the randomized sample.
- **Suggested fix:** "This analysis of a randomized controlled trial examined whether exercise benefits reached clinical significance for 118 analyzed participants (from 188 randomized) who were older adults (75+) with mild cognitive impairment."

**Issue B. Adherence rate not verifiable** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "modest adherence (40–50%)"
- **Abstract says:** No adherence rate mentioned.
- **Problem:** The 40-50% adherence figure cannot be verified from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** Remove the specific adherence percentage or flag as "Unverifiable from abstract only."

### Study 21: Physical performance strongly associated with depressive symptoms in older adults
**PMID:** 42549000 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Correlation strength description** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "SPPB scores were moderately correlated with depression"
- **Abstract says:** No explicit correlation coefficient provided for characterizing the strength; the abstract states "SPPB scores were moderately negatively correlated with depressive symptoms (r = -0.404, P < 0.05)"
- **Problem:** The digest accurately describes this as "moderately correlated." No error here upon review.

**Issue B. ROC cutoff description** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a score below 8.5 identified depression with 86% sensitivity"
- **Abstract says:** "ROC analysis showed that SPPB score was associated with depressive symptoms (AUC = 0.859), with sensitivity of 0.86 and specificity of 0.67 at the optimal cutoff of 8.5."
- **Problem:** The digest says "below 8.5" but the abstract says "at the optimal cutoff of 8.5" without specifying direction. This is a minor ambiguity but the interpretation is reasonable.

No significant issues. **Verdict changed to:** ✅ Accurate

- **Abstract says:** "Changes in psychological flexibility were influenced by carers' resonance with metaphors and case examples, and by their engagement with intervention content (e.g. note-taking) and therapists (e.g. therapeutic relationships)."
