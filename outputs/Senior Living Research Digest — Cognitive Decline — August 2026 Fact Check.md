### Study 3: Resistance Training Improves Thinking Speed, Working Memory, and Executive Function in Healthy Adults

**PMID:** 42508500 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Omission of cautious interpretation language** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Resistance training was associated with improvements in global cognition, executive function, working memory, and processing speed."
- **Abstract says:** "Resistance exercise was associated with improvements in global cognition and in several domains, including executive function, working memory, and processing speed... interpretation should remain cautious given heterogeneity across studies, limited evidence in some domains, and variation in intervention duration, intensity, and cognitive measurement approaches."
- **Problem:** The digest captures the caveat about heterogeneity but could more prominently flag the "interpretation should remain cautious" framing from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** No revision required; caveat section adequately addresses this.

**Issue B. Population age range** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** Caveats state "most evidence from younger healthy adults, with limited data specific to older populations."
- **Abstract says:** The abstract does not specify age distribution of included studies.
- **Problem:** The caveat about younger adults is unverifiable from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** Revise caveat to: "Heterogeneity across studies in intervention type, duration, and cognitive measures; not all domains showed benefits; age distribution of included participants not specified in abstract."

### Study 14: Zumba Plus Video Games Improve Cognition in Older Malaysian Adults

**PMID:** 42398018 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Randomization description** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A semi-randomized trial assigned 96 older adults..."
- **Abstract says:** "96 participants... were assigned to 4 groups" — the abstract title says "Semirandomized Trial"
- **Problem:** The digest correctly uses "semi-randomized" but could clarify what this means.
- **Suggested fix:** No revision required; term is accurate per abstract.

**Issue B. Statistical interpretation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "All intervention groups showed cognitive improvements over time."
- **Abstract says:** "Global cognition improved significantly over time across all groups (time main effect: F1,92=7.03, P=.008; time × group interaction: F3,92=0.57, P=.64)"
- **Problem:** The non-significant interaction means improvements were not specific to intervention groups vs. control. The digest wording could be clearer that the control group also improved.
- **Suggested fix:** Revise to: "All groups, including the control group, showed cognitive improvements over time, though participants who attended at least half of sessions in the combined or cognitive-only groups showed the largest within-group gains."
