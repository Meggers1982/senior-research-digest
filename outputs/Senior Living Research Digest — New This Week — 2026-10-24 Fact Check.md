### Study 1: A short video changed what older kidney patients wanted — and half chose to skip dialysis
**PMID:** 42911803 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Preference reported as a decision** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "A short video changed what older kidney patients wanted — and half chose to skip dialysis"
- **Abstract says:** "After viewing the video, fewer intervention participants than controls at enrollment were undecided about treatment (30 of 227 [13.2%] vs 92 of 223 [41.3%]), and more preferred conservative kidney management (115 of 227 [50.7%] vs 71 of 223 [31.8%]; P < .001 for the overall distribution)."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a stated preference recorded at enrollment, not an actual treatment choice or documented forgoing of dialysis. The headline implies half the participants actually went without dialysis.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "half said they preferred management without dialysis" or "half stated a preference for conservative kidney management.""

**Issue B. Unverifiable video length** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a 12-minute video shifted both the conversation and the choice"
- **Abstract says:** "Intervention participants viewed a certified video decision aid depicting 3 treatment options for advanced CKD (hemodialysis, peritoneal dialysis, and conservative kidney management)"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not state the length of the video decision aid. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the running time or attribute it to the full text/trial protocol."

### Study 3: An ear infection that won't quit can eat into the skull — and older adults with diabetes are the main target
**PMID:** 42904817 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Cure rate attributed to prolonged antibiotics** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Pooled clinical cure reached 84.7% with prolonged antibiotic therapy"
- **Abstract says:** "Combined fluoroquinolone-based therapy yielded a cure rate of 71.4%... In a proportion meta-analysis of primary cohorts, pooled estimates were: ... clinical cure 84.7% (74.6-91.2)"
- **Problem:** The 84.7% figure is a pooled proportion across cohorts, not a cure rate tied to prolonged antibiotic therapy; the abstract's treatment-specific cure figure is 71.4% for fluoroquinolone-based therapy.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "pooled clinical cure across cohorts was 84.7% (fluoroquinolone-based regimens specifically: 71.4%)" and keep the prolonged-therapy point as a separate conclusion."

**Issue B. "Once-rare" not supported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "This once-rare condition still concentrates in older adults with diabetes"
- **Abstract says:** "Skull base osteomyelitis (SBO) is a life-threatening infection arising predominantly from necrotizing external otitis... Originally described in elderly diabetic patients, its demographic profile has broadened to encompass immunocompromised hosts of diverse etiologies."
- **Problem:** The abstract makes no statement about rarity or changing incidence, and notes the patient profile has broadened beyond older diabetic patients. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "once-rare" and note that the demographic profile now also includes immunocompromised patients."

### Study 4: A blood test for dementia risk? A 13-year study finds it adds less than you'd hope
**PMID:** 42913444 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Specific risk factors not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The same data suggest your blood pressure, activity, hearing, education, and other everyday risk factors carry just as much predictive weight"
- **Abstract says:** "a model containing sociodemographic, behavioral, and health-related risk factors to have a C-index of 0.765 (95% CI 0.746-0.785)"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes the comparison model only in general categories; blood pressure, activity, hearing and education are not named. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "a model built from sociodemographic, behavioural and health risk factors" without itemising specific factors."

**Issue B. Cohort characteristics beyond abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "participants were UK civil servants (only 27% female, relatively advantaged and predominantly white)"
- **Abstract says:** "the UK Whitehall II longitudinal, population-based cohort study established in 1985 among civil servants... Of the 5,225 (27.2% female) participants"
- **Problem:** The civil-servant setting and 27% female figure are correct, but "relatively advantaged and predominantly white" is not stated in the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Keep the civil-servant and sex-ratio points; attribute the ethnicity/socioeconomic characterisation to the full text or drop it."

### Study 5: Half of nursing home residents showed depression, anxiety or loneliness during COVID — and staffing consistency may be part of the answer
**PMID:** 42909849 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Protective factors overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "identify specific, modifiable facility practices — stable staff assignments and preserved family contact — that distinguished homes where residents fared better"
- **Abstract says:** "Vulnerability reflected clinical, social and facility-level factors; consistent staffing, social connection and family communication were potential protective conditions."
- **Problem:** The abstract calls these "potential protective conditions" identified narratively; it does not report a comparison that distinguished better- from worse-performing homes.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "conditions the reviewers flagged as potentially protective.""

**Issue B. Unsupported contrast with amenities** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "these were the factors associated with better mental health through the pandemic, not building quality or amenities"
- **Abstract says:** "Vulnerability reflected clinical, social and facility-level factors; consistent staffing, social connection and family communication were potential protective conditions."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not test or rule out building quality or amenities, so the contrast is not supported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Delete "not building quality or amenities" or note these factors were not assessed."

### Study 9: Walking enough steps protects women's bones — but only before menopause
**PMID:** 42909484 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for observational data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Walking enough steps protects women's bones — but only before menopause"
- **Abstract says:** "Achieving 7000 steps/day on ≥3 days/week significantly reduced the risk of decline in quantitative ultrasound measurements in premenopausal women, but not in postmenopausal women... In this prospective cohort study..."
- **Problem:** "Protects" asserts causation from a prospective observational cohort; the digest's own caveat says causation cannot be established.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "Hitting step targets is linked to better bone measures — but only before menopause.""

### Study 10: Parkinson's patients can have their brain stimulators fine-tuned from home, trial finds
**PMID:** 42920097 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Direction of the between-group difference** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "motor scores (MDS-UPDRS Part III, medication on/stimulation ON) differed by a median of -2.5 points favoring remote care"
- **Abstract says:** "The between-group median difference of the primary endpoint was -2.5 points (95% confidence interval, -9.0 to 2.0), meeting the prespecified noninferiority criterion."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not state which group the negative sign favours, and the confidence interval crosses zero, so no advantage for remote care can be claimed. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the median between-group difference was -2.5 points (95% CI -9.0 to 2.0), consistent with noninferiority and with no significant difference between approaches."

### Study 12: Why older adults abandon the technology meant to help them stay independent
**PMID:** 42910771 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Strength of evidence wording** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with the strongest and most consistent evidence for perceived usefulness, ease of use, facilitating conditions, social influence, and anxiety about technology"
- **Abstract says:** "Re-meta-analysis showed highly suggestive evidence for associations involving perceived usefulness, ease of use, facilitating conditions, social influence, and anxiety, although substantial heterogeneity was observed."
- **Problem:** "Most consistent" implies low variability, whereas the abstract explicitly reports substantial heterogeneity for these same associations (the digest's Caveats do note heterogeneity generally).
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "with the strongest ("highly suggestive") evidence for ... though with substantial heterogeneity across reviews"."

**Issue B. Overstated AI-review gap** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "reviews of AI-enabled tools focused heavily on human-like interaction features while largely ignoring accessibility, user diversity, and how the technology fits into real care settings"
- **Abstract says:** "Reviews of AI-enabled technologies emphasised anthropomorphic and interactive features but gave comparatively limited attention to accessibility, contextual integration, and user diversity."
- **Problem:** "Largely ignoring" is stronger than "comparatively limited attention."
- **Suggested fix:** "Replace "largely ignoring" with "giving comparatively little attention to"."

### Study 13: Three years on donepezil: brain networks improve briefly, then decline continues
**PMID:** 42911381 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Provenance of longitudinal sample** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "then followed 35 of the MCI patients who stayed on donepezil continuously for 36 months"
- **Abstract says:** "Subsequently, 35 AD-MCI patients who completed 36-month continuous donepezil treatment were retrospectively enrolled"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes the 35 patients as retrospectively enrolled and does not state that they were a subset of the 107 cross-sectional AD-MCI patients, nor that they were prospectively followed.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "then separately analysed 35 retrospectively enrolled AD-MCI patients who had completed 36 months of continuous donepezil"."

### Study 21: A blood test for Alzheimer's tracks decline — but only one of the two leading markers did the job
**PMID:** 42909513 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Strength of hippocampal correlation overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Individual p-tau217 trajectories tracked closely with cognitive test decline and hippocampal shrinkage, while GFAP trajectories did not"
- **Abstract says:** "p-tau217 co-varied with ADAS-Cog 13 (ρ = +0.67), MMSE (ρ = -0.69), and hippocampal ratio (ρ = - 0.39)"
- **Problem:** The cognitive correlations were strong (ρ ≈ 0.67–0.69), but the hippocampal correlation was only moderate (ρ = -0.39); grouping both under "tracked closely" slightly overstates the imaging result.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "tracked closely with cognitive test decline and, more modestly, with hippocampal shrinkage (ρ = -0.39)"."

**Issue B. Parallel-vs-predict framing slightly firmer than the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the honest framing is that p-tau217 trajectory appears to parallel cognitive decline rather than clearly predict it in advance — useful for tracking, not for forecasting an individual's future"
- **Abstract says:** "Plasma p-tau217 trajectory is informative of cognitive trajectory, although whether it predicts or parallels decline cannot be determined from these data."
- **Problem:** The abstract says the data cannot distinguish prediction from parallel change; the digest states it "appears to parallel," which picks one side of an explicitly unresolved question.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the study cannot tell whether p-tau217 changes precede or simply accompany cognitive decline, so it should not be assumed to forecast an individual's future."
