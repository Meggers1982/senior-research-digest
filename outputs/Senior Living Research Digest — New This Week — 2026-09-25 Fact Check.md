### Study 1: Big trial finds the type of anesthesia barely matters for older surgery patients — with a few comfort exceptions
**PMID:** 42584898 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Awareness cases described as "probable" only** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the only two cases of probable awareness under anesthesia occurred in the TIVA group"
- **Abstract says:** "Two cases of certain or probable unintentional awareness under anesthesia were reported, both in the TIVA group."
- **Problem:** The digest drops "certain," slightly understating the classification of the two awareness events.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "the only two cases of certain or probable awareness under anesthesia occurred in the TIVA group.""

**Issue B. Causal phrasing for a secondary, patient-reported outcome** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "it is still reasonable to ask your anesthesiologist about post-op nausea, since intravenous anesthesia caused less of it"
- **Abstract says:** "Lower rates of thirst, hoarseness, and nausea and vomiting were reported in the TIVA group."
- **Problem:** These were secondary, patient-reported outcomes in an open-label trial where patients knew their assignment; "caused less" is stronger than "lower rates were reported."
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as "since patients given intravenous anesthesia reported less of it.""

### Study 2: Fecal transplants flop for irritable bowel syndrome in the largest trial yet
**PMID:** 42785338 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Largest trial yet" claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Fecal transplants flop for irritable bowel syndrome in the largest trial yet"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract describes a "randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, phase 3 trial" of 448 analysed participants at five Norwegian hospitals but makes no claim about being the largest."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only; the abstract makes no size-ranking claim.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "the largest trial yet" or replace with "a large phase 3 trial.""

**Issue B. Advice extended to seniors although trial skewed young** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "If you have chronic IBS symptoms and have seen ads for microbiome or fecal transplant treatments, this trial suggests your money is better spent elsewhere"
- **Abstract says:** "we enrolled men and women aged 18-65 years with moderate-to-severe IBS ... the median age was 36 years (IQR 29-44)"
- **Problem:** The story angle applies the findings directly to older readers when the caveat notes the 18-65 range; the abstract also shows a median age of 36, so the cohort was predominantly young adults, a fact the digest never mentions.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the median age (36) to the study description and soften the angle to note the findings are extrapolated to older adults."

### Study 3: Exercise measurably cools inflammation in people over 65 — but the effect is modest
**PMID:** 42786581 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Number of trials pooled** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers pooled 50 randomized controlled trials covering 4,379 participants aged 65 and older"
- **Abstract says:** "A total of 50 reports (4,379 participants) were included in the qualitative synthesis, and 38 of them in the different meta-analyses performed."
- **Problem:** Only 38 of the 50 reports entered the meta-analyses; saying all 50 were "pooled" overstates the quantitative evidence base.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "reviewed 50 randomized controlled trials (4,379 participants), 38 of which were pooled statistically.""

### Study 4: Blood tests track what lecanemab is doing in the brain — and some markers move in the wrong direction
**PMID:** 42785333 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Control group described as registry-only** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "comparison groups were drawn from a separate research registry, not concurrently randomized"
- **Abstract says:** "173 patients in whom lecanemab had been ordered but not yet started ... were combined with 1139 research participants with cognitive impairment from the Knight ADRC cohort to form the untreated control group with cognitive impairment (n=1312)"
- **Problem:** The untreated comparison group was a mix of clinic patients awaiting lecanemab and Knight ADRC research participants, not purely registry volunteers.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the untreated group combined 173 patients awaiting lecanemab with 1,139 research-registry participants."

### Study 5: Which nudge gets an advance directive signed? Paper forms start the conversation, people finish it
**PMID:** 42778351 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Incomplete list of what beat usual care for signing, plus "trained" added** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "But for actually getting an advance directive signed, interventions involving a trained person — including motivational interviewing — ranked highest."
- **Abstract says:** "in terms of increasing the signing rate of advance directives (AD) documents, personnel-supported interventions, motivational interviewing interventions and video tools were more effective than usual care; the intervention with the highest percentage of the cumulative ranked area under the curve was the personnel-supported intervention."
- **Problem:** Video tools also outperformed usual care for signing rates and are omitted; only personnel-supported interventions ranked first, not motivational interviewing as well; and the abstract never says the personnel were specifically "trained."
- **Suggested fix:** "Say that personnel-supported interventions, motivational interviewing and video tools all beat usual care for signing, with personnel-supported interventions ranking first."

### Study 6: A "mini-stroke" doubles dementia risk in the first year — and the risk never fully fades
**PMID:** 42777184 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Headline overstates first-year risk** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "A "mini-stroke" doubles dementia risk in the first year — and the risk never fully fades"
- **Abstract says:** "The hazard ratio for dementia after TIA was highest within the first year (hazard ratio [HR] 1.75, 95% CI 1.66-1.85)"
- **Problem:** A hazard ratio of 1.75 is a 75% increase, not a doubling; the headline quantifies the effect more strongly than the data support, even though the body text gets it right.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change the headline to "A 'mini-stroke' raises dementia risk by 75% in the first year — and the risk never fully fades.""

### Study 12: Cardiac rehab tailored for frail older heart patients shows gains in walking and strength — but the evidence is thin
**PMID:** 42772577 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Omits null result on unadjusted SPPB** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Programs improved Short Physical Performance Battery scores by about 1.2 points"
- **Abstract says:** "Significant effects favoured the intervention on adjusted/model-based Short Physical Performance Battery (SPPB) (MD 1.20 points, 95% CI 0.54 to 1.87; k=2) ... but not on unadjusted SPPB, SPPB improvement, handgrip strength, Barthel scores, or all-cause mortality."
- **Problem:** The SPPB gain was significant only in adjusted/model-based analyses from two studies; the unadjusted SPPB and SPPB-improvement outcomes were null, which the digest does not mention.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that the SPPB gain came from adjusted/model-based estimates in just two studies and that unadjusted SPPB showed no significant benefit."

**Issue B. Independence claim conflicts with null Barthel result** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the measured gains in walking distance and physical performance are the kind that translate into staying independent at home"
- **Abstract says:** "Significant effects ... but not on unadjusted SPPB, SPPB improvement, handgrip strength, Barthel scores, or all-cause mortality ... clinically meaningful benefit has not been established"
- **Problem:** The review found no benefit on the one measure of daily-living independence (Barthel) and states clinically meaningful benefit is unestablished, so asserting translation to independence at home goes beyond the data even though the body text notes the null.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to note that despite walking/performance gains, the review found no measurable benefit on independence scores, so any link to staying at home is speculative."

### Study 15: Deep brain stimulation buys years of independence in Parkinson's — but the edge fades by year five
**PMID:** 42765824 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Overstates durability at 1 year** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "still meaningful at 1-2 years"
- **Abstract says:** "effect estimates favoring DBS at 6 months (SMD = 0.83[0.38;1.29]) and at 1 year (SMD = 0.49[-0.02;0.99]) for autonomy ... QoL, with SMD = 0.60[0.49;0.71] at 6 months and SMD = 0.48[0.0;0.96] at 1 year. At 2 years, small but significant pooled effects persisted, for both autonomy SMD = 0.40[0.13;0.67] and QoL SMD = 0.25[-0.04;0.53]"
- **Problem:** At 1 year the autonomy confidence interval crossed zero and the QoL interval touched zero, and the 2-year QoL interval also crossed zero, so 'still meaningful at 1-2 years' is stronger than the intervals allow.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say benefits attenuated after 6 months, with 1-year estimates no longer clearly significant and only small pooled effects at 2 years."

**Issue B. Causal headline verb for pooled, largely uncontrolled data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Deep brain stimulation buys years of independence in Parkinson's"
- **Abstract says:** "STN-DBS provides robust short- and mid-term benefits in functional autonomy and QoL in PD. Long-term outcomes are variable and influenced by disease progression and non-motor symptoms."
- **Problem:** 'Buys years of independence' implies a proven causal, multi-year gain, whereas the pooled estimates are heterogeneous, mostly pre-post comparisons, and significance is lost by 5 years — the digest's own caveat acknowledges this.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to 'linked to better independence for the first year or two' to match the meta-analysis design."

### Study 17: A four-month program to ease older adults' fear of falling worked no better than the sham version

- **Suggested fix:** "Drop 'single-center' or say the trial was run by a single German university group. Unverifiable from abstract only."

### Study 18: Why a poor diet ages you: biological aging accounts for up to 40% of diet's link to heart disease
**PMID:** 42763827 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for observational mediation analysis** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Why a poor diet ages you: biological aging accounts for up to 40% of diet's link to heart disease"

### Study 20: Staying Put Pays Off: Older Koreans Who Didn't Move Were Twice as Likely to Age Well
**PMID:** 42786686 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline and odds-ratio-as-likelihood** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Staying Put Pays Off: Older Koreans Who Didn't Move Were Twice as Likely to Age Well"
- **Abstract says:** "Binary logistic regression revealed that individuals without residential movement had significantly higher odds of successful aging, particularly those who remained within the same administrative division (OR = 2.06) or housing type (OR = 1.59)."
- **Problem:** 'Pays off' asserts a benefit of staying put from a cross-sectional-style observational association, and an odds ratio of 2.06 is not the same as being 'twice as likely' when the outcome is common; the digest's own caveat concedes reverse causation is plausible.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'Older Koreans who stayed put had about double the odds of aging well' and drop 'pays off.'"

### Study 21: A Two-Minute Questionnaire Can Now Tell Mild Impairment From Dementia — Researchers Just Set the Cutoffs
**PMID:** 42786504 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Administration time not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A Two-Minute Questionnaire Can Now Tell Mild Impairment From Dementia"
- **Abstract says:** "the 8-item Informant Interview to Differentiate Aging and Dementia (AD8)... the AD8 offers a brief, reliable option for measuring baseline cognition"
- **Problem:** The abstract calls the AD8 "brief" and "8-item" but never states it takes two minutes. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "An Eight-Question Survey" or "A Brief Proxy Questionnaire"."

**Issue B. Overstated certainty of the crosswalk** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "They found that an AD8 score of 2 corresponds to mild cognitive impairment and a score of 4 corresponds to dementia — thresholds the short tool previously lacked."
- **Abstract says:** "An AD8 score of 2, corresponding to an IQCODE score of 3.2, was identified as the cutoff for MCI, while an AD8 score of 4, corresponding to an IQCODE score of 3.5, was identified as the cutoff for dementia."
- **Problem:** The cutoffs are derived by statistical equating to IQCODE thresholds, not by validation against a clinical diagnosis of MCI or dementia; the body text reads as if the AD8 scores were shown to identify the conditions themselves. (The Caveats note this, but the headline and body do not.)
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "as benchmarked against the longer IQCODE's existing cutoffs" in the body sentence."

### Study 22: Older Adults' Own Accounts of How Much They Exercise Predicted Nothing — Their Step Counters Predicted Plenty
**PMID:** 42785603 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Predictive language for cross-sectional associations** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Older Adults' Own Accounts of How Much They Exercise Predicted Nothing — Their Step Counters Predicted Plenty"
- **Abstract says:** "Wearable-derived step counts were significantly associated with 9 of 16 cardiometabolic and fitness outcomes... self-reported exercise... was not significantly associated with any outcome."
- **Problem:** "Predicted" implies a prospective/predictive relationship; the abstract reports cross-sectional associations within a follow-up cohort.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "Tracked With Nothing — Their Step Counters Tracked With Plenty" or "Linked to Nothing.""

**Issue B. Overestimation claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Most of us overestimate how much we move."
- **Abstract says:** "Regression calibration suggested that measurement limitations in self-reported exercise may have contributed to attenuated associations compared with Garmin-derived and calibration-corrected estimates."
- **Problem:** The abstract documents measurement error in self-report but does not establish its direction (over- vs under-reporting).
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as "Our own sense of how much we move is an unreliable measure.""

### Study 23: Three in Four Palliative Care Inpatients Had a Medication Problem — Opioids Drove Most of Them
**PMID:** 42785578 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. ADEs vs ADRs conflated in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Three in Four Palliative Care Inpatients Had a Medication Problem — Opioids Drove Most of Them"
- **Abstract says:** "The cumulative incidence of in-hospital ADEs was 73.9%. A total of 70 adverse drug reactions (ADRs) were identified, most frequently involving opioids (61.4%)."
- **Problem:** The 61.4% opioid figure applies to adverse drug reactions specifically, not to all adverse drug events (which also include medication errors); the headline implies opioids drove three-quarters of all medication problems.
- **Suggested fix:** ""...Opioids Were Behind Most Adverse Drug Reactions.""

**Issue B. Intervention inference from an association** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this study suggests that trimming inappropriate drugs may itself be a quality-of-life intervention — not just a safety exercise"
- **Abstract says:** "A higher PIM count was associated with worse quality of life (β=0.386; p=0.031)."
- **Problem:** A cross-sectional association between inappropriate-medication count and quality of life cannot show that deprescribing improves quality of life; the hedged "may" softens but does not remove the causal leap.
- **Suggested fix:** ""...a higher count of inappropriate drugs went along with worse quality of life, though the study cannot show that removing them would help.""

**Issue C. "Highest-yield checkpoint" not supported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the study found medication errors clustered at discharge, making handoff reconciliation the highest-yield checkpoint"
- **Abstract says:** "ADRs predominated at admission and among patients who died; medication errors were more frequent at discharge."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports where errors were more frequent but makes no claim about which checkpoint yields the greatest benefit; adverse drug reactions actually predominated at admission.
- **Suggested fix:** ""...medication errors were more frequent at discharge while adverse reactions predominated at admission, so both handoffs warrant review.""

### Study 24: Long-Term Steroids for Polymyalgia and Giant Cell Arteritis Didn't Erode Quality of Life — but Blood Pressure Rose
**PMID:** 42785189 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies change over time** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Long-Term Steroids ... Didn't Erode Quality of Life — but Blood Pressure Rose"
- **Abstract says:** "A cross-sectional study included 50 patients... Corticosteroid treatment was associated with... an increased risk of high blood pressure (HBP) (p = 0.038)."
- **Problem:** "Didn't erode" and "rose" describe change over time, but the design is a single-timepoint cross-sectional survey reporting associations only; the study also has no steroid-free comparison group.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Steroid Dose Wasn't Linked to Worse Quality of Life — but Was Linked to High Blood Pressure.""

**Issue B. Disease control not assessed** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the day-to-day quality-of-life toll may be smaller than feared when the underlying disease is well controlled"
- **Abstract says:** "suggesting a favourable balance between disease control and patient-reported outcomes despite treatment-related risks"
- **Problem:** The abstract's closing inference mentions disease control but the study measured only steroid doses, SF-36 and PREDIMED; no measure of disease activity or control is reported, so the conditional clause is unverifiable.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "when the underlying disease is well controlled" or attribute it as the authors' interpretation."
