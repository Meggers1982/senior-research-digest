### Study 10: Long-Term Cancer Survivors Face Elevated Osteoporosis Risk and Physical Limitations
**PMID:** 42349049 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Sample size description** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "over 5,800 Irish adults"
- **Abstract says:** Total calculated from subgroups: 157 + 208 + 5,526 = 5,891
- **Problem:** "Over 5,800" is accurate (5,891 participants).
- **Suggested fix:** No revision required.

**Issue B. Osteoporosis risk magnitude** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "twice the risk of osteoporosis"
- **Abstract says:** "RRR 2.26 (95% CI: 1.29, 3.94)" for T-scores and "RRR 2.21 (95% CI: 1.20, 4.08)" for stiffness index

- **Abstract says:** "1.81 (95% CI: 1.08, 3.06) times more likely"
- **Problem:** OR of 1.81 means 81% higher odds, correctly stated.
- **Suggested fix:** No revision required.

### Study 11: Polypharmacy, Diuretics, and Antipsychotics Each Independently Raise Injurious Fall Risk
**PMID:** 42489786 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Hazard ratio vs. subdistribution hazard ratio terminology** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "polypharmacy (5+ medications) was associated with 2.6 times higher risk"
- **Abstract says:** "aSHR = 2.62, 95% CI: 1.23-5.59"
- **Problem:** The digest says "2.6 times higher risk" when the aSHR is 2.62, which technically means 2.62 times the hazard (or ~162% higher), not "2.6 times higher." However, in common usage, this phrasing is often used interchangeably.
- **Suggested fix:** For precision, could say "2.6 times the risk" rather than "2.6 times higher risk," but this is a minor semantic issue.

**Issue B. Diuretic risk magnitude** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "diuretic use with 3 times higher risk"
- **Abstract says:** "aSHR = 2.98"
- **Problem:** Same issue as above; 2.98 times the hazard is approximately "3 times the risk."
- **Suggested fix:** Acceptable as written.

All other statistics (PAF percentages, follow-up time, sample size) are accurate.

### Study 14: Common Plasticizer Chemical Linked to Higher Osteoporosis Risk in National Survey
**PMID:** 41990860 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Statistical finding precision** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Those in the highest quartile of DEHP exposure had 32% higher odds of osteoporosis"
- **Abstract says:** "OR = 1.32, 95% CI = 0.76-1.66"
- **Problem:** The confidence interval (0.76–1.66) crosses 1.0, meaning this finding is not statistically significant. The digest does not mention this non-significance, which could mislead readers about the strength of the evidence.
- **Suggested fix:** Add to caveats or body: "the association was not statistically significant in fully adjusted models" or revise the wording to acknowledge uncertainty.

**Issue B. Missing context on significance** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** Digest presents the 32% figure without noting the wide confidence interval crossing null.
- **Abstract says:** "P for trend <0.001" suggests significance for trend, but the specific quartile OR confidence interval includes 1.0.
- **Problem:** The reader may overinterpret the certainty of this single-quartile finding.
- **Suggested fix:** Revise to: "Those in the highest quartile of DEHP exposure had 32% higher odds of osteoporosis, though the confidence interval was wide (0.76–1.66). A significant dose-response trend was observed across quartiles."
