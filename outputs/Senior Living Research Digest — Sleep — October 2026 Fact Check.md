### Study 1: Poor Sleep Before Heart Surgery Doubles the Odds of Post-Op Confusion
**PMID:** 42665464 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Borderline significance of apnea finding presented as firm** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Moderate-to-severe sleep apnea (15 or more events per hour) tripled the odds of delirium."
- **Abstract says:** "Apnea-hypopnea index ≥15 events/h was associated with higher odds of delirium (OR 3.4 [1.01, 11.7]) but not delirium severity."
- **Problem:** The confidence interval (1.01 to 11.7) barely excludes 1 and is very wide, so the tripling estimate is highly imprecise; stating it flatly overstates certainty. The abstract also notes no association with delirium severity.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a qualifier, e.g. "was linked to roughly triple the odds of delirium, though this estimate was imprecise (OR 3.4, 95% CI 1.01–11.7) and did not extend to delirium severity.""

**Issue B. Author disclosure omitted from Caveats** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Caveats: Small sample (N=100), single tertiary-care center, observational design that cannot prove poor sleep causes delirium, and limited to one type of cardiac surgery"
- **Abstract says:** "DISCLOSURES APS has served as a consultant to Sequoia Neurovitality, BellSant, Inc., Amissa, Inc., and Synaptic Health, LLC. The other authors report no conflicts of interest."
- **Problem:** The digest flags industry relationships in several other entries but omits the consulting disclosures reported here.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a line noting that one co-author reports consulting relationships with several health-technology companies."

### Study 2: Sleep Problems Linked to a 43% Higher Risk of Developing Urinary Incontinence After 50
**PMID:** 42748569 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Incidence range understated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "New urinary incontinence developed in roughly one in five to one in four participants."
- **Abstract says:** "Weighted UI incidence was 28.6% in HRS, 23.1% in ELSA, and 19.7% in CHARLS."
- **Problem:** 28.6% in the US cohort is closer to between one in four and one in three, so the stated upper bound is too low.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "roughly one in five to nearly three in ten participants, depending on the country.""

**Issue B. Causal shading in mediation description** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "sleep problems appeared to partly explain the protective link between exercise and continence"
- **Abstract says:** "Mediation analyses suggested that sleep problems partially mediated the association between physical activity and incident UI."
- **Problem:** "Protective" implies causation in an observational cohort; the abstract describes an association that was statistically mediated.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "partly accounted for the association between physical activity and lower incontinence risk.""

### Study 3: Acupuncture and Massage Both Eased Insomnia in Advanced Cancer Patients — With Pain Relief Following
**PMID:** 42471129 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Omits that pain response rates differed between arms** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this trial found both worked about equally well for sleep, so you can reasonably pick the one you'd actually enjoy and stick with"
- **Abstract says:** "ISI responder rates were similar (35.1% vs 33.3%), whereas BPI responder rates were higher (48.0% vs 62.9%)."
- **Problem:** Sleep outcomes were equivalent, but pain responder rates differed between the two arms — relevant to readers choosing a treatment for pain-related insomnia.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that although insomnia responses were nearly identical, pain responder rates differed (48.0% vs 62.9%), so the choice may matter more for pain than for sleep."

**Issue B. Setting description not verifiable from abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "conducted largely at a major cancer center"
- **Abstract says:** "Data were drawn from a multicenter pragmatic randomized clinical trial comparing acupuncture and massage therapy for pain in advanced cancer."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes a multicenter trial without specifying site distribution; the author affiliations suggest a lead cancer center but the proportion of participants is not stated.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "conducted across multiple centers, led by a major cancer center" or mark as unverifiable from the abstract."

### Study 4: Nonhormonal Menopause Drugs Also Improved Sleep, Pooled Trial Data Show
**PMID:** 42837121 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Industry sponsorship of underlying trials not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "underlying trials were industry-sponsored"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract reports funding/disclosure only for the review authors (A.M.K.'s employer receives research funding from Bayer; past funding from Mylan; ongoing relationship with Estetra); it says nothing about sponsorship of the included trials."
- **Problem:** Plausible but unverifiable from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the sponsorship point to the underlying trial reports, or drop it and keep only the author disclosure."

**Issue B. Liver monitoring advice not from this study** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "discuss cost, liver monitoring requirements, and how long you'd expect to stay on it"
- **Abstract says:** "No safety, monitoring, or cost data are reported; the outcome was PROMIS Sleep Disturbance at 4, 12, and 24 weeks."
- **Problem:** Liver monitoring is a real labeling requirement for fezolinetant but comes from outside this meta-analysis, and is presented here as if it flows from the study.
- **Suggested fix:** "Signal the source, e.g. "ask about cost and about monitoring requirements that apply to some of these drugs (not assessed in this review).""

### Study 5: Sleeping Pills After Hip Fracture Have Dropped Sharply — But Opioid Use Hasn't Budged Much
**PMID:** 42852585 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Survival exclusion window misstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "cohort excluded people who died within roughly three months of fracture"
- **Abstract says:** "The cohort included individuals aged 66 years and older ... and who survived for at least 275 days in the following year."
- **Problem:** The requirement was survival of at least 275 days (about nine months), so the cohort excluded people who died within roughly nine months — a substantially larger survivor-selection effect than the digest implies.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "cohort required survival of at least 275 days (about nine months) after fracture, excluding those who died sooner.""

### Study 7: Sleep Length and Brain Health: Added Vascular Damage Changes the Equation
**PMID:** 42861684 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Groups showing the amplified association described imprecisely** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "In people with cognitive impairment plus high vascular burden, the extra vascular injury sharply amplified the link between shorter sleep and heavier white matter damage."
- **Abstract says:** "In MCI+V and AD, WMH+ significantly amplified the association between shorter sleep and greater WMH burden."
- **Problem:** The effect was seen in MCI with high vascular burden and in the Alzheimer's dementia group generally — not only in people with "cognitive impairment plus high vascular burden." "Sharply" also embellishes "significantly," which speaks to statistical, not clinical, magnitude.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "In people with MCI plus high vascular burden and in those with Alzheimer's dementia, additional vascular injury significantly strengthened the link between shorter sleep and greater white matter damage.""

### Study 9: Opening the Windows: A Daily Habit Linked to Better Sleep and Fewer Depressive Symptoms in Older Adults
**PMID:** 42860785 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Direction of the sleep mediator not specified in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Statistical mediation analysis suggested the link ran partly through longer self-reported sleep duration and greater psychological resilience."
- **Abstract says:** "Window ventilation frequency also had a significant link with depressive symptoms through three significant mediating pathways: the mediating role of self-reported total sleep duration, the mediating role of psychological resilience, and the mediating role of self-reported total sleep duration and psychological resilience, separately."
- **Problem:** The abstract names sleep duration and resilience as mediators but does not state that ventilation was linked to *longer* sleep; the headline's "Better Sleep" similarly goes beyond a duration measure.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the link ran partly through self-reported total sleep duration and psychological resilience, and refer to "longer sleep" rather than "better sleep" only if the direction can be confirmed."

### Study 10: Too little or too much: both ends of the sleep spectrum tied to earlier death in a 16,000-person study
**PMID:** 42648048 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

**Issue A. Null result phrased as reassurance** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Reassuringly, this study found no mortality penalty attached to daytime napping."
- **Abstract says:** "Daytime napping and sleep disturbance were not significantly associated with mortality."
- **Problem:** A non-significant association is not the same as demonstrated absence of risk; the wording nudges a null finding toward a positive safety claim.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "this study found no statistically significant link between daytime napping and mortality.""

### Study 12: Less REM sleep linked to faster buildup of Alzheimer's-related proteins in healthy older adults
**PMID:** 42851166 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Slow-wave activity null for rate of change not stated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Frontal slow-wave activity was related to amyloid levels at a single point in time but not to the rate of change."
- **Abstract says:** "Frontal slow-wave activity (SWA) was inversely associated with plasma Aβ42/40 at cross-section in bivariate analysis and in a covariate-adjusted model. In comparison, low rapid eye movement (REM) sleep percentage was associated with a large longitudinal decline in annualized blood-based Aβ42/40."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports the cross-sectional SWA association and contrasts it with the REM longitudinal finding, but never explicitly states that SWA was unrelated to the rate of change. The claim is an inference.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to 'Frontal slow-wave activity was related to amyloid levels at a single point in time, while the longitudinal signal came from REM sleep.'"

**Issue B. Author conflict of interest omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Caveats: Small sample (N=64); observational — cannot establish causation..."
- **Abstract says:** "Conflict of interest statement: A.W.V. has served as a consultant for Axsome Therapeutics and Jazz Pharmaceuticals in the last 3 years."
- **Problem:** The senior author's pharmaceutical consulting relationships are disclosed in the abstract record but not noted in the caveats, unlike comparable entries elsewhere in this digest.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a short note that the senior author reports consulting for two pharmaceutical companies."

### Study 19: Experimental Pill Dramatically Restored Daytime Wakefulness in Narcolepsy Trial
**PMID:** 42636845 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Hype in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Experimental Pill Dramatically Restored Daytime Wakefulness in Narcolepsy Trial"
- **Abstract says:** "In this phase 2 trial, once-daily oral alixorexton provided clinically meaningful improvements at 6 weeks for participants with narcolepsy type 1, including in wakefulness..."
- **Problem:** 'Dramatically Restored' is stronger than the authors' 'clinically meaningful improvements' and implies normalization of wakefulness from a 6-week phase 2 trial.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like 'Experimental Pill Sharply Improved Daytime Wakefulness in Early Narcolepsy Trial.'"

**Issue B. Phase 3 status overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a new class of drug aimed at the underlying cause is heading into phase 3 trials, and it may be worth asking your sleep specialist about trial participation"
- **Abstract says:** "these findings support the further phase 3 evaluation of alixorexton as a potential therapeutic option for people with narcolepsy type 1"
- **Problem:** The abstract says the findings support further phase 3 evaluation; it does not confirm that phase 3 trials have been launched or are recruiting.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'the authors say the results support moving to phase 3 testing' and drop the implication that trials are already enrolling."

**Issue C. Count of sponsor-employed authors** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "funded by Alkermes, which also employs seven of the authors"
- **Abstract says:** "HC, AL, MJD, CH, BR, and JH are employees at Alkermes."
- **Problem:** Six authors, not seven, are listed as Alkermes employees.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change 'seven of the authors' to 'six of the authors.'"

**Issue A. Sleep measured per day, not per night** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "comparing those who slept at least 8 hours a night with those who slept less"
- **Abstract says:** "significantly higher among participants with ≥8 hours of sleep per day than among those with <8 hours"
