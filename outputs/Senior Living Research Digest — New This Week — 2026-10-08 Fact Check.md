### Study 1: A trained guide helped Black older adults plan end-of-life care — a self-serve workbook worked just as well for White patients
**PMID:** 42842242 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline asserts a non-significant within-group effect** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A trained guide helped Black older adults plan end-of-life care — a self-serve workbook worked just as well for White patients"
- **Abstract says:** "Among Black participants, 152 of 213 (71%) in facilitated clinics and 131 of 213 (62%) in self-guided clinics completed ACP within 1 year (adjusted risk ratio [RR], 1.15 [95% CI, 0.83-1.59])... CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE: ACP completion within 1 year did not differ significantly between facilitated vs self-guided ACP approaches within Black or White racial groups."
- **Problem:** The headline reads as if facilitation produced a real benefit for Black participants, but the within-group comparison was not statistically significant; only the race-by-intervention interaction was. The body and caveats state this correctly, so the headline is out of step with the digest's own hedging.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to signal the relative nature of the finding, e.g. "Neither advance care planning format beat the other — but the facilitated version did relatively better for Black patients"."

**Issue B. Definition of "formal" ACP narrowed** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "only 130 of 789 had a formal advance directive or order"
- **Abstract says:** "new formal (advance directive, clinician note, or orders for life-sustaining treatment form in the medical record)"
- **Problem:** The study's formal category also included a clinician note in the medical record; the digest lists only directives and orders.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "only 130 of 789 had formal documentation in the medical record (advance directive, clinician note or treatment orders).""

### Study 2: Back pain that stops you from doing things predicts frailty seven years later
**PMID:** 42844230 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Outcome comparison not specified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "only activity-limiting back pain remained independently associated with progressing to frailty (OR 1.40, 95% CI 1.03–1.90)"
- **Abstract says:** "only the activity-limiting back pain phenotype was associated with significantly higher odds of later being classified as frail vs. prefrail (OR = 1.40 [1.03-1.90]), and no back pain phenotypes were associated with higher odds of later being classified as frail vs. robust"
- **Problem:** The OR applies to the frail-versus-pre-frail comparison only; in the fully adjusted models nothing predicted becoming frail relative to staying robust. Readers may assume a broader result than the data show.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the comparison, e.g. "...associated with later being classified as frail rather than pre-frail (OR 1.40); no back pain measure predicted frailty versus staying robust.""

### Study 5: Older adults quit their dizziness exercises within a year — and most never tell anyone
**PMID:** 42844416 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported headline claim** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "and most never tell anyone"
- **Abstract says:** "Self-reported adherence was measured using a five-point Likert scale and dichotomised at predefined cut-offs... adherence to specific exercises was lower at 12 than at 3 months (odds ratio (OR) 0.28; 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.12-0.67)."
- **Problem:** The study measured only self-reported adherence; nothing in the abstract addresses whether patients disclose stopping to clinicians or anyone else. The claim is invented.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the clause or replace with something the study supports, e.g. "— and the drop-off happens after the first follow-up visit.""

**Issue B. Timepoint not studied** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a quiet drop-off at the nine-month mark can undo the gains of treatment"
- **Abstract says:** "The participants were assessed at baseline and followed up after 3 and 12 months."
- **Problem:** Assessments were at 3 and 12 months; the study cannot locate the drop-off at nine months.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "a quiet drop-off somewhere between the 3- and 12-month checks"."

**Issue C. Falls claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "dizziness is a leading contributor to falls"
- **Abstract says:** "Specific exercises and lifestyle-related recommendations are used to manage vertigo, dizziness, and balance disorders (VDB), but evidence on adherence and its determinants in older adults is limited."
- **Problem:** Background assertion not supported by this abstract; it is general context rather than a study finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute it as general background or cite a source, e.g. "dizziness is widely recognized as a fall risk factor"."

### Study 6: Myasthenia gravis starting after 65 hits harder at the start — but long-term outcomes may be just as good
**PMID:** 42842206 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Long-term outcome consistency overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "long-term disease control appeared broadly comparable across age groups, especially when immunotherapy was used"
- **Abstract says:** "long-term outcomes were inconsistent though often comparable among different age-at-onset groups, particularly when immunotherapy was used"
- **Problem:** The abstract explicitly calls the long-term results inconsistent across studies; "broadly comparable" reads as a more settled finding than the evidence supports.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "long-term results were inconsistent across studies but often comparable, particularly when immunotherapy was used.""

### Study 7: Brain white-matter damage grows faster with age — and lower blood pressure didn't predict slower progression
**PMID:** 42843461 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Largest synthesis to date" claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is the largest synthesis to date of how quickly they actually advance"
- **Abstract says:** "we conducted a meta-analysis of 54 longitudinal studies"
- **Problem:** The abstract makes no priority or size claim relative to other reviews. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "a meta-analysis pooling 54 longitudinal MRI studies" without the superlative."

**Issue B. Clinical consequences not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "White matter changes are tied to slower walking, falls and cognitive decline"
- **Abstract says:** "White matter hyperintensities (WMH) are ubiquitous on magnetic resonance images (MRI) in older adults. However, the rate and mechanisms of their proliferation remain unclear"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not report associations with gait, falls or cognitive decline (only baseline cognitive status as a moderator of progression). Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Flag as outside background or drop, e.g. "White matter changes are a marker of small-vessel disease"."

### Study 8: Living in a neighborhood without toilets raises depression risk for older adults — even for those who have one at home
**PMID:** 42842074 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for cross-sectional data** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Living in a neighborhood without toilets raises depression risk for older adults"
- **Abstract says:** "This cross-sectional study examines community-level toilet coverage in India as a possible determinant of depression among adults over 45... an increase in the proportion of individuals living in no-toilet households within communities is associated with a greater risk of depressive symptoms"
- **Problem:** "Raises" asserts causation from a single cross-sectional survey; the digest's own caveat says causation cannot be established, so the headline contradicts it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use associational wording: "Living in a neighborhood where few households have toilets is linked to higher depression risk in older adults.""

**Issue B. Unsupported individual-level qualifier** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "even for those who have one at home"
- **Abstract says:** "people living in communities with the lowest quintile of toilet coverage had a higher risk of experiencing depressive symptoms (OR 1.38, 95% CI 1.08 to 1.76) compared to those living in communities with the highest quintile of toilet coverage"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not report results stratified by, or adjusted for, whether the respondent's own household had a toilet, so this claim cannot be verified from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Remove the clause, or say "the study looked at community-level toilet coverage rather than individual households" if the full text supports it."

**Issue C. Population described as older adults** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "predicted depression among adults over 45"
- **Abstract says:** "depression among adults over 45"
- **Problem:** The body text is correct, but the headline and audience framing present a sample starting at age 45 as a finding about "older adults"; worth a brief note so readers understand the age span.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note in the body that the sample included middle-aged adults from 45 upward, not only seniors."

### Study 9: Adding methadone to existing opioids eased stubborn cancer pain in every study reviewed — but the evidence is thin
**PMID:** 42843430 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Low-dose" not specified in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "641 patients (mean age 62.8) who received low-dose methadone added to an ongoing opioid regimen"
- **Abstract says:** "13 studies including 641 participants (mean age 62.8 years, 53% male)... the efficacy, safety and tolerability of adjunct methadone in combination with ongoing opioid regimens for cancer-related pain"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes adjunct methadone without specifying dose; "low-dose" is unverifiable from abstract only and repeats in the story angle.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "adjunct methadone added to an ongoing opioid regimen" unless dose details can be confirmed from the full text."

**Issue B. Cost and availability claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this review suggests a widely available, inexpensive option"
- **Abstract says:** "Although findings across all studies suggest analgesic benefit for cancer-related pain, high-quality, larger-scale RCTs are required to better inform clinical recommendations."
- **Problem:** The review says nothing about cost or availability; these are added editorial claims.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the cost framing or attribute it separately as context, not as a review finding."

### Study 10: Adults with Down syndrome develop Alzheimer's early — and language changes may be an overlooked warning sign
**PMID:** 42844026 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Age-of-onset detail not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a group at very high genetic risk, often with onset in their 40s and 50s"
- **Abstract says:** "To explore the cognitive and linguistic impairments associated with Alzheimer's disease (AD) in individuals with Down syndrome (DS), identify gaps in current research, and propose directions for future investigation."
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no onset ages or genetic risk figures; this is outside background. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the onset-age context to general literature rather than to this review."

**Issue B. Study design characterization** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "most underlying studies are cross-sectional, so the sequence of decline is inferred rather than demonstrated"
- **Abstract says:** "Future research should prioritize longitudinal studies and population-sensitive assessment tools to improve early detection and individualized interventions."
- **Problem:** The abstract calls for more longitudinal research but does not state that most included studies were cross-sectional. The inference is plausible but unverifiable from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "the authors call for longitudinal studies, suggesting the current evidence base is largely non-longitudinal.""

### Study 11: Brief sessions of thin-air breathing proved safe in Parkinson's — and shifted stress and metabolic hormones
**PMID:** 42574864 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Proved safe" overstates a single-session pilot** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Brief sessions of thin-air breathing proved safe in Parkinson's — and shifted stress and metabolic hormones"
- **Abstract says:** "A single IH session is a safe and effective method for inducing hypoxia and modulating biomarkers in pwPD, without disrupting sleep behavior. These findings support the safety of the protocol and encourage investigation into long-term IH"
- **Problem:** The authors say the findings "support the safety" of one session in 15 patients; "proved safe" implies a level of certainty that a tiny crossover trial with 24-hour follow-up cannot deliver.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "appeared safe in a small Parkinson's trial" or "was well tolerated in a small Parkinson's trial.""

**Issue B. Device detail not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with wrist actigraphy and blood biomarkers measured at baseline, right after, and 24 hours later"
- **Abstract says:** "Actigraphy-based sleep metrics and biomarkers (BDNF, cortisol, IL-6, iron, leptin, and adiponectin) were assessed at baseline, immediately post-session, and at 24-h after follow-up."
- **Problem:** The abstract says "actigraphy-based" without specifying wrist placement. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "actigraphy" without specifying the wrist, or confirm from the full text."

### Study 12: A cancer drug restored muscle strength in aged mice by clearing a metabolic traffic jam
**PMID:** 42841610 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline says "aged mice"; animals were progeroid** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A cancer drug restored muscle strength in aged mice by clearing a metabolic traffic jam"
- **Abstract says:** "CB-839 administration to progeroid mice similarly upregulated ETC complexes and enhanced mitochondrial respiratory capacity in skeletal muscle"
- **Problem:** Progeroid mice model accelerated aging rather than normal aging; the headline implies naturally aged animals. The body text and caveats get this right, so only the headline misleads.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "in rapidly aging mice" or "in a mouse model of accelerated aging.""

**Issue B. Cell species not specified in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Working in human muscle cells and in progeroid (rapidly aging) mice"
- **Abstract says:** "senescent skeletal muscle myoblasts exhibit elevated activity and expression of the enzyme glutaminase (GLS1)"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not state that the myoblasts were human. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "in cultured muscle cells" unless the full text confirms human origin."

**Issue C. CB-839's cancer-drug status not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Blocking GLS1 with CB-839, a drug already developed for cancer"
- **Abstract says:** "Pharmacological inhibition of GLS1 with CB-839 reduced urea levels, restored the expression of electron transport chain (ETC) complexes, and improved mitochondrial function."
- **Problem:** The abstract makes no mention of CB-839's oncology development history; accurate externally but unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the cancer-drug description to outside sources or drop it from the lead."

### Study 14: Sudden wild, flinging movements on one side of the body: a big review rewrites what doctors thought they knew
**PMID:** 42469138 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Stroke as most common cause not stated in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "586 cases, most commonly triggered by strokes and other acute brain injuries that disproportionately affect older adults"
- **Abstract says:** "Of the 586 cases identified, only 24.6 % had lesions intersecting the STN. The majority of cases (83.1 %) had acute symptom-onset within 7 days."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports lesion locations and onset timing but gives no breakdown of etiology or patient age. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the etiology claim or attribute it to background knowledge rather than this review's findings."

**Issue B. Cortical-lesion prognosis comparison narrowed in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "while purely cortical lesions had the best prognosis"
- **Abstract says:** "Within cases with non-STN lesions, pure cortical lesions were associated with a more favorable prognosis and a shorter delay to remission compared to non-STN lesions involving subcortical structures"
- **Problem:** The favorable prognosis was established only against other non-STN (subcortical) lesions, not against all lesion locations including STN.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "purely cortical lesions had a better prognosis than other non-subthalamic lesions.""

### Study 15: The exercise research guiding senior fitness programs is measuring activity with tools never designed for older bodies
**PMID:** 41638194 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Most relied on … not adapted" conflates two separate findings** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Most relied on self-reported questionnaires that were not adapted for older adults"
- **Abstract says:** "Studies often used either self-report or device-measured approaches, with most utilizing self-report measures. Self-report measures used were often not tailored to older adult populations."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports that most studies used self-report and, separately, that self-report tools were "often" (not uniformly) untailored; the digest merges these into a stronger single claim.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "Most relied on self-reported questionnaires, which were often not tailored to older adults.""

**Issue B. "Undercount real effort" goes beyond the review** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "remember that tools calibrated on younger, faster walkers can undercount real effort"
- **Abstract says:** "Cutoff points for device-measured approaches frequently varied when assessing physical activity levels."
- **Problem:** The review documents inconsistent cut-points and untailored instruments but does not establish the direction of error (undercounting) in older adults.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the tools "may misclassify how hard older adults are actually working" rather than specifying undercounting."
