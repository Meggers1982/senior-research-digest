### Study 2: A genetic cause of dementia thought to mimic Alzheimer's turned up in none of 410 early-onset patients — but in a third of those with a rarer white-matter disease
**PMID:** 42802826 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline denominator contradicts the finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A genetic cause of dementia thought to mimic Alzheimer's turned up in none of 410 early-onset patients — but in a third of those with a rarer white-matter disease"
- **Abstract says:** "We screened NOTCH2NLC GGC repeat expansions in 410 Korean patients with EOD... GGC repeat expansions were not identified in early-onset Alzheimer's disease (AD) or frontotemporal dementia (FTD), while four of eleven patients with adult-onset leukodystrophy (36.3%) had abnormal expansions."
- **Problem:** The 11 adult-onset leukodystrophy patients were part of the 410-patient cohort, so the expansion was found in four of the 410, not none. The null result applies specifically to the early-onset AD and FTD subgroups, as the body text correctly states.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to, e.g., "turned up in no early-onset Alzheimer's or frontotemporal dementia patients among 410 screened — but in a third of those with a rarer white-matter disease.""

### Study 3: A common gene variant may help explain why some stroke survivors recover thinking skills and others don't
**PMID:** 42828579 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Pooled" implies meta-analysis** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers pooled seven observational studies covering 2,494 adults who had a stroke"
- **Abstract says:** "Findings were synthesized narratively because of substantial heterogeneity in study population, genotype classification, and cognitive measures. Seven studies involving 2,494 participants met the inclusion criteria."
- **Problem:** No data were statistically pooled; the review used narrative synthesis. The caveats say so, but the body text's "pooled" suggests a quantitative meta-analysis.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "reviewed seven observational studies covering 2,494 adults"."

**Issue B. Reversed finding attached to the wrong domain** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Memory was the most consistently affected domain, though one cohort found the opposite pattern"
- **Abstract says:** "Memory was the most consistently affected domain, with poorer performance among Met carriers... One cohort reported an opposite association, with Val-allele carriers demonstrating poorer global cognition and language performance."
- **Problem:** The cohort reporting the reverse association did so for global cognition and language, not memory; placing it immediately after the memory sentence implies the reversal was in memory.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Memory was the most consistently affected domain, although one cohort reported the reverse pattern for global cognition and language.""

**Issue C. Data-extraction caveat omits verification step** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "single reviewer extracted data"
- **Abstract says:** "Two reviewers independently screened studies; one reviewer extracted and another verified the data."
- **Problem:** One reviewer extracted but a second verified the data, which is a weaker limitation than the caveat implies.
- **Suggested fix:** ""data were extracted by one reviewer and verified by a second"."

### Study 6: "Exercise snacks" boost fitness in younger adults — but the payoff looks different after 50
**PMID:** 42571752 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Claim about trials in adults over 65 unverifiable** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Few trials focused specifically on adults over 65."
- **Abstract says:** "VO2max improved in studies with mean participant ages <25 and 25-50 years, but not >50 years, whereas PPO improved across all three age strata."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports age strata only as <25, 25-50 and >50; it says nothing about how many trials targeted adults over 65. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "the oldest age stratum was simply 'over 50' — the abstract does not report how many trials enrolled adults over 65.""

**Issue B. "Edged up" understates the body-fat effect size** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "body fat percentage actually edged up"
- **Abstract says:** "body fat percentage showed an overall increase (g = 0.51, 95% CI = 0.01-1.02; low-certainty evidence)"
- **Problem:** A Hedges' g of 0.51 is a moderate effect, though the confidence interval barely excludes zero and certainty was low; "edged up" reads as a trivial change.
- **Suggested fix:** ""body fat percentage rose modestly (a moderate but low-certainty effect that only just reached significance)"."

### Study 10: Older adults learned to use a digital memory aid on their own — a coach added nothing
**PMID:** 42043435 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Null result stated more firmly than a pilot supports** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a coach added nothing"
- **Abstract says:** "Both groups showed comparable EMMA engagement and improvements in applied everyday cognition (η² = .14), with no significant group-by-time interaction... Medium effect sizes (η² = .06-.09) were observed for other outcomes but did not reach significance... A self-guided, adaptive training platform with minimal clinician support may be sufficient"
- **Problem:** The pilot found no significant between-group difference, but several medium effect sizes fell short of significance in a small sample — absence of a detected advantage is not proof the coaching added nothing. The authors hedge with "may be sufficient".
- **Suggested fix:** ""a coach added no measurable advantage in this small pilot"."

**Issue B. "Why it matters" overstates the conclusion** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It suggests older adults with memory concerns don't need an expensive human coach to adopt assistive technology"
- **Abstract says:** "A self-guided, adaptive training platform with minimal clinician support may be sufficient to promote engagement with a digital memory aid."
- **Problem:** The abstract's conclusion is conditional ("may be sufficient") and based on a pilot; the digest converts it into a general statement of need.
- **Suggested fix:** ""It suggests self-guided training may be enough for many older adults to adopt these tools, though the pilot was too small to settle the question.""

### Study 11: When an urban hospital closes, older and uninsured residents bear the brunt
**PMID:** 42343058 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Date range describes search, not publication years** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers systematically reviewed 24 studies (17 quantitative, 7 qualitative) published between 1961 and 2024 on the effects of full hospital closures in U.S. urban areas."
- **Abstract says:** "Databases searched included MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science, CINAHL, EconLit, Business Source Complete, Cochrane Library, and HCUP publications, covering 1961-2024."
- **Problem:** 1961–2024 is the span of the database search, not a confirmed statement that the 24 included studies were published across that whole range.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "...24 studies (17 quantitative, 7 qualitative) identified in a search of literature from 1961 to 2024...""

**Issue B. Slightly firmer than the abstract on subgroup harms** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "but harms were consistently concentrated among elderly, uninsured, Medicaid-enrolled and minority residents"
- **Abstract says:** "Impacts were greatest among uninsured, Medicaid, elderly, and minority populations."
- **Problem:** "Consistently concentrated" implies uniformity across studies that the abstract does not claim; it states only that impacts were greatest in these groups.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "but impacts were greatest among elderly, uninsured, Medicaid-enrolled and minority residents.""

### Study 13: Exergaming didn't beat doing nothing for thinking skills in Parkinson's — but anxiety dropped and stayed down
**PMID:** 42576230 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Omits non-significant interaction effect for anxiety** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Anxiety, however, fell in the gaming group and remained lower 30 days later, with a significant difference between groups."
- **Abstract says:** "Although no significant interaction effect was observed (p = 0.126), a reduction in anxiety levels was verified up to 30 days post-training (p = 0.03), exclusively within the XBK group, accompanied by a significant between-group difference (p = 0.01), also in follow-up."
- **Problem:** The abstract's own qualifier — that the group-by-time interaction was not statistically significant — materially weakens the anxiety claim and is not mentioned anywhere in the item, including the Caveats.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add after the sentence: "though the overall group-by-time interaction was not statistically significant (p = 0.126), and the authors describe the anxiety effect only as a potential one.""

**Issue B. "Home-feasible" not supported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this hints that an enjoyable, home-feasible activity-based program may help mood even if it doesn't demonstrably sharpen cognition"
- **Abstract says:** "the experimental group underwent 10 sessions employing four XBK games (single-blind, supervised non-randomized trial)"
- **Problem:** The trial delivered supervised sessions; the abstract says nothing about home use or feasibility outside a supervised setting, or about enjoyment.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "this hints that a supervised activity-based gaming program may help mood...""

### Study 19: The supplement NAC is being studied for Parkinson's — but the human evidence is thinner than the headlines suggest
**PMID:** 40988585 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Merges route-specific NAC findings** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "human studies confirmed that intravenous and high-dose oral NAC raises brain glutathione and reaches the cerebrospinal fluid"
- **Abstract says:** "Intravenous NAC raised brain glutathione levels; high-dose oral NAC reached CSF."
- **Problem:** The abstract attributes the glutathione increase to intravenous NAC and CSF penetration to high-dose oral NAC; the digest credits both effects to both routes.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "intravenous NAC raised brain glutathione, and high-dose oral NAC reached the cerebrospinal fluid.""

**Issue B. UPDRS described as motor-specific** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "reported about a 13% improvement in UPDRS motor scores"
- **Abstract says:** "Two open-label trials (n = 65), reported ~ 13% improvement in UPDRS scores"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports UPDRS scores without specifying the motor subscale.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "about a 13% improvement in UPDRS scores.""
