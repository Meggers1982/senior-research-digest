### Study 2: Reading plays aloud together lifted quality of life and eased loneliness for nursing home residents with dementia
**PMID:** 42838128 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Population described as nursing home residents with dementia** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Reading plays aloud together lifted quality of life and eased loneliness for nursing home residents with dementia"
- **Abstract says:** "A randomized controlled trial with two parallel groups was conducted among older adults with mild-to-moderate neurocognitive disorders (MMSE < 25/30)."
- **Problem:** The abstract mentions nursing homes only as context and in clinical implications; it does not state that participants were nursing home residents, and "neurocognitive disorders" is broader than "dementia."
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "older adults living with mild-to-moderate neurocognitive disorders" and drop the nursing-home framing from the headline, or mark it as unverified."

**Issue B. Loneliness result at the significance boundary** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The intervention group showed significant improvements in quality of life, well-being, and loneliness compared with controls."
- **Abstract says:** "Significant improvements were observed in the intervention group compared to the control group for quality of life (p = .010), well-being (p = .004), and loneliness (p = .050)."
- **Problem:** The loneliness result sits exactly at the conventional p = .05 cutoff, making it far weaker evidence than the other two outcomes; presenting all three equally overstates it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the loneliness improvement was borderline (p = .050) while quality of life and well-being were more clearly significant."

**Issue C. Unstated resource detail** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the workshop requires scripts, a facilitator and an hour a week"
- **Abstract says:** "Participants were randomly assigned to a weekly Lulue® dramatized reading program delivered over three months or a waiting-list control group."
- **Problem:** Session length and staffing requirements are not reported in the abstract. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say simply "a weekly facilitated session over three months" without specifying duration or materials."

### Study 3: Today's middle-aged Americans are heading into old age sicker than the generation before them
**PMID:** 42837509 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Number of NHANES cohorts** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "compared it with six birth cohorts in NHANES"
- **Abstract says:** "Using data from Add Health and NHANES on 6 birth cohorts observed from adolescence to middle age"
- **Problem:** The six birth cohorts span both data sources; the abstract does not say there were six separate NHANES comparison cohorts in addition to Add Health.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "across six birth cohorts drawn from Add Health and NHANES.""

**Issue B. Omitted nuance in cohort comparison** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "they were in worse health than older cohorts were at the same age"
- **Abstract says:** "Compared to the NHANES cohorts, the Add Health cohort had worse health than older cohorts, generally worse health compared to the same birth cohort, with some exceptions, and mixed health compared to younger cohorts."
- **Problem:** The digest reports only the cleanest comparison and leaves out that results were mixed versus younger cohorts and had exceptions, which softens the generational-decline story.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that comparisons with younger cohorts were mixed and that there were exceptions."

**Issue C. Education gradient stability overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Gaps by education (four-year degree or not) were stable across age but have widened across successive generations."
- **Abstract says:** "Educational gradients were relatively stable across age, with some evidence of increases, while educational gradients generally increased across cohorts."
- **Problem:** "Stable" drops the authors' qualifier that there was some evidence of increases with age.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say gaps were "relatively stable across age, with some evidence of widening.""

### Study 6: A common diabetes pill may give older adults a small cognitive edge, pooled studies suggest
**PMID:** 42841129 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Most widely prescribed" overstatement** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "metformin — the most widely prescribed drug for type 2 diabetes"
- **Abstract says:** "Metformin is a widely prescribed drug for managing diabetes mellitus."
- **Problem:** The abstract says "a widely prescribed drug," not the most widely prescribed; the superlative is not supported by the source.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "a widely prescribed drug for type 2 diabetes.""

**Issue B. Reference to earlier harm signals** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the drug may carry a modest bonus for memory and thinking rather than the harm some earlier reports hinted at"
- **Abstract says:** "The findings indicate that metformin has a small but positive effect on overall cognitive function in older adults."
- **Problem:** The abstract makes no mention of prior reports of cognitive harm from metformin. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the comparison to earlier harm reports or attribute it to a separate source."

### Study 8: Depression in later life isn't one story — and education may be the strongest shield
**PMID:** 42839307 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Strongest" vs "most consistent" protective factor** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "education may be the strongest shield"
- **Abstract says:** "Higher educational attainment was the most consistent protective factor."
- **Problem:** "Most consistent" means it appeared reliably across studies, not that it had the largest protective effect; the abstract reports no effect-size ranking.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change the headline to "education is the most consistent protective factor.""

**Issue B. Variation by country, sex and poverty** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the review's finding that outcomes varied enormously by country, sex, and poverty status"
- **Abstract says:** "Twenty-one studies (median N = 3428; 26 observations after accounting for within-study stratification by sex, poverty status, and country) met inclusion criteria."
- **Problem:** The abstract mentions sex, poverty and country only as the bases on which some studies were stratified, not as factors shown to drive enormous variation in outcomes.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say some studies stratified results by sex, poverty status and country, and that trajectory prevalence varied widely across the included studies overall."

### Study 9: RSV shots cut hospitalizations in older adults — and the people at highest risk may benefit most
**PMID:** 42838078 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies greater benefit in immunosuppressed** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "RSV shots cut hospitalizations in older adults — and the people at highest risk may benefit most"
- **Abstract says:** "vaccine effectiveness 60·5% [95% CI −141·4 to 96·2] among those with immunosuppression and 92·3% [48·7 to 99·8] among those without immunosuppression; p for interaction=0·22... The point estimate was numerically lower among participants with immunosuppression"
- **Problem:** Relative effectiveness was numerically lower, not higher, in the immunosuppressed group; only the absolute rate reduction (itself non-significant, CI −2·00 to 7·66) was larger. The headline risks suggesting the vaccine worked better in this group.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "and those at highest baseline risk stand to gain the most in absolute terms, though the estimate is imprecise.""

**Issue B. Design described without noting subgroup analysis** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "In a nationwide Danish pragmatic randomized trial, 131,276 adults aged 60 and older were assigned to either a single dose of the bivalent RSV prefusion F vaccine (RSVpreF) or no vaccine"
- **Abstract says:** "This study was a prespecified subgroup analysis of a pragmatic, open-label, nationwide randomised clinical trial conducted in Denmark during November-December, 2024."
- **Problem:** The paper is a prespecified subgroup analysis of the DAN-RSV trial, not the primary trial report; readers should know the immunosuppression comparison is a subgroup analysis.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "in a prespecified subgroup analysis of the DAN-RSV trial.""

**Issue C. Overall effectiveness precision not given** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Overall vaccine effectiveness against RSV-related respiratory hospitalization was 83.3%."
- **Abstract says:** "vaccine effectiveness 83·3% [95% CI 42·9 to 96·9]"
- **Problem:** The confidence interval is very wide (based on 21 events total); quoting the point estimate alone conveys more precision than the data support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the 95% CI (42.9 to 96.9) alongside the point estimate."

### Study 10: Biological 'aging clocks' predict who lives to 90 — but not who stays sharp
**PMID:** 42679383 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported superlative** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "in the largest study of its kind, these clocks tracked survival to 90"
- **Abstract says:** "We examined 5844 women from the Women's Health Initiative Memory Study."
- **Problem:** The abstract makes no claim that this is the largest study of its kind. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Replace with "in this large study of nearly 5,900 women.""

**Issue B. "No signal at all" overstates the null finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "gave no signal at all about whether those years would be cognitively healthy ones"
- **Abstract says:** "None of the clocks was more strongly associated with survival to age 90 with preserved cognition than with survival to age 90 with cognitive impairment, relative to death before age 90."
- **Problem:** The clocks were associated with both longevity outcomes; what they failed to do was discriminate between them. "No signal at all" misstates the nature of the null result.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the clocks could not distinguish surviving to 90 with preserved cognition from surviving to 90 with cognitive impairment."

**Issue C. Dementia added to outcome description** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "surviving to 90 with dementia or cognitive impairment"
- **Abstract says:** "survival to age 90 with cognitive impairment (n = 956, 16.4%)"
- **Problem:** The abstract defines the outcome as cognitive impairment; "dementia" is an added specification not stated there.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "cognitive impairment" to match the reported outcome."
