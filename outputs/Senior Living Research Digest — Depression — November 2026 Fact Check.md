### Study 1: Being offline may hurt older adults' mood as much as being alone hurts their sense of connection
**PMID:** 42971795 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline comparative claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Being offline may hurt older adults' mood as much as being alone hurts their sense of connection"
- **Abstract says:** "social isolation was more strongly associated with loneliness (ELSA: HR = 2.36, 95% CI: 1.81-3.09; CHARLS: HR = 1.57, 95% CI: 1.21-2.05), whereas internet isolation showed a stronger association with depression (ELSA: HR = 1.29, 95% CI: 1.05-1.58; CHARLS: HR = 1.57, 95% CI: 1.11-2.23)"
- **Problem:** The "as much as" equivalence only holds in CHARLS (1.57 vs 1.57); in ELSA the internet-isolation/depression link (1.29) is far weaker than the social-isolation/loneliness link (2.36). The study also only compared each exposure with each outcome, not the relative size of the two effects.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like "Being offline is tied to depression, while being socially isolated is tied to loneliness" without asserting equal magnitude."

**Issue B. Cross-cultural consistency overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "People experiencing both kinds of isolation had the highest risk of both outcomes, a pattern that held across two very different cultures."
- **Abstract says:** "Participants experiencing both forms of isolation had the highest risks of depression and loneliness, particularly in the ELSA cohort."
- **Problem:** The abstract qualifies the joint-exposure finding as most pronounced in ELSA; the digest presents it as equally evident in both cohorts.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the qualifier: "...a pattern seen in both cohorts but strongest in the English sample.""

### Study 3: A year of home-delivered produce improved diet, loneliness, and blood pressure in low-income adults with chronic illness
**PMID:** 41979725 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for non-randomized study** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "A year of home-delivered produce improved diet, loneliness, and blood pressure in low-income adults with chronic illness"
- **Abstract says:** "DESIGN: Prospective, non-randomized cohort study with a matched comparison group... In this study, PRx was associated with improved diet quality and loneliness compared with a SNAP-only comparison."
- **Problem:** The abstract consistently uses "was associated with" and calls for randomized evaluation; the headline asserts the program improved outcomes. The blood pressure drop also comes from an uncontrolled within-group comparison.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "linked to better diet, less loneliness, and lower blood pressure" and note the BP result is within-group only."

**Issue B. "Mood-related benefits" overstates depression result** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this suggests they may deliver mood- and energy-related benefits alongside nutritional ones"
- **Abstract says:** "Between-group differences for FI (-8.2 percentage points, p = 0.26) and moderate-to-severe depression (-10.8 percentage points, p = 0.07) favored PRx but were not statistically significant."
- **Problem:** The only significant psychosocial between-group finding was loneliness; depression did not reach significance, so "mood" benefits are not demonstrated.
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify "loneliness- and fatigue-related benefits" rather than "mood.""

### Study 5: Depression Plus Frailty: A Combination That Lands Older Adults in the Hospital
**PMID:** 42849853 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Depression Plus Frailty: A Combination That Lands Older Adults in the Hospital"
- **Abstract says:** "Depressive symptoms were associated with lower ambulatory care regardless of frailty but with higher hospitalization primarily in frail individuals"
- **Problem:** "Lands older adults in the hospital" asserts causation from an observational person-interval cohort; the body text and caveats are appropriately hedged, so the headline is out of step.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "Depression Plus Frailty: A Combination Linked to More Hospital Admissions.""

**Issue B. "Emergency" hospitalization not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "pushing the frailest toward emergency hospitalization"
- **Abstract says:** "Effect modification on outpatient visits, inpatient admission, and mortality (long-term care hospital [LTCH] admission, exploratory) was tested"
- **Problem:** The outcome was inpatient admission generally; the abstract does not distinguish emergency from planned admissions.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "toward hospital admission" instead of "emergency hospitalization.""

### Study 8: After a Stroke, the Type of Exercise May Matter for Mood and Memory
**PMID:** 42711194 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. China claim unverifiable** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "many trials were conducted in China and may not generalize"
- **Abstract says:** "We systematically searched five international and two Chinese databases from inception to July 2025"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports searching Chinese databases but does not state how many included trials were conducted in China. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the claim or rephrase as "the search included Chinese-language databases; trial locations are not specified in the abstract.""

**Issue B. Cognition findings not reported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "For depression, dual-task training, mind-body exercise ... and balance/coordination training ranked highest and showed credible benefit over usual care. For anxiety, resistance training and dual-task training showed credible benefit."
- **Abstract says:** "For cognitive function, MBE, general aerobic exercise (GA), and moderate-intensity continuous training (MICT) ranked highest, whereas BCT was the only intervention showing a statistically credible benefit versus usual care."
- **Problem:** The headline promises findings for "Memory," but the body gives no cognition results — in particular that only balance/coordination training showed credible cognitive benefit.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add one sentence on the cognition network: only balance/coordination training showed credible benefit versus usual care."

### Study 10: Depression and Loneliness — But Not Living Alone — Linked to Visual Hallucinations in Dementia
**PMID:** 42996468 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Untreated" low mood** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the subjective feeling of loneliness and untreated low mood may travel with distressing visual hallucinations"
- **Abstract says:** "Depression and loneliness were associated with VHs among patients with diagnoses of AD and/or Lewy body dementia, but not in those with neither diagnosis."
- **Problem:** The abstract says nothing about whether participants' depression was treated or untreated, and nothing about the hallucinations being distressing.
- **Suggested fix:** "Remove "untreated" and "distressing": "loneliness and depressive symptoms may travel with visual hallucinations.""

### Study 11: Antidepressant Trial in Alzheimer's Hints at Relief for Low Mood — and Possibly Fewer Hallucinations
**PMID:** 42697812 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

**Issue A. Inferred placebo group size** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "versus 5 of about 70 (7%) on placebo"
- **Abstract says:** "no participants (0%) in the escitalopram group reported new hallucinations versus 5 participants (7%) in the placebo group"
- **Problem:** The abstract gives only the count and percentage; the placebo arm size of "about 70" is back-calculated and not stated. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "versus 5 participants (7%) on placebo" without supplying a denominator."

### Study 12: Phone and Tablet-Based Mental Health Programs Produce Small but Real Gains for Rural Seniors
**PMID:** 42976720 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Delivery modality not specified in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Phone and Tablet-Based Mental Health Programs Produce Small but Real Gains for Rural Seniors"
- **Abstract says:** "randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and quasi-experimental studies evaluating the impact of digital health interventions on mental health outcomes among rural older adults (aged ≥60 years)"
- **Problem:** The abstract refers only to "digital health interventions" and never identifies phones, tablets, or remote delivery as the modalities pooled.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change the headline to "Digital Mental Health Programs Produce Small but Real Gains for Rural Seniors" and drop "delivered remotely" from the study description."

**Issue B. Unsupported novelty claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is the first pooled evidence suggesting remote digital programs can move the needle on depression and anxiety in exactly that population"
- **Abstract says:** "Digital health interventions offer a promising approach to bridge this gap; yet, evidence in this population remains limited."
- **Problem:** The abstract says evidence is limited but makes no priority claim; "first pooled evidence" cannot be verified from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Replace with "this adds pooled evidence that digital programs can modestly improve depression and anxiety in this population.""

### Study 17: Depression Often Precedes Parkinson's — But It Doesn't Predict a Faster-Progressing Disease
**PMID:** 42766893 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Null result stated more firmly than abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "pre-diagnosis depression rates did not differ according to how much dopaminergic medication patients needed afterward or how quickly their doses escalated"
- **Abstract says:** "no consistent differences in pre-diagnostic depression rates were observed across treatment intensity groups or LEDD escalation trajectories"
- **Problem:** "Did not differ" asserts a flat null, whereas the authors report no *consistent* differences, leaving room for variation across groups.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "showed no consistent differences according to how much dopaminergic medication patients needed afterward.""

### Study 20: Brain stimulation improved Parkinson's quality of life — yet patients felt less hopeful about the future
**PMID:** 42731544 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Single-center claim unsupported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Prospective but uncontrolled pre-post design with no comparison group, single-center, moderate sample (N=64)"
- **Abstract says:** "This prospective pre-post study enrolled 70 patients with PD, of whom 64 underwent STN-DBS and were analyzed. … Lazio DBS Study Group [collaborators]"
- **Problem:** The abstract never states the study was single-center, and the collaborating Lazio DBS Study Group suggests multiple sites. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "single-center" or replace with "conducted in Italian specialist centers.""

**Issue B. Causal verb for uncontrolled design** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It shows that a technically successful procedure can leave real psychological aftereffects"
- **Abstract says:** "The findings suggest that although several quality-of-life dimensions in PD patients improve after STN-DBS, hopelessness related to future feelings and psychological well-being related to autonomy and personal growth worsen."
- **Problem:** "It shows" asserts more certainty than an uncontrolled pre-post study supports; the authors use "suggest."
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "It suggests that a technically successful procedure can be accompanied by psychological aftereffects.""

### Study 21: Treadmill walking eased both back pain and low mood in a chronic pain trial
**PMID:** 42892150 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Gait speed outcome described imprecisely** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Both treadmill groups beat the control group on Timed Up and Go, walking speed, pain intensity, and depressive symptoms."
- **Abstract says:** "Both treadmill groups showed greater improvements than controls in TUG performance, self-selected gait speed, pain intensity, and depressive symptoms... VT + TE also improved fast gait speed more than both comparison groups at post-intervention."
- **Problem:** The abstract specifies that both treadmill groups beat controls on self-selected gait speed only; fast gait speed advantage belonged to the vertical-traction group alone. The unqualified phrase "walking speed" slightly broadens the finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "self-selected walking speed" (and, if desired, note that only the traction group improved fast walking speed more than both comparators)."
