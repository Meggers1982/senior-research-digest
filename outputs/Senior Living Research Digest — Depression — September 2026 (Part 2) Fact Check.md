### Study 8: Adding Palliative Care to Cancer Clinics Eased Pain, Anxiety and Depression Over a Year
**PMID:** 42444638 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for uncontrolled cohort** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Adding Palliative Care to Cancer Clinics Eased Pain, Anxiety and Depression Over a Year"
- **Abstract says:** "Patients managed at a dedicated palliative care outpatient clinic showed improvements in symptom control and quality of life over time, which may reflect the contribution of integrated palliative care in the ambulatory setting."
- **Problem:** 'Eased' asserts that the clinic caused the improvement, while this was a prospective cohort with no comparison group; the authors hedge with 'may reflect the contribution'. The digest's own caveat acknowledges this, so the headline sits in tension with the body.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'Pain, Anxiety and Depression Improved Over a Year in a Cancer Clinic With Embedded Palliative Care'."

### Study 9: When Services Are Easy to Reach, Loneliness Hurts Less
**PMID:** 42789932 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Protective/causal framing of cross-sectional associations** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "practical, reachable services — not just the existence of clubs and programs on paper — are what actually protect older adults' mood, and that accessible neighborhoods can soften the emotional damage of loneliness"
- **Abstract says:** "Service accessibility was negatively associated with loneliness and depression and moderated their association... the interaction between loneliness and service accessibility was significant (β = -0.083, p < .05), indicating a buffering effect."
- **Problem:** 'Protect' and 'soften the emotional damage' imply a causal, directional effect; the design is a cross-sectional survey that can only show association and statistical moderation, as the digest's caveat notes.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as 'older adults who could reach services easily reported less loneliness and depression, and the link between the two was weaker for them'."

### Study 10: Older Cannabis Users: Smoking and Edibles Tied to Problem Use, Not to Depression
**PMID:** 42799588 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported 'first' claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this offers the first large national picture of which delivery methods carry the most risk of problem use"
- **Abstract says:** "This study estimates the prevalence of different modes of cannabis use in this population and examines their associations with mental health outcomes."
- **Problem:** The abstract makes no priority claim; 'the first large national picture' cannot be verified from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop 'first' or write 'offers a large national picture'."

**Issue B. Prevalence ratio described as a rate** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "smoking (about 4 times) and ingested forms (about 1.5 times) were associated with higher rates of cannabis use disorder"
- **Abstract says:** "smoking (adjusted prevalence ratio [aPR] = 3.99; 95% CI: 1.92-8.28) and ingested forms (aPR = 1.46; 95% CI: 1.03-2.09) were associated with increased prevalence of CUD"
- **Problem:** These are adjusted prevalence ratios from a cross-sectional survey, not incidence rates; 'higher rates' can read as a risk over time.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'higher prevalence of cannabis use disorder' and note the wide confidence interval for smoking (1.92–8.28)."

### Study 12: Doctors Who Show Empathy Linked to Less Depression in Chronic Pain Patients a Year Later
**PMID:** 42741899 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Sleep benefit overstated in 'Why it matters'** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "feeling heard by a clinician tracks with meaningfully better mood and sleep over a full year"
- **Abstract says:** "The mean difference (95% CI) for depression was -4.7 (-7.3 to -2.1), which surpassed the threshold for a medium effect size. Other differences represented small effects."
- **Problem:** Only the depression difference reached a medium effect; anxiety, fatigue and sleep differences were small, so describing sleep as 'meaningfully better' inflates a small effect.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'meaningfully better mood, with smaller gains in anxiety, fatigue and sleep.'"

**Issue B. Registry described as 'national'** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers followed 226 adults with fibromyalgia (average age 53, 90% female) in a national pain registry for 12 months"
- **Abstract says:** "A retrospective cohort study was conducted within the Pain Registry for Epidemiological, Clinical, and Interventional Studies and Innovation."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not state that the registry is national. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Call it 'a US pain research registry' or simply 'a pain registry.'"

### Study 13: Nearly 1 in 20 Americans Over 50 Had Major Depression Last Year — and a Third Got No Treatment At All
**PMID:** 42624700 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Bivariable comparison presented as adjusted finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "adults 65 and older were significantly less likely than those aged 50-64 to receive outpatient care (38.7% vs. 50.1%)"
- **Abstract says:** "In bivariable comparisons, adults aged ≥65 were less likely than those aged 50-64 to receive outpatient mental health services (38.7% versus 50.1%, p = 0.032)"
- **Problem:** The abstract explicitly labels this an unadjusted bivariable comparison; the digest presents it without that qualifier.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add 'in unadjusted comparisons' before the 38.7% vs 50.1% figures."

**Issue B. Barrier denominator broadened** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "financial and access barriers affected roughly half to three-quarters of untreated respondents"
- **Abstract says:** "Among untreated individuals with perceived unmet need, barriers to treatment were attitudinal (86.2%...), access-related (73.1%), and financial (48.6%)."
- **Problem:** Those percentages apply only to untreated people who perceived an unmet need, a subgroup — not all untreated respondents. The digest body states this correctly, but the story angle drops the qualifier.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write 'untreated respondents who recognised they needed help.'"

### Study 14: Depression Doubled the Odds of Probable Dementia in a National Survey of Older Americans — and the Burden Was Far From Equal
**PMID:** 42731172 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Hearing aid finding in Hispanic adults understated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "hearing aid use did not appear protective in that group"
- **Abstract says:** "Among hearing aid users, NH White and NH Black adults showed lower of Prb-D probability than non-users, whereas Hispanic users showed a higher probability."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a higher probable-dementia probability among Hispanic hearing aid users than non-users, not merely an absent protective effect; the digest softens the direction of the result.
- **Suggested fix:** "State that Hispanic hearing aid users showed a higher probability of probable dementia than non-users, while noting this is cross-sectional and may reflect reverse causation."

### Study 16: Telephone Palliative Care Was No Better — or Worse — for Caregivers Than In-Person Specialty Care
**PMID:** 42749467 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Within-arm improvement claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "neither arm improved strain or bereavement"
- **Abstract says:** "The analysis did not reveal any differences in caregiver strain, QOL, and bereavement between the two care models... The study did not demonstrate that nurse-led telephonic care improved caregiver strain, QOL or bereavement outcomes compared with traditional outpatient palliative care."

- **Suggested fix:** "Say the trial found no difference between the two models, and that within-group change was not reported in the abstract."

### Study 20: In Older Adults With Rheumatoid Arthritis, Fatigue Drives the Symptom Cascade — and Mood Absorbs the Damage
**PMID:** 42775296 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal verb in headline for observational network analysis** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Fatigue Drives the Symptom Cascade — and Mood Absorbs the Damage"
- **Abstract says:** "fatigue emerges as the primary longitudinal predictor of subsequent sleep disturbance and functional limitation... These findings may inform future symptom management strategies and generate hypotheses for interventional research."
- **Problem:** 'Drives' implies causation from a two-wave cross-lagged panel network that the authors themselves frame as hypothesis-generating.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'Fatigue Comes First in the Symptom Cascade' or 'Fatigue Predicts Later Sleep and Function Problems.'"

### Study 21: What Protects Black Dementia Caregivers — and What Wears Them Down
**PMID:** 42791221 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Discrimination setting overspecified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "experiences of discrimination in healthcare settings were tied to higher anxiety"
- **Abstract says:** "Daily discrimination endorsement was associated with higher anxiety symptoms."
- **Problem:** The measure was everyday/daily discrimination generally, not discrimination in healthcare settings. The abstract only mentions healthcare navigation as a speculative explanation in the conclusion, not as the setting measured.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'experiences of everyday discrimination were tied to higher anxiety — which the authors attribute in part to the strain of navigating the healthcare system.'"

**Issue B. Implied change over time from placement** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "caregivers reported real relief in mood symptoms once a loved one moved into a care setting"
- **Abstract says:** "Post-hoc analyses showed that individuals with care recipients in external settings reported lower anxiety and depression symptoms and non-death interpersonal loss than those co-inhabiting."
- **Problem:** This was a post-hoc between-group comparison in a cross-sectional survey, not a before/after measurement; 'once a loved one moved' implies within-person change the design cannot show. The Caveats do flag the direction problem, but the body text phrasing still implies a temporal effect.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to 'caregivers whose loved one was in an external care setting reported lower anxiety and depression symptoms than those living with the care recipient.'"

**Issue C. Omitted finding on caregiver identity** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Caregivers who reported stronger spiritual beliefs perceived less impairment in their own thinking and mood, while those reporting more everyday discrimination had higher anxiety."
- **Abstract says:** "Greater caregiver identity centrality was associated with higher anxiety symptoms. Higher acquired non-death interpersonal loss was linked to greater perceived impairment in cognitive/emotional functioning..."
- **Problem:** The digest omits one of the abstract's headline findings (caregiver identity centrality linked to higher anxiety), which is relevant to the 'what wears them down' framing in the headline.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a clause noting that caregivers for whom the caregiving role had become more central to their identity also reported higher anxiety."
