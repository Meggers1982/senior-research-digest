### Study 1: Flu's hidden toll on older adults: heart attacks and strokes, not just coughs
**PMID:** 42976901 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Specific events and timing not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the most dangerous consequences may be heart attacks, strokes and other cardiovascular events triggered in the weeks after infection"
- **Abstract says:** "Cardiovascular events were particularly notable, contributing to more than 50 deaths and 60 hospitalisations per 100 000 older adults."
- **Problem:** The abstract refers generically to "cardiovascular events" and gives no post-infection time window; naming heart attacks and strokes and specifying "the weeks after infection" adds detail the abstract does not supply.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "cardiovascular events such as heart problems and strokes" and drop or soften the "weeks after infection" timing, e.g. "around and after an influenza infection.""

### Study 3: Singing may do what pills can't for Parkinson's voice, balance and mood
**PMID:** 42975677 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Balance" not among reported improvements** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Singing may do what pills can't for Parkinson's voice, balance and mood"
- **Abstract says:** "Singing-based interventions were associated with improvements in motor symptoms, gait, posture, voice quality, respiratory control, and maximum phonation time."
- **Problem:** Balance appears in the abstract only as an example of persistent impairment in Parkinson's, not as an outcome that improved; the reported motor outcomes were gait and posture.
- **Suggested fix:** "Replace "balance" with "gait and posture" in the headline."

### Study 4: Digital mental health tools help rural seniors — modestly, and not for loneliness
**PMID:** 42976720 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Overstated evidential status** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is the clearest evidence yet that remote digital programs can help with depression and anxiety"
- **Abstract says:** "Digital health interventions offer a promising approach to bridge this gap; yet, evidence in this population remains limited... Digital health interventions demonstrated small but statistically detectable improvements in depressive and anxiety symptoms among rural older adults."
- **Problem:** The authors describe the evidence base as limited and the improvements as small and "statistically detectable"; "clearest evidence yet" is a superlative the abstract does not support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "adds pooled evidence that remote digital programs can modestly reduce depression and anxiety symptoms.""

**Issue B. Null result framed as demonstrated absence of effect** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "but don't expect it to solve loneliness, which these programs failed to shift"
- **Abstract says:** "No significant effects were observed regarding quality of life, loneliness, or cognitive function."
- **Problem:** With only 11 small studies, a non-significant pooled result is absence of evidence rather than evidence that the programs cannot help loneliness.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "no measurable benefit for loneliness was detected in these studies, though the evidence base is small.""

### Study 5: Autopsies of 7,300 brains find Lewy body pathology in more than one in four older adults — and men and women carry it in different places
**PMID:** 41885166 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Odds ratio read as near-universal co-occurrence** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "amygdala-predominant pathology was more common in women and almost always accompanied Alzheimer's changes"
- **Abstract says:** "amygdala-predominant LBP was associated with increased odds of comorbid ADNC [OR = 12.7 (95% CI 5.30-30.6)]"
- **Problem:** An odds ratio of 12.7 describes strongly elevated odds, not a proportion; the abstract gives no figure showing that Alzheimer's change was present in nearly every case.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "was far more likely to occur alongside Alzheimer's changes (odds ratio 12.7).""

**Issue B. Causal and prevalence claim about mixed pathology** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "dementia in late life is usually caused by several overlapping pathologies rather than a single disease"
- **Abstract says:** "Neocortical LBP was associated with dementia... and with increased odds of ADNC... and LATE-NC"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports associations between co-pathologies, not that mixed pathology causes most late-life dementia; "usually caused by" is both causal and a prevalence claim not quantified here.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "late-life dementia frequently occurs alongside more than one underlying brain pathology.""

### Study 6: For age-related muscle loss, exercise plus nutrition beats either alone — but exercise is what gets you moving better
**PMID:** 42980314 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Ranking presented as head-to-head superiority** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Combined exercise and nutrition produced the largest gain in handgrip strength (mean difference 4.29 kg versus control) and the biggest increase in muscle mass index."
- **Abstract says:** "Compared with Control, Combined, Nutrition, and Exercise improved handgrip strength, with the largest estimate for Combined... Combined exercise and nutrition ranked more favorably for selected muscle-related outcomes"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports network rankings and point estimates versus control, not a demonstrated statistical advantage of combined over each single intervention; the headline's "beats either alone" is stronger than "ranked more favorably."
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "ranked highest" or "had the largest estimated effect" rather than "beats either alone.""

**Issue B. Intervention specifics not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "pair resistance-style activity with adequate protein and nutrition for strength and muscle"
- **Abstract says:** "A frequentist network meta-analysis compared four intervention nodes (Control, Exercise, Nutrition, and Combined)."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not specify resistance training or protein supplementation as the exercise and nutrition components analyzed.
- **Suggested fix:** "Refer generically to "structured exercise" and "nutritional support" unless the full text specifies resistance training and protein."

### Study 7: Why older Americans eat the way they do: a review of 40 studies finds food choices are rarely just about nutrition knowledge
**PMID:** 42977035 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Theme examples added by the digest** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "environmental influences such as access and transportation, knowledge and skills, economic constraints, and physiological changes including appetite, chewing and taste"
- **Abstract says:** "six interconnected themes illustrating how older adults' food and nutrition experiences reflect social and cultural contexts, behavioral habits, environmental influences, knowledge and skills, economic constraints, and physiological factors"
- **Problem:** The abstract names the themes but not the specific examples (transportation, appetite, chewing, taste); these are plausible but unverifiable from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Flag the examples as illustrative or drop them, e.g. "environmental influences and physiological factors.""

### Study 8: Psilocybin therapy eased dread and depression in cancer patients for months — but the evidence base is still just six cohorts
**PMID:** 42975895 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "All" criteria added to MMAT statement** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "only 5 of 11 reports met all Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool quality criteria"
- **Abstract says:** "Five of the 11 reports met Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool (MMAT) criteria; concerns in others involved assessor blinding, representativeness, confounding control, or mixed-methods integration."
- **Problem:** The abstract says five reports met MMAT criteria; the digest adds "all," which asserts a stricter threshold than stated.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "all": "only 5 of 11 reports met Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool criteria.""

### Study 9: Nearly half of early Parkinson's diagnoses are wrong at the first visit, autopsy data show
**PMID:** 42759118 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Sensitivity misread as proportion of wrong diagnoses** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Nearly half of early Parkinson's diagnoses are wrong at the first visit, autopsy data show"
- **Abstract says:** "At the initial assessment, pooled sensitivity was 57% and specificity was 76%, with substantial heterogeneity."
- **Problem:** Sensitivity of 57% means roughly 43% of autopsy-confirmed Parkinson's cases were missed at first assessment, not that 43% of people given the diagnosis do not have it. The abstract provides no positive predictive value.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "At the first visit, doctors catch only about 57% of true Parkinson's cases, autopsy data show.""

**Issue B. Specific test recommended beyond the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Ask whether follow-up reassessment or an objective test such as a dopamine transporter scan is appropriate in your case."
- **Abstract says:** "Integrating objective biomarkers is essential to improve diagnostic reliability and support patient selection for early-intervention trials."
- **Problem:** The abstract calls generally for objective biomarkers; it does not evaluate or endorse dopamine transporter imaging.
- **Suggested fix:** "Refer to "objective biomarker testing" without naming a specific scan, or note that the review does not assess specific tests."

**Issue C. Superlative framing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is the clearest quantification yet that an early diagnosis is a working hypothesis rather than a settled fact"
- **Abstract says:** "these findings are based on a limited number of studies and require further clinicopathological validation"
- **Problem:** The authors stress the limited and heterogeneous evidence base, which sits awkwardly with "clearest quantification yet."
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "this pooled analysis quantifies how provisional an early diagnosis can be.""

### Study 10: Experimental Parkinson's pill tavapadon improves movement — but with more side effects
**PMID:** 42822261 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Class-specific adverse effects not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "structured watching for adverse effects, including impulse-control and blood-pressure issues associated with this drug class"
- **Abstract says:** "Tavapadon increased the risk of TRAEs (RR 1.96; 95% CI 1.19 to 3.25; p = 0.0236) with substantial heterogeneity."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports only an overall increase in treatment-related adverse events; it names no specific adverse effects such as impulse-control disorders or blood-pressure changes.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the abstract does not break down which adverse events occurred, or attribute the impulse-control/blood-pressure point to general dopamine agonist knowledge rather than this study."

### Study 11: Diet counseling improved constipation in Parkinson's patients — but so did simply being watched
**PMID:** 42815344 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Trial duration described as three months** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "three-month intervention with limited follow-up"
- **Abstract says:** "Participants were randomly allocated (1:1 ratio) to two groups: Immediate (intervention from baseline to the third month) and Waitlist (intervention from the third to the sixth month) nutritional counseling, with an initial in-person assessment and monthly remote assessments."
- **Problem:** The intervention itself lasted three months per participant, but the study ran over six months with monthly remote assessments; the caveat may leave readers with an incomplete picture of the design.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the trial ran six months overall (immediate group months 0-3, waitlist group months 3-6), with results reported after the first three months."

**Issue B. "Being watched" mechanism is an inference** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "regular monitoring and attention appear to move the needle as much as a structured diet program did"
- **Abstract says:** "Although bowel habits and dietary adherence improved throughout the study, the absence of between-group differences limits causal attribution to the counseling."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports only that both groups improved without a between-group difference; it does not test or attribute the improvement to monitoring/attention. Regression to the mean, natural variation or spillover of dietary advice are equally consistent.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say that both groups improved and the trial cannot tell us why — monitoring, expectation effects or regression to the mean are all possible explanations."

### Study 13: Brain stimulation surgery works best for tremor — and the evidence now supports wider use in some psychiatric conditions
**PMID:** 42550465 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Strongest trial evidence in all of neurology" overreach** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "this analysis suggests deep brain stimulation has some of the strongest trial evidence in all of neurology"
- **Abstract says:** "Effect sizes in clinical trials were highest in ET in neurology and in GTS in psychiatry."
- **Problem:** The meta-analysis compared DBS across its own indications only; it did not benchmark DBS against other neurological treatments. The claim goes well beyond the data and could nudge readers toward surgery.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as "within this analysis, essential tremor showed the largest effect of any DBS indication in neurology"."

### Study 16: The share of frail older people in England barely moved in 20 years — but the number of them jumped by 1.2 million
**PMID:** 42976199 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Population aging named as the offset driver** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "healthier behaviors actually pushed prevalence down and offset much of what population aging would otherwise have added"
- **Abstract says:** "Opposing contributions netted to this overall change: improvements in sociodemographic conditions and educational attainment and healthier lifestyle behaviors offset 222.5%, 94.9%, and 51.6% of the observed increase, respectively."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes offsets against the observed increase in prevalence; it does not identify population aging as the opposing force, and the offsets together far exceed 100% rather than "much of" it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the decomposition found rising education, better sociodemographic conditions and healthier behaviors each offset a large share of the observed increase in prevalence."

### Study 17: Why mRNA vaccines may work less well if you're frail — and what researchers want to do about it
**PMID:** 42976900 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Rockwood described as senior author** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Senior author Kenneth Rockwood licenses the Clinical Frailty Scale commercially to numerous pharmaceutical and biotech firms, including Pfizer, and co-founded Ardea Outcomes."
- **Abstract says:** "Vijayan VV(1), Nair PG(1), Sheela UB(1), Cullis P(2), Howlett SE(3), Rockwood K(4), Gujar S(5). … Electronic address: shashi.gujar@dal.ca"
- **Problem:** Rockwood is the fourth of seven authors; Gujar is the last and corresponding (senior) author. The licensing is also held through Dalhousie University rather than by Rockwood personally.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Co-author Kenneth Rockwood, through Dalhousie University, licenses the Clinical Frailty Scale to pharmaceutical and biotech firms including Pfizer, and co-founded Ardea Outcomes.""

**Issue B. "Often" firms up a hedged claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "frail older adults often mount weaker and less durable antibody and cellular responses than their non-frail peers"
- **Abstract says:** "Evidence … suggests that people living with frailty can mount weaker or less durable responses than non-frail peers, although findings remain heterogeneous and frailty-specific evidence is scarce."
- **Problem:** The abstract says "can mount weaker or less durable" responses; "often … weaker and less durable" implies a more consistent and broader effect than the authors claim, and the abstract does not break the finding down into antibody versus cellular responses.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "can mount weaker or less durable responses" and drop the antibody/cellular specification."

### Study 18: Childhood poverty still shows up in the body decades later, large Chinese study finds
**PMID:** 42974810 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Participant age range not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the measurable physiological health of people in their 60s, 70s and beyond tracks with conditions they experienced as children"
- **Abstract says:** "To examine the association between early-life circumstances and healthy aging among community-dwelling older Chinese adults. … Of 3,069 participants, 4.82% had the healthiest CHAI score (0-2)."
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no age range or mean age; CHARLS enrolls adults from middle age onward, so the specific "60s, 70s and beyond" framing is unverifiable from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "community-dwelling older Chinese adults" rather than specifying decades of age."

**Issue B. "Large" study in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Childhood poverty still shows up in the body decades later, large Chinese study finds"
- **Abstract says:** "Of 3,069 participants, 4.82% had the healthiest CHAI score (0-2)."
- **Problem:** 3,069 participants is a moderate analytic sample for a national cohort analysis; "large" slightly inflates the study's weight, and "shows up in the body" edges toward causal phrasing for a cross-sectional association based on retrospective recall.
- **Suggested fix:** "Consider "Childhood hardship is linked to poorer physiological health in later life, Chinese cohort study finds"."
