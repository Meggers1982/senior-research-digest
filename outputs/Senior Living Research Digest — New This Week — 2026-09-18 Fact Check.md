### Study 1: Big trial finds carotid surgery doesn't rescue thinking skills in people with symptom-free artery narrowing
**PMID:** 42747834 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline broader than the analysis** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Big trial finds carotid surgery doesn't rescue thinking skills in people with symptom-free artery narrowing"
- **Abstract says:** "The primary analysis tested for an interaction between treatment group (revascularization vs IMM alone) and degree of baseline hemodynamic impairment among those with reduced baseline cognition (cognitive z score ≤-1.0 SD)."
- **Problem:** The headline implies a null cognitive effect across all people with asymptomatic stenosis, while the reported primary result applies to the 51 participants with reduced baseline cognition and the hemodynamic-impairment interaction. The body text does confine it correctly, but the headline and the first story angle read more broadly.
- **Suggested fix:** "Narrow to the tested group, e.g. "...doesn't rescue thinking skills in symptom-free artery narrowing, even in those with reduced cognition and poor brain blood flow"."

**Issue B. Unverifiable superlative** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is the strongest randomized evidence to date that the cognitive rationale doesn't hold up"
- **Abstract says:** "In this substudy of a randomized clinical trial, there was no differential effect of revascularization vs IMM alone on cognition over 1 year by cerebral hemodynamic status among those with baseline reduced cognition, suggesting that revascularization does not improve cognitive dysfunction by restoring flow to the brain in chronic asymptomatic carotid stenosis."
- **Problem:** The abstract makes no claim about being the strongest or largest such evidence. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as "this randomized substudy found no support for the cognitive rationale" or attribute the superlative to a source."

### Study 2: Three weeks of pre-surgery conditioning didn't keep frail older patients independent a year later
**PMID:** 42743945 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Safety stated more firmly than abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a short burst of supervised exercise and nutrition sessions beforehand is safe and you may value how it feels"
- **Abstract says:** "32 safety events occurred in the prehabilitation group, mostly unrelated or only possibly related to the intervention."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports safety events in the intervention group without concluding the programme "is safe"; the digest's flat reassurance goes slightly beyond the reported data.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "appeared well tolerated, with few safety events judged related to the programme"."

**Issue B. Unverifiable superlative** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is the largest test of whether it preserves independence"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract describes a multicentre randomised controlled trial of 1382 participants across 19 hospitals but makes no claim about being the largest such trial."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the superlative or write "one of the largest trials to test this"."

### Study 9: Loneliness in later life tracks with income, housing and internet access — not just personality
**PMID:** 42742397 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Outcome variable loosely named in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Loneliness in later life tracks with income, housing and internet access — not just personality"
- **Abstract says:** "Social asymmetry was operationalised as a continuous discrepancy score between standardised loneliness (UCLA-3 Loneliness) and social isolation (modified Berkman-Syme Social Network Index), with positive values indicating social resilience... and negative values indicating social vulnerability."
- **Problem:** The measured outcome was the mismatch between felt loneliness and objective isolation, not loneliness levels as such. The body text and Caveats explain this, but the headline names the wrong variable.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like "Feeling lonelier than your social network would predict tracks with income, housing and internet access"."

### Study 14: A brain scan changed the diagnosis for one in three patients with unclear parkinsonism
**PMID:** 42753528 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Pathological-confirmation claim unverifiable** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "'diagnostic revision' does not by itself prove the new diagnosis was correct, as few studies had pathological confirmation"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract describes pooled proportions of diagnostic revision and risk-of-bias assessment with ROBINS-I, but says nothing about pathological (autopsy) confirmation in the included studies."

### Study 15: Smartphone brain-training apps showed cognitive gains in nearly every trial reviewed
**PMID:** 42754558 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Interventions relabeled as "brain-training"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Smartphone brain-training apps showed cognitive gains in nearly every trial reviewed"
- **Abstract says:** ""Mobile application-based interventions for subjective memory decline and mild cognitive impairment" — described as non-pharmacological, multidomain interventions, not specifically cognitive-training games."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not characterize the 19 trials as brain-training apps; some may have been multidomain lifestyle or engagement apps, so the headline narrows and slightly mischaracterizes the intervention.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "mobile app-based programs" or "health apps" in the headline instead of "brain-training apps.""

**Issue B. Program-purchase recommendation from narrative review** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "this review supports building structured, evidence-based app programs into wellness offerings"
- **Abstract says:** ""Mobile application-based interventions show promising benefits… However, further research with larger samples and more extended follow-up periods is needed to confirm these findings and optimize intervention strategies.""
- **Problem:** The review reports only that most small trials improved at least one cognitive measure, with no pooled effect size; recommending that communities build programs around it also contradicts the digest's own caveat that the benchmark is lenient and gains may not translate to function.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "may justify piloting app-based programs while tracking whether residents actually benefit," and keep it consistent with the caveats."

**Issue C. Unsourced claim about resident preferences** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "pairs naturally with the digital-skills support residents say they want but rarely receive"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract contains no data on residents' preferences for or access to digital-skills support."
- **Problem:** Presented as an established fact but not from this study.
- **Suggested fix:** "Remove or attribute to other sources."

### Study 18: Apathy — not depression or anxiety — signals who will progress from mild cognitive impairment to Alzheimer's
**PMID:** 42749536 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Overstated ranking of apathy** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this analysis suggests it is the single most informative behavioral warning sign in people already diagnosed with mild cognitive impairment"
- **Abstract says:** ""Apathy is a robust and consistent behavioral predictor of progression from MCI to clinical AD dementia" — the meta-analysis compared only apathy, depression and anxiety."
- **Problem:** Only three neuropsychiatric symptoms were tested, so the data cannot establish apathy as the most informative behavioral sign overall; the headline's "signals who will progress" is also more deterministic than a pooled hazard ratio warrants.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say apathy was the strongest of the three symptoms examined and is associated with higher risk of progression."

**Issue B. Heterogeneity characterization not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Substantial heterogeneity between studies required statistical adjustment"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract reports random-effects models with Hartung-Knapp adjustment but gives no I² or other heterogeneity statistic."
- **Problem:** The degree of heterogeneity is asserted but not reported in the abstract; Hartung-Knapp is a standard random-effects adjustment rather than a response to documented "substantial" heterogeneity.
- **Suggested fix:** "State that pooled estimates used random-effects models with Hartung-Knapp adjustment and that heterogeneity statistics were not reported in the abstract."

### Study 19: Autopsies of Alzheimer's trial volunteers find most had multiple brain diseases, not just one
**PMID:** 42743907 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Generalization beyond trial participants** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "it means an older adult with memory problems most likely has several overlapping brain diseases rather than pure Alzheimer's"
- **Abstract says:** ""Multi-etiology dementia was the norm rather than the exception for AD/ADRD trial participants.""
- **Problem:** The conclusion is framed for clinical-trial participants in a single self-selected autopsy cohort (n=67); extending it to older adults with memory problems generally goes beyond what this study shows.
- **Suggested fix:** "Limit the claim to trial participants, e.g. "among these trial participants, mixed pathology was the norm.""

### Study 20: RSV shots look effective for older adults on immune-suppressing drugs — but protection may fade faster
**PMID:** 42755043 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Vaccination advice firmer than authors' wording** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the available evidence supports RSV vaccination for you"
- **Abstract says:** ""Until further evidence emerges, vaccination should be considered for eligible patients using a personalised framework.""
- **Problem:** The review stops at "should be considered… personalised framework" for a group in which only one study reported RSV outcomes; the digest turns that into a general endorsement for the reader.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "the authors say vaccination should be considered for eligible patients on an individualized basis.""

**Issue B. Effectiveness figures drawn from immunocompromised adults broadly** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "RSV shots look effective for older adults on immune-suppressing drugs"
- **Abstract says:** ""RSV vaccines demonstrated first-season effectiveness of 60-73% in immunocompromised adults… Only one study reported RSV outcomes in SARD patients. No study evaluated vaccine effectiveness stratified by DMARD-class.""
- **Problem:** The headline attaches the effectiveness estimate to people on immunosuppressants for rheumatic disease, while the underlying data come from immunocompromised adults generally; the body text and caveats state this correctly but the headline does not.
- **Suggested fix:** "Adjust the headline to "RSV shots look effective in immunocompromised adults — but data for rheumatic-disease patients are thin.""

### Study 22: Where it hurts may predict how depression unfolds in midlife and later life
**PMID:** 42749164 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Sleep-duration mediation extended to sleep interventions** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Sleep support alone appears unlikely to break the pain-depression cycle."
- **Abstract says:** "Exploratory indirect-effect analyses suggested modest indirect effects through sleep duration for several pain sites, with the largest estimated indirect-effect proportion being approximately 8.9%. ... Sleep duration showed only modest exploratory indirect effects."
- **Problem:** The study measured self-reported sleep duration as a statistical mediator; it did not test any sleep intervention, so a claim about what 'sleep support' can or cannot achieve is not supported by the data.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to: 'Differences in sleep duration accounted for only a small part of the link between pain and worsening depression, so pain itself may need direct attention.'"

**Issue B. Treatment implication from observational trajectory data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "treating pain — not just sleep — deserves attention in depression care"
- **Abstract says:** "Site-specific pain was associated with unfavorable depressive symptom trajectories, particularly the High-Persistent trajectory."
- **Problem:** An association between pain sites and depression trajectories does not establish that treating pain changes depression outcomes; the phrasing edges toward a clinical recommendation from observational data.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to: 'asking precisely where the pain is may carry prognostic information, though this study cannot show that treating pain improves depression.'"

### Study 23: A common anesthesia add-on may cut post-surgery confusion in older patients by nearly half
**PMID:** 42750012 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Number of trials in primary analysis** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers pooled 17 randomized controlled trials involving 2,914 surgical patients aged 60 and older to test whether adding esketamine ... reduced postoperative delirium."
- **Abstract says:** "Seventeen randomized controlled trials involving 2,914 participants were included, with eleven trials contributing to the primary POD analysis."
- **Problem:** The 43% lower odds of delirium came from 11 trials, not all 17; readers may assume the full pooled sample underpins the headline result.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add 'eleven of which contributed to the delirium analysis' after the pooled totals."

**Issue B. Description of esketamine** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "esketamine — a ketamine derivative used for pain and sedation"
- **Abstract says:** "Esketamine, the S-enantiomer of ketamine and an N-methyl-D-aspartate (NMDA) receptor antagonist with established analgesic effects"
- **Problem:** Esketamine is the S-enantiomer of ketamine rather than a derivative; a small technical inaccuracy.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'esketamine — the S-enantiomer (mirror-image form) of ketamine'."

**Issue C. Cost and availability claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a low-cost drug already stocked in operating rooms"
- **Abstract says:** "No statement about cost or operating-room availability appears in the abstract."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only; the price and stocking of esketamine vary by country and setting.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the cost/availability descriptor or attribute it explicitly outside the study."
