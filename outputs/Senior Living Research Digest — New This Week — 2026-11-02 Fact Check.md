### Study 2: Motivation and social support — not lung function — are the strongest levers on whether people with COPD actually exercise
**PMID:** 42635504 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Social support ranked above lung function** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Motivation and social support — not lung function — are the strongest levers on whether people with COPD actually exercise"
- **Abstract says:** "Motivation (r = 0.15, p = .035), symptoms (r = -0.14, p = .015), educational level (r = 0.13, p = .003), pulmonary function (r = 0.09, p = .049), and social support (r = 0.05, p = .040) were associated with exercise in COPD"
- **Problem:** Social support had the smallest correlation of the five factors — smaller than pulmonary function — so pairing it with motivation as a "strongest lever" against lung function reverses the reported ordering. The same inversion appears in the caregiver angle ("social support and motivation were measurable correlates of participation, while lung function barely moved the needle").
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "Motivation is the strongest correlate of exercise participation in COPD — lung function ranks lower" and, in the caregiver angle, note that social support was statistically significant but the weakest association measured."

**Issue B. Causal framing of correlational findings** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "are the strongest levers on whether people with COPD actually exercise"
- **Abstract says:** "were associated with exercise in COPD; ... These findings provide evidence informing the development of targeted interventions"
- **Problem:** "Levers" implies that changing motivation or support changes exercise behavior; the meta-analysis pooled cross-sectional correlations and cannot support a causal or interventional claim.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "most strongly associated with" or "track most closely with" instead of "strongest levers"."

**Issue C. Self-report reliance not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "heavy reliance on self-reported exercise participation in the underlying studies"
- **Abstract says:** "No description of how exercise participation was measured in the included studies."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the claim or attribute it as a general limitation of this literature rather than a finding of this review."

### Study 4: New Alzheimer's drugs force a hard question: can someone with early dementia truly consent?
**PMID:** 42969459 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Author disciplines** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A multidisciplinary team of neurologists, geriatricians, legal scholars and ethicists"
- **Abstract says:** "The authors, with expertise in neurology, geriatrics, law, and history, examined challenges to patient participation"
- **Problem:** The abstract lists history, not ethics, as the fourth discipline; the digest substitutes "ethicists."
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "neurology, geriatrics, law and history" or say "a multidisciplinary team writing on the ethics of consent.""

**Issue B. Specific drug risks named** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "These infusion therapies carry real risks including brain swelling and bleeding"
- **Abstract says:** "safety risks and uncertainties regarding long-term effects make treatment decisions complex"
- **Problem:** The abstract refers only generically to safety risks; ARIA-type brain swelling and bleeding are not named. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the specific risks to the drug labels/other sources, or write "carry safety risks and long-term uncertainties, per the authors.""

### Study 5: Strength and balance workouts ease more than stiffness in Parkinson's — mood, thinking and quality of life improved too
**PMID:** 41144919 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Superiority of combined training overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the trials pooled here suggest the combination, not either alone, produced the broadest benefit"
- **Abstract says:** "Analyses indicated that combined training was associated with notable improvements across multiple outcomes. ... Evidence suggests that combined training may be a particularly promising approach."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports that combined training showed notable improvements and "may be" promising; it does not report a head-to-head demonstration that combined training outperformed resistance or balance training alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "combined training looked particularly promising across outcomes, though the review did not establish it is superior to either approach alone.""

### Study 6: Childhood violence leaves a measurable mark decades later — more chronic illness and depression in two national aging cohorts
**PMID:** 42248533 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal wording in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Childhood violence leaves a measurable mark decades later"
- **Abstract says:** "Childhood violence exposure is a significant risk factor for depressive symptoms, multimorbidity, and their comorbidity in middle-aged and older adults."
- **Problem:** "Leaves a mark" reads as cause and effect; the study is observational with retrospectively reported exposure, as the digest's own caveats acknowledge.

### Study 7: Epilepsy speeds up brain shrinkage beyond normal aging — but only for some people
**PMID:** 42638126 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Reassurance angle omits pervasive regional thinning** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the population-based data are more reassuring than earlier hospital-based studies suggested, with overall gray matter loss only slightly faster than normal aging for most people"
- **Abstract says:** "Cortical regions identified as vulnerable were characterized by markedly accelerated thinning compared to controls (5.3 vs 3.4 μm/year; p < 0.001) and were pervasive across most epilepsy phenotypes."
- **Problem:** The global-volume reassurance is accurate, but the abstract specifies that markedly accelerated thinning in vulnerable regions occurred across most phenotypes — not only the focal/lesional/ongoing-seizure groups — which the angle leaves out.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that regional thinning in vulnerable cortex was accelerated across most epilepsy types even when global loss was near-normal."

### Study 8: Parkinson's quietly erodes attention — especially when you're juggling more than one thing
**PMID:** 42492675 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Heterogeneity claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Meta-analysis of observational comparison studies with substantial heterogeneity across tasks and measures"
- **Abstract says:** "Meta-analytic estimates were derived using Hedges' g within random-effects models, and studies that could not be quantitatively integrated were summarized narratively."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not report heterogeneity statistics or characterize heterogeneity as substantial. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the estimates came from random-effects models pooling varied attention tasks, without asserting the degree of heterogeneity."

### Study 12: Two nerve blocks for hip fracture pain: one works faster, the other lasts longer
**PMID:** 42427231 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Imaging speed vs. procedure speed** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The PENG block was faster to place under ultrasound"
- **Abstract says:** "The PENG group achieved optimal ultrasound imaging faster... Procedure duration, vital signs, and recovery quality did not differ significantly."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports faster time to optimal ultrasound imaging but explicitly no difference in procedure duration, so saying the block was "faster to place" overstates the finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "The PENG block reached optimal ultrasound imaging faster (though overall procedure time was the same)"."

**Issue B. Two-center claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "single-trial, two-center Chinese hospital setting"
- **Abstract says:** "Author affiliations list The Second Affiliated Hospital, Jiangxi Medical College, Nanchang University and Ganzhou People's Hospital; the abstract does not describe the number of study sites."
- **Problem:** The number of recruiting centers is inferred from author affiliations, not stated. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "single trial conducted in China; number of recruiting sites not stated in the abstract"."

### Study 13: Bladder treatments for Parkinson's: both help, but nobody has compared them head-to-head
**PMID:** 42226719 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Both help" overstates uncontrolled evidence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Bladder treatments for Parkinson's: both help, but nobody has compared them head-to-head"
- **Abstract says:** "Available study data confirm that both mirabegron and BoNT-A were associated with directional reductions in several OAB symptoms in PD... The overall certainty of evidence was low to moderate... These findings are hypothesis-generating."
- **Problem:** All eight pooled studies were single-arm pre-post with no control group and low-to-moderate certainty, so "both help" implies more causal confidence than the evidence supports.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "both are linked to symptom improvement in uncontrolled studies, but nobody has compared them head-to-head"."

### Study 14: Adding talk therapy to exercise lifted mood and quality of life for nursing home residents with muscle loss
**PMID:** 42966207 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Incorrect DOI** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "**DOI:** 10.1016/j.gerinurse.2026.104426"
- **Abstract says:** "Geriatr Nurs. 2026 Oct 31;74(Pt B):104415. doi: 10.1016/j.gerinurse.2026.104415."
- **Problem:** The digest cites the DOI of study 15 (the caregiver resilience review) for this trial, both in the entry header and in the citation table, so readers following the link would reach the wrong article.
- **Suggested fix:** "Correct to 10.1016/j.gerinurse.2026.104415 in both the entry and the citation table."

**Issue B. "Usual care" control description** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "eight weeks of exercise, exercise plus psychoeducation, or usual care"
- **Abstract says:** "Participants were randomly assigned to one of three groups: exercise (E), exercise plus psychoeducation (EP), or control (C)."
- **Problem:** The abstract labels the third arm simply "control" and does not specify that it received usual care.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "or a control group" instead of "or usual care"."

### Study 15: Family caregivers don't just need relief — programs that build resilience may protect their health
**PMID:** 42966208 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Benefits fade" extrapolation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "ask whether there's ongoing contact after the program ends, since benefits appear to fade without it"
- **Abstract says:** "Sustained follow-up appeared relevant to maintaining programme-related benefits."
- **Problem:** The abstract says sustained follow-up seemed relevant to maintaining benefits; it does not report that benefits fade when follow-up is absent.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "since sustained follow-up appeared to matter for keeping gains"."

**Issue B. "Greatest risk of depletion"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "caregivers managing behavioral symptoms are the group at greatest risk of depletion"
- **Abstract says:** "behavioural and neuropsychiatric symptoms of the person with dementia were associated with lower resilience"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports an association with lower resilience, not a ranking of which caregivers are at greatest risk.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "caregivers managing behavioural and neuropsychiatric symptoms showed lower resilience"."

### Study 16: Having family at the bedside didn't make dying at home more likely — professional home visits did
**PMID:** 42966200 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline from observational review** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Having family at the bedside didn't make dying at home more likely — professional home visits did"
- **Abstract says:** "Most observational studies examined place of death, with some associations involving informal care pointing towards lower home death or greater hospital death. In contrast, physician and nurse home visits were associated with a greater likelihood of home death."
- **Problem:** "Didn't make... did" is causal phrasing applied to associations from mostly observational studies, though the Caveats do note causation cannot be established.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "Family caregiving wasn't linked to dying at home — professional home visits were"."

**Issue B. Overstated 'often ends in hospital transfer'** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "family effort without clinical backup often ends in a hospital transfer"
- **Abstract says:** "some associations involving informal care pointing towards lower home death or greater hospital death"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports only that some associations pointed in that direction; "often ends in a hospital transfer" asserts a frequency and a mechanism the review did not quantify.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "some studies found informal care alone was associated with more hospital deaths"."

### Study 17: Where you live shapes where you die: counties with more palliative care doctors see fewer hospital deaths
**PMID:** 42965848 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal language from ecological data** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "these data support investment in or partnership for local palliative capacity as a driver of end-of-life outcomes"
- **Abstract says:** "Higher palliative care physician density was associated with higher percentage of non-hospital deaths from cancer and heart disease."
- **Problem:** A county-level cross-sectional association cannot identify physician density as a "driver" of outcomes, and the digest's own caveat says causation cannot be established.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "these data show counties with more palliative care physicians have more non-hospital deaths, which may argue for local capacity-building"."

**Issue B. "Depends partly on" framing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this suggests whether that happens depends partly on the supply of palliative care specialists in your county"
- **Abstract says:** "Higher palliative care physician density was associated with higher percentage of non-hospital deaths from cancer and heart disease."
- **Problem:** "Depends on" implies individual-level causation from aggregate data where no individual patient data or preferences were measured.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "this suggests non-hospital death rates track with the local supply of palliative care specialists"."

### Study 18: Cannabis products for Parkinson's: meta-analysis finds no clear benefit and a four-fold risk of confusion
**PMID:** 42966680 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Clearest signal yet" claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is the clearest signal yet that the randomized evidence doesn't support them"
- **Abstract says:** "Current randomized evidence is insufficient to establish clinically meaningful benefits of cannabinoid-based interventions in Parkinson's disease... Interpretation is limited by few trials and participants, short follow-up, intervention heterogeneity, and imprecise estimates."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes the evidence as insufficient and imprecise rather than as a clear signal of no benefit; the superlative is a comparative claim about the literature not supported by the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "the randomized evidence so far is insufficient to show benefit"."
