### Study 1: Wobbly-surface strength training sharpened thinking in older adults with early memory problems
**PMID:** 42998615 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Verbal fluency result generalized** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "verbal fluency (about one extra word) and digit span forward"
- **Abstract says:** "Verbal Fluency - Letter A: +1.2 letters; 95% CI 0.2-2.2; p=0.017; Digit Span Forward: +0.7 points"
- **Problem:** The gain was on one specific letter-fluency subtest (Letter A), not verbal fluency overall; readers may assume the whole fluency battery improved.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "one letter-fluency subtest (words beginning with A — about one extra word) and digit span forward"."

**Issue B. Headline wording vs effect size** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Wobbly-surface strength training sharpened thinking in older adults with early memory problems"
- **Abstract says:** "the IRT group demonstrated significant improvements in executive function performance (Verbal Fluency - Letter A: +1.2 letters ...; Digit Span Forward: +0.7 points)"
- **Problem:** "Sharpened thinking" oversells gains of roughly one word and 0.7 points on two of several tests, with no memory benefit; the body text does qualify this.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to something like "produced small gains on two executive-function tests"."

### Study 2: Virtual reality and active video games show cognitive benefits for older adults — but the evidence quality is low
**PMID:** 42998936 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Participant description** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "covering 2,873 Chinese older adults with cognitive impairment who used virtual reality or exergame interventions"
- **Abstract says:** "Thirty-eight studies involving 2873 participants and 202 effect sizes were included"
- **Problem:** The 2,873 figure is all participants, including control groups, not only those who received VR/exergame interventions.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "2,873 Chinese older adults with cognitive impairment enrolled in trials of virtual reality or exergame interventions"."

**Issue B. "Large" effect characterization** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The pooled effect on cognition was large and statistically significant across all four cognitive domains examined."
- **Abstract says:** "the pooled effect was significant (Hedges' g = 0.817, 95% CI [0.602, 1.032]) ... Significant positive effects were observed across all four cognitive domains"
- **Problem:** Conflates the single overall pooled estimate (g ≈ 0.74–0.82, moderate-to-large) with the domain-level results; the abstract does not report a large effect in each domain.
- **Suggested fix:** ""The overall pooled effect was moderate-to-large (Hedges' g ≈ 0.8), and effects were significant in each of the four domains examined.""

**Issue C. Claim about study characteristics** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this review found the field dominated by small, short, heterogeneous studies with possible publication bias favoring positive results"
- **Abstract says:** "Heterogeneity was substantial ... Modified Egger's regression suggested possible small-study effects, although publication bias could not be determined conclusively ... More rigorously designed randomized trials with longer follow-up are needed."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports possible small-study effects and a call for longer follow-up, but does not state the field is "dominated by" small, short studies or confirm publication bias.
- **Suggested fix:** ""this review found substantial heterogeneity and signs of small-study effects, and called for more rigorous trials with longer follow-up"."

### Study 3: Anti-tau drugs for Alzheimer's move the biomarkers but not the patients — at least so far
**PMID:** 42998661 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Blood" biomarkers not studied** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "changing the blood and spinal fluid numbers has not yet translated into patients thinking or functioning better"
- **Abstract says:** "effects in reducing levels of p-tau ... and t-tau ... in the cerebrospinal fluid were observed"
- **Problem:** Only cerebrospinal fluid and PET measures are reported; the abstract says nothing about blood biomarkers.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "blood and" and refer to spinal fluid and PET measures."

**Issue B. Trial phase and follow-up claims** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "many included trials were early-phase and not powered to detect clinical benefit; short follow-up relative to the disease course"
- **Abstract says:** "Twelve high-quality studies with 11 drugs were evaluated ... the clinical value of anti-tau therapies should be confirmed in adequately powered phase III trials"
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only — the abstract does not report the phase or follow-up length of included trials, though it does call for adequately powered phase III trials.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute to the authors' call for "adequately powered phase III trials" rather than asserting the phase and duration of included studies."

### Study 4: Two-minute word tests reliably flag dementia — but they can't tell you which kind
**PMID:** 42998135 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Test timing not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Verbal fluency tests take about a minute each, need no equipment, and are already used in primary care and memory clinics"
- **Abstract says:** "A systematic search ... identified 127 peer-reviewed studies ... which compared FAS and Animal Fluency Test performances of people with AD or FTD to cognitively-healthy controls."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only — administration time (and the headline's "two-minute") and current primary-care use are background context the abstract does not supply.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the timing/clinical-use details to general practice, or drop the specific durations."

**Issue B. "Reliably flag dementia" framing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Two-minute word tests reliably flag dementia"
- **Abstract says:** "FAS and Animal Fluency Total Scores can effectively detect cognitive decline in both AD and FTD, although they cannot be recommended for differentiating between AD and bvFTD ... significant publication bias may have inflated the effects for AD"
- **Problem:** The pooled studies compared diagnosed patients with healthy controls, which shows group separation rather than real-world screening reliability; "reliably flag" slightly overstates this (the digest does note the limitation in Caveats).
- **Suggested fix:** ""Two-minute word tests separate people with dementia from healthy peers — but can't tell you which kind.""

### Study 5: Special insoles are widely sold to prevent falls. The evidence barely supports it.
**PMID:** 42998665 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Almost every" vs "most" credible intervals** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "but almost every credible interval crossed zero"
- **Abstract says:** "Most CrIs crossed the null."
- **Problem:** Upgrades "most" to "almost every", a slightly stronger claim than the abstract supports.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "most credible intervals crossed zero"."

**Issue B. Seven insole types** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "comparing seven types of insole against control"
- **Abstract says:** "Interventions were classified into seven nodes."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes seven network nodes, which may include the control condition rather than seven distinct insole types.
- **Suggested fix:** ""comparing insole interventions grouped into seven network nodes, including control"."

### Study 6: Four sessions of stumble training cut falls over six months — and the schedule didn't matter
**PMID:** 42998663 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Causal headline from uncontrolled design** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Four sessions of stumble training cut falls over six months"
- **Abstract says:** "Improvements in dynamic balance and fewer falls were observed over time, but these should be interpreted cautiously given the study design."
- **Problem:** Both arms received training, so the pre-post drop in falls cannot be attributed to the intervention; the headline makes a causal claim the authors explicitly warn against, contradicting the digest's own caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Falls dropped over six months after four sessions of stumble training — and the schedule didn't matter (no untrained comparison group).""

**Issue B. Perturbation type specified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "96 deliberate trips and slips on a custom treadmill designed to retrain reactive balance"
- **Abstract says:** "Training consisted of 96 perturbations on a custom treadmill."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not specify that the perturbations were trips and slips.
- **Suggested fix:** ""96 treadmill perturbations designed to retrain reactive balance"."

**Issue C. Eligibility window misstated in story angle** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "If you have fallen in the past year, ask your physical therapist whether perturbation-based balance training ... is available near you."
- **Abstract says:** "30 community-dwelling older adults who had fallen at least once in the past 6 months"
- **Problem:** Participants had fallen within the past six months, not the past year; the angle widens the population beyond the trial.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "If you have fallen in the past six months"."

### Study 7: Talking to a dietitian may improve quality of life during cancer treatment — and older patients saw the biggest gains
**PMID:** 42998187 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Analyzed sample size** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers pooled 20 randomized controlled trials involving 1,668 adults with cancer"
- **Abstract says:** "A total of 20 trials involving 1,668 participants were included in the review, with 1,301 participants contributing post-intervention QoL data to the meta-analysis."
- **Problem:** Only 1,301 participants contributed data to the pooled quality-of-life estimate; 1,668 is the review total.
- **Suggested fix:** ""20 trials involving 1,668 adults, of whom 1,301 contributed quality-of-life data to the pooled analysis"."

**Issue B. Prediction interval description** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the prediction interval spanned from harm to large benefit"
- **Abstract says:** "The 95% prediction interval ranged from - 0.94 to 2.36"
- **Problem:** Accurate in substance, but the digest's caveat separately calls it "a prediction interval crossing zero" — worth stating the actual range once for consistency.
- **Suggested fix:** "Give the range (−0.94 to 2.36) in the body text."

### Study 8: "Integrated" frailty care programs didn't beat usual care in pooled trial data
**PMID:** 42995697 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported comparative claim about components** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "resistance exercise and protein intake remain the best-supported pieces"
- **Abstract says:** "Evidence that IMC improves frailty and ADL in older adults with frailty or pre-frailty remains inconclusive ... Future high-quality research with longer follow-up is needed."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only — this review did not compare individual components or endorse any of them.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute to the wider frailty literature or remove the claim."

**Issue B. Heterogeneity generalized** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "heterogeneity between studies was very high"
- **Abstract says:** "Q(5) = 6.73, p = 0.241; I2 = 25.8% [frailty analysis] ... heterogeneity was substantial (I2 = 95.1%) [ADL analysis]"
- **Problem:** Very high heterogeneity applied to the three-study ADL analysis; the main frailty analysis had low heterogeneity (I² = 25.8%).
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify that heterogeneity was very high for the daily-function outcome but low for the frailty-reversal analysis."

### Study 10: Before radiation therapy, a 10-minute nutrition questionnaire beat blood tests at predicting who would suffer
**PMID:** 42998707 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Questionnaire duration** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a 10-minute nutrition questionnaire"
- **Abstract says:** "Baseline nutritional status was assessed using PG-SGA, MUST, MNA-SF, NUTRISCORE, GNRI, and PNI."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only — no administration time is reported for PG-SGA.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "a short nutrition questionnaire" or attribute the timing to the instrument's standard use."

**Issue B. Margin over other tools overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The questionnaire-based PG-SGA predicted who would develop significant toxicity far better than anything else (AUC 0.925)"
- **Abstract says:** "PG-SGA showed the highest predictive accuracy (AUC=0.925), followed by MNA-SF (AUC=0.882) and MUST (AUC=0.867)."
- **Problem:** PG-SGA ranked first, but MNA-SF (0.882) and MUST (0.867) were close behind, so "far better than anything else" overstates the gap among questionnaires.
- **Suggested fix:** ""PG-SGA performed best (AUC 0.925), with MNA-SF and MUST close behind, while the lab-based indices lagged well behind.""

**Issue C. Number of centers** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Modest sample (N=150) from two Turkish centers"
- **Abstract says:** "This prospective observational study included 150 cancer patients aged ≥65 years treated with radiotherapy."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only — the abstract does not state how many centers contributed patients (author affiliations list two institutions, which is not the same thing).
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "a Turkish radiotherapy cohort" unless the number of sites can be confirmed from the full text."

### Study 11: Babies born under wartime sugar rationing had fewer osteoporosis diagnoses decades later — but the signal wobbles
**PMID:** 42998334 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Wartime" rationing mislabel** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Babies born under wartime sugar rationing had fewer osteoporosis diagnoses decades later"
- **Abstract says:** "We studied 65,346 UK-born UK Biobank participants born between October 1951 and March 1956... Birth month defined four categories of early-life sugar rationing exposure"
- **Problem:** The abstract never calls the rationing "wartime," and the births studied (Oct 1951–Mar 1956) occurred years after the Second World War, during the continuation of post-war rationing that ended in 1953.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "post-war sugar rationing" or simply "UK sugar rationing.""

### Study 12: A cheap mineral supplement killed off 'zombie' cells and extended lifespan — in mice
**PMID:** 42995257 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Compound screen not described in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers at the University of Chicago and the Buck Institute screened for compounds that selectively destroy senescent cells"
- **Abstract says:** "Here we report vanadyl sulfate (VS), a mineral dietary supplement, as a senolytic agent. Oral VS ameliorates phenotypes of aging and diverse age-related disorders and extends lifespan in male mice."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports VS as a senolytic and describes integrated functional omics analyses, but says nothing about a screen of compounds. Unverifiable from abstract only. (Author affiliations also span Fudan, UT Southwestern, City of Hope and Utah, not just Chicago and the Buck Institute.)
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "Researchers led from the University of Chicago, with collaborators including the Buck Institute, identified vanadyl sulfate as a senolytic" unless the screen is confirmed in the full text."

**Issue B. Vanadium toxicity claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "vanadium compounds can be toxic to the kidneys and gut"
- **Abstract says:** "(no safety or toxicity data reported)"
- **Problem:** A reasonable general-pharmacology caution, but it is not supported by anything in this abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute to outside sources, e.g. "separate safety literature reports vanadium compounds can be toxic to the kidneys and gut.""

### Study 14: Lung cancer screening still hasn't recovered from the pandemic — and it was low to begin with
**PMID:** 42995348 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline contradicts recovery finding** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Lung cancer screening still hasn't recovered from the pandemic — and it was low to begin with"
- **Abstract says:** "annual screening fell from 7.3% in January 2020 to 5.7%, recovering to pre-pandemic rates by March 2022, ending at 11% (June 2023)... recovery exceeded 2 years and screening remains low"
- **Problem:** Screening did recover — and by mid-2023 was higher than the pre-pandemic 7.3%. The accurate point is that recovery took more than two years and absolute uptake remains low, which is what the body text says; the headline tells readers the opposite.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "Lung cancer screening took more than two years to bounce back from the pandemic — and it's still low.""

**Issue B. Eligibility criteria not from this study** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "If you're between 50 and 80, have a 20 pack-year smoking history, and currently smoke or quit within the past 15 years, you likely qualify"
- **Abstract says:** "PARTICIPANTS: Veterans who were users of Veterans Affairs (VA) healthcare system, and subgroup of Veterans eligible for lung cancer screening."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not state the eligibility criteria used; these are USPSTF criteria brought in from elsewhere. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the criteria to the USPSTF rather than implying they come from the study."

### Study 15: Anxiety disorders add measurably to the world's dementia burden — and the gap between countries is widening
**PMID:** 42996801 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Widening" gap overstates** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "and the gap between countries is widening"
- **Abstract says:** "Global inequalities persisted over time."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports that inequalities persisted, not that they widened — a different claim, and one that the headline presents as a finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "and the gap between countries hasn't closed.""

**Issue B. Sociodemographic index described as income** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "was highest in Europe and high-middle-income regions, and rose fastest in South-East Asia and lower-middle-income countries"
- **Abstract says:** "Highest burdens were observed in Europe and high-middle sociodemographic index (SDI) regions in 2021, while the fastest increases occurred in South-East Asian and low-middle SDI regions."
- **Problem:** SDI is a composite of income, education and fertility, not an income band; relabeling it as income misstates the grouping variable.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "high-middle sociodemographic index (SDI) regions" and "low-middle SDI regions," with a brief gloss."

### Study 16: Popular dementia risk calculators may not work well for healthy, affluent older adults
**PMID:** 42995191 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Interpretation the authors declined to make** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this suggests they may give falsely reassuring or poorly discriminating results in exactly the health-conscious people most likely to use them"
- **Abstract says:** "Whether this reflects uniformly low risk or reduced ability to separate individuals cannot be determined without a cognitive outcome."
- **Problem:** The authors state they cannot tell whether the narrow score range means genuinely low risk or poor discrimination; the digest picks the less favorable of the two readings. The Caveats do note the absence of a cognitive outcome, but the framing here still leans past it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the authors' own hedge, e.g. "the authors say they cannot tell whether this reflects genuinely low risk or a loss of discriminating power.""

**Issue B. Population described as older adults** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "may not work well for healthy, affluent older adults"
- **Abstract says:** "Four scores were computed for 895 participants aged ≥45 years in the Zoī cohort"
- **Problem:** The cohort is adults 45 and over, a middle-aged-plus sample; the headline implies an older-adult population.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "healthy, affluent middle-aged and older adults.""
