### Study 1: When death is closer, loneliness creeps in — new national data tracks how social life shrinks
**PMID:** 42974667 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Non-significant socializing result stated as finding** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "They were also more likely to drop below monthly socializing"
- **Abstract says:** "Among those socializing at least monthly at baseline, 19.0% of higher versus 12.3% of lower risk participants transitioned to less-than-monthly socializing (aOR 1.60; 95% CI: 0.97-2.65)."
- **Problem:** The adjusted odds ratio's confidence interval crosses 1, so this difference did not reach statistical significance, yet the digest presents it alongside the significant loneliness result as an equally established finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a qualifier, e.g. 'and showed a similar — though not statistically significant — trend toward dropping below monthly socializing (19% vs 12%).'"

**Issue B. Baseline claim omits the exception** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Those at higher mortality risk already had poorer social wellbeing at baseline"
- **Abstract says:** "At baseline, higher mortality risk participants had poorer social wellbeing on all measures except socializing."
- **Problem:** The digest drops the authors' explicit exception for socializing, making the baseline difference look uniform across all measures.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write 'poorer social wellbeing at baseline on every measure except socializing.'"

### Study 3: Adding paracetamol to strong opioids didn't ease cancer pain — and stopping it caused no harm
**PMID:** 42309698 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Efficacy headline from a feasibility pilot** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Adding paracetamol to strong opioids didn't ease cancer pain — and stopping it caused no harm"
- **Abstract says:** "This pilot study aimed to evaluate the feasibility of a larger randomised controlled trial… Outcomes were recruitment, retention, appropriateness of study design, patient acceptability and early signals of pain outcome."
- **Problem:** The headline states a definitive efficacy result, while the trial was powered only for feasibility and reported 'early signals' — the digest's own caveat says the study was not designed to test efficacy.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to something like 'Pilot trial finds no early signal that paracetamol adds to strong opioids for cancer pain.'"

**Issue B. 'Halted early' overstates** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "though the trial was underpowered and halted early"
- **Abstract says:** "Patient recruitment was slow. Due to this, the study did not progress to a definitive RCT."
- **Problem:** The pilot ran to completion (with the intervention phase shortened from 13 to 7 days); what did not happen was progression to the definitive trial. 'Halted early' implies the pilot itself was stopped.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'the pilot was underpowered and never progressed to the planned definitive trial.'"

### Study 4: Switching HIV drug regimens drove steeper weight gain in older adults — especially women and those over 65
**PMID:** 42974888 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Slope change misread as weight loss** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Switching away from an integrase inhibitor back to an NNRTI was associated with weight loss (−2.07 kg/year)."
- **Abstract says:** "Slope changes were… -2.07 kg/year for INSTI→NNRTI (p = 0.03)… switching from an INSTI to an NNRTI was associated with attenuation."
- **Problem:** −2.07 kg/year is a change in the weight-trajectory slope, not an absolute loss of weight; the authors describe it as attenuation of weight gain. A reader could conclude the switch makes people lose weight.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as 'switching back from an integrase inhibitor to an NNRTI slowed the weight trajectory (slope change −2.07 kg/year).'"

**Issue B. Causal language for observational cohort** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Switching HIV drug regimens drove steeper weight gain in older adults"
- **Abstract says:** "We conducted a retrospective cohort study… Switching to INSTI-based regimens was associated with steeper weight-gain trajectories compared to switching to other regimens."
- **Problem:** The authors consistently use 'associated with'; 'drove' asserts causation from a retrospective observational design, directly contradicting the digest's own caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'was linked to' or 'was followed by' in the headline."

**Issue C. Subgroup findings stated as established in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "especially women and those over 65"
- **Abstract says:** "Exploratory analyses suggested numerically stronger associations among females, adults aged ≥ 65 years, and participants with baseline metabolic risk."
- **Problem:** The headline presents exploratory, numerically-stronger-only subgroup signals as a confirmed pattern, although the body text and caveats do hedge appropriately.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add 'possibly' or move the subgroup detail out of the headline."

### Study 5: Losing your teeth may rival depression and loneliness as a dementia risk factor
**PMID:** 42971073 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Social isolation relabeled as loneliness** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Losing your teeth may rival depression and loneliness as a dementia risk factor"
- **Abstract says:** "The PAF for tooth loss was 7.2%, ranking third among the 13 risk factors examined, following depressive symptoms (10.5%) and social isolation (9.4%)."
- **Problem:** Social isolation (objective lack of contact) and loneliness (subjective feeling) are distinct constructs in the Lancet Commission list; the body text correctly says 'social isolation' but the headline swaps in loneliness.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change the headline to 'may rival depression and social isolation.'"

### Study 6: For better sleep after 65, mixing cardio and strength training beat any single type of exercise
**PMID:** 42970245 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Ranking presented as proven superiority** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "mixing cardio and strength training beat any single type of exercise"
- **Abstract says:** "combined exercise (SUCRA=74.8%) may have ranked first, followed by aerobic exercise (65.4%), resistance exercise (64.4%)… Combined exercise may demonstrate the optimal effect… However, these findings should be regarded as provisional."
- **Problem:** SUCRA values are probabilistic rankings, not significance tests, and the top three are closely clustered (74.8 vs 65.4 vs 64.4). The authors twice hedge with 'may'; 'beat' asserts a demonstrated win.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'ranked ahead of' or 'came out on top in rankings' instead of 'beat.'"

**Issue B. Age threshold not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "For better sleep after 65"
- **Abstract says:** "sleep quality in older adults"
- **Problem:** The abstract specifies only 'older adults' with no age cutoff; '65' is an assumption.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'for better sleep in later life' or 'in older adults.'"

**Issue C. Named examples of mind-body exercise** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "mind-body practices such as tai chi and yoga"
- **Abstract says:** "mind-body exercise (49.7%)"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not specify which modalities made up the mind-body node. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the examples or mark them as typical of this category rather than as what the trials used."

### Study 7: Advance care planning records jumped in care homes — but England still can't see who gets specialist palliative care
**PMID:** 41980783 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported certainty about undercounting** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "too sparse to be useful for monitoring, even though many more people certainly received such care"
- **Abstract says:** "unlike the specialist palliative care measure, where sparse recorded use rendered it ineffectual for service monitoring. Improved coding in primary care records would enhance the reliability of measures."
- **Problem:** The abstract attributes the problem to poor coding but never quantifies or asserts how many people actually received specialist palliative care; 'certainly' states as fact something the study could not observe.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'a figure the authors attribute to incomplete coding rather than to actual service use.'"

### Study 8: In a head-to-head spine surgery trial, the cheaper operation also produced the better year
**PMID:** 42975416 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. N used for the cost analysis** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers analyzed costs and quality of life over one year for 153 US patients (mean age 62)"
- **Abstract says:** "153 from the United States… were included as-treated… Economic data were not available for 9 patients. The remaining 144 patients treated… had cost data through a 1-year follow-up."
- **Problem:** Only 144 of the 153 as-treated US patients contributed cost data; the digest attributes the cost analysis to all 153.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that 153 US patients were analyzed as-treated, with cost data available for 144."

**Issue B. ACDF comparison significance not noted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Laminoplasty produced the largest quality-adjusted life-year gains (0.792) at the lowest total cost ($47,426), compared with anterior cervical diskectomy and fusion ($53,074)"
- **Abstract says:** "LP yielded greater QALY gains than ACDF (0.792 vs 0.732; P = .08) and PCDF (0.792 vs 0.698; P = .01)."
- **Problem:** The digest flags the PCDF quality-of-life difference as significant but leaves the ACDF comparison unqualified, where the difference did not reach significance (P = .08).
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that the QALY advantage over ACDF did not reach statistical significance (P = .08)."

### Study 10: A hidden second protein may explain why some Alzheimer's patients decline faster
**PMID:** 42970748 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies clinical decline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A hidden second protein may explain why some Alzheimer's patients decline faster"
- **Abstract says:** "α-synuclein positivity identifies an AD-aSyn+ subgroup with steeper increases in p-tau217 and NfL, supporting biomarker-based stratification for prognosis and trial enrichment."
- **Problem:** The study tracked blood biomarker trajectories, not cognitive or functional decline. The digest's own caveat says 'findings concern biomarker trajectories, not measured clinical decline,' which the headline contradicts.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'may mark a faster-changing biological subgroup in Alzheimer's.'"

**Issue B. Claim about current blood tests** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a co-occurring second protein — which existing blood tests don't detect"
- **Abstract says:** "aSyn pathology assessed using real-time quaking-induced conversion assay were determined at baseline"
- **Problem:** The abstract states only that α-synuclein status was determined by CSF RT-QuIC; it makes no claim about what commercial blood tests can or cannot detect. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'a second protein that in this study required a spinal fluid assay to detect.'"

### Study 11: Cranking up deep brain stimulation to a higher frequency doesn't tame tremor any better, trial finds
**PMID:** 42972941 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Battery-drain inference not supported by energy-matched design** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this suggests that extra energy buys nothing for tremor and may simply drain the device battery faster, meaning earlier surgical replacements"
- **Abstract says:** "Tremor-suppressive effects, evaluated by accelerometry, were compared with TEED-equivalent stimulation at 130 and 185 Hz... Post-hoc equivalence testing confirmed equivalence of stimulation frequencies under TEED-equivalent conditions within a ±20% margin"
- **Problem:** The trial deliberately held total electrical energy delivered constant between the two frequencies, so it did not test whether delivering extra energy helps, nor did it measure battery consumption or device longevity.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to: "at equal energy delivery, 185 Hz offered no tremor advantage over 130 Hz — and in routine practice raising frequency usually raises energy use, a battery consideration the trial did not directly measure.""

**Issue B. Post hoc equivalence testing presented as confirmatory** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "formal equivalence testing confirmed the two settings performed the same within a ±20% margin"
- **Abstract says:** "Post-hoc equivalence testing confirmed equivalence of stimulation frequencies under TEED-equivalent conditions within a ±20% margin of relative tremor improvement"
- **Problem:** The abstract labels the equivalence testing as post hoc; presenting it as a planned confirmatory analysis slightly overstates its weight.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "post hoc": "post hoc equivalence testing indicated the two settings performed the same within a ±20% margin.""

**Issue C. Single-center claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Small N (43 participants total), single-center, acute testing only"
- **Abstract says:** "Author affiliations list University Hospital Cologne (with additional Berlin and Jülich affiliations); the abstract does not describe the number of centers."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only — the abstract does not state that recruitment was at a single site.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "small N (43 participants total), apparently recruited at one German university center, acute testing only.""

### Study 12: Exercise builds strength and steadies blood sugar in older adults with diabetes — even when muscle mass barely budges

### Study 13: Ultrasound brain stimulation for Parkinson's: promising buzz, thin evidence so far
**PMID:** 42975199 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Marketing/cost claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "TPS is already being marketed by private neurology and wellness clinics, often at considerable out-of-pocket cost"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract describes TPS only as "an emerging ultrasound-based neuromodulation technique with potential applications in Parkinson's disease" and makes no mention of commercial availability or cost."

### Study 14: The brain signature behind Parkinson's 'freezing' isn't where researchers thought it was
**PMID:** 42957570 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Treatment claim not supported by an imaging meta-analysis** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "pinning down a reproducible brain correlate helps explain why it responds better to attention-based cueing strategies than to dopamine medication alone"
- **Abstract says:** "We identified a significant convergent cluster in the right inferior frontal gyrus (IFG) pars opercularis/dorsolateral prefrontal cortex in PD freezers compared with nonfreezers... potentially reflecting heightened frontal recruitment"
- **Problem:** The study compared brain imaging in freezers versus nonfreezers; it tested no treatment and reports nothing about cueing versus dopaminergic medication response.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as: "the authors suggest the finding may reflect heightened frontal recruitment during freezing; whether that explains the response to cueing strategies was not tested here.""

**Issue B. Clinical recommendation from coordinate-based imaging data** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "which is why cueing tricks like counting, stepping over a laser line, or marching to a beat often work when medication adjustments don't. Ask your neurologist or physical therapist about cueing training."
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract reports only convergent brain alterations in the right IFG and the absence of hemispheric lateralization; no intervention, cueing, or medication comparison appears."
- **Problem:** A clinical recommendation is drawn from a meta-analysis of existing imaging coordinates that studied no intervention, and the causal connector "which is why" attributes the efficacy of cueing to this finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "Separate the two: note that cueing strategies are used clinically (citing other evidence) and that this study identifies a brain correlate only, without testing any therapy."

**Issue C. Headline overstates the surprise** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The brain signature behind Parkinson's 'freezing' isn't where researchers thought it was"
- **Abstract says:** "We identified a significant convergent cluster in the right inferior frontal gyrus... No evidence of overall hemispheric lateralization was observed"
- **Problem:** The study confirmed a right-hemisphere frontal region; what it failed to confirm was an overall right-hemispheric bias across studies. The headline suggests the location itself was wrong.
- **Suggested fix:** "Try: "Parkinson's 'freezing' converges on one frontal region — but the assumed right-brain bias didn't hold up.""

### Study 15: Muscle-loss risk calculators for people with type 2 diabetes look good on paper — and have barely been tested in the real world
**PMID:** 42790129 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Proven" overstates evidence for bedside measures** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "simple measures, not a fancy risk score, are what's available and proven today"
- **Abstract says:** "Existing prediction models for sarcopenia or low muscle mass in T2DM patients show promising discrimination, but limitations in analysis methods, reporting and external validation may affect their robustness and generalisability."
- **Problem:** The review evaluated multivariable prediction models only; it did not compare them with, or validate, grip strength/chair-stand/gait-speed screening, so "proven" is not supported here.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "simple hands-on measures remain the practical option while no risk score has been externally validated.""

### Study 17: Virtual reality training helps Parkinson's mobility and balance confidence — but only at the right dose

**Issue A. Cost framing added to platform comparison** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "inexpensive commercial gaming platforms worked about as well as specialized medical systems... plus reassurance that costly specialized equipment isn't required"
- **Abstract says:** "Commercial and specialized platforms showed comparable efficacy."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports comparable efficacy but says nothing about the cost of either platform type, so the price contrast is an inference.

### Study 19: Rehab by video after a hip replacement appears to work as well as in person — but almost nobody has tested it on older patients
**PMID:** 42307257 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Older-adult exclusion framed more firmly than the abstract states** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "almost nobody has tested it on older patients... have not adequately included older patients — the very group who receives most hip replacements"
- **Abstract says:** "Challenges include methodological differences, demographic variations, and the lack of long-term data... future research should focus on long-term outcomes, including older patients"
- **Problem:** The abstract notes "demographic variations" and recommends that future research include older patients; it does not quantify how many older adults were enrolled or state that they were inadequately represented.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "the authors call for future trials to include older patients and longer-term outcomes, flagging demographic variation across the existing studies.""
