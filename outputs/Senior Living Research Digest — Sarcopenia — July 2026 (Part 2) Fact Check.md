## Study 4: Long-Term Statin Use Shows No Harmful Effects on Muscle in Older Adults
**PMID:** 42373884 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Incomplete characterization of RASi finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A modest association between blood pressure medications (ACE inhibitors and ARBs) and slightly lower muscle mass was observed in women, though clinical significance remains unclear."
- **Abstract says:** "RASi use was associated with modestly lower ALM/BMI (β = -0.036, p = 0.004). Sex-stratified analyses showed that RASi use was significantly associated with lower ALM/BMI in women (β = -0.033, p = 0.029) but not in men (β = -0.037, p = 0.072), without a significant sex interaction."
- **Problem:** The digest says the association was observed "in women," but the abstract clarifies there was no significant sex interaction and the effect was similar in magnitude for men (just didn't reach significance). The framing slightly overstates the sex-specificity.
- **Suggested fix:** "A modest association between blood pressure medications (ACE inhibitors and ARBs) and slightly lower muscle mass was observed, reaching statistical significance in women but not men, though clinical significance remains unclear."

## Study 5: Sarcopenia More Than Doubles Cognitive Impairment Risk in Latin American Older Adults
**PMID:** 42155950 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Imprecise reporting of OR with stricter criteria** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "When stricter diagnostic criteria were used, the association strengthened to nearly 3 times higher odds."
- **Abstract says:** "diagnosis criteria of sarcopenia by EWGSOP2 (OR = 2.96; 95% CI: 2.15-4.08, P < 0.001)"
- **Problem:** The digest says "nearly 3 times higher odds," which could be interpreted as OR ≈ 3.0. The actual OR of 2.96 is indeed very close to 3, so this is accurate but the phrasing is slightly imprecise. More significantly, the abstract reports the OR of 2.56 for "probable cognitive impairment" as the other stratified result, not explicitly "stricter diagnostic criteria."
- **Suggested fix:** "When using EWGSOP2 criteria for sarcopenia diagnosis, the association strengthened to nearly 3 times higher odds (OR = 2.96)."

## Study 6: Sarcopenic Obesity Poses Highest Dementia Risk, UK Biobank Study Shows
**PMID:** 42155951 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Incorrect hazard ratio for sarcopenic obesity** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "people with sarcopenic obesity—the combination of muscle loss and excess weight—had 69% higher dementia risk compared to those with neither condition."
- **Abstract says:** "Individuals with sarcopenic obesity (hazard ratios = 1.69, 1.41-2.03) had the highest risk of all-cause dementia than those with obesity only, sarcopenia only or neither."
- **Problem:** The digest correctly states 69% higher risk (HR 1.69), but the comparison group in the abstract is "those with obesity only, sarcopenia only or neither" — it's comparing to all other groups combined rather than specifically to "neither condition." This slightly misrepresents the reference group.
- **Suggested fix:** "people with sarcopenic obesity—the combination of muscle loss and excess weight—had 69% higher dementia risk compared to those with obesity alone, sarcopenia alone, or neither condition."

**Issue B. Incorrect HR for possible sarcopenia** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Even 'probable' sarcopenia (reduced muscle strength without confirmed muscle loss) increased dementia risk by 44%."
- **Abstract says:** "the multivariable-adjusted hazard ratios (95% confidence interval) of all-cause dementia were 1.44 (1.32-1.58)... for those with possible sarcopenia"
- **Problem:** The abstract uses "possible sarcopenia" while the digest uses "probable" — this is a terminology difference. The EWGSOP2 criteria distinguish between "probable" (low strength) and "confirmed" (low strength + low mass), so "possible" in the abstract likely refers to what EWGSOP2 calls "probable." This is a minor terminology inconsistency but the number (44%) is accurate.
- **Suggested fix:** "Even 'possible' sarcopenia (as defined in the study) increased dementia risk by 44%."

## Study 10: Grip Strength and Chair Stand Tests Predict Hospital Length of Stay
**PMID:** 42348081 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Minor sample size discrepancy** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Among 1,071 hospitalized older adults in Copenhagen"
- **Abstract says:** "1071 older inpatients"
- **Problem:** The digest says "1,071" while the abstract says "1071" — this is functionally identical but the abstract actually states the study "included" these patients while the digest implies analysis. The abstract notes these were assessed "within 24 h of admission." No substantive error.

**Issue B. Direction of association could be clearer** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "those with longer hospital stays had weaker grip strength and poorer performance on the 30-second chair stand test"
- **Abstract says:** "Higher 30CST and HGS were consistently associated with shorter LoS"
- **Problem:** The digest frames this as "longer stays associated with weaker strength," while the abstract frames it as "higher strength associated with shorter stays." Both are logically equivalent, but the cross-sectional nature makes the direction of relationship ambiguous. The digest's framing is acceptable but slightly emphasizes a causal direction.
- **Suggested fix:** No change necessary, but could add clarity: "Higher grip strength and better chair stand performance were associated with shorter hospitalizations."

## Study 13: Muscle Strength, Not Just Mass, May Predict Recovery After Hip Replacement
**PMID:** 42104205 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline may slightly misrepresent findings** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** Headline states "Muscle Strength, Not Just Mass, May Predict Recovery"
- **Abstract says:** "Sarcopenia showed a weaker but relevant association with delayed walking ability" and "Advanced age, cognitive impairment, and low prefracture functional capacity were associated with delayed recovery"
- **Problem:** The headline emphasizes muscle strength over mass, but the abstract emphasizes age, cognitive status, and functional capacity as the strongest predictors, with sarcopenia showing only a "weaker but relevant" association. The headline somewhat overstates the muscle strength finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "Age, Cognition, and Pre-Surgery Function Predict Recovery After Hip Replacement" or keep current headline but ensure body text accurately reflects the hierarchy of predictors.

The body text does accurately note that "Advanced age, cognitive impairment, and low pre-fracture functional capacity were the strongest predictors" and sarcopenia showed "a weaker but relevant association."

## Study 14: Food Insecurity Linked to Earlier Death in Older Adults
**PMID:** 42201632 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Follow-up period not specified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "over an extended follow-up period"
- **Abstract says:** "During the follow-up period" (no specific duration given)
- **Problem:** Neither the digest nor the abstract provides the exact follow-up duration. The digest's vague language is acceptable given the abstract's similar vagueness. The baseline wave was 2004-2005, but endpoint is not specified in abstract.

**Issue B. Sarcopenia connection is indirect** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The researchers suggest this relates to conditions like sarcopenia."
- **Abstract says:** "Findings suggest that addressing food insecurity could represent a novel strategy to prevent sarcopenia and related mortality in older adults."
- **Problem:** The abstract's discussion section suggests sarcopenia as a potential mechanism, but this is interpretive rather than directly measured in the study. The digest appropriately attributes this to "the researchers suggest" but the body text makes this seem like a study finding.
- **Suggested fix:** Add to caveats: "Sarcopenia was not directly measured; the sarcopenia connection is hypothesized by authors."

## Study 19: Thigh Muscle Loss Over 5 Years Predicts Strength and Mobility Decline
**PMID:** 42287341 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Risk increase percentages need clarification** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "each square centimeter decrease in front thigh muscle doubled the risk of grip strength decline and more than doubled the risk of mobility decline"
- **Abstract says:** "each decrease (per cm2) of anterior compartment of thigh muscle size was associated with a 101% decrease of HGS (95% confidence interval [CI], 1.10-3.67; P = 0.02) and an 121% longer TUG"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a "101% decrease of HGS" and "121% longer TUG" — the digest interprets this as "doubled the risk" and "more than doubled," which is approximately correct (OR ~2.0 and ~2.2 based on the CIs). However, the abstract's phrasing is somewhat ambiguous (is it 101% increased odds or a 101% change in the outcome?). The digest's interpretation appears reasonable but the underlying statistic is unclear.
