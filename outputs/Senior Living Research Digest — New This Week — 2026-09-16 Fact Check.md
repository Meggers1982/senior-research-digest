### Study 1: Three weeks of supervised 'prehab' before surgery didn't keep frail older patients independent, big German trial finds
**PMID:** 42743945 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Largest trial of its kind" claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the largest trial of its kind found no effect on whether frail older patients stayed independent a year later"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract describes a multicentre randomised controlled trial of 1382 participants across 19 German hospitals but makes no claim about being the largest trial of its kind."
- **Problem:** Superlative ranking cannot be verified from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "a large multicentre trial found no effect…" or attribute the superlative to a source."

### Study 2: Online caregiver support helped most the family carers who were also juggling a paid job
**PMID:** 42745561 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline conflates employment with role conflict** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Online caregiver support helped most the family carers who were also juggling a paid job"
- **Abstract says:** ""WCRC was operationalised as self-report of missed work or reduced productivity due to competing care and employment demands" and "Forty-two of 93 carers reported WCRC.""
- **Problem:** The moderator was reported work-care role conflict, not simply holding a job; employed carers without conflict did not show the larger benefit.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "…carers whose job and caregiving were already clashing" or "…carers reporting work-care role conflict.""

**Issue B. Unsupported cost characterization** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this suggests low-cost online support may deliver the biggest relief"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract describes Draw Care as "an online and culturally-adapted carer support intervention" with no information on cost."
- **Problem:** Cost is not reported in the abstract, so "low-cost" is unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "low-cost" or say "online support" without a cost claim."

### Study 11: Burned-out caregivers have a harder time understanding health information, study finds
**PMID:** 42745685 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Directional framing in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Burned-out caregivers have a harder time understanding health information, study finds"
- **Abstract says:** "A cross-sectional study was conducted with 150 family caregivers... lower levels of burnout, and the absence of depressive symptoms were associated with increased odds of having higher levels of health literacy."
- **Problem:** The headline presents burnout as producing worse comprehension, whereas the abstract reports only a cross-sectional association whose direction is unknown (the digest's own caveat says as much).
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to an association, e.g. 'Caregiver burnout is linked to lower health literacy, study finds'."

### Study 12: A blood-test-based 'biological age' predicted pancreatic disease years later in 327,000 adults
**PMID:** 42743792 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Risk estimates attributed to both aging measures** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "researchers calculated two measures of how fast people were aging biologically compared with their chronological age (KDM and Phenotypic Age Acceleration) and followed them for pancreatic outcomes. Those in the most accelerated-aging group had substantially higher risk of chronic pancreatitis (3.6-fold), acute pancreatitis (1.6-fold)..."
- **Abstract says:** "In the fully adjusted Cox model, compared with G1, the PhenoAgeAccel G4 group had higher risks of all pancreatic diseases (HR 1.706)... chronic pancreatitis (3.551...), acute pancreatitis (1.637...), pancreatic cysts (1.547...), pancreatic cancer (1.387...), and pancreas-related mortality (1.442...)."
- **Problem:** All the quoted hazard ratios are for Phenotypic Age Acceleration only; the abstract gives no comparable group-level estimates for KDM, so readers may assume both measures produced these numbers.
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify that the quoted risks come from the Phenotypic Age Acceleration measure (highest vs lowest group)."

### Study 15: Depression in Parkinson's: in real-world practice, standard antidepressants are dropped soonest
**PMID:** 42742618 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline overstates persistence ranking** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Depression in Parkinson's: in real-world practice, standard antidepressants are dropped soonest"
- **Abstract says:** "AAP were less commonly discontinued compared to SSRIs (HR 0.838 [0.771, 0.911])... In PD depression, AAP outlast SSRIs in real-world persistence and non-SSRIs are more commonly switched or augmented."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports only that atypical antipsychotics outlast SSRIs; it does not establish that SSRIs are discontinued soonest of all classes studied (non-SSRI antidepressants and benzodiazepines included).
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'SSRIs are dropped sooner than atypical antipsychotics' rather than 'soonest'."

**Issue B. Direction of age effect not specified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the association grew stronger with age in both sexes"
- **Abstract says:** "The PD-DD association (pooled OR 8.37, 95% CI 5.02-13.98) is age-dependent in both sexes."
- **Problem:** The abstract says only that the association is age-dependent; the direction (stronger with older age) is not stated and cannot be verified from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the association varied with age in both sexes, or mark the direction as unverifiable from the abstract."

**Issue C. Odds ratio described as prevalence ratio** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Depression was roughly eight times more common in the Parkinson's group"
- **Abstract says:** "The PD-DD association (pooled OR 8.37, 95% CI 5.02-13.98)"
- **Problem:** An odds ratio of 8.37 is not the same as depression being eight times more common, particularly for a common outcome.
- **Suggested fix:** "Phrase as 'the odds of depression were about eight times higher in the Parkinson's group'."

### Study 16: Pharmacy vaccination boomed for everyone except the oldest old
**PMID:** 42742864 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Number of pharmacy-employed authors** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "four co-authors are employed by the pharmacy group studied"
- **Abstract says:** "(4)Pharmacy 777, Group Administration, Osborne Park, WA 6016, Australia. — affiliation (4) applies to Tonkin A and Ray K"
- **Problem:** Only two of the six authors carry the pharmacy group affiliation in the abstract, not four.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'two co-authors are employed by the pharmacy group studied'."

**Issue B. Causal explanation for low uptake in oldest adults** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "convenience, not eligibility, appears to be what keeps the oldest adults from being vaccinated"
- **Abstract says:** "Vaccination rate per 100,000 population was highly variable among age groups, with an overall lower vaccination rate among younger individuals (aged 5-19 years) and older people (aged ≥85 years). Vaccination services were concentrated in metropolitan areas and pharmacies near medical centres."
- **Problem:** The study is a descriptive analysis of one pharmacy group's records and offers no data on why the ≥85 group had lower pharmacy vaccination rates; the digest's own caveat notes those adults may be vaccinated elsewhere, which contradicts the confident 'convenience, not eligibility' explanation.
- **Suggested fix:** "Hedge to 'the reasons are not established here, but limited mobility and services clustered in metropolitan areas are plausible contributors'."

**Issue C. 'Access rather than availability' inference** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "pointing to access rather than availability as the barrier"
- **Abstract says:** "Further research should develop strategies to enhance equitable access across geographic areas."
- **Problem:** The abstract identifies geographic concentration of services but does not adjudicate between access and availability as the barrier.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to 'raising questions about access that the study itself cannot answer'."

### Study 17: Frontline staff say municipal aged care isn't set up to prevent decline — only to react to it
**PMID:** 42742822 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. 'Frontline staff' mischaracterizes the sample** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Frontline staff say municipal aged care isn't set up to prevent decline — only to react to it"
- **Abstract says:** "Twenty-two participants, including occupational therapists, development managers, area managers, operations managers, and case managers from seven municipalities, participated in five focus group discussions."
- **Problem:** Most participant categories were managerial roles rather than frontline care staff, so the headline slightly misrepresents whose views were captured.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'staff and managers' or 'municipal care professionals' in the headline."

### Study 18: Who answers the questions matters: a wife and a husband describe the same memory problems differently — and the direction flips between the U.S. and Mexico
**PMID:** 42742165 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Spouse framing in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a wife and a husband describe the same memory problems differently"
- **Abstract says:** "This study investigates the influence of respondents' and informants' gender on informants' assessments of cognitive functioning... Female informants in the United States reported 0.57 more CSI-D symptoms compared to male informants while in Mexico they reported 1.35 fewer symptoms."
- **Problem:** The analysis compares female versus male informants of any relationship type, not spouses rating the same person; the headline implies a within-couple comparison the study did not perform.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'female and male relatives describe the same memory problems differently'."

### Study 19: Erratic test scores may be an early signal in people already showing cognitive problems — but not in those still testing normal
**PMID:** 42741049 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. 'Early signal' implies prediction** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Erratic test scores may be an early signal in people already showing cognitive problems"
- **Abstract says:** "Increased AD biomarker burden was associated with increased IICV among cognitively impaired individuals... The findings underscore the potential of IICV as a sensitive outcome measure. Future studies should replicate findings longitudinally."
- **Problem:** The study is a cross-sectional association between biomarker burden and score scatter; it does not show that variability signals anything ahead of time, and the authors call for longitudinal replication.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as 'Erratic test scores track with Alzheimer's biomarkers in people already showing cognitive problems'."

### Study 21: Older adults who need help at home are losing their teeth in a system where nobody is clearly responsible for their mouths
**PMID:** 42745240 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies tooth-loss findings** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Older adults who need help at home are losing their teeth in a system where nobody is clearly responsible for their mouths"
- **Abstract says:** "With the growing number of people requiring long-term care in Germany, not only is the prevalence of chronic diseases increasing, but oral health is also deteriorating."
- **Problem:** The study is qualitative and measured no clinical oral-health outcomes; tooth loss was never assessed. The phrase reads as a measured finding rather than background context.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe, e.g. "Home care leaves daily mouth care in a gap between medicine, nursing and dentistry, German stakeholders say"."

**Issue B. Fragmentation attributed to participants** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Participants described a fragmented system in which separate responsibilities and reimbursement models leave daily mouth care largely dependent on whoever is at the bedside, often with no training."
- **Abstract says:** "the fragmented German healthcare system, characterised by distinct responsibilities and reimbursement models, often results in poor coordination among those stakeholders (Background); conclusions note the need for "structured interprofessional collaboration, alongside reforms in reimbursement models, caregiver training, digitalisation, and coordination of care"."
- **Problem:** The fragmentation/reimbursement framing appears in the authors' background and conclusions rather than as reported participant statements; the "often with no training" detail is an inference from the call for caregiver training. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute to the authors, e.g. "The authors describe a fragmented system ... and their findings point to a need for caregiver training.""

**Issue C. Downstream harms not from this study** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Poor oral health in frail older adults feeds into pain, malnutrition, pneumonia risk and social withdrawal, yet this study shows the task falls into a gap between medicine, nursing and dentistry that no single professional currently owns."
- **Abstract says:** "Improving oral health care for older adults in need of home-based care requires greater awareness of its relevance to overall health and well-being."
- **Problem:** The specific harms (malnutrition, pneumonia, social withdrawal) are outside the abstract, and "this study shows" overstates what themes from focus groups can demonstrate.
- **Suggested fix:** "Mark the harms as general background and soften to "this study describes" or "stakeholders in this study describe"."

### Study 22: Blood fats may harm memory by way of the hippocampus — and the pathway looks different in women and in APOE4 carriers
**PMID:** 42729514 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Outcome named as memory** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Blood fats may harm memory by way of the hippocampus"
- **Abstract says:** "Cox models identified lipids and brain regions associated with time-to-AD progression... left hippocampus volume as the sole significant mediator"
- **Problem:** The outcome was time to progression to Alzheimer's disease, not memory test performance; no memory measure is reported in the abstract. "Harm" also leans causal for a mediation analysis in observational data, though it is hedged with "may".
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Blood fats may raise Alzheimer's risk by way of the hippocampus" or "are linked to Alzheimer's progression via hippocampal volume"."
