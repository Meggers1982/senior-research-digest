### Study 2: A one-question screen at hospital admission got dying patients palliative care nearly three days sooner
**PMID:** 42806888 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for observational design** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A one-question screen at hospital admission got dying patients palliative care nearly three days sooner"
- **Abstract says:** "An SQ response of "No" at the time of admission was associated with an increased likelihood and earlier timing of PC interventions."
- **Problem:** "Got" attributes the earlier consultations to the screening question, whereas the study is a retrospective propensity-matched cohort that can only show association — which the digest's own caveats acknowledge.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to something like "A one-question screen at admission was linked to palliative care nearly three days sooner.""

**Issue B. Encounters described as admissions** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "analyzed 8,970 propensity-matched hospital admissions from 2020 to 2025"
- **Abstract says:** "After propensity matching, 8,970 encounters were analyzed."
- **Problem:** The unit of analysis was hospital encounters on the Hospital Medicine service, not necessarily unique admissions or patients.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "8,970 propensity-matched hospital encounters.""

### Study 8: A simple health checklist before the next crisis could flag which older adults will struggle most
**PMID:** 42804380 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Ranking and reframing of predictors** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Notice that the strongest protective factors here were things you have some influence over: staying physically active, keeping social connection going so loneliness doesn't take hold, and getting vision problems checked and corrected."
- **Abstract says:** "Poor self-rated health, perceived loneliness, functional impairment, chronic disease burden, visual impairment, higher BMI, and female sex/gender were associated with higher odds of high stress response, whereas older age, higher life satisfaction, greater household wealth, better cognitive function, and greater physical activity were associated with lower odds."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not rank predictors by strength, and visual impairment and loneliness were risk factors rather than protective factors; presenting corrected vision and social connection as the "strongest protective factors" goes beyond the reported results.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say that loneliness and vision impairment were among the risk factors identified and physical activity among the protective ones, without claiming which were strongest."

### Study 9: Childhood hardship still shows up in health decades later — and living alone may sharpen the effect
**PMID:** 42803784 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Living alone" misstates the moderator** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and living alone may sharpen the effect"
- **Abstract says:** "Threat-related ACEs showed a stronger association with lower baseline IC among those not living with children, and deprivation-related ACEs were linked to greater sensory decline ... only in this group."
- **Problem:** The modifier was not living with one's children — which includes people living with a spouse or others — not living alone; the headline implies solitary living was the condition studied.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "— and not living with your children may sharpen the effect.""

**Issue B. Baseline vs. overall capacity** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the link between threat-related adversity and lower capacity was stronger among older adults not living with their children"
- **Abstract says:** "Threat-related ACEs showed a stronger association with lower baseline IC among those not living with children"
- **Problem:** The stronger association applied specifically to baseline intrinsic capacity, not capacity generally or its change over time.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "baseline": "...and lower baseline intrinsic capacity was stronger among...""

### Study 11: VA doctors order far fewer PSA tests in men least likely to benefit than Medicare fee-for-service doctors do
**PMID:** 42804174 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Sample description ambiguous** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "researchers tracked PSA testing and prostate biopsy rates among a 30% random sample of 1.41 million male veterans aged 65 and older (mean age 71)"
- **Abstract says:** "A 30% random sample of male veterans 65 years or older who were dually enrolled in VA and FFS Medicare Parts A and B were included... Among 1.41 million dually enrolled male veterans (mean [SD] age, 71.3 [7.1] years), 8.67 million person-years were observed."
- **Problem:** The 1.41 million veterans are the 30% sample, not the pool from which 30% was drawn; as written a reader could infer the analytic sample was about 423,000.
- **Suggested fix:** ""a 30% national random sample comprising 1.41 million male veterans aged 65 and older (mean age 71)""

**Issue B. Inference about cause of the setting difference** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "This study suggests the pattern is system-driven rather than patient-driven"
- **Abstract says:** "compared with FFS Medicare, VA primary care was associated with lower PSA testing and biopsy rates among veterans least likely to benefit, suggesting closer alignment of testing intensity with likelihood of benefit"
- **Problem:** The retrospective design shows an association between predominant care setting and testing intensity; it cannot separate system behavior from patient self-selection into settings. "Where a man gets his primary care meaningfully changes how likely he is to be tested" is similarly causal.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "testing rates differed by care setting, which points to system-level practice patterns" and avoid "changes.""

### Study 12: An off-kilter sodium level before surgery flagged a 10-fold higher death rate in older patients
**PMID:** 42802297 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mortality outcomes were exploratory** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "in-hospital death in 17.0% versus 1.6%, 90-day death in 27.7% versus 2.8%"
- **Abstract says:** "We assessed our primary outcome, postoperative delirium... Our secondary, exploratory outcomes were postoperative complications, and in-hospital as well as 90-day mortality."
- **Problem:** The headline and "Why it matters" are built on outcomes the authors label secondary and exploratory (based on 8 and 13 deaths), while the primary outcome analysis showed sodium added essentially nothing.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "secondary, exploratory outcome" when introducing the mortality figures, as the digest already does for imprecision in Caveats."

### Study 14: Ten years of brain scans show epilepsy quietly erodes a key memory pathway faster than aging alone
**PMID:** 42800214 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Seizure control not studied** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "which reframes long-term seizure control as potentially brain-protective, not just seizure-preventive"
- **Abstract says:** "Greater longitudinal fornix FA decline was associated with longer disease duration (r = 0.570, P = 0.021)... TLE shows progressive white matter degeneration beyond normal aging, with preferential involvement of the fornix."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports an association with disease duration only; seizure frequency or control was not examined, so the study offers no evidence that better control slows the decline.
- **Suggested fix:** "Frame as "longer disease duration tracked with steeper decline," and note that whether seizure control modifies this was not tested."

**Issue B. Participant ages not reported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "If you or a partner has lived with temporal lobe epilepsy for decades"
- **Abstract says:** "Sixteen patients with unilateral TLE and 15 age- and sex-matched healthy controls underwent diffusion tensor imaging at baseline and approximately 10-year follow-up."
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no participant ages or mean disease duration, so it is unverifiable from the abstract alone that this cohort represents older adults with decades-long epilepsy — the audience the story angles address.
- **Suggested fix:** "State that the abstract does not report participant ages, or soften the angle to "adults living with temporal lobe epilepsy long term.""

### Study 15: Feeling lonely — not simply being alone — tracks with worse menopause symptoms
**PMID:** 42805125 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Effect size characterized as "markedly"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Women who reported loneliness without objective isolation had markedly higher symptom scores and 68% greater odds of severe symptom burden, after adjustment."
- **Abstract says:** "loneliness alone was associated with a higher continuous GCS score (B = 2.91, 95% CI 1.61 to 4.22)... with having severe symptom burden (OR = 1.68, 95% CI 1.13 to 2.50). The GCS median was 17 (interquartile range 12-24)."
- **Problem:** A 2.91-point higher GCS score against a median of 17 (IQR 12-24) is a modest shift; "markedly higher" inflates it.
- **Suggested fix:** ""had modestly higher symptom scores (about 3 points on the Greene scale) and 68% greater odds of severe symptom burden""

### Study 16: Adding a geriatrician to the hip fracture team cut in-hospital deaths by more than half
**PMID:** 42806170 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline from before-after data** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Adding a geriatrician to the hip fracture team cut in-hospital deaths by more than half"
- **Abstract says:** "A 23-month retrospective, observational study... Unadjusted intra-hospital mortality declined significantly (7.7% vs 2.9%, p = 0.005)."
- **Problem:** "Cut" asserts causation from an unadjusted retrospective before-after comparison; secular trends and case-mix differences cannot be ruled out. The digest's own Caveats say causation cannot be established.
- **Suggested fix:** ""In-hospital deaths more than halved after a geriatrician joined the hip fracture team" or "...was followed by less than half the in-hospital death rate.""

### Study 17: Strokes are falling in older adults — and nearly doubling in people under 55
**PMID:** 42777185 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Case-fatality decline applies to young adults** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and 30-day death rates declined modestly overall"
- **Abstract says:** "There was a modest decline in the 30-day CFR in young adults (11.7% in 1993/4 to 9.4% in 2020, trend p = 0.002), driven by intracerebral hemorrhage... and subarachnoid hemorrhage"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports 30-day case fatality only for young adults; "overall" implies it covers all ages, including the older adults the digest's audience belongs to.
- **Suggested fix:** ""and 30-day death rates among the young patients declined modestly (11.7% to 9.4%)""

**Issue B. Attributing the older-adult decline to risk factor control** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The decades-long push to control blood pressure, diabetes and atrial fibrillation appears to be paying off in older adults"
- **Abstract says:** "Observational surveillance study; "The incidence of stroke in young adults is rising over time, while the CFR simultaneously declines. This coincides with a rise in both traditional vascular risk factors and substance use.""
- **Problem:** The study does not test why older-adult incidence fell; attributing it to risk factor control is speculation, and the digest's Caveats acknowledge the design cannot establish why rates changed.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Older adults' stroke rates fell over this period, though this surveillance study cannot say why.""

**Issue C. Risk factor trend generalized to the young population** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "even as the same risk factors spread to younger generations"
- **Abstract says:** "Hypertension, diabetes, atrial fibrillation, and substance use were more common over time (p < 0.001) [among the 2,076 first-ever strokes in young adults]"
- **Problem:** The rising prevalence was measured among young stroke patients in the registry, not in the general population of young adults, so the caregiver-screening angle rests on an extrapolation the data do not support.
- **Suggested fix:** ""even as these risk factors became more common among the young people who had strokes" and soften the caregiver-screening angle accordingly."

### Study 18: What makes a family caregiver feel a loved one died well? Less burden, not more duty alone
**PMID:** 42804262 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline downplays filial piety** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Less burden, not more duty alone"
- **Abstract says:** "filial piety (β = 0.33, p = .001), religious belief (β = 0.35, p = .012), and older age... were significant positive predictors"
- **Problem:** Filial piety was a significant positive predictor of good-death perception, not a null factor; the headline (and the "most modifiable lever is not the family's values" line) could read as dismissing it.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Caregiver burden weighed most — but duty and faith helped" or similar, keeping filial piety's positive role visible."
