### Study 1: A Simple Blood Test Ratio Predicts Survival in People Over 100
**PMID:** 42777518 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Added detail about rural access** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Muscle mass is usually measured with DXA scans that many clinics, especially in primary care and rural areas, simply do not have"
- **Abstract says:** "Traditional sarcopenia assessment tools including dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) are costly and poorly accessible in the primary care."
- **Problem:** The abstract refers only to poor accessibility in primary care; the reference to rural areas is not supported by the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "and rural areas" or attribute it as general context rather than a study finding."

### Study 4: Half of Dialysis Patients Screened at Risk for Muscle Loss — Including the Younger Ones
**PMID:** 42806518 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Fall risk score treated as observed falls** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this study shows the combination is both extremely common and tightly linked to falls and disability"
- **Abstract says:** "M + S patients had lower albumin, TIBC, Karnofsky score and higher comorbidity index and Morse fall risk score (all p < 0.001)"
- **Problem:** The study measured a falls-risk screening score and a physical function score at a single time point, not actual falls or disability events, so "tightly linked to falls" overstates what was observed.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "linked to higher falls-risk and lower physical function scores" to reflect that these were screening measures, not recorded falls."

### Study 5: Diabetes Nearly Triples the Odds of Weak Muscles in Patients Awaiting Liver Transplant
**PMID:** 42692360 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline precision vs. wide confidence interval** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Diabetes Nearly Triples the Odds of Weak Muscles in Patients Awaiting Liver Transplant"
- **Abstract says:** "Regression analysis identified DM as a predictor of reduced handgrip strength (OR 2.72, 95% CI 1.15-6.43; p = 0.022), independently from age."
- **Problem:** The confidence interval spans from barely raised (1.15) to more than six-fold, so a flat "nearly triples" implies far more precision than 119 patients support; the digest never mentions the interval.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "roughly doubles to triples" and add the 95% CI (1.15–6.43) in the body text or caveats."

**Issue B. Omitted dietary detail** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "even though total daily calorie intake was similar between groups"
- **Abstract says:** "while total daily energy intake was similar between both groups, patients with DM consumed a higher relative percentage of dietary protein"
- **Problem:** The digest reports only half of the nutrition comparison; the diabetes group actually ate relatively more protein, which strengthens the point that intake alone did not protect muscle but is a detail readers may want.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that the diabetes group consumed a higher relative proportion of protein despite similar total energy intake."

### Study 6: Does the time of day you lift weights matter? A trial says no
**PMID:** 42791208 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Intervention description incomplete** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "12 weeks of supervised whole-body resistance training either at their personally measured peak performance time of day, at their trough time, or to a no-training control group"
- **Abstract says:** "two supervised whole-body resistance training sessions per week, each consisting of five exercises, three sets and 8-15 repetitions, as well as one moderate-intensity, 30-min endurance cycling session at 60% of the individual's peak oxygen uptake"
- **Problem:** The programme also included a weekly endurance cycling session, so it was not resistance training alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the programme combined two resistance sessions with one 30-minute moderate cycling session per week."

**Issue B. Inference about consistency not tested** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "consistency with twice-weekly resistance training appears to matter far more than finding the physiologically 'optimal' hour"
- **Abstract says:** "Aligning resistance training with an individual's peak performance time did not provide evidence of superiority over trough conditions for skeletal muscle adaptations in older adults."
- **Problem:** The trial compared training times, not adherence or consistency, so it cannot show that consistency "matters far more" — only that timing made no clear difference.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "the hour you choose does not appear to change the result, so pick a time you can stick to" without claiming the trial measured consistency."

### Study 8: Three-year MRI study: fat creeping into muscle, not shrinkage, predicts loss of leg power
**PMID:** 42753450 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Predicts" overstates a change-on-change association** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "fat creeping into muscle, not shrinkage, predicts loss of leg power"
- **Abstract says:** "Higher PC1 scores were independently associated with greater declines in lower-limb power, whereas PC2 was not."
- **Problem:** The analysis related three-year changes in muscle composition to concurrent changes in power, an association rather than a prediction from baseline.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "tracks with" or "was associated with" instead of "predicts"."

**Issue B. Partial coupling figure** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the two processes were largely uncoupled — only about 4% of participants showed robust coupling"
- **Abstract says:** "Directionally coupled degeneration was observed in 38.6% of participants, but robust coupling in only 4.3%."
- **Problem:** Quoting only the 4.3% figure omits that nearly 39% showed directional coupling, which makes the uncoupling sound more complete than reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that about 39% showed directional coupling while only 4.3% showed robust coupling."

### Study 9: Nurse-led exercise program lifted mood and quality of life in older adults with dementia
**PMID:** 42461169 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies between-group mood benefit** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Nurse-led exercise program lifted mood and quality of life in older adults with dementia"
- **Abstract says:** "Postintervention, the intervention group showed significantly higher QoL than the control group (t = 3.63, p < .001). Within-group analyses revealed significant reductions in depressive symptoms (t = 5.12, p < .001) and improvements in QoL (t = 3.36, p = .002)."
- **Problem:** Only quality of life was significantly better than the control group; the reduction in depressive symptoms was a within-group pre-post change with no reported comparison against usual care, so "lifted mood" implies more than the abstract shows. The body text does state this correctly.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword the headline to "lifted quality of life — and mood scores improved within the exercise group" or similar."

### Study 10: A home exercise plan helped older liver patients walk farther and become less frail
**PMID:** 42668018 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal wording for an uncontrolled study** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A home exercise plan helped older liver patients walk farther and become less frail"
- **Abstract says:** "A home-based exercise program ... was feasible, safe, and associated with improvements in functional capacity in patients with CLD"
- **Problem:** The study was a single-arm prospective trial; the authors say "associated with" rather than that the programme caused improvement. The Caveats do note the absence of a control group, but the headline still asserts a causal effect.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "was linked to older liver patients walking farther and scoring less frail"."

**Issue B. Statistic described as a median change** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Six-minute walk distance improved by a median 33 meters"
- **Abstract says:** "6-min walking test (Hodges-Lehmann estimate, 33 m; 95% confidence interval [CI] 14-49)"
- **Problem:** 33 m is a Hodges-Lehmann estimate of the median change, not a simple median improvement, and the confidence interval (14–49 m) is omitted.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "improved by an estimated 33 metres (95% CI 14–49)"."

### Study 12: Muscle Loss Plus Metabolic Syndrome Tracked With Faster Cognitive Decline in a 7-Year Chinese Cohort
**PMID:** 42822208 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Borderline precision of the rapid-decline estimate** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "2.4 times the odds of rapid decline compared with those who had neither"
- **Abstract says:** "a 2.38-fold increased odds of the rapidly declining trajectory (95% confidence interval 1.03-5.47)"
- **Problem:** The confidence interval for the rapid-decline outcome only just excludes 1.0, so the estimate is imprecise; presenting it alongside the firmer persistently-low estimate (CI 1.49–4.68) gives it more weight than the data support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a phrase such as "though that second estimate was borderline, with a confidence interval only just excluding no effect.""

### Study 13: Grip Strength Is Not Just Pass/Fail: Every Extra 5 Kilograms Meant More Recovered Daily Skills After Stroke or Hip Fracture
**PMID:** 42697178 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline verb implies causation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Every Extra 5 Kilograms Meant More Recovered Daily Skills After Stroke or Hip Fracture"
- **Abstract says:** "Each 5-kg higher admission HGS was associated with 3.3 (95% CI, 1.9-4.7) and 6.4 (4.4-8.5) additional recoveries per 100 eligible items after stroke and hip fracture, respectively."
- **Problem:** "Meant" reads as cause-and-effect in a retrospective observational analysis; the body text correctly uses "corresponded to" and the caveats disclaim causation, so the headline is out of step.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "Every Extra 5 Kilograms Tracked With More Recovered Daily Skills After Stroke or Hip Fracture.""

### Study 20: After a heart attack, sarcopenia signaled sharply higher risk over the next two years
**PMID:** 42660496 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Heart attack" narrows acute coronary syndrome** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "After a heart attack, sarcopenia signaled sharply higher risk over the next two years"
- **Abstract says:** "149 individuals aged ≥50 years admitted to a university hospital with a clinical diagnosis of ACS [acute coronary syndrome]"
- **Problem:** Acute coronary syndrome covers unstable angina as well as myocardial infarction, so "heart attack" describes only part of the enrolled population; the body text correctly says acute coronary syndrome.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change the headline to "After an acute coronary event, sarcopenia signaled sharply higher risk over the next two years.""

### Study 22: Chronic stress hormone levels in hair showed no link to muscle loss in a large Brazilian cohort
**PMID:** 42689261 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Null result framed as a settled conclusion** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and suggests that hair cortisol testing is not a useful screening tool for sarcopenia risk"
- **Abstract says:** "no significant association was seen between chronic stress and sarcopenia (OR: 1.106; 95% CI: 0.555-2.206) or sarcopenic obesity (OR: 0.607; 95% CI: 0.162-2.279) ... hair cortisol level ... was not associated with reduced muscle strength, sarcopenia, or sarcopenic obesity"
- **Problem:** The confidence intervals are wide enough to be compatible with meaningful associations in either direction, so a single cross-sectional null cannot rule the measure out as a screening tool; this slightly contradicts the digest's own caveat that "a null result is not proof of no association."
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to something like "and provides no support so far for hair cortisol as a screening marker for sarcopenia risk, though the estimates were imprecise.""

### Study 23: Light Activity Protects Frailer Seniors; Vigorous Exercise Helps the Fit — Canadian Study of 10,871 Adults
**PMID:** 42537596 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal language in headline for observational data** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Light Activity Protects Frailer Seniors; Vigorous Exercise Helps the Fit — Canadian Study of 10,871 Adults"
- **Abstract says:** "more LMPA was associated with lower sarcopenia incidence among High Frailty participants (OR: 0.9593, p = 0.002), but more strenuous activity was associated with lower sarcopenia incidence in Low Frailty participants (OR = 0.8860, p < 0.001)"
- **Problem:** "Protects" and "Helps" assert causation from a prospective observational analysis with a single self-reported baseline activity measure; the body text and caveats correctly say "linked to" and "cannot establish that activity causes lower sarcopenia risk."
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to association language, e.g. "Light Activity Tracks With Less Muscle Loss in Frailer Seniors; Vigorous Activity in the Fit.""

### Study 24: Where You Draw the Line on Grip Strength Changes Who Gets Flagged for Future Disability
**PMID:** 42556116 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Deterministic prediction language** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Grip strength is a simple, cheap test that predicts whether you'll struggle with dressing, bathing, or getting out of a chair years from now."
- **Abstract says:** "Probable sarcopenia was associated with worse trajectories of disability progression in BADLs only when LMS was defined by higher cut-offs: <30/20 kg (β = 0.013; 95%CI: 0.007-0.020) ... with a modest effect size for severe sarcopenia when LMS was defined as <36/23 kg (β = 0.021; 95%CI: 0.011-0.031)"
- **Problem:** The study reports group-level associations with small beta coefficients for disability trajectories, not individual-level prediction of whether a given person will become disabled.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Grip strength is a simple, cheap test that, on average, tracks with how people's ability to dress, bathe and rise from a chair changes over the years.""

### Study 25: Weak Muscles — Not Extra Weight — Track With Depression and Memory Problems in Older Adults
**PMID:** 42741804 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Confused comparison of the two muscle tests** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A quick sit-to-stand or grip test performed just as well as the more equipment-dependent handgrip measure, making it a practical screening option in community and residential settings."
- **Abstract says:** "Relative to the normal group, the combined HGS-based group was associated with depressive symptoms (odds ratio 4.18 ...) and cognitive impairment (2.58 ...); 30-s STS-based estimates were 2.99 and 1.89 ... Discriminative ability was similar for the two tests."
- **Problem:** The sentence compares "sit-to-stand or grip test" against the "handgrip measure" — i.e. grip against itself. The abstract's comparison is between the 30-second sit-to-stand test and handgrip strength.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "A quick 30-second sit-to-stand test discriminated about as well as the equipment-dependent handgrip measure, making it a practical screening option.""

### Study 26: Staff and Residents Agree Diet Matters for Muscle Loss — but Nobody Knows Who's Responsible
**PMID:** 42807943 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported "main reason" attribution** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Participants in this study said vague advice was the main reason good intentions went nowhere."
- **Abstract says:** "Anti-inflammatory dietary management is constrained by inadequate intake and multiple implementation barriers ... Respondents expect more concrete, individualized, and continuous support ... barriers related to individual capability, environmental opportunity, and behavioral motivation"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes multiple barriers and a desire for more concrete guidance, but does not rank vague advice as the main reason implementation failed.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Participants in this study asked for concrete, individualized guidance rather than general advice, and described multiple barriers to following it.""

### Study 27: Fatty Muscle on a CT Scan Predicted Colon Cancer Return Better Than Muscle Loss Alone
**PMID:** 42648596 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Wrong study period** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Researchers reviewed records of 415 patients (mean age 63.7) who had colorectal cancer surgery at a Louisiana hospital between 2018 and 2024"
- **Abstract says:** "Patients discussed at the CRC multidisciplinary team meeting between 2015 and 2021 at a single institution were collated prospectively ... 615 patients with stage II-III colon cancer underwent surgery"
- **Problem:** The dates given (2018–2024) do not match the abstract's 2015–2021 recruitment window. (The sentence also appears to have borrowed the N and setting text from a different study before correcting to 615 patients at a Glasgow institution elsewhere in the entry.)
- **Suggested fix:** "State "615 patients with stage II–III colon cancer treated at a single Glasgow institution, identified through multidisciplinary team meetings between 2015 and 2021.""

**Issue B. Population described as older patients** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It suggests that muscle quality, not just muscle size, carries prognostic information for older cancer patients"
- **Abstract says:** "615 patients with stage II-III colon cancer underwent surgery and 120 developed a recurrence (no age restriction or mean age reported)"
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no age data or age-based inclusion criterion, so the finding cannot be attributed specifically to older patients.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "for patients with stage II–III colon cancer" and note that the abstract does not report participants' ages."
