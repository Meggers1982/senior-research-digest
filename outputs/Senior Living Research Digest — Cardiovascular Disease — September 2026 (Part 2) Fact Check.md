# Fact-Check Report: Senior Living Research Digest — Cardiovascular Disease — September 2026
**Checked:** 2026-09-26 | **Studies reviewed:** 23
**Primary audience:** older adults and seniors | **Secondary audience:** families, caregivers, and senior living professionals

### Study 1: Your walking speed, mood and hearing may predict heart trouble as well as your blood pressure does
**PMID:** 42612584 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies untested comparison** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Your walking speed, mood and hearing may predict heart trouble as well as your blood pressure does"
- **Abstract says:** "impaired IC alone (HR 1.53, 95% CI 1.34-1.74) and hypertension alone (HR 1.68, 95% CI 1.46-1.93) were associated with higher CVD risk... Adding IC impairment and hypertension modestly improved model discrimination"
- **Problem:** The study reported separate hazard ratios and a modest combined improvement in discrimination; it did not formally compare the predictive performance of intrinsic capacity against blood pressure, so 'as well as your blood pressure does' is an inference.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like 'carry heart-risk information on top of your blood pressure' or 'were linked to heart trouble about as strongly as hypertension was in this analysis'."

### Study 2: A blood-protein fingerprint of healthy eating tracked lower heart disease risk over 25 years
**PMID:** 42617399 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Attenuation treated as demonstrated mechanism** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It offers a biological explanation for how diet quality translates into heart health — apparently working largely through the standard risk factors clinicians already measure and treat."
- **Abstract says:** "these associations were attenuated after further adjustment for known cardiometabolic risk factors (1-SD: HR 0.94, [0.85, 1.04]; T3 vs T1: 0.81 [0.63, 1.03])"
- **Problem:** Attenuation after covariate adjustment is suggestive but is not a formal mediation analysis; the abstract only says the findings 'provide insight into the mechanisms' and calls for further study.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the association weakened once blood pressure, lipids and glucose were accounted for, which is consistent with — but does not prove — those factors being the pathway."

### Study 3: The artery disease in your legs that kills more people than many cancers — and is routinely missed
**PMID:** 42772316 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline conflates mortality rate with number of deaths** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The artery disease in your legs that kills more people than many cancers"
- **Abstract says:** "the overall 5-year mortality is between 30% and 50%, thus even surpassing outcomes of severe cancer and acute myocardial infarction"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes a higher five-year mortality rate among diagnosed patients, not a higher absolute number of deaths than cancers.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'whose five-year survival is worse than that of many severe cancers'."

**Issue B. Ankle-brachial index recommendation not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "ask your doctor specifically about peripheral arterial disease and whether an ankle-brachial index test is appropriate"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract discusses underdiagnosis and preventive measures but does not mention the ankle-brachial index or any specific diagnostic test."
- **Problem:** A specific test recommendation is attributed to a narrative Series paper that does not mention it in the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Flag as 'Unverifiable from abstract only' or attribute the ABI suggestion to general clinical practice rather than this paper."

**Issue C. Claim about reduced walking distance as first sign** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "reduced walking distance is often the first and only sign, and it is easy to misattribute to arthritis or general frailty"
- **Abstract says:** "Individuals with peripheral arterial disease frequently remain asymptomatic for many years, often decades, and are typically diagnosed only at an advanced stage"
- **Problem:** The abstract says patients are often asymptomatic; it makes no claim that reduced walking distance is the first and only sign.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to 'symptoms can be subtle or absent for years' or note the detail is not verifiable from the abstract."

### Study 4: People over 80 are dying less of heart disease — and living longer with disability instead
**PMID:** 42594758 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline asserts the conclusion the authors disclaim** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "People over 80 are dying less of heart disease — and living longer with disability instead"
- **Abstract says:** "The findings support greater emphasis on function-preserving geriatric care and long-term care, but do not by themselves establish individual-level expansion or compression of morbidity... no country reached a YLD-over-YLL crossover"
- **Problem:** 'Living longer with disability instead' is precisely the individual-level expansion-of-morbidity claim the authors say their data cannot establish; the digest's own caveat contradicts the headline and 'Why it matters' text.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'a growing share of their health loss comes from disability rather than early death' and keep the aggregate-level framing throughout."

**Issue B. YLL rates described as death rates** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Death rates fell in all 10 countries (median −31.3%), while disability rates rose in six"
- **Abstract says:** "YLL ASRs declined in all 10 countries (median change, -31.3%), whereas YLD ASRs increased in six (median change, +1.9%)"
- **Problem:** The measures are age-standardized years of life lost and years lived with disability, not crude death or disability rates.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write 'rates of years of life lost fell... rates of years lived with disability rose in six'."

### Study 5: Heart risk factors were linked to more self-reported memory and thinking complaints in Hispanic/Latina women
**PMID:** 42424748 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline misstates the finding and the domains** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Heart risk factors were linked to more self-reported memory and thinking complaints in Hispanic/Latina women"
- **Abstract says:** "Overall, females reported more SCD in language and visuospatial domains. This was less pronounced for 1) language SCD at lower levels of CVD risk, and 2) visuospatial SCD with higher language acculturation."
- **Problem:** The study's primary finding is a sex difference in language and visuospatial subjective decline; cardiovascular risk appeared only as a modifier of that sex gap, not as a direct association with 'memory and thinking complaints'.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'The gap between Hispanic/Latina women's and men's language-related cognitive complaints was narrower among those with lower cardiovascular risk'."

**Issue B. Acculturation measure specified as English-language** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the gap in visuospatial complaints was smaller among those with higher English-language acculturation"
- **Abstract says:** "visuospatial SCD with higher language acculturation"
- **Problem:** The abstract refers to 'language acculturation' without specifying English; the specification is an inference.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'higher language acculturation' as the abstract does."

### Study 6: In Diabetes, Depression Raises Heart Risk — and Blood Sugar Doesn't Explain Why
**PMID:** 42624218 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal verb in headline** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "In Diabetes, Depression Raises Heart Risk — and Blood Sugar Doesn't Explain Why"
- **Abstract says:** "Depression is associated with greater risks for incident cardiovascular disease and all-cause death in people with type 2 diabetes."
- **Problem:** 'Raises' asserts causation from an observational registry analysis; the abstract consistently uses 'associated with'.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'Depression Is Linked to Higher Heart Risk — and Blood Sugar Doesn't Explain Why'."

**Issue B. Named mediators not specified in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "standard cardiometabolic risk factors such as HbA1c, blood pressure and cholesterol explained only a small fraction of the excess risk"
- **Abstract says:** "We examined the mediating effects of eight cardiometabolic risk factors... Mediation analyses revealed minor contributions from metabolic risk factors"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not list which eight cardiometabolic factors were tested, so the specific examples are unverifiable from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'eight standard cardiometabolic risk factors' without naming specific ones, or note the detail is from the full text."

**Issue C. Cohort demographics attributed to whole sample** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "571,129 Chinese adults with type 2 diabetes (mean age 60, two-thirds women)"
- **Abstract says:** "Among 571,129 people with type 2 diabetes, 7.5% had depression (mean age 60.4 years, 66.1% women, mean HbA1c 7.2%)"
- **Problem:** The abstract places the age and sex figures in a parenthetical immediately after the depression subgroup, so it is ambiguous whether they describe the whole cohort or the depressed group.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the figures with a hedge, or omit them if the abstract's referent is unclear."

### Study 11: For the Oldest Kidney Patients, a Lower-Quality Donor Kidney May Not Beat Staying on Dialysis
**PMID:** 42759976 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Countries vs. regions** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Using registry data from five European countries"
- **Abstract says:** "European Renal Association Registry data from five countries or regions: Catalonia (Spain), Denmark, France, Norway, and the UK."
- **Problem:** Catalonia is a region of Spain, not a country; the abstract deliberately says "countries or regions."
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "five European countries or regions (Catalonia, Denmark, France, Norway and the UK)"."

**Issue B. Superlative framing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is the clearest evidence yet that for the oldest patients, that trade-off may not extend life"
- **Abstract says:** "the oldest recipients had a smaller and more statistically uncertain estimated survival advantage over continued dialysis"
- **Problem:** "Clearest evidence yet" is a comparative claim about the whole literature that the abstract does not make, and the abstract stresses statistical uncertainty in exactly this group.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "adds large-scale evidence that, for the oldest patients, the survival advantage shrinks and becomes statistically uncertain.""

**Issue C. Background claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Older adults facing kidney failure are often offered marginal-quality kidneys to shorten waiting time"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract describes donor quality categories and survival estimates but makes no statement about how often older patients are offered expanded-criteria kidneys or why."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute to background/context or drop."

### Study 12: Fear of Moving After a Heart Event Is Common — and Exercise Programs Appear to Ease It
**PMID:** 42762436 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Effect stated more firmly than authors** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Exercise interventions were associated with meaningfully lower fear-of-movement scores (standardized mean difference -0.91)"
- **Abstract says:** "The pooled effect estimate indicated a numerical trend toward lower kinesiophobia scores with exercise interventions (SMD = -0.91; 95% CI [-1.33, -0.49]; I2 = 93%), although all included studies were rated as having moderate risk of bias."
- **Problem:** The authors describe the pooled result only as a "numerical trend" and say heterogeneity and quality "restrict definitive inferences"; "meaningfully lower" overstates their own characterization.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use the authors' language: "pooled results showed a trend toward lower fear-of-movement scores (SMD -0.91), though the authors caution that heterogeneity and study quality limit firm conclusions.""

**Issue B. Prevalence claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Fear of exertion is a major and under-recognized reason older heart patients avoid rehabilitation"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract reports only pooled effect sizes on kinesiophobia scores; it gives no prevalence data and does not restrict the population to older adults."
- **Problem:** Prevalence and age-specific framing are unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Frame as context ("clinicians describe fear of movement as common after cardiac events") or drop the age qualifier."

**Issue C. "Supervised" not specified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this review suggests supervised exercise itself can help break that cycle"
- **Abstract says:** "Ten studies involving 1,505 cardiovascular disease patients ... exploratory subgroup analyses detected numerical differences in pooled effect sizes across different exercise parameters, yet these observational trends cannot support definitive exercise recommendations"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not specify that interventions were supervised, and explicitly says exercise parameters cannot support recommendations.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "structured exercise programs" rather than "supervised exercise.""

### Study 16: Having Several Chronic Conditions Doubles Death Risk — And Mixing Physical and Mental Illness Is the Riskiest Combination
**PMID:** 42475970 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal verb in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Having Several Chronic Conditions Doubles Death Risk"
- **Abstract says:** "The pooled hazard ratios ... for multiple chronic conditions and risk of all-cause mortality were 2.015 (95% CI:1.870-2.171) in the general population (meta-analysis of observational cohort studies)"
- **Problem:** "Doubles" reads as causal for a meta-analysis of observational data; the body and caveats correctly say association.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Having Several Chronic Conditions Is Linked to Double the Death Risk.""

**Issue B. Precision of the physical-mental estimate** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "most strikingly, those with combined physical and mental health conditions (HR 4.01)"
- **Abstract says:** "4.006 (95% CI:2.094-7.666, I2:99.8%, n = 5) in patients with physical-mental multimorbidity"
- **Problem:** The most dramatic figure in the digest comes from just five studies with a confidence interval spanning 2.1 to 7.7 and near-total heterogeneity; the caveats flag thin evidence only for the cancer-mortality estimate.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "(based on just five studies, 95% CI 2.1–7.7)" or extend the caveat to cover this estimate."

### Study 17: One in Five Hospitalized Older Patients Had a Swallowing Problem Nobody Had Flagged
**PMID:** 42570796 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Headline overstates undiagnosed prevalence** — Severity: Major

- **As written:** "One in Five Hospitalized Older Patients Had a Swallowing Problem Nobody Had Flagged"
- **Abstract says:** "Overall, 160 patients (17%) screened positive for dysphagia risk ... Among these, 74 (46%) had previously been diagnosed with dysphagia. Ultimately, 118 patients underwent further assessment, with dysphagia confirmed in 111 (94%)."
- **Problem:** 17% screened positive (about one in six), and only about half of those (54%) had no prior diagnosis — so roughly one in eleven screened patients had a previously unflagged problem, not one in five. The headline roughly doubles the undiagnosed rate.
- **Suggested fix:** ""About One in Six Hospitalized Older Patients Screened Positive for Swallowing Trouble — and Half Had Never Been Diagnosed.""

**Issue B. 32% attributed to cardiovascular admissions** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "screening positivity was actually higher among patients admitted for non-pulmonary reasons, including cardiovascular disease (32%), than for lung conditions (21%)"
- **Abstract says:** "A positive screening test was identified in 21% of patients admitted with pulmonary diseases. In comparison, 32% of patients admitted with other medical conditions, including those with a working or admission diagnosis of cardiovascular disease, non-pulmonary infections, or cancer, screened positive."
- **Problem:** The 32% is the rate for the whole non-pulmonary group (cardiovascular, non-pulmonary infection and cancer combined); no cardiovascular-specific rate is reported. The story angle repeats this by saying "cardiac and infection admissions screened positive more often than pulmonary ones."
- **Suggested fix:** "State that 32% of all non-pulmonary admissions (a group that included cardiovascular, infection and cancer cases) screened positive, and remove the diagnosis-specific comparison from the story angle."

**Issue C. Screening sensitivity implied** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this study shows a bedside test taking a few minutes catches most of it"
- **Abstract says:** "Ultimately, 118 patients underwent further assessment, with dysphagia confirmed in 111 (94%) ... simple bedside screening can effectively identify patients at risk of dysphagia"
- **Problem:** The 94% is the confirmation rate among screen-positives (positive predictive value); the study cannot show what proportion of true cases the screen "catches," since screen-negatives were not assessed.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "nearly all patients who screened positive and were fully assessed did have dysphagia" rather than implying the test catches most cases."

### Study 19: Heart Disease — Not Stroke — Tracks With Faster Memory Loss Across Four Western Countries
**PMID:** 42767983 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Cohorts vs. countries in headline** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Heart Disease — Not Stroke — Tracks With Faster Memory Loss Across Four Western Countries"
- **Abstract says:** "pooled data from five multinational cohorts ... (83,159 adults ≥50 years) ... heart disease correlated with accelerated cognitive decline across four Western cohorts"
- **Problem:** The four Western cohorts (HRS, SHARE, ELSA, MHAS) span many countries — SHARE alone covers much of Europe, and the pooled data come from 24 countries — so "four Western countries" is factually wrong.
- **Suggested fix:** ""...Across Four Western Cohorts.""

**Issue B. Caveat wording on measurement** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Cardiovascular diagnoses and medication use were measured inconsistently across studies."
- **Abstract says:** "This heterogeneity may be partially explained by variations in the composition of heart disease and the use of medication across five cohorts."
- **Problem:** The authors point to variation in the composition of heart disease and in medication use, not explicitly to inconsistent measurement.
- **Suggested fix:** ""The mix of heart conditions and medication use differed across cohorts, which the authors say may partly explain the heterogeneity.""

### Study 20: Belly Fat Plus a Weak Grip: A Two-Part Warning Sign for Heart Disease and Stroke
**PMID:** 42447644 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Incremental value claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Two simple, inexpensive measures — waist-based fat distribution and how hard you can squeeze — together flag cardiovascular vulnerability better than either alone"
- **Abstract says:** "Jointly, the highest risk of CVD was observed in participants with higher VAI (≥2.04) and lower handgrip strength (the quartile 1-2) (HR, 1.51; 95%CI:1.24-1.85). High VAI and low handgrip strength were independently associated with increased CVD risk."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a joint category with the highest risk (HR 1.51 vs 1.36 for high VAI alone) but no test of added predictive value or interaction, so "flag ... better than either alone" is stronger than the data shown.
- **Suggested fix:** ""People with both high visceral adiposity and weak grip had the highest risk of any group.""

**Issue B. Outcome ascertainment caveat** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Cardiovascular events were based on self-reported physician diagnoses."
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract states only that the primary outcome was incident CVD in the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study; it does not describe how events were ascertained."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only (though consistent with CHARLS methodology).
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute to CHARLS methodology generally or note it is not specified in the abstract."

### Study 21: Vitamin D and Fish Oil Failed to Move Alzheimer's Blood Markers Over Four Years
**PMID:** 42746141 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Subgroup analyses mischaracterized as exploratory** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Exploratory subgroup analyses hinted at possible effects among men and among Black participants, but these were not corrected for multiple testing."
- **Abstract says:** "Repeated-measures models were used, with sex and race were pre-specified effect modifiers... Pre-specified subgroup analyses suggested potential interactions by sex and race."
- **Problem:** The abstract states sex and race were pre-specified effect modifiers; calling the analyses "exploratory" understates their planned status, even though the authors do caution they were uncorrected for multiple testing and hypothesis-generating.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Pre-specified subgroup analyses hinted at possible effects among men and among Black participants, though they were not corrected for multiple testing and the authors call them hypothesis-generating.""

### Study 22: A Combined Blood Test Score May Sharpen Heart Risk Prediction in Older Adults
**PMID:** 42392540 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Outcome for the 45–59 age group** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the association was strongest for heart disease in those aged 45–59 and for stroke in those aged 60–69 (69% higher risk)"
- **Abstract says:** "The risk was particularly pronounced in the age group of 45-59 years, with an aHR of 1.325 (95% CI 1.131-1.552) for cardiovascular diseases and 1.687 (95% CI 1.143-2.490) for stroke in the 60-69 years age group."
- **Problem:** The 45–59 estimate is for composite cardiovascular disease, not specifically "heart disease." The digest narrows the outcome label.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change "heart disease in those aged 45–59" to "cardiovascular disease overall in those aged 45–59.""

**Issue B. "Blood test score" framing in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A Combined Blood Test Score May Sharpen Heart Risk Prediction in Older Adults"
- **Abstract says:** "Baseline TyG-BMI and hs-CRP were measured."
- **Problem:** TyG-BMI combines blood triglycerides and glucose with body mass index, so the composite is not purely a blood test; the headline slightly overstates the simplicity of the measure.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword, e.g., "A Combined Blood-and-Body-Measure Score May Sharpen Heart Risk Prediction.""

**Issue C. Age range of study population** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Using 9,190 participants from a nationwide Chinese health and retirement cohort who had no history of stroke or heart disease at baseline"
- **Abstract says:** "Data from 9190 participants in a nationwide health and retirement longitudinal study were analyzed... in an aging Chinese population, with the strongest associations observed for CVD in adults aged 45-59 years"
- **Problem:** The cohort includes middle-aged adults from age 45, yet the headline frames the finding as applying to "Older Adults"; the body text never states the age range.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note in the study description that participants were middle-aged and older (from about age 45)."
