### Study 5: Botox for Depression? Pooled Trials Show Benefit — With Interest for Patients on Many Medications
**PMID:** 42669382 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Participant age not reported in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "while recognizing the evidence base is still small and mostly not in people over 65"
- **Abstract says:** "Eight RCTs comprising 636 participants were included. (No participant age distribution is reported.)"
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no information about participant ages, so the statement that the trials were 'mostly not in people over 65' cannot be verified from the abstract alone, even though it is also reasonably framed as a caution.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'the trials did not report being conducted in older adults specifically' or label the point as unverifiable from the published abstract."

### Study 6: AI Chatbots Aren't Ready to Assess Older Adults' Mental Health — and Can Show Ageist Bias

### Study 8: Not All "Fall Risk" Drugs Carry Equal Risk: Blood Pressure Pills Weren't the Problem
**PMID:** 42308749 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal-sounding phrasing for an observational association** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "any use raised the odds of fall injury 35%, and each additional such drug raised the odds of a non-injurious fall by 18%"
- **Abstract says:** "other FRIDs use (yes, N= 483/1273; AOR = 1.35; 95%CI: 1.03-1.76) had increased fall injuries and other FRIDs count (AOR = 1.18; 95%CI: 1.06-1.31) had increased non-injurious falls"
- **Problem:** 'Raised the odds' reads as a causal effect of the drugs in a cohort study; the Caveats correctly note causation cannot be established, so the body text is stronger than the design supports.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'was associated with 35% higher odds' and 'each additional such drug was associated with 18% higher odds'."

### Study 11: Delirium Plus Dementia Plus Poor Nutrition: A Combination That Cut Survival in Half
**PMID:** 42896356 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for observational data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Delirium Plus Dementia Plus Poor Nutrition: A Combination That Cut Survival in Half"
- **Abstract says:** "Older adults with delirium superimposed on dementia and malnutrition have an increased risk of short- and long-term mortality after hospitalisation for delirium. This combination can serve as an indicator for the identification of high-risk patients."
- **Problem:** "Cut survival in half" attributes a causal effect to the combination, while this is a retrospective observational cohort reporting differences in median survival between groups; the body text and caveats correctly say association only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like "A Combination Linked to Half the Survival Time After Discharge.""

**Issue B. Admission window vs pandemic timing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Admissions predate and span the start of the pandemic period."
- **Abstract says:** "554 patients ... admitted between 01/04/2019 and 31/01/2020 with acute delirium at a single hospital (United Kingdom)"
- **Problem:** All admissions ended 31 January 2020, before the UK pandemic period began; it is the six-year follow-up, not the admissions, that spans the pandemic.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Admissions all occurred before the pandemic, though the six-year follow-up period spans it.""

### Study 13: More Pills, More Risk: Each Added Medication Raised Odds of a Risky Prescription by 55%
**PMID:** 42934542 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal verb in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Each Added Medication Raised Odds of a Risky Prescription by 55%"
- **Abstract says:** "each additional medication was associated with 55% higher odds of PIM use (OR 1.55, 95% CI 1.40-1.73)"

### Study 20: In Black Older Adults, Disease Burden — Not Pill Count — Tracked With Weaker Thinking Scores
**PMID:** 42842789 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Clinical advice from cross-sectional data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "it is also a reason to ask your clinician about managing the underlying conditions aggressively, since disease burden was what tracked with cognitive scores here"
- **Abstract says:** "Higher modified CCI was associated with lower cognitive performance (B = -0.071; 95% CI: -0.130, -0.012). Polypharmacy was not independently associated with cognition after modified CCI adjustment"
- **Problem:** A single cross-sectional association cannot support the implication that treating comorbidities more aggressively will protect cognition; the digest's own caveats note the design cannot establish causation or direction.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to noting that disease burden, not pill count, was what tracked with cognition in this snapshot, and that whether better disease control protects cognition was not tested."

### Study 22: Survey of Nearly 5,000 European Clinicians Finds Big Gaps in Who Feels Equipped to Tackle Medication Overload
**PMID:** 42969553 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. 'Consistently' overstates regional difference** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "professionals in EU-27 countries consistently reported higher scores than those outside"
- **Abstract says:** "EU-27 professionals reported higher scores than non-EU-27 professionals across most topics (P < .001)."
- **Problem:** The abstract says 'most topics,' not all; 'consistently' implies a uniform difference across every domain.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'professionals in EU-27 countries reported higher scores than those outside across most topics.'"

**Issue B. Respondents were health AND social care professionals, not only clinicians** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Survey of Nearly 5,000 European Clinicians"
- **Abstract says:** "Four thousand seven hundred eighty-eight professionals from 10 core health and social care groups were included."
- **Problem:** The sample included social workers and other non-clinical groups, and also covered non-EU-27 countries; 'clinicians' narrows the population.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'health and social care professionals' in the headline."

**Issue C. Secondary analysis not stated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "As part of the COST Action PROGRAMMING project, researchers surveyed 4,788 health and social care professionals"
- **Abstract says:** "Secondary analysis of a COST Action multinational multilingual web-based survey ... (PROGRAMMING)"
- **Problem:** The digest presents the work as a primary survey; the abstract labels it a secondary analysis of existing survey data.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note in The study or Caveats that this is a secondary analysis of the PROGRAMMING survey."

### Study 24: What Makes Families Open to Stopping a Nursing Home Resident's Medications
**PMID:** 42850595 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Medication count linked to burden, not concern about stopping** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "families whose relative has had an adverse drug event, a long medication list, or high out-of-pocket drug costs report the most burden and the most concern about stopping"
- **Abstract says:** "adverse drug events with higher burden and concerns about stopping and lower appropriateness; medication cost with higher burden and concerns about stopping; number of medications with higher burden"
- **Problem:** Lumping all three predictors together implies number of medications was also associated with concerns about stopping, which it was not after FDR correction.
- **Suggested fix:** "Separate the predictors: adverse drug events and cost tracked with both burden and concerns about stopping; number of medications tracked with burden only."

### Study 26: Tools for Safely Stopping Medicines Near the End of Life Show Promise — and Shaky Evidence
**PMID:** 42799555 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Claim of no superior tool not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the review found no single tool with clearly superior clinical outcomes, so tool choice should be guided by fit with your setting rather than claimed effectiveness"
- **Abstract says:** "Clinical outcomes comparing deprescribing with control interventions were mostly non-significant ... High risk of bias associated with these results limits strong conclusions regarding the validity and clinical impact of deprescribing tools"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports comparisons against control interventions, not head-to-head comparisons between tools, so 'no single tool with clearly superior clinical outcomes' is an inference. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to 'the review could not identify which tool works best, because comparisons with usual care were mostly non-significant and risk of bias was moderate to high.'"

### Study 28: Nurses Are Often the First to Spot Medications That No Longer Help at the End of Life
**PMID:** 42519959 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline overstates nurses' role** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Nurses Are Often the First to Spot Medications That No Longer Help at the End of Life"
- **Abstract says:** "Nurses play a central role in medication reconciliation, identifying potentially inappropriate medications, facilitating shared decision-making, and monitoring patient response after medication withdrawal."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes a central role, not evidence that nurses are typically the first to identify such medications; the review reported no data on who identifies them first.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'Nurses Play a Central Role in Spotting Medications That No Longer Help at the End of Life.'"
