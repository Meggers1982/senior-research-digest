# Fact-Check Report: Senior Living Research Digest — Polypharmacy — October 2026
**Checked:** 2026-10-06 | **Studies reviewed:** 24
**Primary audience:** older adults and seniors | **Secondary audience:** families, caregivers, and senior living professionals

### Study 1: Frail patients with atrial fibrillation are less likely to get stroke-preventing blood thinners — and the gap is widest in Asia
**PMID:** 41345290 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

**Issue A. Mildly causal phrasing for an observational association** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Frailty and a long medication list appear to make doctors hesitant to prescribe the anticoagulants that prevent AF-related strokes"
- **Abstract says:** "Being frail was associated with a higher likelihood of not being prescribed OAC, particularly in Asian patients."
- **Problem:** The registry shows an association between frailty and lower anticoagulant prescribing; it did not examine prescriber reasoning. The phrasing attributes a motive to clinicians, though "appear to" hedges it and the Caveats note the observational design.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "Frail patients and those on long medication lists were less likely to be prescribed anticoagulants — yet those same patients are at the highest risk of bad outcomes.""

### Study 2: Four years of tracking older adults shows frailty rarely reverses — and certain drug classes track with decline
**PMID:** 42669283 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Cohort descriptor not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "single Mexican cohort of social-security beneficiaries, so may not generalize"
- **Abstract says:** "Secondary analysis of data from the study "Cohort of Obesity, Sarcopenia and Frailty of Older Mexican Adults". The baseline sample included adults ≥ 60 years, with annual monitoring for four years."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes a community-dwelling Mexican cohort but does not state that participants were social-security (IMSS) beneficiaries. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "single community-dwelling Mexican cohort" unless the enrolment source is confirmed in the full text."

### Study 3: Why stopping unnecessary medicines near the end of life is so hard — and what actually makes it work
**PMID:** 42533730 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported causal explanation in "Why it matters"** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Potentially inappropriate medicines persist at the end of life largely because the conversation never happens — not because patients refuse"
- **Abstract says:** "Patients with palliative needs often use potentially inappropriate medications, leading to adverse health outcomes. Deprescribing remains poorly enacted in primary care... Four themes were established: (i) initial stance towards deprescribing: shaped by values, general perceptions of (de)prescribing, and competencies..."
- **Problem:** The review maps influences on deprescribing; it does not quantify why inappropriate medicines persist, and it explicitly identifies patients' initial stance and values as one of the four influencing themes, so ruling out patient reluctance overstates the findings.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "The review identifies what shapes whether the deprescribing conversation happens at all — patients' and clinicians' starting attitudes, how the conversation is held, and how teams are organized.""

**Issue B. Geographic claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "findings are drawn largely from European and other high-income primary care systems"
- **Abstract says:** "MEDLINE, Embase, CINAHL, and PsycINFO were searched from 2000 to January 2025... 23 studies were included."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not report the countries or health systems of the included studies. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the geographic claim or verify the included studies' settings from the full text."

### Study 4: Hospice nurses are often the ones who spot the pills that no longer help
**PMID:** 42519959 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Safely reducible" overstates the evidence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It identifies the specific medication categories most likely to be safely reducible in late-stage illness"
- **Abstract says:** "Evidence indicates that long-term preventive medications near the end-of-life, including statins, antihypertensives, hypoglycemics, antithrombotic agents, and vitamins, often provide limited short-term benefit while increasing the risk of side effects and treatment burden."
- **Problem:** The review reports limited benefit and added burden; it does not report safety outcomes of withdrawal, so "safely reducible" imputes a finding the abstract does not make.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "the categories most often judged to add burden without short-term benefit in late-stage illness.""

**Issue B. "Underused" tools not stated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "structured tools like STOPPFrail and OncPal exist and are underused"
- **Abstract says:** "37 studies met the inclusion criteria, including... evaluations of frameworks such as STOPPFrail and OncPal... Despite this, deprescribing remains inconsistent and frequently reactive rather than proactive."
- **Problem:** The abstract says deprescribing practice is inconsistent and reactive but does not state that these specific tools are underused.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "structured tools like STOPPFrail and OncPal exist, yet deprescribing remains inconsistent and reactive.""

### Study 5: Pelvic fractures after 50 look a lot like hip fractures — and may deserve the same seriousness
**PMID:** 42832039 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Rounding of mortality rate** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "yet one in six of these patients died within a year"
- **Abstract says:** "New major fracture rates (6.6% vs 8.4%) and 1-year mortality rates (15% vs 17%) were comparable."
- **Problem:** Pelvic fracture one-year mortality was 15%, closer to one in seven; "one in six" applies better to the acetabular group (17%) and nudges the pelvic figure upward.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "about one in seven pelvic fracture patients (15%) died within a year" or "15–17% of these patients died within a year.""

### Study 6: Botox for depression: pooled trial data hint at a drug-free alternative for people already on many medicines
**PMID:** 42669382 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Drug-free" mischaracterizes the intervention** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "pooled trial data hint at a drug-free alternative for people already on many medicines"
- **Abstract says:** "These findings provide valuable insights into a non-systemic therapeutic alternative, particularly for patients intolerant to oral antidepressants or those with polypharmacy burdens."
- **Problem:** Botulinum toxin A is a drug administered by injection; the abstract calls it "non-systemic," not drug-free, and the headline could mislead readers into thinking no medication is involved.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "a non-systemic alternative for people already on many medicines.""

**Issue B. Drug-interaction claim not tested** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a treatment that does not add another daily systemic drug — and does not interact with existing ones"
- **Abstract says:** "While overall adverse event rates were similar to controls (RR = 1.04), BoNT/A was associated with transient local reactions, avoiding the systemic side effects observed with sertraline."
- **Problem:** The meta-analysis reports adverse events and the absence of systemic side effects; it did not assess drug–drug interactions, so the no-interaction claim is unverifiable from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Trim to "a treatment that does not add another daily systemic drug" or add "interactions were not specifically studied.""

### Study 10: In Hospitalized Seniors, Combined Memory and Mobility Problems Multiply the Risk of Falls, Pressure Injuries and Other Complications
**PMID:** 42777277 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for cross-sectional data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Combined Memory and Mobility Problems Multiply the Risk of Falls, Pressure Injuries and Other Complications"
- **Abstract says:** "A cross-sectional study... The strongest interaction was observed for concurrent cognitive and mobility impairments (OR = 19.594, 95% CI: 8.207-46.783). CONCLUSION: IC impairment was associated with cumulative nursing risk among hospitalized older adults."
- **Problem:** "Multiply the risk" implies the impairments cause the complications, but the design measures capacity and risk status at the same time and cannot establish direction; the Caveats acknowledge this, so the headline contradicts the body's own hedge.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "Combined Memory and Mobility Problems Go With Far Higher Odds of Falls, Pressure Injuries and Other Risks.""

**Issue B. Sample size wording** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "assessed 459 inpatients aged 60 and older"
- **Abstract says:** "A cross-sectional study was conducted among 470 inpatients aged ≥60 years from a tertiary hospital in China using convenience sampling... A total of 459 older inpatients were included in the final analysis"
- **Problem:** 470 were assessed; 459 were included in the analysis. The digest attaches the analysed figure to the assessment step.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "assessed 470 inpatients aged 60 and older, 459 of whom were included in the analysis.""

### Study 12: Sedating and Anticholinergic Drug Loads Rarely Change in the Hospital — and That Shapes Where Patients Go Next
**PMID:** 42530030 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Outpatient-visit finding misattributed to the 'any change' group** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "flagging patients whose brain-active drug burden shifted during hospitalization — in either direction — as a group needing more intensive post-discharge follow-up, since they are far less likely to go straight home and tend to need more clinic visits"
- **Abstract says:** "High DBIs at admission or discharge were linked to more clinical visits post-discharge, while a DBI increase during hospitalization was associated with fewer visits (RR: 0.86 [0.73-1.00])."
- **Problem:** More post-discharge visits were associated with high DBI at admission/discharge, not with a change in DBI; for patients whose burden increased during the stay, visits were actually fewer. The angle reverses the direction for part of the group it describes.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to: '...far less likely to go straight home; separately, patients with a high drug burden at admission or discharge had more outpatient visits, while those whose burden increased during the stay had slightly fewer.'"

### Study 13: National Snapshot: How Much Older Americans Mix Prescriptions, Over-the-Counter Drugs and Supplements
**PMID:** 42783349 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Claim about riskiest combinations not in the record** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "some of the riskiest combinations in older adults involve something that was never prescribed by a doctor"
- **Abstract says:** "This study uses a nationally representative sample to estimate changes in the use and concurrent use of prescription and over-the-counter (OTC) medications and dietary supplements among older adults in the US between 2015-2016 and 2021-2023, including use of potentially major drug-drug interactions."
- **Problem:** The abstract record contains only a plain-language summary with no results, so no statement about which combinations are riskiest can be supported. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to note that the study counted OTC products and supplements alongside prescriptions and measured potentially major interactions, without asserting which combinations carry the most risk."

**Issue B. Study design stated as cross-sectional survey** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "cross-sectional survey design cannot establish causation; medication use in national surveys relies in part on respondent report"
- **Abstract says:** "This study uses a nationally representative sample to estimate changes in the use and concurrent use of prescription and over-the-counter (OTC) medications and dietary supplements among older adults in the US between 2015-2016 and 2021-2023"
- **Problem:** The design and data-collection method are not described in the available record; calling it a cross-sectional survey with self-report is an inference.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the design description as an assumption, e.g. 'the published record does not describe the design or data source in detail.'"

### Study 14: Pharmacist Medication Reviews for Patients on Five-Plus Drugs Paid for Themselves — About €181 Per Patient
**PMID:** 42467355 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Scope of the null predictor finding overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "No patient characteristic — age, kidney function, medication count, or ward — predicted who would yield the most savings."
- **Abstract says:** "No significant associations were found between any of the included patient characteristics and budget impact related to medication recommendations."
- **Problem:** The regression tested budget impact from medication recommendations only (the €46 component), not total savings including avoided readmissions, so 'who would yield the most savings' overstates what was modeled.
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify that no patient characteristic predicted the budget impact attributable to medication recommendations."

### Study 15: Fear of Falling Runs Deepest in Older Adults With More Falls, More Hospital Visits and Longer Medication Lists
**PMID:** 42628444 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal claim about activity restriction** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "it drives people to restrict activity in ways that make future falls more likely"
- **Abstract says:** "CAF was associated with a multidimensional clinical phenotype linked to frailty, functional decline, polypharmacy, and health care utilization."
- **Problem:** The study is cross-sectional and reports associations only; it did not measure activity restriction or subsequent falls, so the causal chain is not supported by this paper.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as 'concern about falling clusters with frailty, polypharmacy and heavy health care use, and has been linked in other research to activity restriction.'"

### Study 16: Pharmacist-led medication reviews cut risky prescriptions in half for older cancer patients
**PMID:** 42731364 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported safety claim** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "this review shows a structured pharmacist review can safely trim that list"
- **Abstract says:** "Evidence for broader patient-centred outcomes was limited and primarily narrative... confidence in this finding is limited by the small number of studies with serious risk of bias."
- **Problem:** The review explicitly found limited evidence on patient-centred outcomes, so it cannot establish that deprescribing was safe — only that PIM use fell in mostly uncontrolled before-after studies.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'this review found that structured pharmacist review was associated with fewer inappropriate medications, though its effects on symptoms, falls and survival were not well measured.'"

**Issue B. Inclusion criteria slightly overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers pooled eight studies involving 1,018 adults aged 65 and older who had cancer and were taking medications flagged as potentially inappropriate."
- **Abstract says:** "A systematic review and meta-analysis... evaluating deprescribing interventions in adults aged ≥65 years with cancer."
- **Problem:** Studies enrolled older adults with cancer receiving deprescribing interventions; not all participants are described as already taking a potentially inappropriate medication.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop 'and were taking medications flagged as potentially inappropriate' or recast as 'who underwent deprescribing interventions.'"

### Study 17: One in five prescriptions for older Chinese outpatients contained a potentially risky medication — and polypharmacy was the biggest red flag
**PMID:** 42594508 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline prevalence figure not reported** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "One in five prescriptions for older Chinese outpatients contained a potentially risky medication"
- **Abstract says:** "The prevalence of PIMs varied significantly across criteria, ranging from 13.33% (Screening Tool of Older Persons' Prescriptions 2023) to 36.48% (PIM-China 2024)."
- **Problem:** The study gives a range across eight criteria with no single pooled estimate; '20%' is an invented midpoint and the body text correctly gives the range, creating an internal mismatch.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite the headline to reflect the range, e.g. 'Between one in seven and one in three prescriptions for older Chinese outpatients was potentially inappropriate — depending on the tool used.'"

**Issue B. 'Different drugs' vs different prevalence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the study found that different screening criteria flag very different drugs"
- **Abstract says:** "The prevalence of PIMs varied significantly across criteria... Overall agreement among the 8 criteria was moderate (κ = 0.477)."
- **Problem:** The abstract documents differing prevalence and only moderate agreement between criteria; it does not report which specific drugs each tool flags.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the criteria produced markedly different prevalence estimates and agreed only moderately with each other."

**Issue C. 'National scale' framing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It quantifies, at a national scale, how quickly the risk of an inappropriate prescription climbs"
- **Abstract says:** "prescription data from 59 health care institutions (comprising 48 tertiary and 11 secondary hospitals) across 6 cities in China"
- **Problem:** Six cities and 59 hospitals is multicenter but not a nationally representative sample.
- **Suggested fix:** "Replace 'at a national scale' with 'across 59 hospitals in six Chinese cities.'"

### Study 19: Polypharmacy raises the odds of being admitted when older adults land in the emergency department
**PMID:** 42701177 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Data collection window** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "across 36 emergency departments in nine European countries during a seven-week window in late 2020"
- **Abstract says:** "Data collection from routine clinical records of all patients aged 65 and older over a 7-day period from October 19 to November 30, 2020 during the Covid-19 pandemic."
- **Problem:** The abstract specifies a 7-day collection period (falling within an October–November 2020 window), not a seven-week window of continuous collection.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say each site collected data over a 7-day period between 19 October and 30 November 2020."

**Issue B. Causal headline for observational data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Polypharmacy raises the odds of being admitted when older adults land in the emergency department"
- **Abstract says:** "Highest age (≥ 85), geriatric risks... polypharmacy, and shortness of breath increased the odds of being admitted to hospital (OR... 1.3 [1.1-1.6]...)"
- **Problem:** The abstract's phrasing is associational within a cross-sectional analysis; 'raises' implies the medication count itself causes admission rather than marking sicker patients.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'is associated with higher odds of admission' in the headline."

### Study 20: Hospitals are testing ways to catch bad prescriptions at the front door — but almost no one is measuring whether patients do better
**PMID:** 42313336 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Quantifier overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Nearly all measured whether prescribing changed; very few looked at whether patients were readmitted, fell less, or felt better."
- **Abstract says:** "While PIP was frequently a primary outcome measure, few studies reported clinical outcomes or explored prescriber adherence and experiences of older adults and prescribers."
- **Problem:** The abstract says potentially inappropriate prescribing was 'frequently' a primary outcome, not that nearly all studies measured it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change 'Nearly all' to 'Most studies made prescribing change the main outcome.'"

### Study 22: Adding Geriatricians to Hip Fracture Care Changed Prescribing — And Deaths in Hospital Fell by More Than Half
**PMID:** 42806170 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal framing of a before-after observational study** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "It shows that who manages an older patient after a fracture changes what drugs they receive — more prevention of the next fracture, fewer sedatives — and is associated with markedly better survival during the hospital stay."
- **Abstract says:** "Implementation of the orthogeriatric unit was associated with significant increases in the prescription of laxatives... Unadjusted intra-hospital mortality declined significantly (7.7% vs 2.9%, p = 0.005)."
- **Problem:** "Changed prescribing"/"changes what drugs they receive" asserts cause and effect from a retrospective, unadjusted pre-/post-implementation comparison at one hospital, contradicting the digest's own caveat that "Causation cannot be established."
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to association, e.g. "After geriatricians joined hip fracture care, prescribing patterns shifted and in-hospital deaths were lower" in both headline and "Why it matters.""

### Study 23: Nearly All Advanced Lung Cancer Patients Were Flagged for a Questionable Medication — And Most Said They'd Happily Stop One
**PMID:** 42126766 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Clinician barriers paraphrased imprecisely** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "doctors cited poor collaboration and uncertainty, pharmacists cited competence and patient-related concerns"
- **Abstract says:** "medical clinicians were most affected by collaboration- and competence-related concerns, whereas pharmacists were constrained by competence- and patient-related factors"
- **Problem:** The abstract attributes competence-related concerns to clinicians as well as pharmacists; the digest substitutes the vaguer "uncertainty" for clinicians, slightly altering the reported barrier.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "doctors cited collaboration- and competence-related concerns; pharmacists cited competence- and patient-related ones.""
