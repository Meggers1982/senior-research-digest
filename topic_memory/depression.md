# Topic Memory: depression
_Last updated: 2026-11-08_

## Established findings
- Physical activity is the most consistently supported mood lever in later life; the question has moved from whether to which kind, with dual-task, mind-body and balance training ranking highest for post-stroke depression, and social engagement the best substitute when exercise isn't possible (PMIDs 42721779, 42711194, 42892150, 42868040, 42897872; Oct 2026–Jan 2027)
- Depression in older adults pulls people away from routine outpatient care and toward hospitalization, most sharply when frailty is also present (PMID 42849853; Jan 2027)
- The treatment gap has two halves: older adults' own belief they can handle it alone (PMID 42624700), and community aging organizations that want to offer depression programs but lack funding, dedicated staff and crisis training (PMID 42995728; Nov 2026)
- Depression and neurodegenerative disease overlap heavily and are only partly separable — biomarkers sort some cases (PMID 42904492), ~23% of treatment-resistant late-life depression carries a dementia diagnosis (PMID 42995052), and depression precedes Parkinson's without predicting faster progression (PMID 42766893)
- "The program worked, the depression stayed" keeps recurring: robots, reminiscence groups, produce delivery and nurse-led symptom programs all improved something other than mood (PMIDs 42987450, 42987468, 41979725, 42908282; Oct–Nov 2026)
- Enjoyable group movement is the exception — weekly dance cut depression scores 27% with 83% attendance in frail nursing home residents (PMID 42843433; Nov 2026)
- Mood mediates the path from social and early-life adversity to physical decline — isolation to frailty, loneliness to osteoarthritis, childhood adversity to heart, kidney and liver disease (PMIDs 42873644, 42911759, 42998744; Oct–Nov 2026)

## Emerging threads
- Technology splits by outcome: internet non-use tracks with depression while social isolation tracks with loneliness; remote digital programs help depression and anxiety but not loneliness; companion robots help loneliness and cognition but not depression, and reduce daily steps; AI tools show age bias and unstable longitudinal reasoning (PMIDs 42971795, 42976720, 42987450, 42786110; Nov–Dec 2026)
- Subjective connection beats objective contact as a marker — felt loneliness, not social contact, predicted hallucinations in dementia; self-disclosure, not conversation frequency, separated depression groups (PMIDs 42996468, 42981151; Nov 2026)
- Elder mistreatment as a measurable medical risk factor for death, hospitalization and depression, with high-certainty but highly heterogeneous evidence (PMID 42981484; Nov 2026)
- Successful procedures with poor inner outcomes — DBS improved motor function and quality of life while hopelessness and sense of autonomy worsened (PMID 42731544; Nov 2026)
- Place and food access as mental health exposures — ethnic enclave residence eased anxiety but not depression; produce delivery reduced loneliness alongside diet and blood pressure (PMIDs 42967326, 41979725; Nov 2026)
- Mood tracks with biological aging markers, with no intervention evidence yet that treating it reverses them (PMIDs 42566822, 42918505; Oct–Nov 2026)
