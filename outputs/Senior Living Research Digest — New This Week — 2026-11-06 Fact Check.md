### Study 2: Companion robots nudged up older adults' memory and mood — but they also walked less
**PMID:** 42987450 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline names outcomes the trials didn't measure as such** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Companion robots nudged up older adults' memory and mood"
- **Abstract says:** "Interventions involving SARs improved cognitive function (SMD, 0.21; 95% CI, 0.05-0.36) and composite psychological outcomes (SMD, 0.45; 95% CI, 0.24-0.66) and reduced loneliness ... No significant effects were found for depression, anxiety, agitation, apathy, or vitality."
- **Problem:** The pooled outcomes were global cognitive function and a composite psychological score plus loneliness, not memory specifically; and because depression, anxiety, agitation and apathy all showed no effect, 'mood' is a loose description of the psychological composite.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like 'nudged up older adults' thinking scores and eased loneliness — but they also walked less'."

**Issue B. Superlative framing of the evidence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is the clearest signal yet that they help with loneliness and thinking skills"
- **Abstract says:** "Interventions involving SARs improved cognitive function and selected psychological outcomes, but their effects were not consistent across all IC domains."
- **Problem:** The abstract makes no claim to be the clearest or strongest evidence to date; the authors emphasize inconsistency across domains.
- **Suggested fix:** "Replace with 'this pooled analysis suggests they help with loneliness and thinking scores, though effects were inconsistent across domains'."

### Study 3: Six everyday factors explained a fifth of new memory decline in a 7-year Shanghai study — and the list includes soy and sleep
**PMID:** 42991179 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Exploratory sex analysis presented as actionable** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the sex difference is the practical hook — modifiable factors explained nearly twice as much risk in women, which argues for not assuming the same prevention checklist applies equally to everyone in your care"
- **Abstract says:** "Sex stratification analyses were used for exploratory purposes. ... The overall PAF was higher in females (22.94%) than in males (13.10%), with a broader range of contributing factors in females."
- **Problem:** The authors explicitly flag the sex-stratified results as exploratory, so building a care recommendation on them goes beyond what the analysis was designed to support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that the sex-stratified findings were exploratory and hypothesis-generating."

### Study 4: Tongue-strengthening exercises with real-time feedback improved swallowing after stroke — and lighter effort worked better
**PMID:** 42987453 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Caveat details not in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Meta-analysis of trials largely conducted in a single region with variable quality; complication definitions differ across studies and the pooled risk ratio of 0.28 rests on relatively few events"
- **Abstract says:** "A total of 16 studies involving 978 PSD patients were included. ... reduced complications (RR, 0.28; 95% CI, 0.14-0.54; I2 = 0%)."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not report the geographic distribution of included trials, how complications were defined, or event counts. Unverifiable from abstract only (though the caveats are conservative rather than overstated).
- **Suggested fix:** "Either attribute these points to the full text or drop them; the abstract does support noting that the authors call for higher-quality multicenter RCTs."

### Study 5: Dementia costs more years of life to the highly educated — a Danish study of nearly 2 million people finds
**PMID:** 42989503 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal phrasing for a register-based cohort** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Dementia raised death rates across the board"
- **Abstract says:** "In 1,991,379 persons, MRRs were higher in dementia, highest in long-term education, and in more recent birth cohorts."
- **Problem:** The study reports mortality rate ratios in an observational register cohort; 'raised' asserts causation that the design cannot establish, and sits in tension with the digest's own caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'Death rates were higher in people with dementia across all groups'."

**Issue B. Mechanism not supported by the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "more-educated people lose more remaining life — likely because they would otherwise have lived longer"
- **Abstract says:** "Dementia was associated with reductions of 5.01 (men) and 5.29 years (women) in the long-term education group, and 3.99 years (men and women) in the short-term education group."
- **Problem:** The abstract offers no explanation for the education gradient; the 'likely because' reasoning is the digest's own inference presented as the study's.
- **Suggested fix:** "Mark it as a possible explanation ('one plausible reason, not tested here, is...')."

### Study 6: The buzziest anti-aging drug didn't extend life in 4,885 people who actually took it for years
**PMID:** 42989006 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Superlative stronger than the abstract's claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is the largest human dataset yet on long-term use"
- **Abstract says:** "Using US national organ transplant registry data, one of the largest real-world datasets examining chronic rapamycin exposure in humans"
- **Problem:** The authors say 'one of the largest'; the story angle upgrades this to 'the largest', which the abstract does not support (the digest body gets it right).
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'one of the largest human datasets on long-term use'."

**Issue B. Matching described imprecisely** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the groups were statistically matched on age, BMI, diabetes and 15 other factors"
- **Abstract says:** "Propensity score weighting was used to adjust for exposure group differences in age, BMI, diabetes, and 15 other factors."
- **Problem:** Propensity score weighting is not the same as matching; the digest's own caveat correctly says 'propensity weighting', so the body text is inconsistent.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'the groups were statistically balanced using propensity score weighting on age, BMI, diabetes and 15 other factors'."

### Study 7: One in four older cancer patients is frail before chemo even starts — and it predicts how the treatment will go
**PMID:** 42987730 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Caveat details not stated in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Frailty was measured with varying instruments across studies, and pooled estimates showed the heterogeneity typical of this literature."
- **Abstract says:** "Nineteen longitudinal studies involving 4127 older adults with cancer were included, with a pooled prevalence of pre-chemotherapy frailty of 24.6%. ... Pooled prevalence and risk estimates were synthesized using random-effects models, as appropriate."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not describe which frailty instruments were used or report heterogeneity statistics. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute to the full text or soften to 'random-effects pooling was used, which implies variation across studies'."

### Study 10: Smart home gadgets for dementia are easy to accept — but the evidence they help caregivers is thin
**PMID:** 42992512 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Null pooled result restated as a device-by-device finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a clear finding that none of the studied systems reduced measured caregiver burden"
- **Abstract says:** "Our meta-analysis did not find evidence supporting the use of the investigated devices for reducing caregiver burden (p = 0.68; Hedges's g = 0.01; 95% CI -0.14 to 0.17)."
- **Problem:** The meta-analysis pooled across devices and found no overall effect; that is not the same as each of the 17 devices individually having been shown not to reduce burden, and the authors frame it as absence of evidence rather than proof of no effect.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'when results were pooled, there was no evidence that the devices studied reduced caregiver burden'."

### Study 14: Frailty isn't a one-way street: nearly 1 in 5 older adults got less frail within a year
**PMID:** 42986749 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. 'Strongest protective factor' overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "ask whether a supervised physical activity program is appropriate for you, since activity was the strongest protective factor in this study"
- **Abstract says:** "higher physical activity was protective (OR 0.70, 95% CI 0.51-0.98)"
- **Problem:** Physical activity was the only protective factor identified in the ordinal model, not demonstrated to be the 'strongest' among several; the abstract makes no ranking. Separately, the multinomial finding that stroke history was associated with improvement (OR 4.23) is not mentioned anywhere in the digest.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'since higher physical activity was the one protective factor identified in this study' and consider noting the unexpected stroke-history association with improvement."

### Study 19: Money and emotional support — not living with family — keep older adults socially engaged
**PMID:** 42988767 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Unsupported contrast with living arrangements** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Money and emotional support — not living with family — keep older adults socially engaged"
- **Abstract says:** "Financial security (OR = .55, p < .001) and emotional support (OR = .30, p = .034) reduced the likelihood of the lower-declining trajectory, consistently across genders. Strengthening financial protection and social support networks beyond household-based care can sustain social participation in later life."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports no analysis of co-residence or living with family, so the digest cannot claim that living with family was tested and found not to matter. The authors' phrase 'beyond household-based care' is a policy recommendation, not a null finding on co-residence.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as 'Financial security and emotional support — beyond household-based care — tracked with sustained social engagement'."

**Issue B. Causal language for observational data** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "what predicted sustained participation was financial breathing room and someone to confide in"
- **Abstract says:** "group-based trajectory modeling and logistic regression were applied. Two trajectories emerged ... Financial security ... and emotional support ... reduced the likelihood of the lower-declining trajectory"
- **Problem:** Combined with the headline verb 'keep', the digest presents cross-sectionally measured predictors from an observational cohort as causes of sustained engagement. The Caveats note the limitation, but the headline and 'Why it matters' text contradict it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use association language throughout, e.g. 'older adults with financial security and emotional support were less likely to be on the declining trajectory'."

**Issue C. Overstated claim in professional angle** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "For families and community program staff: co-residence does not guarantee engagement."
- **Abstract says:** "(no data on co-residence reported)"
- **Problem:** Presents a conclusion about co-residence as though the study tested it. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Remove the co-residence claim and keep the actionable point about screening for financial strain and confidant availability."
