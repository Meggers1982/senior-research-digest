### Study 1: Your doctor's records don't know you're a caregiver — and researchers are building workarounds
**PMID:** 42861868 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Emergency contact" field not specified in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "they queried the clinical research data warehouse for patients with dementia and the emergency contacts listed in their charts"
- **Abstract says:** "fee-based queries of the clinical research data warehouse (CRDW) that identified patients with dementia and their listed contacts (potential caregivers)"
- **Problem:** The abstract says "listed contacts," not emergency contacts; the digest narrows the field type and then builds a story angle around it ("the 'emergency contact' field is an underused map").
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "the contacts listed in their charts" and adjust the story angle to refer to the chart's listed-contact field."

### Study 3: Laser therapy added to back exercises did nothing extra — a year of follow-up confirms it
**PMID:** 42864195 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Attributing benefit to exercise when there was no non-exercise control** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this trial indicates the money is better spent on the exercise component that actually did the work"
- **Abstract says:** "Participants were randomly allocated to active PBMT plus MCE or sham PBMT plus MCE... Adding PBMT to MCE did not provide clinically important additional improvements"
- **Problem:** Both arms received the same exercise program and there was no untreated or exercise-free comparator, so the trial can only show that photobiomodulation added nothing — not that exercise was responsible for any improvement.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "this trial indicates the add-on provided no additional benefit over the exercise program both groups received.""

### Study 10: When pandemic-era food aid ended, older adults living alone went hungry fastest
**PMID:** 42647175 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Between-group contrast overstated in headline and story angle** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this study identifies solo-dwelling older adults as the group hit hardest by benefit cuts"
- **Abstract says:** "The difference in effects between those living alone and those not living alone was not statistically significant at conventional levels (p = .091)."
- **Problem:** The digest's caveat correctly notes the contrast was not statistically significant, but the headline ("went hungry fastest") and this story angle assert the comparative claim as established, which contradicts the caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "older adults living alone showed the largest estimated increase, though the difference from those living with others was not statistically significant.""

### Study 11: Fats in your cells get longer as you age — and shortening them extended life in lab animals
**PMID:** 42839094 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal language on human frailty finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "whose expression tracked lifespan in mice and showed a causal link to human frailty in genetic analyses"
- **Abstract says:** "with expression correlating with lifespan in mice and genetic analyses supporting a causal role in human frailty"
- **Problem:** "Showed a causal link" is firmer than "supporting a causal role," and it contradicts the digest's own caveat that the human genetic association "cannot establish causation in humans."
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "and whose genetic analyses supported a causal role in human frailty.""

### Study 12: A ballooning artery at the base of the brain grows faster in younger patients — and raises stroke risk when it does
**PMID:** 42836955 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal phrasing in headline for observational data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and raises stroke risk when it does"
- **Abstract says:** "Nontraditional factors associated with increased risk of subsequent posterior-circulation ischemic stroke in VBD patients were complex shapes of VBA (OR = 1.74) and VBD progression (OR = 2.47)."
- **Problem:** The retrospective cohort shows an association between progression and later posterior-circulation stroke, not that progression raises risk causally.
- **Suggested fix:** ""— and progression is linked to higher stroke risk" or "— and those that grow are more likely to be followed by a stroke.""

**Issue B. Nomogram validation claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "modest sample (N=191) with risk-prediction nomograms that were not validated in an independent cohort"
- **Abstract says:** "Nomograms incorporating these factors demonstrated excellent discriminative performance for both outcomes."
- **Problem:** The abstract says nothing about whether external validation was or was not performed; the absence of validation is an inference. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "risk-prediction nomograms whose performance was reported only within the same cohort (no external validation described).""

### Study 13: Where you live may shape the hospice care you get at the end of life
**PMID:** 42635982 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Findings not verifiable from abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The cross-sectional analysis documents systematic rural-urban differences in both the delivery and the intensity of the Medicare hospice benefit."
- **Abstract says:** "This cross-sectional study examines how hospice care varies across the rural-urban continuum, including length of stay, care setting, visits by hospice staff, and rates of higher-level care."
- **Problem:** The available abstract is a plain-language summary with no results; the existence and size of "systematic" differences is supported only by the article title. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute to the title/framing, e.g., "The study, titled for the rural-urban disparities it reports, examined..." and note that no effect sizes are available in the abstract."

**Issue B. Author framing claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The authors frame the gaps as a question of equitable access to the same federal benefit regardless of geography."
- **Abstract says:** "No discussion or conclusion text is provided in the available abstract."
- **Problem:** This characterization of the authors' framing cannot be checked. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the sentence or mark it as drawn from the full text."

### Study 17: Stretching out MS infusions cuts infections — but patients over 60 remain at higher risk
**PMID:** 42854181 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Rounded percentage slightly off** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "fewer infections overall (21% lower risk with one infusion per two years)"
- **Abstract says:** "(HR 0.78 [95% CI 0.69-0.88] for 1 infusion)"
- **Problem:** A hazard ratio of 0.78 corresponds to a 22% lower risk, not 21%.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "22% lower risk (HR 0.78).""

**Issue B. Causal verb in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Stretching out MS infusions cuts infections"
- **Abstract says:** "Extended rituximab intervals are associated with reduced overall infection risk, but the benefit for hospital-treated infections is limited."
- **Problem:** "Cuts" asserts causation from an observational registry analysis in which the authors themselves use "associated with."
- **Suggested fix:** ""Stretching out MS infusions is linked to fewer infections...""
