### Study 2: Poor Sleep Before Heart Valve Surgery Predicts Confusion Afterward
**PMID:** 42665464 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Timing of sleep measurement not specified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this suggests measurable sleep problems in the weeks before an operation may identify who is most at risk"
- **Abstract says:** "Before surgery, we assessed sleep using self-report, ambulatory polysomnography, and actigraphy."
- **Problem:** The abstract states only that sleep was assessed before surgery; it does not say how far in advance (weeks) the measurements were taken.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "measurable sleep problems before an operation" or note that the interval before surgery is not reported in the abstract."

### Study 3: Even Highly Motivated Alzheimer's Patients Miss Most Brain Health Targets
**PMID:** 42430964 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline overstates per-patient non-adherence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Even Highly Motivated Alzheimer's Patients Miss Most Brain Health Targets"
- **Abstract says:** "Less than 3% of patients with early AD pursuing AAT were following or had anthropometric measures/lab values in line with all 15 brain health recommendations... For 11 of 15 factors, >25% of the group were not adhering to guidelines."
- **Problem:** The abstract shows very few patients met all 15 targets and that more than a quarter fell short on 11 factors, but it does not report that the typical patient missed a majority of the 15 targets.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like "Almost No Highly Motivated Alzheimer's Patients Meet All Brain Health Targets.""

**Issue B. Missing-lab figures presented as a closed range** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "key lab values were simply missing from the medical record for 23-42% of patients"
- **Abstract says:** "There were no data in the EMR for HbA1c in >42% of patients and for LDL in >23% of patients in either group."
- **Problem:** The abstract gives lower bounds (more than 42% for HbA1c, more than 23% for LDL), not a 23-42% range; the digest slightly understates the HbA1c gap.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "more than 23% of patients were missing LDL values and more than 42% were missing HbA1c.""

### Study 4: Sleep Problems Top the List of Menopause Complaints — and Most Women Are Never Asked
**PMID:** 42770459 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Predictive wording for cross-sectional associations** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Higher education predicted hormone therapy and mental health treatment use"
- **Abstract says:** "Higher education was associated with hormone therapy use (aOR: 5.63...) and mental health treatment (aOR: 4.11...)"
- **Problem:** "Predicted" suggests temporal/forecasting information that a single cross-sectional survey cannot provide; the abstract reports associations only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Higher education was associated with greater use of hormone therapy and mental health treatment.""

### Study 6: New Pill for Narcolepsy Delivers Dramatic Wakefulness Gains in Two Large Trials
**PMID:** 42714024 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Placebo cataplexy reduction omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "weekly cataplexy attacks dropped 79% to 89%"
- **Abstract says:** "Median percent reductions in the weekly cataplexy rate ranged from 79.0 to 88.8% with oveporexton, as compared with 27.7 to 39.1% with placebo"
- **Problem:** Reporting only the drug-arm reduction without the substantial placebo-arm reduction (28-39%) exaggerates the apparent size of the drug effect.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "versus 28% to 39% on placebo.""

**Issue B. Adverse-event rate lacks placebo comparison** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Side effects were common — 86% to 89% of treated participants reported adverse events"
- **Abstract says:** "Adverse events occurred in 86 to 89% of the participants with oveporexton, as compared with 43 to 54% with placebo"
- **Problem:** Without the placebo figure, readers cannot judge how much of the high adverse-event rate is attributable to the drug; the caveats call it "a very high adverse event rate."
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "compared with 43% to 54% on placebo.""

### Study 7: Losing an Adult Child Erodes Sleep, Memory and Mood in Midlife and Older Adults, 7-Year Study Finds
**PMID:** 42791223 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Adult child" not supported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Losing an Adult Child Erodes Sleep, Memory and Mood"
- **Abstract says:** "To assess the longitudinal association between the onset of child death and psychosocial outcomes among adults aged 40 years and older"
- **Problem:** The abstract refers to the death of a child of parents aged 40+, without specifying that the children who died were adults.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "Losing a Child" in the headline."

**Issue B. Causal verb in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Erodes Sleep, Memory and Mood"
- **Abstract says:** "the onset of child death was associated with increased depressive symptoms, poorer sleep quality, more PTSD symptoms, poorer self-rated health, reduced cognitive functioning"
- **Problem:** "Erodes" asserts causation for an observational fixed-effects analysis; the digest's own caveat says causation cannot be proven.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "Tracks With Worse Sleep, Memory and Mood" or "Is Followed by.""

### Study 11: Sleep and Mood Symptoms Differ by Dementia Type Even in the Earliest Stages
**PMID:** 42768715 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Sleep emphasis not in reported results** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Sleep and Mood Symptoms Differ by Dementia Type Even in the Earliest Stages"
- **Abstract says:** "DLB patients exhibited the highest prevalence of non-motor symptoms across all domains. bvFTD and AD patients did not show disease group specific symptoms, but apathy and paranoidal thoughts appeared earlier in bvFTD than AD or DLB."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not report separate results for the sleep-related domain; the only differences described are DLB's higher prevalence across all domains and the earlier timing of apathy/paranoia in bvFTD. A sleep-specific headline claim goes slightly beyond what is reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like "Non-Motor Symptoms — Including Sleep — Differ by Dementia Type in the Earliest Stages," and note in the body that results were reported at the domain level."

### Study 12: Sleep Problems Are the Symptom Most Likely to Linger on Antidepressants, Huge Claims Analysis Finds
**PMID:** 42665164 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Prevalence overstated in "Why it matters"** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "this scale of data shows the pattern is the rule rather than an unusual complaint worth keeping quiet about"
- **Abstract says:** "Of those receiving ADT, 97.5% (n = 2,752,508) received monotherapy and were assessed for persistent symptoms; 445,399 (16.2%) experienced ≥1 persistent symptom. Sleep disturbances were most common (82.0%)"
- **Problem:** Only 16.2% of monotherapy patients had any documented persistent symptom, so documented persistent sleep disturbance affected roughly 13% of them — sleep was the most common residual symptom, not "the rule."
- **Suggested fix:** "Say that sleep disturbance dominated among the minority of patients with a documented residual symptom (16.2% had at least one, and 82% of those involved sleep)."

**Issue B. Only three symptom categories examined** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "sleep disturbance was by far the most common (82.0% of those cases), ahead of cognitive impairment (14.1%) and sexual dysfunction (9.2%)"
- **Abstract says:** "persistent symptoms (within 3 categories: sleep disturbances, cognitive impairment, sexual dysfunction) in patients receiving ADT monotherapy"
- **Problem:** The study only looked for three predefined symptom categories, so "the symptom most likely to linger" is limited to that shortlist rather than all possible residual symptoms.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that the analysis considered only three predefined persistent-symptom categories."

### Study 14: Mindfulness therapy helped older adults with depression sleep better — at least in the short term
**PMID:** 42648548 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Scope of the null 3-month result** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The advantage was no longer statistically significant at the 3-month follow-up"
- **Abstract says:** "At Week 8, the between-group difference in change in global PSQI scores was -3.80 (95% CI, -6.50 to -1.10), but was nonsignificant at 3-month follow-up."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports loss of significance only for the PSQI (subjective) outcome; it does not report a 3-month result for objectively measured stable sleep, so "the advantage" as a whole is broader than the abstract supports.
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify that the questionnaire-measured (PSQI) advantage was no longer significant at 3 months; the abstract does not report a 3-month figure for stable sleep."

### Study 15: Short or long sleep amplifies the depression risk that comes with heart and metabolic disease
**PMID:** 42628578 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal verb in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Short or long sleep amplifies the depression risk that comes with heart and metabolic disease"
- **Abstract says:** "Significant additive interactions between CMM and unhealthy sleep duration were observed (P for additive interaction <0.001). ... Compared with individuals with good sleep duration, these associations were stronger among those with poor sleep duration."
- **Problem:** "Amplifies" asserts an active causal effect from two observational cohorts that only demonstrate a statistical interaction; the digest's own Caveats acknowledge causation cannot be established.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "tracks with" or "is linked to a stronger association," e.g. "Short or long sleep goes with a stronger depression link in heart and metabolic disease.""

### Study 16: One in ten older adults started on a dementia drug is later prescribed a sleep medication
**PMID:** 42727946 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mechanism and harm claims not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Cholinesterase inhibitors are well known to disturb sleep and cause vivid dreams, yet more than one in ten new users ends up on a sleep medication — a class that raises fall, fracture and confusion risk in exactly the population being treated for dementia."
- **Abstract says:** "The highest incidence cascades were iron supplement to laxative (11.9%...), hydroxymethylglutaryl coenzyme A reductase inhibitor to pain reliever (10.9%...), and cholinesterase inhibitor to sleep agent (10.3%, 9.9% to 10.7%)."
- **Problem:** The abstract documents the prescribing sequence only; it makes no statement that cholinesterase inhibitors disturb sleep or that sleep agents raise fall, fracture and confusion risk. Unverifiable from abstract only (though consistent with general geriatrics knowledge).
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute these background claims to outside sources or soften to "sleep disturbance is a recognised side effect of these drugs" with a citation, keeping the study's own contribution (the sequence data) distinct."

### Study 17: Asked what they want from insomnia treatment, older adults named side effects first — not sleep itself
**PMID:** 42746961 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Claim about resistance to deprescribing** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "raising concerns about a resident's or relative's sleep medication is unlikely to meet resistance"
- **Abstract says:** "Risk of side effects (n = 10) and effectiveness (n = 7) were most commonly prioritized and frequently reported across all participant groups. ... Decisions about insomnia treatments are influenced not only by expected and/or experienced treatment effectiveness, but also by perceived risks and health beliefs."
- **Problem:** The study elicited stated preferences in 20 people; it did not test how deprescribing conversations actually go or whether they meet resistance, so this predicts an outcome the data do not address.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "participants in this small study already shared concerns about sedation and cognition, which may make such conversations easier to open" rather than predicting no resistance."

**Issue B. Recruitment method** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Australian participants recruited for interviews via Zoom may not represent all older adults"
- **Abstract says:** "The interviews were conducted and transcribed in Zoom."
- **Problem:** The abstract says interviews were conducted in Zoom, not that participants were recruited via Zoom; recruitment method is not described.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "interviews were conducted via Zoom; recruitment source is not described in the abstract.""

### Study 18: Poor Sleep and Brain Inflammation: What 40 Studies Say About the Alzheimer's Connection
**PMID:** 42780441 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Consistently" overstates the human evidence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "loss of deep slow-wave sleep, fragmented sleep, and disrupted REM sleep were consistently linked to markers of glial (immune-cell) activation in the brain and to Alzheimer's-related pathology"
- **Abstract says:** "Human studies indicate that slow-wave sleep impairment, sleep fragmentation, and REM sleep dysregulation are linked to neuroinflammation and AD-related pathology."
- **Problem:** The abstract says these are "linked" and describes the studies as heterogeneous with important translational gaps; it does not claim consistency across studies.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "consistently" — "were linked to markers of glial activation.""

**Issue B. Institutional practice claim beyond the review** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "overnight checks, corridor light spill, alarm noise, and medication rounds that interrupt deep sleep all have a measurable biological cost worth weighing against their benefits"
- **Abstract says:** "The available evidence supports a biologically plausible, potentially bidirectional relationship between sleep disruption and AD pathology, while highlighting important gaps in translation. Further longitudinal and interventional investigations are required to clarify causality..."
- **Problem:** The review examined laboratory and clinical sleep measures, not care-home night-time practices, and it explicitly says causality is unresolved. Asserting a "measurable biological cost" for specific operational practices states as fact something the review cannot support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "raises the question of whether routine night-time interruptions carry a biological cost, which this review cannot answer directly.""

### Study 19: Does Hot Weather Make Menopause Worse? A Global Review Finds Less Evidence Than Expected
**PMID:** 42772086 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Strength of the cooling recommendation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the most reliably supported step is simple and within your control: cool the sleeping environment. Fans, lighter bedding, and a cooler room temperature have the best evidence here"
- **Abstract says:** "Four studies addressed sleep and mental health outcomes, with evidence suggesting that cooler environments reduce symptoms and may aid sleep. ... More research is needed to inform heat guidance for menopausal women."
- **Problem:** The abstract offers only four studies with tentative ("suggesting," "may aid") evidence and calls the base insufficient for guidance; "most reliably supported" and "best evidence" imply firmer backing, and fans and bedding specifically are not mentioned.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say that a small number of studies suggest cooler sleeping environments may help, and note that the review authors consider the evidence base too thin for guidance."

### Study 20: Night-Shift Nurses Show Measurable Brain and Metabolic Changes Linked to Low Mood
**PMID:** 42624217 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Changes" implies within-person change** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Night-Shift Nurses Show Measurable Brain and Metabolic Changes Linked to Low Mood"
- **Abstract says:** "Compared with DSN, NSN showed decreased ReHo in the left superior temporal cortex and left anterior hippocampus, and increased ReHo in the right medial amygdala and right superior parietal lobule. ... Night-shift work in nurses is associated with alterations in peripheral metabolism..."
- **Problem:** This was a single-timepoint between-group comparison; nothing was tracked over time, so "changes" suggests more than group differences (the digest's caveats do note the cross-sectional design).
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "Differences" instead of "Changes" in the headline."
