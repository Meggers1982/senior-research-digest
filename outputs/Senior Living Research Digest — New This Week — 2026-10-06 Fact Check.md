### Study 1: A simple blood test moved Alzheimer's diagnoses forward by six months — without adding distress
**PMID:** 42832237 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Secondary outcomes overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "earlier disclosure cut redundant neuropsychological retesting nearly in half and roughly tripled discharges back to primary care"
- **Abstract says:** "fewer planned follow-up neuropsychological reassessments for diagnostic clarification (34/112 [30.4%] vs 57/108 [52.8%]) ... and more planned discharge from the memory clinic to primary care (35/112 [31.3%] vs 13/108 [12.0%])"
- **Problem:** Retesting fell by about 42% (not "nearly in half" in the sense of a halving, though close), and discharges rose roughly 2.6-fold, not threefold. Both are rounded in the favorable direction.
- **Suggested fix:** ""cut planned neuropsychological retesting from 53% to 30% and roughly doubled-to-tripled planned discharges back to primary care (31% vs 12%)""

**Issue B. "First randomized evidence" claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It is the first randomized evidence that telling patients their Alzheimer's blood biomarker results early speeds up a definitive answer"
- **Abstract says:** "evidence on their impact on diagnostic certainty, clinical management, and emotional outcomes in routine care is limited"
- **Problem:** The abstract says evidence is limited but never claims primacy. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** ""It is among the first randomized evidence..." or drop the primacy claim."

**Issue C. Omitted nuance: certainty converged** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "At the 3-month mark, 50% of the earlier-disclosure group had a very high-confidence diagnosis of the underlying cause versus 4.6% of the delayed group."
- **Abstract says:** "Diagnostic certainty converged after disclosure in both arms, except among participants with MCI, in whom higher certainty persisted in the earlier-disclosure arm."
- **Problem:** Readers may infer a lasting diagnostic advantage; in fact the gap closed once the delayed group received results, except in the MCI subgroup.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a sentence noting that certainty evened out between groups once the delayed arm received results, except among patients with MCI."

### Study 5: One in three adults whose bloodwork says diabetes never got a diagnosis or treatment — and the sickest were most likely to be missed
**PMID:** 42832327 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Ambiguous "sickest" in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and the sickest were most likely to be missed"
- **Abstract says:** "higher HbA1c was associated with progressively higher probabilities of diagnostic coding and treatment initiation, whereas greater comorbidity burden was associated with lower probabilities of both outcomes"
- **Problem:** "Sickest" could be read as most severe diabetes, which is the opposite of the finding — those patients were more likely to be diagnosed and treated. Only comorbidity burden ran the other way.
- **Suggested fix:** ""and patients with the most other chronic conditions were most likely to be missed""

**Issue B. Headline wording vs. composite outcome** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "One in three adults whose bloodwork says diabetes never got a diagnosis or treatment"
- **Abstract says:** "One-third of adults with laboratory-confirmed diabetes lacked claims-documented diagnostic coding, treatment, or both"
- **Problem:** The one-third figure covers people missing coding OR treatment OR both; only 9.6% received neither. The phrasing could be read as 33% receiving nothing at all.
- **Suggested fix:** ""One in three adults whose bloodwork says diabetes was missing a diagnosis code, treatment, or both""

### Study 6: Acupuncture for memory loss: nine trials show short-term gains, but researchers say don't read too much into them yet
**PMID:** 42831987 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Safety claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Acupuncture is widely available and low-risk"
- **Abstract says:** "(no safety or adverse-event data reported)"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports no safety outcomes, so the low-risk characterization is unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the safety claim to general background or drop it."

**Issue B. Geographic generalizability caveat unverifiable** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "studies were predominantly from one region, limiting generalizability"
- **Abstract says:** "Severe methodological heterogeneity across existing trials-including inconsistent acupoint prescriptions, divergent EA stimulation parameters, and variable treatment cycles"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not report where the pooled trials were conducted; the regional concentration cannot be confirmed from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Mark as inferred, or replace with the heterogeneity limitations the authors do state."

### Study 8: That study saying a drink a day keeps your organs young? Read the fine print
**PMID:** 42782071 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Conclusion stated more firmly than the author does** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The conclusion: the apparent organ-level benefits of drinking most likely reflect the long-recognized "healthy drinker" bias rather than any true protective effect."
- **Abstract says:** "these findings call for careful interpretation of observational studies linking alcohol consumption to systemic and organ-specific biological aging, which may, if taken at face value, understate the known damage that alcohol causes to human health"
- **Problem:** The Perspective urges cautious interpretation and further work; it does not conclude that the apparent benefits are most likely bias.
- **Suggested fix:** ""The author argues these apparent organ-level benefits should be read cautiously and may reflect the long-recognized 'healthy drinker' paradox.""

**Issue B. Specific confounders and phenotyping details not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "how drinking was measured (often self-reported, with lifelong abstainers and people who quit for health reasons lumped together), and how little the studies controlled for confounding factors like income, smoking and overall health"
- **Abstract says:** "differences in clock construction, alcohol phenotyping, and confounding control across studies"
- **Problem:** The abstract names the categories but not these specific examples; they are unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Generalize to "differences in how aging clocks were built, how drinking was measured, and how confounding was handled.""

### Study 10: A Nobel laureate raises an uncomfortable question about amyloid and the brain's connections
**PMID:** 42625110 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Correlates most closely with memory loss" not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "why synapses — the connections between brain cells, and the feature that correlates most closely with memory loss — are lost in Alzheimer's disease"
- **Abstract says:** "A key feature of AD is synapse loss."
- **Problem:** The abstract calls synapse loss a key feature but makes no claim about it being the strongest correlate of memory loss. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** ""synapses — the connections between brain cells, whose loss is a key feature of Alzheimer's""

**Issue B. Anti-amyloid drug framing attributed to the review** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this review argues the underlying biology is less settled than the treatment pipeline implies"
- **Abstract says:** "It is unclear whether AD is driven primarily by the loss of free Aβ, the increase of aggregated Aβ, or both, nor do we know how ApoE4 affects synapses."
- **Problem:** The abstract makes no reference to approved anti-amyloid drugs or the treatment pipeline; the connection is the digest's inference, though the Caveats do note the paper evaluates no specific drug.
- **Suggested fix:** ""this review notes the underlying biology remains unresolved — context worth having when weighing a demanding, costly therapy.""

### Study 13: Eight sessions in four weeks: hands-on resistance exercise reversed functional decline in adults around age 80
**PMID:** 40983316 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline overstates effect as "reversed"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "hands-on resistance exercise reversed functional decline in adults around age 80"
- **Abstract says:** "The PNF concept is a promising exercise intervention to improve physical functioning in older adults at high risk of disability."
- **Problem:** The trial measured improvement in functional capacity over four weeks; it did not demonstrate reversal of an established decline trajectory, and the authors describe the approach only as "promising."
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "improved physical function in adults around age 80" or "produced measurable functional gains.""

**Issue B. Clinical-meaningfulness benchmark not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A gain of 0.1 m/s in gait speed is generally considered clinically meaningful for older adults; this group doubled that in four weeks"
- **Abstract says:** "GS (Mchange = 0.20 m/s, 95% confidence interval [0.05, 0.37], p = .012)"
- **Problem:** The 0.1 m/s threshold is external context not stated in the abstract, and the wide confidence interval (0.05–0.37) means the true effect could be at or below that threshold.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the 0.1 m/s benchmark to outside literature and note the confidence interval spans 0.05 to 0.37 m/s."

### Study 14: Ginkgo biloba extract shifted inflammation markers in people with mild cognitive impairment — but nobody got measurably sharper
**PMID:** 42810842 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Cognitive null result not reported in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "but the authors report no demonstrated cognitive or clinical benefit"
- **Abstract says:** "Clinical, neurological, and neuropsychological assessments were performed at each visit. ResultsEGb 761 was associated with nominally significant longitudinal decreases in 15 plasma proteins linked to innate immunity and axon guidance, replicated in the delayed-start group."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not report cognitive or clinical outcome results at all — it is silent, not negative. Stating that the authors reported no benefit (and the headline's "nobody got measurably sharper") goes beyond what can be verified from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the abstract reports only biomarker results and does not present cognitive findings, and that the authors call for larger trials to see whether this translates into clinically meaningful neuroprotection."

### Study 15: Adding exercise to osteoporosis drugs improved hip bone density and quality of life — but not spine density
**PMID:** 42815459 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline conflates femoral neck with hip** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Adding exercise to osteoporosis drugs improved hip bone density and quality of life — but not spine density"
- **Abstract says:** "Combined therapy resulted in a statistically significant improvement in femoral BMD... No statistically significant differences were observed for lumbar spine BMD... total hip BMD (MD = 2.77; 95% CI: [-0.69, 6.23]; p = .12), or total body BMD"
- **Problem:** Total hip BMD showed no significant benefit, so a headline promising improved "hip bone density" contradicts one of the study's own null outcomes, even though the body text clarifies the femoral neck finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "femoral neck bone density" in the headline, or add "— but not total hip or spine.""

**Issue B. Fracture-severity claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The femoral neck is where the most devastating osteoporotic fractures happen"
- **Abstract says:** "Osteoporosis is a major global health concern associated with increased fracture risk, reduced functional capacity, and impaired quality of life (QoL)."
- **Problem:** The abstract makes no site-specific claim about fracture severity; this is outside context presented as part of the study's significance. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the point to general osteoporosis literature rather than to this analysis."

### Study 17: Six weeks of augmented-reality exercise improved balance and breathing strength in older adults — while the comparison group lost leg muscle
**PMID:** 41087006 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Control-group improvements omitted** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "The AR group improved significantly on the Timed Up and Go mobility test, the one-minute sit-to-stand test, and both maximal inspiratory and expiratory pressure."
- **Abstract says:** "The control group improved in maximal expiratory pressure (p < .01) and the Five Times Sit-to-Stand Test (p < .01), but leg muscle mass significantly decreased (p < .05)."
- **Problem:** Maximal expiratory pressure improved in the usual-activity control group too, so presenting it as an AR-specific gain overstates the intervention's distinct benefit; the control's five-times sit-to-stand gain is likewise unmentioned. No between-group comparison is reported in the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the control group also improved on expiratory pressure and the five-times sit-to-stand test, and that the abstract reports within-group rather than between-group results."

**Issue B. Cognitive null extended to both groups** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Cognitive function did not improve in either group"
- **Abstract says:** "No significant changes were observed in the Five Times Sit-to-Stand Test, pulmonary capacity, or cognitive function. The control group improved in maximal expiratory pressure (p < .01) and the Five Times Sit-to-Stand Test (p < .01), but leg muscle mass significantly decreased (p < .05)."
- **Problem:** The abstract's null cognitive finding is stated for the AR group; it does not explicitly report cognitive results for the control group.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say cognitive function did not improve in the AR group, with no cognitive gain reported for controls."

**Issue C. "Balance" mislabels the mobility outcome** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "improved balance and breathing strength in older adults"
- **Abstract says:** "The AR-exercise group showed significant improvements in Timed Up and Go (p < .01), One-Minute Sit-to-Stand Test (p < .05), maximal inspiratory pressure (p < .01), and maximal expiratory pressure (p < .01)."
- **Problem:** No standalone balance test result is reported; Timed Up and Go is a mobility measure, which the body text correctly names but the headline recasts as balance.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change the headline to "improved mobility and breathing strength.""
