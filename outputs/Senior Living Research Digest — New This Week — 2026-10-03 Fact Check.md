### Study 1: Brain shunt surgery clearly restores walking in older adults with 'reversible dementia' — but the bleeding risk can't be ruled out
**PMID:** 42825924 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline overstates magnitude** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Brain shunt surgery clearly restores walking in older adults with 'reversible dementia'"
- **Abstract says:** "Shunting significantly improved the primary outcome of gait velocity (SMD 0.73, 95% CI 0.45 to 1.01; P < 0.0001)... appears to provide meaningful clinical benefit"
- **Problem:** "Clearly restores walking" implies walking is returned to normal; the trials measured a moderate-to-large improvement in gait velocity, not restoration of normal gait.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "clearly improves walking speed" or "produces meaningful gains in walking and independence"."

**Issue B. Exhaustiveness claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers pooled the four randomized controlled trials ever conducted on cerebrospinal fluid shunting for idiopathic normal pressure hydrocephalus (iNPH)"
- **Abstract says:** "Four RCTs comprising 224 patients were included."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports four trials met the inclusion criteria (immediate shunting vs placebo/no shunting); it does not state these are the only shunt RCTs ever conducted.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "pooled the four randomized controlled trials that met inclusion criteria"."

### Study 3: New Alzheimer's drugs: brain swelling and bleeding hit 1 in 10 — and your genes predict much of the risk
**PMID:** 42825842 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Participant count attributed to treated arm** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "21 phase 3 randomized controlled trials covering 12,610 people with early Alzheimer's disease who received anti-amyloid monoclonal antibodies"
- **Abstract says:** "20 articles, representing 21 phase 3 randomized controlled trials that involved 12,610 AD patients"
- **Problem:** The 12,610 figure is the total trial population, which includes placebo arms; not all received an anti-amyloid antibody.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "21 phase 3 trials involving 12,610 people with early Alzheimer's disease randomized to anti-amyloid antibodies or placebo"."

**Issue B. Headline conflates two ARIA rates** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "brain swelling and bleeding hit 1 in 10"
- **Abstract says:** "Pooled incidence was 5.4% for ARIA-E and 10.27% for ARIA-H."
- **Problem:** Only the bleeding (ARIA-H) rate is about 1 in 10; swelling (ARIA-E) was roughly 1 in 20, and the combined rate is not reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "brain bleeds hit 1 in 10, swelling 1 in 20"."

**Issue C. Drug names not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "anti-amyloid monoclonal antibodies such as lecanemab and donanemab"
- **Abstract says:** "patients with early AD receiving anti-amyloid mAbs therapy"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not name the individual agents included. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the named examples or attribute them generically to the drug class."

**Issue D. Industry-sponsorship caveat unverifiable** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the underlying trials were largely industry-sponsored"
- **Abstract says:** "Authors declare that there is not any conflict of interest to declare. (No funding information on included trials is given.)"
- **Problem:** Plausible but not stated anywhere in the abstract. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute to general knowledge of phase 3 trials or remove."

### Study 4: In people at genetic risk for Alzheimer's, the brain's blood vessels start slipping years before memory does
**PMID:** 41353310 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies untested time course** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the brain's blood vessels start slipping years before memory does"
- **Abstract says:** "APOE4 carriers uniquely presented a significant decline in relative CVR within the left PCA... higher relative CVR was positively associated with better cognitive performance in APOE4 carriers."
- **Problem:** Participants remained cognitively unimpaired throughout; the study did not show that vascular change precedes memory decline by years, only that it occurs in people whose cognition is still intact.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "blood vessels start slipping while memory still tests normal"."

**Issue B. Cohort description not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "participants were volunteers with a family history of Alzheimer's"
- **Abstract says:** "We utilized the PREVENT-AD cohort, including 101 APOE4 carriers... and 152 non-APOE4 carriers... aged 55 and older."
- **Problem:** The family-history enrollment criterion of PREVENT-AD is not stated in the abstract. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute to the cohort's published design or soften to "a volunteer research cohort"."

### Study 5: A sense of meaning tracks with less despair at the end of life — but the research behind it is thinner than you'd think
**PMID:** 41762175 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Reader advice outruns the evidence base** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "It's reasonable to ask your palliative care team directly whether they offer dignity therapy, chaplaincy, or meaning-centered counseling, and to request it if they don't volunteer it."
- **Abstract says:** "Further high-quality, longitudinal research is needed before MIL-centered interventions can be recommended for routine clinical practice."
- **Problem:** The review explicitly withholds a recommendation for meaning-centered interventions, and dignity therapy is not named in the abstract; telling readers to request these services goes beyond what the evidence supports.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as a conversation prompt about existential concerns rather than a request for named interventions, and note the review stopped short of recommending them."

**Issue B. "Consistently" overstates agreement** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Across them, a stronger sense of meaning was consistently associated with less depression, anxiety, and demoralization"
- **Abstract says:** "MIL was inversely associated with depression, anxiety, demoralization, and WTHD... However, heterogeneity in MIL conceptualization and measurement, combined with low methodological quality, limited comparability and certainty of findings."
- **Problem:** The authors stress that heterogeneity and weak methods limited comparability, so "consistently" implies more agreement than the review claims.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "was generally associated with" and keep the heterogeneity caveat adjacent."

### Study 7: Clot-removal surgery after stroke works for patients in their 70s, 80s and beyond, pooled trial data show
**PMID:** 41706582 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported old-versus-young comparison** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "this analysis suggests carefully selected patients in their 70s and 80s gain just as much as younger ones, including a survival benefit"
- **Abstract says:** "Fifteen RCTs, involving 1481 elderly patients, were included... Subgroup analyses based on age threshold (>80 years), anterior/posterior circulation, and country socioeconomic status were consistent with the overall results."
- **Problem:** The pooled data cover only patients over 70; no comparison with younger patients was performed, so the claim of equal benefit is not supported by the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "older patients who received thrombectomy did better than those managed medically, with benefits holding above age 80"."

**Issue B. Headline firmer than authors' conclusion** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Clot-removal surgery after stroke works for patients in their 70s, 80s and beyond, pooled trial data show"
- **Abstract says:** "This updated meta-analysis suggests that MT may be associated with better functional outcomes and lower mortality in selected elderly patients with AIS; however, limited elderly specific safety data and selective trial populations warrant cautious interpretation."
- **Problem:** "Works" presents as settled what the authors describe as a suggestive association requiring cautious interpretation in selected patients.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "appears to benefit selected patients in their 70s, 80s and beyond"."

### Study 8: Online dementia courses really do teach families what they need to know — but no one has checked whether the knowledge sticks
**PMID:** 40831193 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Age-range finding stretched to benefit claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the evidence suggests people across the full adult age range, into their 90s and beyond, complete and benefit from them"
- **Abstract says:** "Findings from a descriptive study, which included participants ranging from primary school age to 100 years in a mixed-method study, highlight the broad age spectrum of dementia e-learning platforms."
- **Problem:** The age span comes from one descriptive study and describes who participated, not evidence that the oldest learners completed courses or gained knowledge.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say that one descriptive study included participants from primary school age to 100, illustrating the broad reach, without claiming demonstrated benefit at the oldest ages."

**Issue B. Headline overstates scope of learning** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Online dementia courses really do teach families what they need to know"
- **Abstract says:** "digital learning modalities... significantly improve dementia knowledge acquisition, with post-intervention assessments showing notable increases in Dementia Knowledge Assessment Scale (DKAS) scores"
- **Problem:** The outcome was a knowledge score, not whether courses covered what caregivers need; the review also included the general public, not only families.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "Online dementia courses measurably raise knowledge scores for the public and family caregivers"."

### Study 16: Turning down the frequency on deep brain stimulation improved balance in Parkinson's patients
**PMID:** 42240331 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Feasibility designation omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Sixteen people with Parkinson's disease who already had subthalamic deep brain stimulation (STN-DBS) implants were tested off medication in a double-blind, randomized crossover trial"
- **Abstract says:** "Low-Frequency STN-DBS for Standing and Gait Initiation in Parkinson's Disease: A Double-Blinded Randomised Cross-Over Feasibility Trial"
- **Problem:** The study is labeled a feasibility trial by its authors, which signals it was not powered for definitive efficacy conclusions; describing it only as a double-blind randomized crossover trial slightly inflates its evidentiary weight.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "feasibility" — e.g., "in a double-blind, randomized crossover feasibility trial.""

**Issue B. Null result on postural phase not mentioned** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "researchers found balance was measurably better on low-frequency stimulation during standing and during the walking phase of gait initiation"
- **Abstract says:** "balance during both standing and the locomotion phase of gait initiation was improved with low-frequency STN-DBS, while no difference in the postural phase of gait initiation"
- **Problem:** The digest reports the two positive findings without noting that the postural (anticipatory) phase of gait initiation showed no difference, giving a slightly more uniform picture of benefit than the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a clause: "...while the preparatory postural phase of gait initiation showed no difference.""

### Study 17: Telling people gout is biological, not a diet problem, made them more open to long-term medication
**PMID:** 41931035 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Belief rating described as willingness/uptake** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this trial shows how the explanation itself shifts willingness to accept the urate-lowering drugs that actually prevent attacks and joint damage"
- **Abstract says:** "Primary outcome was rating of "How likely is it that long-term medications would help gout?" on an 11-point Likert scale... Those in the biologic group were more likely to believe that long-term medication would help gout (mean score 7.6 vs 6.5, P = 0.001)."
- **Problem:** The measured outcome was a belief about whether medication would help, not willingness to take it; the body text overstates in a way the Caveats then contradict ("not actual medication uptake or long-term behavior").
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to "shifts beliefs about whether long-term urate-lowering medication would help" and keep the uptake question as an open one."

### Study 18: Eight years of bone scans suggest type 2 diabetes doesn't break bones the way we assumed
**PMID:** 42555304 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Non-significant difference presented as a finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "in fact, cortical bone density at the radius declined slightly less in the diabetes group"
- **Abstract says:** "cortical volumetric bone mineral density at the radius changed by -1.50% (95% CI -2.43, -0.56) in type 2 diabetes and -1.96% (-2.53, -1.39) in prediabetes, compared with -2.42% (-2.86, -1.97) in normoglycemia (reference group; all P > 0.05)"
- **Problem:** The between-group difference was explicitly non-significant (all P > 0.05); describing it as a slower decline in the diabetes group implies a real difference the data do not support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note it as numerically but not statistically different, e.g., "the numerical trend at the radius even slightly favored the diabetes group, though no between-group comparison was statistically significant.""

**Issue B. Alternative mechanisms not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "pointing attention instead toward falls, neuropathy, vision loss, hypoglycemia, and medication side effects as the things worth targeting"
- **Abstract says:** "suggesting that bone deterioration alone does not explain the higher fracture risk in older adults with type 2 diabetes. Future studies should address other contributors to skeletal fragility."
- **Problem:** The specific list of alternative drivers is the digest's own extrapolation; the abstract only calls generically for study of "other contributors." Plausible but unverifiable from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the list as informed speculation, e.g., "the authors call for study of other contributors to fragility — plausibly falls, neuropathy, vision and medication effects.""

### Study 19: Muscle loss more than doubles the odds of a poor recovery after stroke
**PMID:** 42811735 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal phrasing in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Muscle loss more than doubles the odds of a poor recovery after stroke"
- **Abstract says:** "Pooled results indicated that stroke patients with comorbid sarcopenia faced a notably elevated risk of poor global functional prognosis (mRS, OR = 2.43, 95% CI: 1.95 to 3.03, p < 0.001)."
- **Problem:** "Doubles" implies sarcopenia causes worse recovery; the pooled data are observational associations, as the digest's own Caveats acknowledge ("no causal claim is justified").
- **Suggested fix:** "Use associational wording: "Muscle loss is linked to more than double the odds of a poor recovery after stroke.""

### Study 20: Not everyone benefits equally from central sleep apnea machines — and the oldest, sickest patients gained the least
**PMID:** 42296895 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline ranks clusters beyond what is reported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the oldest, sickest patients gained the least"
- **Abstract says:** "Patients from C3 versus C1/C2 had the highest apnea-hypopnea index at baseline (55 vs. 48/39.5 events/h), were the most symptomatic, had the worst quality of life (QoL), and had the greatest symptomatic and QoL improvement on ASV."
- **Problem:** The abstract states only that the younger cluster improved most; it does not rank C1 and C2 against each other or label them as gaining "the least," and the "sickest" patients by apnea severity were in fact the younger cluster.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "the younger, most symptomatic patients gained the most — the two older clusters improved less.""

**Issue B. Funding statement partly unverifiable** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "funded and co-authored by ResMed employees (the device manufacturer)"
- **Abstract says:** "FL, AVB and SF are employees of Resmed. SB reports fees from Resmed relating to the current study. [no funding statement given]"
- **Problem:** ResMed co-authorship and consulting/lecture fees are documented, but the abstract contains no explicit funding statement, so "funded ... by ResMed" cannot be confirmed from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "co-authored by ResMed employees, with one author reporting ResMed fees for this study and most senior authors reporting ResMed consulting or lecture fees.""
