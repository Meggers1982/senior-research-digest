### Study 2: Hip Fractures in Nursing Homes Fell 18% in Five Years — But Who Gets Hurt Comes Down to the Resident, Not the Building
**PMID:** 42987451 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal verb for observational associations** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this very large study suggests individual clinical factors drive hip fracture risk far more than structural features of the building or its location"
- **Abstract says:** "Fracture occurrence was associated primarily with resident-level clinical characteristics rather than broad facility-level structural indicators."
- **Problem:** "Drive" implies causation, while the design is a retrospective cohort that can only show association. The headline ("comes down to the resident") pushes in the same direction.
- **Suggested fix:** "Replace with "individual clinical factors were far more strongly associated with hip fracture risk than structural features.""

### Study 4: Depression That Never Lifts Speeds Up Physical Decline, Four Global Studies Agree
**PMID:** 42822543 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for cohort data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Depression That Never Lifts Speeds Up Physical Decline, Four Global Studies Agree"
- **Abstract says:** "Persistent high burden thus represents a reproducible risk marker of accelerated frailty accumulation"
- **Problem:** "Speeds up" asserts causation; the authors describe persistent high symptom burden as a risk marker that predicted faster frailty accumulation, and the digest's own caveat says causation cannot be established.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Depression That Never Lifts Tracks With Faster Physical Decline, Four Global Studies Find.""

**Issue B. Education direction inferred** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "while lower education predicted membership in the worse trajectories"
- **Abstract says:** "education was a directionally consistent predictor of phenotype membership across the four cohorts (OR 0.32-0.80)"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports only that education was a directionally consistent predictor with ORs below 1; the specific statement that lower education predicted the worse trajectories is a reasonable but unstated inference.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "education level was consistently associated with which trajectory people fell into (ORs 0.32–0.80), with more education linked to better trajectories.""

### Study 6: Brain Scans Show Why Uneven Ground Trips Up Some Older Adults More Than Others
**PMID:** 42679689 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Which gait measure the brain findings tracked** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "these brain changes tracked most strongly with unsteady walking in the lower-functioning group"
- **Abstract says:** "A significant group-by-behavior interaction showed that higher FW was more strongly associated with greater baseline gait variability in lower-functioning than higher-functioning older adults, particularly in the body of corpus callosum. Extracellular free water and tissue-specific white matter measures showed distinct associations with baseline versus uneven-terrain walking"
- **Problem:** The interaction reported was specifically for free water and baseline gait variability (notably in the corpus callosum), not for all the brain measures or for walking on uneven terrain; the abstract stresses that baseline and uneven-terrain associations were distinct.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Higher extracellular fluid was most strongly linked to irregular step timing at baseline in the lower-functioning group, especially in the corpus callosum.""

**Issue B. Causal framing of cross-sectional imaging** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the difficulty is rooted partly in measurable brain changes, not simply carelessness"
- **Abstract says:** "We examined age and physical function group differences in white matter microstructure and tested whether associations between white matter measures and uneven-terrain walking behavior differed between these groups."
- **Problem:** "Rooted in" implies the brain changes cause the walking difficulty; the design is cross-sectional and only shows associations, as the digest's own caveat notes.
- **Suggested fix:** ""the difficulty is associated with measurable brain differences, not simply carelessness.""

### Study 8: A One-Page Checklist Transformed How Hospitals Examine Patients After a Fall
**PMID:** 42877601 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Documentation treated as examination performed** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "this shows that without a structured form, critical checks like head examination and clotting risk are skipped most of the time"
- **Abstract says:** "Documentation was significantly higher in the proforma group across examination domains: ... external head (97.6% vs. 35.7%) ... coagulopathy (98.4% vs. 16.7%)"
- **Problem:** The study measured whether assessments were documented, not whether they were performed. Undocumented checks may still have happened; the digest's own caveat says the study measures documentation completeness, so the body text contradicts it.
- **Suggested fix:** ""this shows that without a structured form, critical checks like head examination and clotting risk go undocumented most of the time.""

**Issue B. Hype verb in headline** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "A One-Page Checklist Transformed How Hospitals Examine Patients After a Fall"
- **Abstract says:** "Use of a structured Falls Proforma was associated with significantly greater completeness of post-fall documentation."
- **Problem:** "Transformed" asserts a causal effect from a non-randomized retrospective comparison, and "how hospitals examine patients" misstates the outcome (documentation, in one Australian hospital).
- **Suggested fix:** ""A Structured Checklist Was Linked to Far More Complete Post-Fall Documentation at One Hospital.""

**Issue C. 'One-page' detail** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A One-Page Checklist"
- **Abstract says:** "a structured Falls Proforma ... Documentation across 12 assessment domains was compared"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not describe the proforma's length. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "one-page" or describe it as a structured proforma covering 12 assessment domains."

### Study 9: People Sent to Falls Prevention Programs Are the Ones Already Getting Hurt, Finnish Data Show
**PMID:** 42986542 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Timing implied by headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "People Sent to Falls Prevention Programs Are the Ones Already Getting Hurt, Finnish Data Show"
- **Abstract says:** "Patients' injury-related specialized health care utilization was followed from their first MFFP visit until death or the end of December 2019."
- **Problem:** Utilization was measured after program entry, not before it, so the data show higher injury care during follow-up rather than documented prior injury; higher baseline risk is the authors' inference, not a measured pre-program finding.
- **Suggested fix:** ""People Sent to Falls Prevention Programs Use Far More Injury Care Afterward — a Sign of Who Gets Referred, Finnish Data Show.""

### Study 10: When Bone Loss and Muscle Loss Travel Together, Fall and Fracture Risk Climbs Sharply
**PMID:** 42975735 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Synergistic risk claim contradicts authors** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "yet the combination is far more dangerous than either alone"
- **Abstract says:** "We advance this systems-level framing as an integrative hypothesis rather than established fact: more-than-additive risk and synergistic treatment effects are not yet demonstrated in humans."
- **Problem:** The review explicitly states that more-than-additive risk has not been shown in humans; the digest asserts it as fact. The caveat mentions only that synergistic treatment effects are undemonstrated, so the body text goes beyond the caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** ""yet having both conditions carries substantial fracture, fall and mortality risk — although the authors caution that a more-than-additive effect has not been demonstrated in humans.""

### Study 12: Feeling Old and Falling Apart Predicts Fear of Falling, Regardless of How Many Illnesses You Have
**PMID:** 42954246 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Predicts" for cross-sectional data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Feeling Old and Falling Apart Predicts Fear of Falling, Regardless of How Many Illnesses You Have"
- **Abstract says:** "This cross-sectional study examined a sample of 150 German community-dwelling older adults aged 71-93... higher loss-related SPA, but not lower gain-related SPA, are associated with greater CaF."
- **Problem:** "Predicts" implies a temporal/forward-looking relationship that a single-time-point survey cannot establish; the abstract uses only "associated with." The body text and caveats are correctly hedged, so only the headline is out of step.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Is Linked to" or "Goes With Greater Fear of Falling"."

**Issue B. Existence of combined balance/aging-beliefs programs** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "since programs that combine balance training with reframing beliefs about aging exist and anxiety does not have to be accepted as part of getting older"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract describes only the cross-sectional association between self-perceptions of aging and concerns about falling; it makes no reference to intervention programs."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only — readers may take this as a finding of the study rather than outside context.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the claim to other sources or drop the reference to specific programs."

### Study 13: AI Fall Prediction Is Arriving in Nursing Homes, But Nobody Has Shown It Prevents Falls Yet
**PMID:** 42696799 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Number of records screened** — Severity: Minor

**Issue A. Retrospective vs prospective faller classification** — Severity: Minor

- **Abstract says:** "This cross-sectional, discriminative study aimed to evaluate which selection of outcomes best discriminate retrospective fallers from non-fallers... Fallers and non-fallers were stratified based on prospective fall reports."

**Issue B. "Week-long" real-world monitoring** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "week-long real-world mobility monitoring with wearable sensors"

- **Problem:** The abstract does not specify the duration of real-world monitoring. Unverifiable from abstract only.

### Study 19: The Hospital Bed Alarm Problem: Common, Costly, and Not Shown to Prevent Falls
**PMID:** 42833806 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Specific harms and cost claims not in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Alarms are nearly ubiquitous on hospital units caring for older patients and contribute to noise, sleep disruption, and restricted movement — all of which can work against recovery — yet the review finds they are not supported as an effective fall-prevention tool."
- **Abstract says:** "The authors conducted a review of fall prevention alarms in hospital settings. The review covers the evidence for fall prevention alarms and other fall prevention practices, factors that contribute to overuse of fall prevention alarms, and strategies that are being tested to de-implement fall prevention alarms."
- **Problem:** Ubiquity, cost ("Costly" in the headline), noise, sleep disruption and restricted movement, and the explicit conclusion that alarms are ineffective, are all inferences from the review's framing of alarms as overused low-value care rather than statements in the abstract. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "the review treats alarms as an overused, low-value practice and examines strategies to scale back their use," and drop or source the cost and sleep-disruption claims."

**Issue B. "Bed and chair" alarms** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "reviewed the evidence base for bed and chair fall-prevention alarms in hospitals"
- **Abstract says:** "a review of fall prevention alarms in hospital settings"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not specify alarm types; the bed/chair distinction is added detail.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "fall-prevention alarms" without specifying bed and chair types."

### Study 20: Centenarian Hospital Care in France: Most Arrive Through the Emergency Room, Often for Broken Bones
**PMID:** 42970244 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Falls described as the largest and fastest-growing driver** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "the data show falls and fractures are the single largest and fastest-growing driver of hospital use at age 100"
- **Abstract says:** "Musculoskeletal conditions accounted for 16.9% of acute care stays and represented an increasing proportion over time."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports only the musculoskeletal share (16.9%) and that it is increasing; it gives no other diagnostic categories, so there is no basis for calling it the largest driver, nor for "fastest-growing" relative to other causes. It also does not state that the musculoskeletal share is "largely falls and fractures."
- **Suggested fix:** ""Musculoskeletal conditions accounted for about one in six acute stays and made up a growing share over the decade.""

**Issue B. Overstated personal risk statement** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "If you are in your late 90s or beyond, a fall is the most likely reason you would end up in a hospital"
- **Abstract says:** "Musculoskeletal conditions accounted for 16.9% of acute care stays"
- **Problem:** A 16.9% share does not establish falls as the most likely reason for admission, and the study covered people aged 100+, not the late 90s. This advises readers on the basis of a claim the data do not support.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Among people aged 100 and over, musculoskeletal problems — including falls and fractures — accounted for about one in six acute hospital stays.""

**Issue C. Omitted declining hospitalization burden** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "As the oldest-old population grows, the data show falls and fractures are the single largest and fastest-growing driver of hospital use at age 100"
- **Abstract says:** "Acute care hospitalization burden decreased from 53.2 stays per 100 centenarians in 2014 to 45.4 per 100 in 2023... rehabilitation hospitalization burden decreased from 10.3 to 8.3 stays per 100 centenarians"
- **Problem:** The digest emphasizes growth while omitting that per-capita acute and rehabilitation hospitalization rates actually fell over the study period.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that hospitalization rates per 100 centenarians declined over the decade even as the emergency-admission and musculoskeletal shares rose."

### Study 21: Broken Wrist at 65? Your Hospital Record May Never Mention Osteoporosis
**PMID:** 42448012 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Comorbidity network figures lack context** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Women had far broader networks of co-occurring conditions than men (330 linked diagnoses vs. 164 at ages 70-79)"
- **Abstract says:** "females exhibited substantially broader M81-associated comorbidity networks than males, with the number of linked diagnoses increasing from 142 to 245 to 330 in females and from 76 to 137 to 164 in males between ages 50-59, 60-69, and 70-79 years"
- **Problem:** The counts refer specifically to diagnoses linked to osteoporosis without pathological fracture (M81), not to all co-occurring conditions generally, so the digest's phrasing is broader than the source.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "women had far broader networks of conditions co-occurring with an osteoporosis diagnosis than men (330 vs. 164 linked diagnoses at ages 70-79)"."

**Issue B. Thoracic fractures omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "forearm, wrist, hand and upper arm fractures showed no significant link to an osteoporosis diagnosis in any age group"
- **Abstract says:** "Upper limb and thoracic fractures were rarely recorded alongside osteoporosis diagnoses despite being recognized as potential fragility fractures."
- **Problem:** The conclusion also covers thoracic fractures, which the digest leaves out; not wrong, just incomplete.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that thoracic fractures showed the same under-coding pattern."

### Study 22: Meals Plus Exercise at Home: What Homebound Older Adults Actually Said About It
**PMID:** 42847733 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Added detail not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "many described ongoing pain and worry about falling while exercising alone"
- **Abstract says:** "Participants (N = 101, mean age 74 years) reported enjoying meals and engaging with the exercise materials, though many reported ongoing pain and concerns about falls."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports concerns about falls generally; it does not tie them to exercising alone or unsupervised.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "while exercising alone" or mark it as unverifiable from the abstract."

**Issue B. Barriers framed as blocking participation** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "fear of falling and unmanaged pain can quietly block participation in exactly the activity meant to reduce fall risk"
- **Abstract says:** "Participants ... reported enjoying meals and engaging with the exercise materials, though many reported ongoing pain and concerns about falls."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports that participants did engage with the exercise materials despite pain and fall concerns; it does not report that these factors reduced or blocked participation.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "pain and worry about falling were common even among participants who engaged with the program, and may need to be addressed directly.""

**Issue C. Unsupported ranking of barriers** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "these were the most common barriers participants raised"
- **Abstract says:** "many reported ongoing pain and concerns about falls"
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no frequency ranking of themes, only that "many" reported these.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "these were among the concerns participants commonly raised.""

### Study 25: Stopping Blood Pressure Pills in Nursing Homes: The Evidence Is Thinner Than You'd Expect
**PMID:** 42888381 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Implied ranking of deprescribing strategies** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the trials that worked best were supervised, with clear restart criteria"
- **Abstract says:** "Within individual trials, supervised deprescribing reduced antihypertensive exposure and raised blood pressure, but the magnitude and clinical consequences varied by strategy and follow-up."
- **Problem:** The review did not identify any trial as working "best"; it reported that supervised deprescribing lowered drug exposure and raised blood pressure, with clinical consequences varying and no pooled comparison.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "the trials all used supervised tapering with defined monitoring and restart criteria, so it is fair to ask what yours would be.""

**Issue B. Mortality figures presented without their uncertainty** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "mortality estimates varied (HR 1.02 in RETREAT-FRAIL over about 38 months vs. 1.65 in DANTON over 32 weeks)"
- **Abstract says:** "RETREAT-FRAIL reported an adjusted hazard ratio (HR) for all-cause mortality of 1.02 (95% confidence interval 0.86-1.21) ... whereas DANTON reported an HR of 1.65 (0.95-2.85) to 32 weeks"
- **Problem:** Both estimates crossed 1, so neither showed a statistically detectable mortality effect; quoting 1.65 without the confidence interval may read as a real excess death signal.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the confidence intervals or note that neither estimate was statistically significant."
