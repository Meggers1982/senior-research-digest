### Study 1: Three in Four Rural Medicare Patients With High Blood Pressure Aren't Actually Controlling It
**PMID:** 42720604 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Medication status overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A hypertension diagnosis and a prescription do not guarantee the numbers are actually where they should be"
- **Abstract says:** "Uncontrolled hypertension was defined as blood pressure ≥130/80 mmHg with or without antihypertensive medications."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not report how many participants were actually on antihypertensive medication, so implying that this cohort was diagnosed and prescribed treatment goes slightly beyond the data.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "A hypertension diagnosis — with or without medication — does not guarantee the numbers are where they should be.""

### Study 3: Optimism Lowers Depression Risk in Older Adults — But Doesn't Cushion the Blow of a Bad Year
**PMID:** 42567210 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for observational cohort** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Optimism Lowers Depression Risk in Older Adults — But Doesn't Cushion the Blow of a Bad Year"
- **Abstract says:** "Optimism was associated with a lower risk of late-life depression."
- **Problem:** "Lowers" asserts causation from a prospective observational cohort; the body text and caveats correctly use associational language, so only the headline overreaches.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Optimism Tracks With Lower Depression Risk in Older Adults — But Doesn't Cushion the Blow of a Bad Year.""

### Study 4: An Eye Photo May Carry Early Clues to Depression — and to Heart Disease That Follows It
**PMID:** 42551563 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Highest vein tortuosity" comparison unspecified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with roughly double the risk for the highest vein tortuosity"
- **Abstract says:** "increased retinal tortuosity was associated with cardiovascular disease risk (vein tortuosity density: HR 2.24, 95% CI 1.25-4.01; artery tortuosity density: HR 1.90, 95% CI 1.17-3.07)"
- **Problem:** The abstract gives a hazard ratio for vein tortuosity density without stating the comparison unit (per standard deviation, quartile, etc.), so "the highest" group framing is unverifiable from the abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "with a roughly two-fold higher risk associated with greater vein tortuosity (HR 2.24)" and drop the implied top-group comparison."

### Study 5: Living Past 80 Increasingly Means Living With Disability, Not Dying From Heart Disease
**PMID:** 42594758 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Headline reverses the abstract's main qualifier** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Living Past 80 Increasingly Means Living With Disability, Not Dying From Heart Disease"
- **Abstract says:** "Median DBR increased from 19.0% to 25.9%, although no country reached a YLD-over-YLL crossover… health loss at ages ≥80 years shifted toward a larger disabling share while fatal burden remained dominant."
- **Problem:** The headline states that disability has replaced fatal burden, whereas the authors specifically note that fatal burden still dominates and that no country crossed over. The digest body never mentions this key qualifier.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "Disability Is Claiming a Growing Share of Health Loss After 80 — Though Fatal Burden Still Dominates," and add the no-crossover point to the body."

**Issue B. YLL rates described as death rates** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Death rates fell in every country (median −31.3%), while disability rates rose in six"
- **Abstract says:** "YLL ASRs declined in all 10 countries (median change, -31.3%), whereas YLD ASRs increased in six (median change, +1.9%)."
- **Problem:** The −31.3% figure is the change in age-standardized years-of-life-lost rates, not in death rates; the two are related but not interchangeable.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "Years of life lost to death fell in every country (median −31.3%), while years lived with disability rose in six.""

### Study 6: When Cancer and Heart Disease Collide, the Heart Gets Deprioritized
**PMID:** 42049274 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Misinterpreted point-allocation comparison** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "among the 42% in active cancer treatment the gap widened dramatically (80 vs 50, p<0.001)"
- **Abstract says:** "Actively treated patients placed greater emphasis on cancer than completed-treatment patients (80 vs 50 points, p<0.001)."
- **Problem:** The 80 and 50 are cancer-concern scores in two different groups (active vs completed treatment), not a within-group cancer-versus-heart split. As written the two numbers sum to 130 out of a 100-point allocation, which is impossible.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "patients in active cancer treatment (42% of the sample) assigned a median 80 of 100 points to cancer concerns, versus 50 among those who had finished treatment (p<0.001).""

### Study 12: Sleeping with the lights on may reshape your heart, large UK study finds
**PMID:** 42715935 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported comparison of effect size** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Bedroom light is one of the few cardiovascular exposures a person can change tonight for free, and the effect sizes here rival those of better-known risk factors."
- **Abstract says:** "During 8-10 years of follow-up, participants with high (vs none) nighttime light exposure had 29% (12%, 49%) higher risk of heart failure, 15% (4%, 27%) higher risk of atrial fibrillation, 24% (7%, 44%) higher risk of myocardial infarction, 33% (11%, 59%) higher risk of stroke, and 26% (5%, 52%) higher risk of CVD mortality."
- **Problem:** The abstract makes no comparison between nighttime light exposure and established risk factors, so the claim that the effect sizes "rival" them cannot be verified from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the comparison or rephrase as "the associations were of a size worth noting" without benchmarking against other risk factors."

**Issue B. Mediation figure attached to the wrong set of results** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Roughly a quarter to a half of the effect was statistically explained by shorter sleep."
- **Abstract says:** "All associations exhibited linear dose-response relationships, with most partly mediated by shorter sleep duration (proportion mediated: 24%-49%)."
- **Problem:** The 24-49% mediation figure is reported for the cardiac structure and function (CMR) associations, but the digest places it immediately after the incident event risks, implying it applies to heart failure, stroke and mortality.
- **Suggested fix:** "Move the sentence next to the imaging findings, e.g. "A quarter to a half of these structural and functional associations was statistically explained by shorter sleep.""

### Study 13: After a stroke, how far your cholesterol drops may matter as much as the final number
**PMID:** 42623575 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Ever achieved" misstates a cross-sectional proportion** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Only about 30% of patients ever achieved the 50% reduction."
- **Abstract says:** "Throughout the follow-up period, the proportion of patients achieving a ≥50% LDL-C reduction from baseline remained at approximately 30%."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports that roughly 30% were at goal at any given time during follow-up, not that only 30% ever reached it at any point; "ever achieved" implies a cumulative figure the abstract does not give.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "About 30% of patients were achieving the 50% reduction at any given point during follow-up.""

### Study 14: A third of cardiac rehab patients have trouble swallowing — and most aren't screened for it
**PMID:** 41716004 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline claim not measured in the study** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "A third of cardiac rehab patients have trouble swallowing — and most aren't screened for it"
- **Abstract says:** "Dysphagia (ASHA-NOMS ≤6) was identified in 33.3% of patients. ... Dysphagia is a frequent and often underrecognized condition in patients admitted to cardiac intensive rehabilitation, including those who have not undergone surgery."
- **Problem:** The study screened all 93 consecutive admissions; it did not measure what proportion of cardiac rehabilitation patients are screened in usual care. "Most aren't screened" is an assertion the abstract does not support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "A third of cardiac rehab patients have trouble swallowing — and it often goes unrecognised.""

**Issue B. Prevention benefit overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "complications that are largely preventable with early screening and speech-language therapy"
- **Abstract says:** "Early identification and speech-language therapy intervention may prevent complications such as malnutrition, aspiration pneumonia, and delays in functional recovery."
- **Problem:** The abstract uses "may prevent"; this cross-sectional study did not test whether screening or therapy changed outcomes, so "largely preventable" is stronger than the evidence.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "complications that early screening and speech-language therapy may help prevent.""

**Issue C. Comparative claim about mealtime observation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Mealtime observation by trained staff picked up safety problems that a chart review of surgical history would miss."
- **Abstract says:** "The MAS safety subscale showed the highest correlation with dysphagia severity (r=-0.93). ... Dysphagia is common in patients with cardiovascular disease admitted to the intensive cardiac rehabilitation, regardless of surgical history."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a correlation between the MAS safety subscale and dysphagia severity and that dysphagia occurred regardless of surgical history; it does not compare mealtime observation with chart review. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as "Dysphagia turned up even in patients who had not had surgery, so surgical history alone is a poor filter for who needs screening.""

### Study 17: Aspirin Linked to Far Less Aortic Valve Calcium — But Only in People With High Lp(a)
**PMID:** 41721439 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. LDL-C comparison group described as having normal Lp(a)** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "No such association appeared in people with high LDL cholesterol but normal Lp(a)."
- **Abstract says:** "For participants with elevated LDL-C, there was no association of regular aspirin use with incident AVC (LDL-C ≥130 mg/dL: HR 1.02 ...) or severe AS ..."
- **Problem:** The analysis was stratified separately by Lp(a) and by LDL-C; the abstract does not describe the elevated-LDL-C strata as being restricted to people with normal Lp(a).
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "No such association appeared when participants were grouped by high LDL cholesterol instead.""

### Study 18: Experimental Injection Cut a Stubborn Inherited Heart Risk Factor by Up to 97% — for Nearly a Year After One Shot
**PMID:** 42664979 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "96-97%" for all doses ≥225 mg not fully reported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "At doses of 225 mg or higher, median lipoprotein(a) levels remained 96-97% below baseline a full 48 weeks after that single injection."
- **Abstract says:** "median percent ... reductions ... at the final follow-up timepoint of 48 weeks for the Kylo-11 groups ranged from -53% ... in the 9 mg cohort to -97% ... in the 600 mg cohort. In cohort 7 (225 mg; baseline lipoprotein(a) >200 nmol/L), the median reductions were -96% ..."
- **Problem:** The abstract gives 48-week reductions only for the 9 mg cohort, the 600 mg cohort and cohort 7 (225 mg, high baseline); values for the 225 mg and 450 mg cohorts 4-5 are not stated, so the "96-97% at 225 mg or higher" range cannot be fully verified from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "reductions reached 97% in the highest-dose cohort and 96% in the high-baseline 225 mg cohort at 48 weeks, with the authors reporting durable lowering at doses of 225 mg or higher.""

**Issue B. Sponsor and employer conflated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "funded by Kylonova Biopharma, with several authors employed by the sponsor company Hygieia Pharmaceuticals and holding related patents"
- **Abstract says:** "FUNDING: Kylonova Biopharma. ... YG, ZL, QZ, and KC are employees of Hygieia Pharmaceuticals."
- **Problem:** The trial funder is Kylonova Biopharma; Hygieia Pharmaceuticals is the employer of four authors (one of whom is also affiliated with Kylonova), not the stated sponsor.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "funded by Kylonova Biopharma, with four authors employed by Hygieia Pharmaceuticals and named on related patents.""

### Study 19: Having Several Chronic Conditions Doubles Death Risk — and the Mind-Body Combination Is the Most Dangerous
**PMID:** 42475970 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal phrasing in headline for pooled observational data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Having Several Chronic Conditions Doubles Death Risk — and the Mind-Body Combination Is the Most Dangerous"
- **Abstract says:** "The pooled hazard ratios (HRs) ... for multiple chronic conditions and risk of all-cause mortality were 2.015 (95% CI:1.870-2.171, I2: 99.8%, n = 69) in the general population ... This meta-analysis demonstrates that multimorbidity is a significant driver of all-cause, CVD specific, and cancer-specific mortality."
- **Problem:** "Doubles Death Risk" states a causal effect; the pooled estimate comes from observational cohorts, as the digest's own Caveats acknowledge.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "Having Several Chronic Conditions Is Linked to Double the Death Risk.""

**Issue B. Specific conditions not named in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the combination — especially depression or anxiety alongside heart disease or diabetes — is where the real danger sits"
- **Abstract says:** "4.006 (95% CI:2.094-7.666, I2:99.8%, n = 5) in patients with physical-mental multimorbidity"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports only a broad "physical-mental multimorbidity" category based on five studies; it does not identify depression, anxiety, heart disease or diabetes as the specific pairings driving the estimate.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use the abstract's own wording, e.g. "combined physical and mental health conditions," and note the estimate rests on five studies with a wide confidence interval."
