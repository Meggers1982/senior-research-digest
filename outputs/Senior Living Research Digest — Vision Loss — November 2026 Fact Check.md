### Study 1: Loneliness in Macular Degeneration Isn't One Problem — It Follows Several Different Paths
**PMID:** 42847076 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Depression finding stripped of its branch context** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and depressive symptoms appeared to override otherwise protective factors"
- **Abstract says:** "Depressive symptoms differentiated loneliness outcomes within a lower-anxiety branch, suggesting depression may overshadow protective factors."
- **Problem:** The abstract locates the depression effect specifically within the lower-anxiety branch of the tree; the digest presents it as a general finding across the sample, and the story angle repeats it ("depression in particular appeared to swamp other protective factors").
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the qualifier, e.g. "among participants with lower anxiety, depressive symptoms appeared to overshadow otherwise protective factors.""

### Study 2: Eye Injections for Diabetic Vision Loss: Patients Describe a Cycle of Hope and Dread
**PMID:** 42008767 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Advice framed as effective at reducing distress and improving adherence** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Arranging the same escort, preparing for post-injection discomfort, and setting expectations that vision may stabilize rather than improve can reduce distress and help maintain adherence."
- **Abstract says:** "These findings highlight the need for empathetic communication, consistent care and individualized support to promote adherence and mitigate emotional distress."
- **Problem:** The abstract identifies a need for supportive care based on interview themes; it does not test or demonstrate that any of these specific practical steps reduce distress or maintain adherence. The digest states the effect as established.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "participants described consistent care and clear expectations as important to coping, suggesting these steps may help.""

**Issue B. "mattered most" ranks factors the study did not rank** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "patients in this study said consistent information and a familiar clinician mattered most to getting through the course"
- **Abstract says:** "Trust in healthcare providers, consistent care and adequate information were crucial for emotional resilience."
- **Problem:** Qualitative themes were described as crucial, not ranked against other factors; "mattered most" implies a comparative weighting the study did not produce.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "patients described trust in their providers, consistent care and clear information as crucial to coping.""

**Issue C. Sample size characterization not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Small qualitative study with no control group"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract reports no participant count, only that longitudinal interviews were conducted at 4 and 12 months."
- **Problem:** The number of interviewees is unverifiable from the abstract alone, so "small" cannot be confirmed (though it is a conservative characterization for a qualitative design).
- **Suggested fix:** "Note "sample size not stated in the abstract" or cite the figure from the methods section."

### Study 4: Tamoxifen Can Damage the Retina — But Almost No One Gets Their Eyes Checked
**PMID:** 42955022 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Extrapolation to older, long-term users** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "eye monitoring is essentially not happening for long-term users — including older women on multi-year regimens"
- **Abstract says:** "Among 2327 individuals taking tamoxifen (mean [SD] age, 53.8 [10.6] years...), 162 (7.0%) had an ophthalmic encounter within 12 months... retrospective cohort study... with 365-day minimum follow-up."
- **Problem:** The cohort had a mean age of 54 and outcomes were measured over only the first 12 months after initiation; the digest extends the conclusion to older women on multi-year regimens, a group the analysis did not separately characterize.
- **Suggested fix:** "Limit the claim to the first year after starting tamoxifen in a cohort averaging 54 years old, and drop or flag the multi-year/older-women extension as an inference."

**Issue B. Claim about aromatase inhibitor retinal risk not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "rates of eye care were no higher than among women taking aromatase inhibitors, which do not carry the same retinal risk"
- **Abstract says:** "Among participants with breast cancer, 126 of 1886 individuals taking tamoxifen (6.7%) had an encounter vs 240 of 2809 individuals taking aromatase inhibitors (8.5%...). After adjustment, tamoxifen use was not associated with greater ophthalmic use."
- **Problem:** The comparison itself is reported correctly, but the characterization of aromatase inhibitors as not carrying the same retinal risk is background not stated in the abstract. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the retinal-risk contrast to outside sources or drop the clause."

### Study 7: Two Decades After Radiation Plaque Treatment for Eye Melanoma, Most Patients Still Have Useful Vision
**PMID:** 42874810 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Strongest predictor" not supported** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "location was the strongest predictor of how vision held up decades later"
- **Abstract says:** "Tumors located closer to the fovea were associated with increased foveal radiation doses and poorer visual acuity outcomes. Treatment of juxtapapillary and circumpapillary tumors was associated with the highest rates of vision loss."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports associations between tumor location and visual outcome but never ranks predictors or identifies location as the strongest one.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "tumors closer to the fovea or optic disc were associated with poorer visual outcomes.""

**Issue B. Causal verb for a retrospective association** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "tumors sitting close to the fovea or the optic nerve head produced the worst visual outcomes"
- **Abstract says:** "Tumors located closer to the fovea were associated with... poorer visual acuity outcomes."
- **Problem:** "Produced" implies causation in a retrospective single-center cohort that reports associations.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "were associated with the worst visual outcomes.""

### Study 9: Newly Identified Gene Explains Some Cases of Adult-Onset Retinitis Pigmentosa
**PMID:** 42955015 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Explains" overstates a candidate-gene case series** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Newly Identified Gene Explains Some Cases of Adult-Onset Retinitis Pigmentosa"
- **Abstract says:** "bi-allelic inactivating variants in PRKCZ were associated with adult-onset RP... highlight PRKCZ as a potentially clinically relevant gene implicated in adult-onset retinal degeneration."
- **Problem:** The abstract frames PRKCZ as a novel disease gene candidate associated with the phenotype; "explains" asserts a settled causal account. The caveats do note that replication is needed, which partly offsets this.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "Newly Identified Gene Linked to Some Cases of Adult-Onset Retinitis Pigmentosa.""

**Issue B. "Retina-specific" vs retinal-enriched isoform** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the variants knock out a long, retina-specific version of the gene"
- **Abstract says:** "an isoform-specific mechanism involving the long retinal-enriched transcript"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes the long transcript as retinal-enriched, not exclusive to the retina; "retina-specific" is slightly stronger.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "a long version of the gene that is concentrated in the retina.""

### Study 10: Trouble Reading Up Close Linked to Weaker Memory Scores in Large Rural Study
**PMID:** 42716809 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. MMSE described as a memory score** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Trouble Reading Up Close Linked to Weaker Memory Scores in Large Rural Study"
- **Abstract says:** "cognitive status assessed by the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE)... Near VI was associated with poorer cognitive performance"
- **Problem:** The MMSE is a global cognitive screening tool covering orientation, attention, language and recall, not a memory test; the body text correctly says "cognitive scores," so the headline is out of step.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Linked to Weaker Cognitive Screening Scores.""

### Study 12: After Wet-AMD-Like Lesions Clear, Half of Eyes Stay Quiet for Two Years — and Half Come Back
**PMID:** 42752539 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Monitoring benefit not demonstrated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Monthly-to-bimonthly OCT monitoring visits remained essential to catching recurrences before vision was lost, so transportation support should not be scaled back."
- **Abstract says:** "At week 12, eyes with regression underwent PRN treatment and follow-up monthly during year 1 and bimonthly during year 2... Among 14 eyes with recurrence, median best-corrected visual acuity change from the prior visit was -3.5 letters (IQR, -5.0 to -0.25); 3 (21%; 95% CI, 5%-51%) lost 10 or more letters"
- **Problem:** The monthly/bimonthly schedule was the study protocol, not a tested intervention, and the data show some eyes did lose vision at recurrence — so the claim that monitoring caught recurrences 'before vision was lost' goes beyond the abstract and slightly contradicts the reported letter losses.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to: 'In this study, eyes were still seen monthly in year 1 and every two months in year 2 even after injections were deferred, so a switch to as-needed dosing did not reduce the number of visits.'"

**Issue B. Incomplete conflict-of-interest summary** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Industry funding and conflicts: grants from Bayer during the study, with multiple authors reporting fees from Bayer, Roche, Novartis, AbbVie and Alcon."
- **Abstract says:** "Dr Bressler reported grants to their employer (Johns Hopkins University) from Regeneron outside the submitted work; and Dr Bressler is the editor in chief for JAMA Ophthalmology, but was not involved in the review process or the acceptance of the manuscript."
- **Problem:** The digest captures the Bayer funding but omits the Regeneron grant and the fact that a coauthor is editor-in-chief of the publishing journal, which readers may consider relevant.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add: '...and a coauthor who is editor-in-chief of JAMA Ophthalmology (recused from review) reported Regeneron grants to his institution.'"

### Study 13: Eye Damage Is Common and Often Missed in a Widely Used Breast Cancer Drug Class
**PMID:** 42331364 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. 'Often missed' not established** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Eye Damage Is Common and Often Missed in a Widely Used Breast Cancer Drug Class"
- **Abstract says:** "Ocular surface toxicity occurred in 85.7% (18/21) of patients after HER2-ADC therapy, with a mean onset at 28.0±8.4 days."
- **Problem:** The study prospectively screened all participants and says nothing about under-recognition or missed diagnoses in routine care; 'often missed' is an unverifiable addition.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change headline to something like 'Eye Surface Damage Showed Up in 86% of Patients on a Widely Used Breast Cancer Drug Class'."

**Issue B. Age of study population unverifiable** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Many older women on these cancer drugs may attribute blurry, gritty eyes to aging or chemotherapy generally"
- **Abstract says:** "21 HER2-positive breast cancer patients receiving HER2-ADC therapy were enrolled."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports no age data, so framing the findings around older women is an extrapolation beyond the described sample.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that participant ages were not reported, and phrase as 'patients on these drugs, including older women, may attribute...'"

### Study 14: Childhood Hardship May Shape Late-Life Memory — Partly Through Untreated Vision Loss
**PMID:** 42371784 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mediation framed as an actionable causal pathway** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "meaning something as ordinary as getting an eye exam and the right glasses or cataract surgery sits on the pathway to brain health"
- **Abstract says:** "Physical neglect and parental divorce or death showed significant indirect negative effects, operating through less education, depression, and untreated vision loss."
- **Problem:** A statistical mediation path in observational survey data does not show that correcting vision changes cognitive outcomes; the wording implies an intervention effect the study did not test.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to: 'untreated vision loss was one of the statistical links in this model, making it a plausible — though untested — target for intervention.'"
