# Fact-Check Report: Senior Living Research Digest — Nutrition — September 2026
**Checked:** 2026-09-28 | **Studies reviewed:** 26
**Primary audience:** older adults and seniors | **Secondary audience:** families, caregivers, and senior living professionals

### Study 1: Insulin Resistance Linked to Enlarged Prostate in Older Men Across Two Countries
**PMID:** 42640724 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mediation analysis cohort not specified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Monocyte counts, a marker of inflammation, appeared to account for part of the association."
- **Abstract says:** "The mediation effect model in the NHANES population showed that the mediating effect of monocyte was significant at -15.14% (p < 0.0001)."
- **Problem:** The mediation finding comes only from the US (NHANES) sample, not from both cohorts as the sentence implies when read alongside the preceding 'In both populations' statement.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add 'in the US sample' — e.g., 'In the NHANES sample, monocyte counts appeared to account for part of the association (about 15%).'"

**Issue B. Subgroup vs. spline results blended** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "lower eGDR (meaning greater insulin resistance) was linked to a higher chance of having an enlarged prostate, in a consistent, linear fashion across every subgroup tested"
- **Abstract says:** "RCS revealed a significant linear relationship between eGDR and BPH in both study populations. Subgroup analyses showed that the effect of eGDR on BPH risk was consistent across all the examined subgroups."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports linearity in the two main populations and consistency across subgroups as two separate findings; the digest merges them into 'linear... across every subgroup'.
- **Suggested fix:** "Split the claims: 'the relationship was linear in both populations, and the association was consistent across all subgroups examined.'"

### Study 2: Among Dozens of Supplements Studied, Only Lycopene Stood Out in a National Diabetes Analysis
**PMID:** 42679454 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Number of supplements tested not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Among Dozens of Supplements Studied, Only Lycopene Stood Out in a National Diabetes Analysis"
- **Abstract says:** "Use of particular dietary supplements during the previous 30 days was analyzed as use versus non-use."
- **Problem:** The abstract never states how many supplements were tested, so 'dozens' cannot be verified from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'Among the dietary supplements studied, only lycopene stood out...'"

**Issue B. Scope of 'exploratory' label** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Mediation analyses were labeled exploratory by the authors."
- **Abstract says:** "Several corrected indirect effects were also observed for calcium, iodine, and molybdenum, particularly through GrimAgeMort, but these findings were exploratory."
- **Problem:** The authors applied the 'exploratory' label specifically to the calcium/iodine/molybdenum indirect effects, not to all mediation analyses.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'The authors labelled the indirect-effect findings for calcium, iodine and molybdenum as exploratory.'"

### Study 3: Where You Live and Who You Are Shape Whether Older Adults Eat Fruits and Vegetables
**PMID:** 42776721 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Untested mechanisms presented as shown** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "It shows that poor diet quality in later life is often less about personal willpower and more about access, geography, and gender roles around cooking and shopping — a distinction that determines what kind of help actually works."
- **Abstract says:** "Sociodemographic factors were associated with both individual and combined consumption of fresh fruit, beans, and vegetables among older adults in Roraima, Brazil."
- **Problem:** The study measured associations with sex, race and place of residence only; it did not examine access, willpower, cooking roles or shopping, so presenting those as demonstrated explanations goes beyond the data.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as a hypothesis: 'The pattern is consistent with access and social roles mattering, though the study measured only who ate what, not why.'"

**Issue B. Findings extrapolated beyond food-group combinations** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the finding that older men eat notably less fruit and fewer vegetables is actionable"
- **Abstract says:** "Male participants had a lower prevalence of consuming fresh fruit + beans and fresh fruit + vegetables."
- **Problem:** The reported sex difference concerns two specific food-group combinations, and the abstract gives no magnitude, so 'notably less fruit and fewer vegetables' overstates it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'men were less likely to report the fruit-plus-beans and fruit-plus-vegetables combinations.'"

**Issue C. Data source described as a survey** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers surveyed 1,322 older adults in Roraima, Brazil"
- **Abstract says:** "Sociodemographic information was obtained from electronic health records within the statewide Primary Health Care network. Healthy food consumption was assessed using the Food Consumption Markers Form..."
- **Problem:** Sociodemographic data came from electronic health records rather than a survey; only the food-marker form was administered.
- **Suggested fix:** "'Researchers analysed records and previous-day food-marker forms for 1,322 older adults...'"

**Issue D. Unquantified magnitude** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Older adults living in the state capital were considerably more likely to have eaten every healthy combination tested"
- **Abstract says:** "Participants residing in the state capital showed a higher prevalence of consuming fresh fruit + beans, fresh fruit + vegetables, beans + vegetables, and all three food groups combined."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a higher prevalence without effect sizes; 'considerably' adds magnitude that cannot be verified.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop 'considerably' or replace with 'more likely'."

### Study 5: An 8-Week Lifestyle Program — By App or By Booklet — Cut Depression Symptoms in a Randomized Trial
**PMID:** 42503319 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Booklet described as equally effective** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Importantly, the printed booklet version worked as well — you don't need to be a smartphone user to benefit"
- **Abstract says:** "At Week 9, AG (d = 0.89) and BG (d = 0.64) showed significantly greater reductions in depressive symptoms than WLG... Clinically significant improvement was achieved by 78% of AG and 55% of BG participants"
- **Problem:** Both formats beat the waitlist, but the app arm had a larger effect size and a higher response rate; the trial was not designed to show the booklet was equivalent.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'the printed booklet also beat the waitlist, though response was somewhat lower than with the app (55% vs 78%).'"

**Issue B. Single-centre claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Small sample (N=122) at a single center in Hong Kong"
- **Abstract says:** "A total of 122 adults with probable depression were randomized to AG (n = 41), BG (n = 40), or WLG (n = 41)."
- **Problem:** The abstract states neither the number of sites nor the recruitment setting, only the authors' Hong Kong affiliations.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'Small sample (N=122) recruited in Hong Kong (number of sites not reported in the abstract).'"

### Study 6: Hidden potassium: more than a quarter of packaged foods carry potassium additives that never show up on the nutrition panel
**PMID:** 42692364 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Denominator broadened in headline** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "more than a quarter of packaged foods carry potassium additives"
- **Abstract says:** "After analysing 5884 UPFs, 27.8% of the products contained at least one potassium-based additive"
- **Problem:** The 27.8% figure applies to ultra-processed foods specifically (as the body text correctly says), not to packaged foods generally, so the headline overstates the scope.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change headline to 'more than a quarter of ultra-processed foods...'"

**Issue B. Absorbability claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Potassium from additives is highly absorbable but is generally not captured in standard nutrient databases or food labels."
- **Abstract says:** "Potassium additives are widespread in UPFs and can be a hidden source of potassium..."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes additives as a hidden source but says nothing about bioavailability or nutrient databases; unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Trim to 'Potassium from additives is generally not reflected in the nutrition panel,' or attribute the absorbability point to other sources."

**Issue C. Unknown-additive products omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Nearly 28% contained at least one"
- **Abstract says:** "27.8% of the products contained at least one potassium-based additive, while the presence of potassium additives was unknown for 6.5% of the products."
- **Problem:** The digest omits that additive status could not be determined for 6.5% of products, which affects how the prevalence should be read.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add 'with additive status undetermined for another 6.5% of products.'"

### Study 7: In nearly 9,000 older adults with chronic illness, better diet, sleep and neighborhood ties tracked with far less depression and anxiety
**PMID:** 42456822 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unquantified magnitude** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "tracked with far less depression and anxiety"
- **Abstract says:** "both the subgroup with a higher number of healthy lifestyle behaviors and the subgroup with a higher number of favorable SDH factors were significantly associated with lower odds of depressive symptoms, anxiety symptoms, and their comorbidity"
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no odds ratios or percentages, so 'far less' (and 'markedly lower rates' in the story angle) implies a size the abstract does not report.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'significantly lower odds of depression and anxiety symptoms' in the headline and angle."

**Issue B. Dose-response described as stacking** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The benefits appeared to stack — more healthy behaviors, lower symptom burden."
- **Abstract says:** "Compared with the reference group (0-1 healthy lifestyle behaviors and 0-1 SDH), both the subgroup with a higher number of healthy lifestyle behaviors and the subgroup with a higher number of favorable SDH factors were significantly associated with lower odds..."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a comparison of higher-count groups against a 0-1 reference group, not a tested graded dose-response, and this is cross-sectional so 'benefits' implies effect.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'participants with more healthy behaviours had lower symptom odds than those with one or none.'"

### Study 13: Cutting Protein for 'Longevity' Didn't Switch On Cellular Cleanup, Randomized Trial Finds

### Study 16: Hearing Loss Plus Multiple Health Conditions More Than Doubled Death Risk in Adults Over 70
**PMID:** 42728658 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Borderline significance of hearing-loss-alone estimate not noted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "was itself associated with higher mortality (adjusted hazard ratio 1.4)"
- **Abstract says:** "Risk of mortality increased independently with hearing loss (aHR = 1.4 95% CI = 1.0-2.0 ...)"
- **Problem:** The confidence interval's lower bound sits at 1.0, so the independent hearing-loss association is at the edge of statistical significance; stating it flatly overstates the certainty.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the interval, e.g. "adjusted hazard ratio 1.4, 95% CI 1.0–2.0 — a borderline result.""

### Study 17: Big U.S. Study Finds No Link Between Cadmium Exposure and Dementia Risk
**PMID:** 42725989 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Combined sample size may double-count** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "in urine and blood samples from more than 16,000 adults"
- **Abstract says:** "In NHANES III (N = 6122) ... In continuous NHANES (urinary cadmium N = 2833; blood cadmium N = 8038)"
- **Problem:** The figure comes from summing three subsamples; participants in continuous NHANES could have both urinary and blood measures, so the number of unique adults is not verifiable from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Report the subsamples separately (6,122 in NHANES III; 2,833 urinary and 8,038 blood in continuous NHANES) rather than a single total."

**Issue B. Reassurance stronger than authors' own reading** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this large study found no such signal — so you can keep eating a varied, plant-rich diet without that fear driving your food choices"
- **Abstract says:** "No association between cadmium exposure and dementia incidence was observed. Our null findings should be interpreted in the context of potential methodological issues and should be verified by subsequent studies."
- **Problem:** The authors explicitly hedge their null result, so presenting it to readers as settled reassurance goes slightly beyond the abstract, even though the Caveats note the hedge.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the authors' caution into the reader-facing angle, e.g. "no signal was detected, though the authors say the null result needs confirmation.""

### Study 18: In Adults Over 65, a Higher Body-and-Blood-Sugar Score Tracked With Lower Death Risk — Not Higher
**PMID:** 42753933 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Interpretation extends beyond the index studied** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It reinforces that thinness in later life is often more dangerous than modest excess weight"
- **Abstract says:** "The association between TyG-BMI and all-cause mortality differed between Chinese and US middle-aged and older adults. However, TyG-BMI provided little or no incremental prognostic discrimination beyond BMI alone."
- **Problem:** The study examined a triglyceride-glucose-BMI composite and its discrimination versus BMI; it did not test body weight categories, so a general claim about thinness versus excess weight is an inference.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "low values of this composite index were linked to higher mortality in older adults, a pattern consistent with concerns about low body weight in later life.""

### Study 19: Standardized Feeding Protocols Get ICU Patients Fed Faster — But Didn't Change Survival
**PMID:** 42674351 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Overgeneralization to feeding intensity** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "pushing for more aggressive feeding is unlikely to alter the outcomes that matter most"
- **Abstract says:** "Evidence-based feeding protocols were associated with improved nutritional delivery but little to no difference in key clinical outcomes in critically ill adults."
- **Problem:** The review compared protocol-driven care with usual care, not more versus less aggressive feeding, and confidence intervals (e.g. mortality RR 0.97, 95% CI 0.83–1.13) cannot rule out benefit or harm; the sentence could shape a real goals-of-care conversation on a claim the data do not support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "standardized feeding protocols delivered more calories and protein but have not been shown to change survival or ventilator time, so decisions can centre on comfort, function and the patient's wishes.""

### Study 22: Three in four hospitalized cancer patients were malnourished — and it cut survival sharply
**PMID:** 42668014 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal phrasing in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Three in four hospitalized cancer patients were malnourished — and it cut survival sharply"
- **Abstract says:** "GLIM-defined malnutrition was highly prevalent and independently associated with poorer survival in hospitalized patients with cancer."
- **Problem:** "It cut survival" asserts causation from an observational cohort; the digest's own caveat says causation cannot be established, so the headline works against the caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to associative language, e.g. "— and it tracked with sharply worse survival.""

**Issue B. Ambiguous attribution of HR 7.49** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "severe malnutrition to roughly triple the risk — with the effect strongest in the first 12 months (HR 7.49)"
- **Abstract says:** "The prognostic effect of severe malnutrition was strongest within the first 12 months after baseline assessment (HR 7.49, 95% CI 3.68-15.24)"
- **Problem:** As written the 12-month figure could be read as applying to malnutrition generally; it is specific to severe malnutrition.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "for severe malnutrition" before the HR 7.49 figure."

### Study 23: When a dietitian is involved, patients leaving intensive care actually get fed properly
**PMID:** 42660495 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for retrospective audit** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "When a dietitian is involved, patients leaving intensive care actually get fed properly"
- **Abstract says:** "Association between dietary counselling and nutrition provision during transition from ICU to post-ICU wards. Three-centre retrospective analysis... dietary counselling, throughout hospital care was associated with lower energy and protein goal deviation"
- **Problem:** The study reports an association from chart review with no control group; the headline implies the counselling produced the better feeding. The abstract also describes "dietary counselling" rather than dietitian involvement specifically (though the authors recommend integrating dietitians).
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to something like "Dietary counselling after the ICU tracked with much better calorie and protein delivery.""

### Study 24: Your genes may determine how much fish oil actually raises your omega-3 levels
**PMID:** 42721579 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Determine" overstates effect modification** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Your genes may determine how much fish oil actually raises your omega-3 levels"
- **Abstract says:** "PGS modify the associations of FOS with circulating omega-3 fatty acids in EUR and CSA populations, with larger FOS effects in participants with lower PGS."
- **Problem:** Polygenic score modified the size of the supplementation effect (0.40 SD vs 0.28 SD); fish oil still raised omega-3 in both extremes, so genes do not "determine" the response.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "influence how much" or "shape how much" instead of "determine.""

### Study 25: Exercise programs modestly lift caregivers' mood — but nobody is studying their diet
**PMID:** 42593223 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Number of trials pooled** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers pooled 21 randomized trials covering 2,099 family caregivers of older adults"
- **Abstract says:** "21 studies (N = 2,099) were identified, with 17 included in the meta-analysis."
- **Problem:** Twenty-one trials were included in the systematic review but only 17 contributed to the pooled estimate.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "reviewed 21 randomized trials (2,099 caregivers) and pooled results from 17 of them.""

**Issue B. Overall vs subgroup significance** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Pooled results showed a small but real reduction in depressive symptoms (SMD -0.34), significant only in hybrid programs that combined in-person sessions with technology-based monitoring"
- **Abstract says:** "Pooled estimates ... suggested a small reduction in depressive symptoms (SMD = -0.34, 95% CI -0.47 to -0.20). Subgroup analyses suggested that depressive symptom reductions reached statistical significance only in hybrid physical activity interventions..."
- **Problem:** The overall pooled effect was itself statistically significant; the "significant only in hybrid" finding comes from exploratory subgroup analysis. The sentence as written can be read as limiting the main result.
- **Suggested fix:** "Separate the clauses: the overall pooled effect was significant, and in exploratory subgroup analysis significance held only for hybrid programs."

### Study 26: Smoking's toll on memory shows up in two national cohorts — and inflammation is part of the path
**PMID:** 42709495 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mediation limited to U.S. cohort** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "White blood cell and neutrophil counts statistically explained about 7-10% of the link, suggesting inflammation is one route from smoke exposure to cognitive decline."
- **Abstract says:** "Mediation analysis revealed that neutrophil and leukocyte partially mediated 7.39% and 9.75% of the smoke-cognitive decline association... with neutrophil and leukocyte partly mediating this link in the U.S. cohort."
- **Problem:** The digest presents the mediation result as if it applied across both cohorts; the abstract confines it to NHANES (neutrophils were not measured in CHARLS).
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "in the U.S. cohort" when describing the mediation percentages."

**Issue B. "Memory" in headline vs cognitive function** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Smoking's toll on memory shows up in two national cohorts"
- **Abstract says:** "Cognitive function was assessed using standard cognitive tests in NHANES and CHARLS."
- **Problem:** Outcome was composite cognitive test performance, not memory specifically; "toll" also asserts causation for an analysis the digest itself describes as cross-sectional.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "cognitive test scores" and associative wording, e.g. "Smoke exposure tracks with lower cognitive scores in two national cohorts.""

**Issue C. Lab-test suggestion not supported by findings** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "consider asking about your white blood cell count at your next routine bloodwork as a general inflammation signal"
- **Abstract says:** "Mediation analysis revealed that neutrophil and leukocyte partially mediated 7.39% and 9.75% of the smoke-cognitive decline association."
- **Problem:** A small population-level mediation percentage does not support checking one's own WBC count as a personal inflammation or brain-health marker; this is a clinical suggestion beyond the data.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the WBC suggestion or reframe it as a mechanism researchers are exploring, not something readers should ask to have measured."

**Issue D. Design label differs from abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Cross-sectional and observational — cannot establish causation"
- **Abstract says:** "Findings from two prospective cohort studies... analyzed data from 2503 participants in the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES, 2011-2014) and 3263 participants in the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS, 2011-2012)"
- **Problem:** The abstract titles the work as prospective cohort analyses, while the digest calls it cross-sectional. The digest's reading is defensible given the single-wave exposures and outcomes, but the discrepancy with the source label should be made explicit.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the authors describe the source studies as prospective cohorts but that exposures and cognition were assessed in the same wave, so the analysis is effectively cross-sectional."
