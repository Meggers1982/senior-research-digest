## Study 2: Hearing aids linked to better memory and lower dementia risk, meta-analysis finds
**PMID:** 41803081 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Slight misstatement of risk reduction metric** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "along with a 16% reduced risk of developing cognitive impairment"
- **Abstract says:** "along with a decreased risk of cognitive impairment (RR = 0.84, 95% CI: 0.80, 0.88)"
- **Problem:** The digest correctly calculated 16% from RR=0.84, but the abstract reports this as "relative risk" not "risk," a minor terminological distinction that doesn't materially affect accuracy.
- **Suggested fix:** No change needed; calculation is correct.

No other issues. Sample size (231,565), 46 studies, and key findings are accurate.

## Study 3: Eating more flavonoid-rich foods tied to lower risk of hearing loss
**PMID:** 42235109 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Food examples include item not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Those with the highest intake of flavonoid-rich foods (berries, citrus, tea, red wine, dark chocolate)"
- **Abstract says:** "FDS was calculated from flavonoid-rich food intake" — no specific foods listed
- **Problem:** The digest lists specific foods (berries, citrus, tea, red wine, dark chocolate) that are not mentioned in the abstract. While these are plausible flavonoid sources, they cannot be verified from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Those with the highest intake of flavonoid-rich foods had a 16% lower risk..." (remove specific food examples, or flag as inferred from general knowledge)

**Issue B. Follow-up duration slightly imprecise** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "an average of 11 years"
- **Abstract says:** "613,590 person-years of follow-up" for 55,859 participants (≈11 years average)
- **Problem:** The calculation is correct but the abstract doesn't state "11 years" explicitly.
- **Suggested fix:** No change strictly necessary; calculation is reasonable.

## Study 5: Depression risk rises with hearing loss across three countries
**PMID:** 42119796 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Effect size range slightly imprecise** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The risk was roughly 50–65% higher for those with poor hearing"
- **Abstract says:** OR values of 1.52, 1.64, and 1.55 for poor hearing across the three cohorts
- **Problem:** 1.52-1.64 represents 52-64% higher odds, not "50-65%." This is a minor rounding difference.
- **Suggested fix:** "The risk was roughly 52–64% higher" or keep as approximate.

No other issues. Study design, countries, and key findings are accurate.

## Study 12: Cognitive impairment common among audiology clinic patients—many unaware

## Study 15: Hearing loss in cognitively frail elders predicts faster progression to dementia

## Study 17: Hearing loss tied to more sitting, but not less physical activity
**PMID:** 42363407 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Odds ratio for sedentary behavior slightly different** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "nearly double the odds of prolonged sedentary behavior"
- **Abstract says:** "hearing loss was associated with high sedentary behavior (OR, 1.81; 95% CI, 1.16; 2.82)"
- **Problem:** OR 1.81 is 81% higher odds, not "nearly double" (which would be ~2.0). This is a minor overstatement.
- **Suggested fix:** "about 80% higher odds of prolonged sedentary behavior"

Sample size (N=3,512), age range (40+), and Chilean setting are accurate.
