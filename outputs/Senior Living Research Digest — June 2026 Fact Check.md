### Study 1: Stopping Antipsychotics Safely in People With Dementia: Tapering Reduces Falls and Confusion Risk

**PMID:** 42302797 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Incorrect denominator for sample description** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Among 24,822 patients who reached the 12-week mark (mean age 84, 67% women)"
- **Abstract says:** "The mean age was 83.57 (SD 7.07) in the 12-week trial" and "16 725 (67.4%) of 24 822 patients were female"
- **Problem:** Mean age rounded incorrectly (83.57 rounded to 84 is acceptable, but 83.6 would be more precise).
- **Suggested fix:** "Among 24,822 patients who reached the 12-week mark (mean age 84, 67% women)" — acceptable as written, rounding is reasonable.

**Issue B. Slight imprecision in effect sizes** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "both tapering and abrupt discontinuation reduced the risk of delirium by 2.5% and fractures by 2.8%"
- **Abstract says:** "ARDs of -2·46% (95% CI -4·10 to -1·27) and -2·80% (-4·14 to -1·29), respectively" for tapering vs continuation
- **Problem:** The digest states both tapering AND abrupt discontinuation had these exact figures, but the abstract only provides specific ARDs for the tapering strategy comparison. The abstract states "Similar results were found" for abrupt discontinuation but doesn't give identical numbers. The 2.5% should be 2.46% (minor rounding issue).
- **Suggested fix:** "tapering reduced the risk of delirium by about 2.5% and fractures by 2.8% over two years compared to continuing the medication, with similar results for abrupt discontinuation."

**Issue C. Missing distinction between strategies** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "stopping antipsychotics did not increase risks of death, stroke, or pneumonia"
- **Abstract says:** "Estimated risks for stroke, pneumonia, and all-cause mortality were similar between strategies"
- **Problem:** The digest accurately captures this finding.
- No fix needed.

### Study 4: Machine Learning Model Predicts Kidney Injury Risk in Older Adults After Heart Surgery

**PMID:** 42306297 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Sample size description imprecision** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "data from 733 older adults who underwent cardiac surgery across three Chinese hospitals"
- **Abstract says:** "data from two centers (n=623) were utilized for model training and internal validation, whereas data from a third, distinct center (n=110) were reserved for external validation"
- **Problem:** Total is 733 (623+110), which is correct, but the abstract specifies two centers for development and a third for external validation, which is a meaningful distinction.
- **Suggested fix:** "data from 733 older adults who underwent cardiac surgery (623 from two centers for development, 110 from a third center for validation)"

**Issue B. Accuracy metric slightly imprecise** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The best-performing model identified high-risk patients with 78% accuracy"
- **Abstract says:** "LightGBM exhibited the greatest AUC (0.784, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.702-0.859)"
- **Problem:** AUC of 0.784 is not the same as "78% accuracy." AUC measures discrimination ability, not accuracy. This conflates two different metrics.
- **Suggested fix:** "The best-performing model achieved an AUC of 0.78, indicating good discrimination for identifying high-risk patients"

### Study 5: Walking Ability Emerges as Central to Healthy Aging in Adults Over 80

**PMID:** 42303956 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mortality risk reduction figure needs clarification** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Each 10-point improvement in overall functional capacity was associated with 32-33% lower mortality risk over 4-7 years"
- **Abstract says:** "Each 10-point increase in IC was associated with lower mortality at 4 years (HR 0.67, 95% CI 0.58-0.77) and 7 years (HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.61-0.77)"
- **Problem:** HR of 0.67 = 33% lower risk; HR of 0.68 = 32% lower risk. The digest correctly represents this as 32-33%.
- No fix needed.

No other issues found. Study details (N=319, mean age 85.4, 67% women, Italian cohort, network analysis finding locomotion as central hub) are accurate.

### Study 11: Food Insecurity in Ghana and South Africa Linked to Disability in Older Adults—Childhood Poverty Plays a Role

**PMID:** 42296619 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Sample size description** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Tracking over 11,000 adults aged 60+"
- **Abstract says:** "Data were from 6,569 and 4,663 older adults (60+years) who participated in WHO-SAGE Ghana and SA (Waves 1-3)"
- **Problem:** 6,569 + 4,663 = 11,232, so "over 11,000" is accurate.
- No fix needed.

**Issue B. Effect size for Ghana** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "food insecurity nearly doubled the odds of functional disability in South Africa and increased odds by 20% in Ghana"
- **Abstract says:** "Ghana: aOR = 1.20, 95%CI: 1.03, 1.41... SA: aOR = 1.98, 95%CI:1.54, 2.56"
- **Problem:** aOR of 1.20 = 20% increased odds (accurate); aOR of 1.98 ≈ "nearly doubled" (accurate).
- No fix needed.

No factual errors found. The study details and mediation findings are accurately represented.

- **As written:** "Over three years"
- **Abstract says:** "3 time points across a 3-year period (October 2021-September 2024)"
- **Problem:** Accurate.
- No fix needed.

**Issue B. Interview count vs clinician count** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "researchers interviewed 25 clinicians from all eight VA tele-geriatric mental health teams at multiple time points"
- **Abstract says:** "Semistructured interviews were conducted with clinicians from all 8 tele-GMH teams (n=25) at 3 time points... Interview (n=46) data"
- **Problem:** The digest says "interviewed 25 clinicians" but the abstract clarifies there were 46 total interviews (25 clinicians interviewed at 3 time points, though not all completed all interviews). This is a minor omission but doesn't misrepresent the study.
