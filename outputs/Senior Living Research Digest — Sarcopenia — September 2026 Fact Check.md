# Fact-Check Report

## Study 1: Frailty—Not Sarcopenia Alone—Drives Disability Risk in Older Adults
**PMID:** 42600484 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. Framing is consistent with the abstract. The digest correctly reports the sample size (2,067), population (Korean community-dwelling adults aged 70+), six-year follow-up, sarcopenia and frailty definitions, and the key finding that frailty alone was associated with all four disability outcomes while sarcopenia alone showed no significant independent association.

## Study 2: Persistent Low Grip Strength Linked to Higher Mortality and Disability
**PMID:** 42570738 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mortality risk percentage slightly rounded** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "those with persistently low grip strength had a 75 percent higher mortality risk"
- **Abstract says:** "HR 1.75, 95% CI 1.16-2.62"
- **Problem:** A hazard ratio of 1.75 represents a 75% higher risk, which is correctly stated. No error here.

**Issue B. Composite outcome percentage** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "76 percent higher risk of the combined outcome of death or severe disability"
- **Abstract says:** "HR 1.76, 95% CI 1.22-2.53"
- **Problem:** The digest correctly interprets HR 1.76 as 76% higher risk. No error.

No factual errors found upon closer review. Framing is consistent with the abstract.

## Study 3: Light Activity May Prevent Sarcopenia in Frailer Older Adults
**PMID:** 42537596 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Average age description** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "10,871 Canadian adults (average age 61)"
- **Abstract says:** "age: 61.3 ± 9.3 years"
- **Problem:** Rounding 61.3 to 61 is acceptable. However, describing a cohort with mean age 61 as studying "older adults" in a digest for seniors may slightly overstate how elderly this cohort is.
- **Suggested fix:** "10,871 Canadian adults (average age 61, ranging widely)"

**Issue B. Physical activity terminology** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "more light-to-moderate physical activity was associated with lower sarcopenia incidence"
- **Abstract says:** "more LMPA was associated with lower sarcopenia incidence"
- **Problem:** LMPA likely stands for "light-to-moderate physical activity," so this interpretation appears reasonable, though the abstract doesn't define the acronym explicitly.

No significant errors found.

## Study 4: Osteosarcopenia Tied to Cognitive Decline in Singapore Study

- **As written:** "was present in about half the cohort"
- **Abstract says:** "Possible osteosarcopenia, observed among 50.3% of the cohort"
- **Problem:** "About half" accurately represents 50.3%. No error.

**Issue B. Study design characterization** — Severity: Minor

- **Abstract says:** "recruited through a national household survey"

No significant errors found.

## Study 9: Simple Nomogram Predicts Sarcopenia Risk in Non-Obese Older Adults
**PMID:** 42616688 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Variable list slightly incomplete** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Seven readily available variables—age, sex, BMI, hemoglobin, difficulty rising from a chair, fall history, and basic-living-ability score"
- **Abstract says:** "Seven variables were included in the final nomogram: age, sex, BMI, hemoglobin (Hb), difficulty rising from a chair, history of falls, and basic living ability."
- **Problem:** The lists match. No error.

**Issue B. AUC range** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "achieved strong discrimination (AUC 0.88–0.94)"
- **Abstract says:** "AUC of 0.88 (95% CI: 0.86-0.90) in the training set... internal (AUC 0.89, 95% CI: 0.85-0.92) and external (AUC 0.94, 95% CI: 0.91-0.97)"
- **Problem:** The range 0.88–0.94 accurately spans training to external validation. No error.

No significant errors found.

## Study 14: GLP-1 Medications Cause Muscle Loss—But Muscle Quality May Improve
**PMID:** 42633386 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline uses causal language for observational findings** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "GLP-1 Medications Cause Muscle Loss"
- **Abstract says:** "Most studies found that GLP-1-RA therapy was associated with a loss of skeletal muscle mass"
- **Problem:** The headline uses "cause" for what the abstract describes as an association. This is inappropriate causal language for observational/review data.
- **Suggested fix:** "GLP-1 Medications Linked to Muscle Loss—But Muscle Quality May Improve"

No other significant errors found. The percentages (8.5–24.5% of weight lost from muscle) and findings about muscle quality improvement and maintained strength are accurately reported.
