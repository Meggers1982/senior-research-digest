### Study 1: Poor Sleep Linked to Higher Risk of Developing Urinary Incontinence After 50
**PMID:** 42748569 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Incidence range rounded** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Between 20% and 29% developed urinary incontinence during follow-up."
- **Abstract says:** "Weighted UI incidence was 28.6% in HRS, 23.1% in ELSA, and 19.7% in CHARLS."
- **Problem:** The lowest cohort incidence was 19.7%, slightly below the stated floor of 20%.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Roughly 20% to 29% developed urinary incontinence" or give the exact figures (19.7%–28.6%)."

**Issue B. Education framing conflicts with reported finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this study points to sleep and activity — two things that are at least partly modifiable — rather than income or education as the factors that travel with it"
- **Abstract says:** "The composite SDOH score was not consistently associated with incident UI (HR = 0.97...). Sleep problems (HR = 1.43), higher body mass index (HR = 1.02), and postsecondary education (HR = 1.37, 95% CI: 1.17-1.61) were associated with higher UI risk"
- **Problem:** Education was in fact significantly associated with higher risk; only the composite social-determinants score was null. The body text's framing is contradicted by the digest's own caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "rather than a composite score of social determinants" and note that postsecondary education was unexpectedly associated with higher risk."

### Study 4: During the Morphine Shortage, a Substitute Drug Brought Faster Sleep at the End of Life
**PMID:** 42927983 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal verb in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "During the Morphine Shortage, a Substitute Drug Brought Faster Sleep at the End of Life"
- **Abstract says:** "Hydromorphone was associated with shorter time to sleep than morphine, with no significant difference in time to death."
- **Problem:** "Brought" asserts causation for a quasi-experimental pre-post comparison that the authors describe only in associational terms.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "was linked to faster sleep" or "tracked with faster sleep.""

**Issue B. Unsupported claim about family distress** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the interval between ingestion and loss of consciousness is often the most distressing part of the process"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract reports only time to sleep and time to death outcomes; it makes no claim about patient or family distress."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only — no distress or experience measure was reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute this to background clinical experience or drop it, e.g. "the wait for the patient to fall asleep is closely watched by families.""

### Study 6: Uneven grip strength between hands flags higher risk of stacking up heart and metabolic disease
**PMID:** 42753932 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Comparison group imprecision** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Those whose grip strength differed by 10% or more between hands had a 23% higher risk of developing two or more of diabetes, heart disease, or stroke"
- **Abstract says:** "Participants were categorized as symmetric with normal strength (G1), asymmetric only (G2), weak only (G3), or both asymmetric and weak (G4)... G2 was associated with a 23% higher risk of incident CMM compared with G1"
- **Problem:** The 23% figure applies specifically to people who were asymmetric but had normal strength, compared with people who were symmetric and strong — not to everyone with ≥10% asymmetry.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the qualifier: "Those who were asymmetric but had normal grip strength had a 23% higher risk than people who were symmetric and strong.""

### Study 7: App-based and online menopause programs modestly improve sleep and hot flashes, review finds
**PMID:** 42822207 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Cost claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this suggests a free or low-cost digital program is a reasonable first try for hot flashes and poor sleep"
- **Abstract says:** "Digital health interventions offer an accessible and scalable approach to symptom management, but their effectiveness remains uncertain."
- **Problem:** The abstract says nothing about the cost or price of the included interventions; "free or low-cost" is an assumption.

### Study 8: Sleep Problems Flag Higher Suicide Risk in People With Dementia — Even Without Depression
**PMID:** 42790647 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline could be misread as a subgroup finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Sleep Problems Flag Higher Suicide Risk in People With Dementia — Even Without Depression"
- **Abstract says:** "The natural indirect effect through depression was not statistically significant... This association persisted after accounting for depression, suggesting involvement of other mechanisms."
- **Problem:** The study tested whether depression mediated the association, not whether the risk held among people who had no depression; the headline invites the latter reading.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "— and Depression Doesn't Explain It" or "— Independent of Depression.""

**Issue B. "Single strongest" not verifiable** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "repeated emergency department visits were the single strongest risk marker in this study"
- **Abstract says:** "Male sex, low urbanization, cardiometabolic comorbidities, and frequent emergency department visits were additional predictors (≥3 visits/year: aHR, 2.54; 95% CI, 2.20-2.94)."
- **Problem:** Effect sizes for the other predictors are not given in the abstract, so a ranking cannot be confirmed from it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "the largest risk estimate reported in the abstract" or "among the strongest signals.""

### Study 13: Too Little or Too Much: Sleep Length Tracks With Death Risk in 16,000 Older Adults
**PMID:** 42648048 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Population described as older adults in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Sleep Length Tracks With Death Risk in 16,000 Older Adults"
- **Abstract says:** "A total of 16,375 participants were included in the study... among middle-aged and older adults"
- **Problem:** The cohort was middle-aged and older adults (CHARLS, from age 45), not exclusively older adults; the body text gets this right but the headline does not.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "...in 16,000 Middle-Aged and Older Adults"."

**Issue B. Short-sleep risk estimate conflates two measures** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "sleeping under 4 hours to about a 2.5-fold higher risk"
- **Abstract says:** "a total daily sleep duration of... <4 h (HR = 2.537, 95%CI: 1.495-4.308)... as well as a nighttime sleep duration of... <4 h (HR = 1.637, 95% CI: 1.007-2.662)"
- **Problem:** The ~2.5-fold figure applies to total daily sleep; the corresponding nighttime-sleep estimate was 1.64, so the single number implies more consistency than the data show.
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify "total daily sleep under 4 hours (about 2.5-fold); the equivalent figure for nighttime sleep alone was about 1.6-fold.""

### Study 14: Where It Hurts Predicts Where Mood Goes: Pain Sites Linked to Worsening Depression in Older Adults
**PMID:** 42749164 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal drift and unsupported 'untreated' qualifier** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It suggests that untreated everyday pain, particularly in the upper body and head, may help drive depression that deepens rather than resolves"
- **Abstract says:** "Site-specific pain was associated with unfavorable depressive symptom trajectories, particularly the High-Persistent trajectory."
- **Problem:** The study measured reported pain by site, not whether pain was treated or untreated, and the design supports association rather than driving/causation.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "pain at particular sites travelled with depression that deepened rather than resolved" and drop "untreated.""

### Study 17: What Older Adults Actually Want When They Can't Sleep — and Why Pills Often Win by Default
**PMID:** 42746961 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Barriers characterized as practical rather than attitudinal** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "For carers and senior living staff: the barrier is often practical, not attitudinal."
- **Abstract says:** "Decisions about insomnia treatments are influenced not only by expected and/or experienced treatment effectiveness, but also by perceived risks and health beliefs."
- **Problem:** The abstract lists "barriers to treatment" as one of five themes alongside beliefs about sleep and treatment expectations; it does not rank practical barriers above attitudes, and it explicitly emphasizes beliefs and perceived risk.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "practical access barriers were one of several influences participants described, alongside beliefs and past experience.""

**Issue B. Inference that removing barriers increases uptake** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this study shows older people are already worried about those harms and may accept alternatives if barriers to access are removed"
- **Abstract says:** "Integrating these perspectives into clinical decision-making may help reduce medication-related harm and promote safer, acceptable alternatives."
- **Problem:** A qualitative preference study cannot show that removing access barriers would change uptake; this extrapolates beyond the reported themes.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "participants voiced concern about these harms and described access difficulties as one obstacle to non-drug options.""

**Issue C. Theme relabelled more specifically than the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "practical barriers to accessing non-drug options"
- **Abstract says:** "barriers to treatment"
- **Problem:** The abstract names the theme generically as barriers to treatment, not specifically practical barriers to non-drug care.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use the authors' wording: "barriers to treatment.""

### Study 19: Sleeping Pills After a Broken Hip Have Dropped Sharply — Opioids Haven't
**PMID:** 42852585 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline overstates opioid stability** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Sleeping Pills After a Broken Hip Have Dropped Sharply — Opioids Haven't"
- **Abstract says:** "opioids dispensing peaked in 2015 at 55.8% and decreased to 44.6% in 2024... opioids dispensing remained consistently high"
- **Problem:** Opioid dispensing did fall from its 2015 peak; it simply remained high. The body text reports this correctly, but the headline implies no decline at all.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "...— Opioids Have Barely Budged" or "— Opioid Use Stays High.""

**Issue B. Survival restriction described as post-discharge** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "cohort restricted to those surviving at least 275 days post-discharge"
- **Abstract says:** "admitted for an acute hip fracture... and who survived for at least 275 days in the following year"
- **Problem:** The 275-day survival window runs from admission in the following year, not specifically from discharge.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "restricted to those surviving at least 275 days in the year following admission.""

### Study 20: Deep Sleep Tracked With Healthier Brain Blood Flow in People With Memory Problems
**PMID:** 42742525 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. N3–coupling association presented as general** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The percentage of the night spent in N3 deep sleep was positively associated with better coupling, and those regional measures in turn correlated with cognitive test scores."
- **Abstract says:** "N3 sleep percentage was positively associated with global CBF-ReHo coupling in mild AD and with regional CBF/ReHo ratios in group-specific ventral temporal clusters; these regional ratios were positively associated with Montreal Cognitive Assessment in MCI and Mini-Mental State Examination in mild AD."
- **Problem:** The global N3-coupling association was found in the mild AD group specifically, and the cognitive correlations differed by group and test; the digest implies a single association across the whole sample.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that the global association was seen in the mild Alzheimer's group, with regional measures correlating with MoCA in MCI and MMSE in mild AD."

### Study 21: A Multi-System 'Vulnerability Score' Before Cancer Surgery Predicted Delirium and Survival in Older Patients
**PMID:** 42600483 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Modifiable domains" framing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and that modifiable domains including sleep, nutrition, and physical function all feed into the risk picture."
- **Abstract says:** "PABI should currently be considered an investigational risk phenotype rather than a validated clinical prediction tool. External multicenter validation and future intervention studies are required to determine whether simplified assessments of physiological vulnerability can improve perioperative risk stratification and patient outcomes."
- **Problem:** The study measured associations only; it did not test whether changing sleep, nutrition or physical function alters outcomes, so describing them as "modifiable" domains that "feed into the risk picture" nudges readers toward an intervention claim the data do not support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to: "and that domains such as sleep, nutrition, and physical function are part of the risk picture — though the study did not test whether improving them changes outcomes.""

**Issue B. Implies individual recovery-pattern prediction** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Knowing which recovery pattern you're likely to follow can shape realistic planning for help at home."
- **Abstract says:** "Longitudinal analyses identified three distinct recovery patterns: rapid recovery, intermediate recovery, and sustained high physiological burden... PABI should currently be considered an investigational risk phenotype rather than a validated clinical prediction tool."
- **Problem:** The trajectories were derived retrospectively from longitudinal modeling; the abstract gives no evidence that a patient's recovery pattern can be identified in advance, and the authors call the index investigational.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to: "Recovery after major surgery varies widely between older adults, which is worth factoring into planning for help at home — though no validated tool yet predicts an individual's recovery path.""

**Issue C. Single-center claim not stated in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Single-center cohort in China with internal (bootstrap) validation only"
- **Abstract says:** "In this prospective cohort study, 450 adults aged ≥60 years undergoing curative gastrointestinal cancer surgery were enrolled... internal validation was performed using bootstrap resampling... External multicenter validation... are required."
- **Problem:** The abstract never states the study was single-center (it is inferable from the author affiliations and the call for multicenter validation, but not asserted).
- **Suggested fix:** "Unverifiable from abstract only — soften to "apparently a single-institution cohort in China, with internal (bootstrap) validation only.""
