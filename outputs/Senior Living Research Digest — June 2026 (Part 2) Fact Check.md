### Study 1: Stopping Antipsychotics in People With Dementia Reduces Risk of Delirium and Fractures Without Raising Stroke or Death Risk
**PMID:** 42302797 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Slightly imprecise effect size reporting** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Discontinuing antipsychotics—whether gradually or abruptly—reduced the 24-month risk of delirium by 2.5% and fractures by 2.8%"
- **Abstract says:** "estimated risks of delirium and fracture under the tapering strategy corresponded to 24-month ARDs of -2·46% (95% CI -4·10 to -1·27) and -2·80% (-4·14 to -1·29), respectively"
- **Problem:** The digest rounds 2.46% to 2.5%, which is acceptable but slightly imprecise. More importantly, the specific figures cited are for the tapering strategy after 12 weeks. The abstract notes "Similar results were found after 24 weeks of treatment" but doesn't provide identical numbers for abrupt discontinuation—the digest implies both methods produced identical results.
- **Suggested fix:** "Discontinuing antipsychotics through tapering reduced the 24-month risk of delirium by approximately 2.5% and fractures by 2.8%, with similar results for abrupt discontinuation."

### Study 4: Machine Learning Model Predicts Frailty Trajectories After Hip Surgery in Older Adults
**PMID:** 42302293 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Trajectory percentages slightly imprecise** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "26% maintained low frailty, 39% started frail but improved, and 35% experienced worsening frailty"
- **Abstract says:** "low-fluctuation frailty (55/209, 26%), high-improvement frailty (81/209, 39%), and high-deterioration frailty (73/209, 35%)"
- **Problem:** The digest describes "low-fluctuation frailty" as "maintained low frailty," which is a reasonable interpretation. However, the abstract calls it "low-fluctuation" which could mean starting low and staying low OR simply having minimal change—the term is ambiguous. The characterization "started frail but improved" for "high-improvement frailty" is an assumption not explicitly stated in the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Three distinct patterns emerged: 26% showed low-fluctuation frailty, 39% showed high-improvement frailty, and 35% experienced worsening (high-deterioration) frailty."

### Study 5: Mobility Function Emerges as Central to "Intrinsic Capacity" and Predicts Survival in Adults Over 80
**PMID:** 42303956 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mortality risk reduction figure needs context** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Each 10-point increase in overall intrinsic capacity was associated with 32-33% lower mortality risk over 4-7 years"
- **Abstract says:** "Each 10-point increase in IC was associated with lower mortality at 4 years (HR 0.67, 95% CI 0.58-0.77) and 7 years (HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.61-0.77)"
- **Problem:** HR of 0.67 corresponds to 33% lower hazard, and HR of 0.68 corresponds to 32% lower hazard. The digest's "32-33%" is accurate but reverses the order (it was 33% at 4 years, 32% at 7 years). This is very minor.
- **Suggested fix:** No change required—the range is accurate.

**Issue B. Sample description** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "319 community-dwelling adults aged 80+"
- **Abstract says:** "319 community-dwelling adults aged ≥ 80 years (mean age 85.4 ± 4.8 years; 67.1% women)"
- **Problem:** Digest omits that 67.1% were women, which is notable demographic information.
- **Suggested fix:** "319 community-dwelling adults aged 80+ (67% women)"

### Study 13: Food Insecurity Linked to Disability in Older Africans—Childhood Circumstances Play a Role
**PMID:** 42296619 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Effect size range interpretation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Food insecurity—not having reliable access to sufficient food—was associated with 20-98% higher odds of developing functional disability"
- **Abstract says:** "older adults who reported being food insecure (Ghana: aOR = 1.20, 95%CI: 1.03, 1.41, p = 0.024; SA: aOR = 1.98, 95%CI:1.54, 2.56, p < 0.001)"
- **Problem:** The digest correctly calculates the range (20% for Ghana, 98% for South Africa), but presenting it as "20-98%" without noting this reflects different countries may be confusing. The range is accurate but could be clearer.
- **Suggested fix:** "Food insecurity was associated with 20% higher odds of functional disability in Ghana and 98% higher odds in South Africa"
