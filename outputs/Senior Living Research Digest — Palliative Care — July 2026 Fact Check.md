## Study 5: Older Adults With Dementia in Nursing Homes Less Likely to Complete Advance Care Planning Despite Higher Needs
**PMID:** 41967253 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Headline misattribution** — Severity: Major

- **As written:** "Older Adults With Dementia in Nursing Homes Less Likely to Complete Advance Care Planning Despite Higher Needs"
- **Abstract says:** The study compares residents **with and without cancer**, not with and without dementia. Dementia was actually more common in the **non-cancer** group.
- **Problem:** The headline and framing fundamentally mischaracterize the study. The study found that **cancer residents** (not dementia residents) less often completed ACP.
- **Suggested fix:** "Nursing Home Residents With Cancer Less Likely to Complete Advance Care Planning Despite Shorter Survival"

**Issue B. Body text misattribution** — Severity: Major

- **As written:** "Cancer residents survived a median of 227 days versus 661 days for those without cancer, yet both groups received ACP consultations at similar rates (~40%). However, cancer residents less often completed the ACP process or documented care preferences before death."
- **Abstract says:** This is accurate, but it contradicts the headline which focuses on dementia.
- **Problem:** The body text is correct, but the headline and story angle framing are wrong.
- **Suggested fix:** Revise headline and story angles to focus on cancer vs. non-cancer comparison as the study actually examined.

**Issue C. Story angle misframing** — Severity: Major

- **As written:** Story angles focus on "dementia" patients needing earlier ACP.
- **Abstract says:** The study compared cancer vs. non-cancer residents; dementia was a covariate, not the exposure of interest.
- **Problem:** Story angles provide guidance based on an incorrect interpretation of the study.
- **Suggested fix:** Revise to focus on cancer diagnosis as the relevant factor for timely ACP initiation.
