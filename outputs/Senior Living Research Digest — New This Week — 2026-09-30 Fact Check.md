### Study 1: Dementia raises the odds of dying from cancer — not just from other causes
**PMID:** 42810378 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for observational data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Dementia raises the odds of dying from cancer — not just from other causes"
- **Abstract says:** "We conducted a population-based matched cohort study... Cancer-specific and all-cause mortality rates were elevated among individuals with cancer and dementia."
- **Problem:** "Raises the odds" reads as causation from a registry-based matched cohort that can only show association; the digest's own caveat says dementia cannot be shown to cause worse outcomes.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "Dementia is linked to higher cancer death rates — not just deaths from other causes.""

### Study 2: Structured life review eases depression in older adults — and the benefit lasts months
**PMID:** 42808214 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Pooled effect may be pre-post, not versus control** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The pooled effect on depression was large (Cohen's d = 0.96), and at three months or longer follow-up the effect was as large or larger (d = 1.32)."
- **Abstract says:** "The meta-analysis showed a large and statistically significant effect of integrative reminiscence in reducing depression from post-baseline to baseline (Cohen's d = 0.9608; p < .0001)."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes the effect as measured "from post-baseline to baseline" — i.e., within-group change — but the digest presents it as the benefit of the intervention, which implies a between-group (versus control) comparison. Pre-post effect sizes are typically inflated.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a clause such as "measured as change from baseline to after the intervention" and note in caveats that this is not a head-to-head comparison against control conditions."

### Study 3: Dementia caregiver training programs cut burden — the pooled evidence from 12 trials
**PMID:** 42811273 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Moderator misidentified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the review cautions that apparent differences between program types rest on exploratory subgroup analyses"
- **Abstract says:** "Comparator type was associated with differences in effect estimates; however, this moderator finding should be interpreted cautiously because subgroup analyses were exploratory and some categories included few studies."
- **Problem:** The moderator that showed differences was comparator (control condition) type, not the type of caregiver program; the digest attributes the caution to the wrong variable.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "apparent differences depending on what the program was compared against rest on exploratory subgroup analyses.""

**Issue B. Null moderator read as equal benefit** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "measurably lightens the load, for caregivers of any age or gender"
- **Abstract says:** "Mean caregiver age and gender composition were not significant moderators."
- **Problem:** A non-significant moderator test in 12 trials does not establish that the programs work equally well across ages and genders; it means no difference was detected in an underpowered analysis.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "with no evidence that caregiver age or gender mix changed the size of the benefit, though the analysis was small.""

### Study 7: Gentle electrical brain stimulation gave dementia patients a short-term memory boost — but the gains didn't last
**PMID:** 42809063 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. GRADE rating not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "evidence quality formally rated by GRADE was limited"
- **Abstract says:** "GRADE was used to rate evidence quality."
- **Problem:** The abstract states that GRADE was applied but does not report the resulting certainty rating, so "limited" cannot be confirmed. Unverifiable from abstract only.

### Study 8: A nerve-stimulation device sped up memory recall — but only younger adults got more answers right
**PMID:** 42803727 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Caveat contradicts stated randomized design** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "an unbalanced, non-randomized-looking group split (22 vs. 9)"
- **Abstract says:** "The Effect of Cervical Transcutaneous Vagus Nerve Stimulation on Word Memory in Healthy Adults: A Randomized Single-Blind Study... Thirty-one participants were stratified into an experimental group (n = 22) receiving 30-min cervical tVNS and a control group (n = 9)."
- **Problem:** The paper describes itself as randomized (participants stratified into groups); implying the allocation was not randomized goes beyond and against the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "a randomized but unbalanced group split (22 vs. 9)" and note only that the imbalance limits power."

**Issue B. Reaction-time benefit overstated as universal** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Stimulation sped up reaction times across the board"
- **Abstract says:** "Cervical tVNS significantly improved memory performance, manifested in faster RT and higher accuracy compared to the control group, particularly for young adults... A single cervical tVNS session can modulate verbal memory, primarily speeding RT."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports faster RT overall and says benefits were "particularly for young adults"; it does not confirm RT gains were equivalent in every age group, so "across the board" is stronger than the data reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "Stimulation mainly sped up reaction times, while the accuracy gain appeared only in younger adults.""

### Study 9: Doctors trained in palliative care say writing about their own practice changed how they treat dying patients
**PMID:** 42810835 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Measurably" applied to qualitative self-report** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "this study suggests a low-cost training element — structured reflection — measurably shifts how physicians communicate and collaborate"
- **Abstract says:** "A descriptive qualitative study was conducted, analysing data from 28 experienced physicians across various clinical specialties using a reflexive thematic analysis... participants described a shift from initial resistance towards sustained reflective engagement."
- **Problem:** Nothing was measured: the findings are themes drawn from physicians' own mandated written accounts, with no quantitative outcome and no patient-level data. The body text contradicts the digest's caveat that there was "no measurement of patient outcomes." "Low-cost" is also not addressed in the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "physicians in this programme described structured reflection as changing how they communicate and collaborate" and drop "measurably" and "low-cost.""

### Study 10: Exercise sends cartilage-repairing cargo through the bloodstream, study finds
**PMID:** 42811122 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. EV source and human sample size not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "older adults who completed a three-month aerobic exercise program had higher levels of a molecule called microRNA-29 inside the tiny extracellular vesicles that circulate in their blood"
- **Abstract says:** "a 3-month aerobic exercise intervention in older adults increased microRNA-29 levels in EVs relative to baseline EVs"

- **Suggested fix:** "Say "in circulating extracellular vesicles (sample source and participant number not given in the abstract)" or drop the "small" descriptor."

### Study 12: Air pollution raises stroke risk most in people with inflammation or genetic susceptibility
**PMID:** 42809522 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for observational data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Air pollution raises stroke risk most in people with inflammation or genetic susceptibility"
- **Abstract says:** "Ambient air pollution was associated with prevalent stroke in the cross-sectional CHARLS analysis and with incident ischemic stroke in the prospective UK Biobank analysis."
- **Problem:** "Raises" asserts causation; one cohort was cross-sectional (prevalent, self-reported stroke) and neither design can establish cause.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Air pollution is linked to higher stroke risk — most strongly in people with inflammation or genetic susceptibility""

**Issue B. Polygenic risk recast as family history** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It identifies who is most vulnerable to dirty air — people with chronic inflammation or a family history of stroke"
- **Abstract says:** "with additional analyses according to polygenic risk score categories… Participants with both high genetic susceptibility and high PM2.5 exposure had the highest hazard ratio"
- **Problem:** Susceptibility was defined by a polygenic risk score, not by reported family history; readers may self-assign risk on the wrong basis.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "people with chronic inflammation or a high genetic risk score for stroke (not the same as family history).""

**Issue C. Intervention advice not tested in the study** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "supports practical steps like indoor air filtration and avoiding outdoor activity on high-pollution days"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract reports only exposure-outcome associations and effect modification; no intervention (filtration, activity avoidance) was evaluated."
- **Problem:** Presents untested mitigation measures as supported by the findings.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note these are plausible but untested precautions that the study itself did not evaluate."

### Study 13: Age over 65 emerges as an independent risk factor in tiny brain aneurysms
**PMID:** 42809132 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Untreated (conservative) mortality not reported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "know that the death risk from leaving it alone in hospital was very low in this study while most deaths among unruptured cases traced back to the treatment itself"
- **Abstract says:** "Management was endovascular in 69%, microsurgical in 11%, and conservative in 20%… mortality in patients with unruptured aneurysms was low and was primarily associated with treatment-related factors."
- **Problem:** The 3% unruptured mortality covers mostly treated patients; the abstract gives no separate mortality figure for conservatively managed aneurysms, so the comparison implied here is unverifiable from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as "in-hospital death among unruptured cases was low overall (3%) and was mainly linked to treatment-related factors" without implying a measured watch-and-wait risk."

### Study 14: Sound waves through the skull: small first-of-its-kind trial tests ultrasound for early Alzheimer's
**PMID:** 42808496 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. MMSE described as a memory test** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "but memory (MMSE), quality of life and attention scores did not change"
- **Abstract says:** "Exploratory outcomes included… Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE)"
- **Problem:** The MMSE is a global cognitive screening measure, not a memory test; calling it "memory" misnames the outcome variable.
- **Suggested fix:** ""but overall cognitive screening scores (MMSE), quality of life and attention did not change""

**Issue B. "First-of-its-kind" overstatement** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "small first-of-its-kind trial tests ultrasound for early Alzheimer's"
- **Abstract says:** "clinical evidence on repeated stimulation in biomarker-confirmed early AD remains limited"
- **Problem:** The abstract claims the evidence base is limited, not that this is the first such study; the phrase edges into hype for a 20-person single-arm pilot.
- **Suggested fix:** ""small early pilot tests repeated ultrasound in biomarker-confirmed early Alzheimer's""

### Study 15: Not all senior dogs decline the same way — and that may teach us about human brains
**PMID:** 42808498 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Non-significant interaction omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "but individual trajectories varied widely — some dogs held steady or even improved while others dropped sharply"
- **Abstract says:** "Despite a non-significant population-level subject*age interaction (p = 0.14), individual random slopes (rate of change with age) ranged from -0.20 and 0.15, suggesting meaningful variability."
- **Problem:** The heterogeneity claim rests on the spread of individual random slopes; the formal population-level test of varying rates was not significant, which the digest does not mention.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that the formal test of differing decline rates across dogs was not statistically significant (p = 0.14), with variability inferred from individual slopes."

### Study 17: An MS-like disease that strikes after 50 is routinely mistaken for stroke — and the delay costs patients their mobility
**PMID:** 42810047 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal framing in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and the delay costs patients their mobility"
- **Abstract says:** "In LONMOSD… delayed diagnosis was associated with EDSS ≥6 at 90 days… LONMOSD was associated with delayed diagnosis and worse functional outcomes."
- **Problem:** Retrospective association is presented as established cause; the digest's own caveat acknowledges this, but the headline states it as fact.
- **Suggested fix:** ""…and the delay is linked to worse disability""

**Issue B. "Permanently" overstates follow-up** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "can cost months and leave patients permanently more disabled from a treatable inflammatory condition"
- **Abstract says:** "LONMOSD patients had higher EDSS at nadir, 90 days, and 180 days, and lower recovery index at 180 days."
- **Problem:** Disability was measured to 180 days; the abstract supports no claim about permanence.
- **Suggested fix:** ""…and leave patients more disabled at six months""

### Study 21: Brain bleeds caused decades ago by surgery keep recurring — first large study of 'iatrogenic' amyloid disease finds a 20% yearly risk
**PMID:** 42809801 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "First large study" claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "first large study of 'iatrogenic' amyloid disease finds a 20% yearly risk"
- **Abstract says:** "given its relatively recent recognition, data on long-term outcomes and prognosis are scarce ... After a systematic literature search for larger case series of iCAA (n ≥ 5)"
- **Problem:** The abstract states that long-term outcome data are scarce but never claims this is the first or largest such study; other case series of n≥5 were identified in the literature search. Priority claims cannot be verified from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "one of the largest studies to date" or "among the first substantial looks at long-term outcomes," mirroring the abstract's "data ... are scarce.""

**Issue B. Exposure route not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a condition believed to follow transmission of amyloid-beta protein during medical procedures such as childhood neurosurgery or dura mater grafts"
- **Abstract says:** "likely caused by transmission of amyloid-beta by medical procedures ... Patients with spinal surgery as an exposure (n = 24) had a lower rate of ICH compared with brain surgery or embolization"
- **Problem:** The abstract names brain surgery, embolization and spinal surgery as exposures; dura mater grafts are not mentioned. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change the examples to "such as childhood brain surgery, embolization or spinal surgery," which the abstract does specify."

**Issue C. Management advice beyond the data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "recurrence risk is high enough to shape discussions about anticoagulation and activity"
- **Abstract says:** "Reports recurrence rates and predictors (initial ICH presentation, brain vs spinal exposure); makes no statement about anticoagulation or activity restriction"
- **Problem:** The abstract offers no data on anticoagulation use or physical activity, so this reads as a clinical recommendation not grounded in the reported findings.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the reference to anticoagulation and activity, or note explicitly that the study did not examine either."
