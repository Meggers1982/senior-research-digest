### Study 1: A Routine Blood Test May Flag Which 100-Year-Olds Are Most at Risk
**PMID:** 42777518 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Incomplete SI formula** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "calculating a "sarcopenia index" (SI) from two routine blood values — serum creatinine divided by cystatin C"
- **Abstract says:** "SI was calculated as follows: serum creatinine (mg/dL)/cystatin C (mg/L) × 100."
- **Problem:** The digest omits the ×100 multiplier, which is why the reported cutoffs are in the 59–64 range; a reader trying to compute SI from their own lab values would get a number two orders of magnitude off the stated thresholds.
- **Suggested fix:** "Describe it as "serum creatinine divided by cystatin C, multiplied by 100.""

### Study 3: A Common Blood Pressure Pill Linked to Better Grip Strength and Walking Speed
**PMID:** 42872079 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline overstates how common the drug is** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A Common Blood Pressure Pill Linked to Better Grip Strength and Walking Speed"
- **Abstract says:** "Azilsartan (AZL), a highly selective angiotensin II type 1 receptor (AT1R) blocker"
- **Problem:** Azilsartan is one specific, comparatively less-prescribed ARB; calling it "a common blood pressure pill" may lead readers to assume the finding applies to blood pressure drugs or ARBs in general, which the study did not test.
- **Suggested fix:** "Name the drug in the headline, e.g. "A Specific Blood Pressure Drug (Azilsartan) Linked to Better Grip Strength and Walking Speed.""

### Study 5: Blood Sugar and Triglycerides — Not a Muscle Marker — Predicted Multiple Heart-Metabolic Diseases Over Nine Years
**PMID:** 42822209 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Comparison the study did not make** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Your routine triglyceride and fasting glucose numbers together say more about your odds of accumulating heart and metabolic conditions than they do separately"
- **Abstract says:** "The triglyceride-glucose index and composite index were independently associated with a higher risk of cardiometabolic multimorbidity (both P < 0.001) ... A composite index derived from a principal component analysis showed a very small incremental improvement over the TyG index alone (ΔAUC = 0.006)"
- **Problem:** The study compared the TyG index with the biochemical sarcopenia index and a composite, not with triglycerides and glucose considered individually, so the claim that the combination beats the separate values is not supported by the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as "The TyG index, calculated from your triglyceride and fasting glucose values, was the strongest of the markers tested for predicting cardiometabolic multimorbidity.""

### Study 9: One in Three Adults Loses Significant Muscle in the Days After Abdominal Surgery
**PMID:** 42946212 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Prevalence drawn from a subset of studies** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A systematic review and meta-analysis pooled 55 studies covering 7,196 adult patients ... Roughly 35% of patients met criteria for acute muscle wasting in the early postoperative period"
- **Abstract says:** "Fifty-five studies with 7,196 patients were included. A cut-off for muscle wasting was found in 13 observational studies, with a pooled proportion prevalence of acute muscle wasting of 0.35 (95% confidence interval 0.30-0.41, I2=89.2%)"
- **Problem:** The 35% prevalence came from only the 13 studies that reported a cut-off, not from all 55 studies or all 7,196 patients, and juxtaposing the figures suggests a larger evidence base than exists.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "in the 13 studies that defined a cut-off" when reporting the 35% figure."

**Issue B. Risk-of-bias statement slightly softened** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The authors stress that nearly all contributing studies carried serious risk of bias"
- **Abstract says:** "Studies included for meta-analysis of postoperative complications were all at serious or critical risk of bias, and the certainty of evidence was very low."
- **Problem:** The abstract says all studies in the complications meta-analysis were at serious or critical risk of bias; "nearly all" and "serious" understate both the proportion and the severity.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "all of the studies in the complications analysis carried serious or critical risk of bias.""

### Study 11: Weak Bones Plus Weak Muscles: The Double Diagnosis That Doubles Death Risk
**PMID:** 41818462 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported superlative and synergy claim** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "this is the strongest evidence yet that the two problems travel together and compound each other, making combined screening and combined treatment worth asking about"
- **Abstract says:** "Osteosarcopenia exhibited a stronger association with mortality than either osteoporosis or sarcopenia alone" and the condition's association with mortality "remains understudied"
- **Problem:** The abstract makes no claim that this is the strongest or best evidence to date, and reporting a larger hazard ratio for the combined condition does not demonstrate that the two conditions "compound" (interact synergistically) — no interaction or additivity test is reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like "adds evidence that the two problems often occur together and that the combination carries the highest mortality risk in these cohorts.""

**Issue B. Consistency across definitions not reported in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The pattern held across all five sarcopenia definitions and in both countries."
- **Abstract says:** "Five definitions for diagnosis of SP were considered... (FNIH1 and FNIH2), (EWGSOP2), (AWGS), and (IWG)" but hazard ratios are reported only for EWGSOP2 in both cohorts."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports results for only one definition; whether the pattern held across all five cannot be confirmed from the abstract alone. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the study tested five definitions and report that EWGSOP2 results are the ones given, or soften to "results were reported using EWGSOP2 criteria, with five definitions examined.""

### Study 12: A Normal BMI Can Hide Muscle Loss in Older Women — Nearly Half Had Sarcopenia
**PMID:** 42928822 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Overstated incremental predictive gain** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Combining BMI with measured fat percentage predicted sarcopenia far better than either alone."
- **Abstract says:** "good discrimination for body mass index (AUC 0.894) and modest discrimination for fat mass percentage (AUC 0.652); a combined model using body mass index and fat mass percentage improved prediction substantially (AUC 0.946)"
- **Problem:** The combined model is only modestly better than BMI alone (0.946 vs 0.894); "far better than either alone" is accurate for fat percentage but overstates the gain over BMI.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Combining BMI with measured fat percentage improved prediction (AUC 0.946) over BMI alone (0.894) and fat percentage alone (0.652).""

### Study 20: A Big Difference in Grip Strength Between Your Two Hands May Signal Heart and Metabolic Trouble Ahead
**PMID:** 42753932 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Imprecise description of the asymmetry-only group** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Participants with grip strength differing by 10% or more between hands had a 23% higher risk of developing two or more of diabetes, heart disease, or stroke"
- **Abstract says:** "Participants were categorized as symmetric with normal strength (G1), asymmetric only (G2), weak only (G3), or both asymmetric and weak (G4)... G2 was associated with a 23% higher risk of incident CMM compared with G1 (HR = 1.23, 95% CI: 1.06-1.42)"
- **Problem:** The 23% figure applies specifically to people who were asymmetric but had normal grip strength, compared with people who were symmetric and strong — not to everyone with a ≥10% between-hand difference (which would also include the weak-and-asymmetric group at 67%).
- **Suggested fix:** ""Participants whose grip differed by 10% or more between hands but whose strength was otherwise normal had a 23% higher risk, compared with people who were both symmetric and strong.""

### Study 21: Weak Breathing Muscles Before Heart or Lung Surgery Double the Odds of Postoperative Complications
**PMID:** 42906881 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Outcome named less specifically than in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Impaired respiratory muscle strength or airflow was associated with more than double the odds of complications (OR 2.25)"
- **Abstract says:** "Cutoff-based respiratory muscle strength or flow impairment was associated with increased odds of PPCs (OR 2.25, 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.50-3.37; I2 = 51.2%)"
- **Problem:** The pooled odds ratio applies specifically to postoperative pulmonary complications, not to complications in general; the headline ("Postoperative Complications") repeats the same omission, which could lead readers to think overall surgical complication risk doubled.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "more than double the odds of postoperative pulmonary complications (OR 2.25, 95% CI 1.50–3.37)" and adjust the headline to "...Double the Odds of Lung Complications After Surgery.""
