### Study 3: Electrical Nerve Stimulation Combined with Exercise Improves Cognition After Stroke
**PMID:** 42287562 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Blinding characterization** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "participants knew which treatment they received (not fully blinded)"
- **Abstract says:** The abstract does not explicitly state whether blinding was successful or failed; it only describes study design as an RCT with sham stimulation.
- **Problem:** The caveat implies known unblinding, but this is an inference not directly supported by the abstract text. However, given that tVNS/tSCS involve perceptible sensations, this is a reasonable assumption.
- **Suggested fix:** No revision required, but could soften to: "Single-blind design with sham control; blinding effectiveness not reported."

All other facts (N=90, mean age 65.12, 18 sessions over 6 weeks, three groups, MoCA and FMAUE outcomes, 1-month follow-up, single-center) are accurate.

### Study 5: Moderate-to-Vigorous Exercise May Protect Against Amyloid-Related Cognitive Decline
**PMID:** 42185590 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Follow-up period description** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "over nearly two years"
- **Abstract says:** "mean interval = 23.6 ± 0.9 months"
- **Problem:** 23.6 months is closer to 2 years than "nearly two years" suggests; this is accurate but could be more precise.
- **Suggested fix:** "over approximately two years (mean 23.6 months)"

**Issue B. Amyloid positivity threshold** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "elevated brain amyloid (a hallmark of Alzheimer's pathology)"
- **Abstract says:** "Aβ positivity was defined as a global standardized uptake value ratio > 1.10"
- **Problem:** The digest's characterization is accurate for lay audience; no correction needed.

All key facts (N=74, mean age 72.7, 24.3% Aβ positive, MVPA threshold 150+ min/week, no walking effect) are accurate. Self-reported physical activity caveat appropriately noted.

### Study 6: Hearing Aids May Help Delay Cognitive Decline—Here's Why It Matters
**PMID:** 42252380 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Prevalence statistic source** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Hearing impairment affects roughly one-third of adults over 65"
- **Abstract says:** "Cognitive impairment is one of the leading causes of disability worldwide and it affects about 6.5% of the population over the age of 65"
- **Problem:** The abstract states cognitive impairment affects 6.5% over 65, but does not provide a specific prevalence for hearing impairment. The "one-third" figure is not verifiable from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** Either remove the statistic or flag as "Unverifiable from abstract only."

All other content (narrative review characterization, mechanisms described, hearing aids recommendation) is consistent with the abstract. Review type caveat appropriately noted.

### Study 9: Frailty and Advanced Age Strongly Predict Both Cognitive Decline and Mortality
**PMID:** 42268499 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Frailty odds ratio precision** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Frailty tripled the odds of cognitive decline"
- **Abstract says:** "Frailty increased the odds of both cognitive deficit (OR = 3.1; 95% CI 1.86-5.18)"
- **Problem:** OR of 3.1 is more precisely "tripled" but the digest's phrasing is acceptable for lay audience.

**Issue B. Mortality odds ratio** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "quadrupled mortality risk"
- **Abstract says:** "mortality (OR = 4.2; 95% CI 2.66-6.69)"
- **Problem:** OR of 4.2 is slightly more than quadrupled; this is acceptable rounding for lay audience.

All other facts (N=1,413, Brazil, 9 years, 51% cognitive decline, age 80+ OR=5.2 for decline and OR=11.9 for mortality) are accurate. AI editing caveat appropriately noted per abstract disclosure.
