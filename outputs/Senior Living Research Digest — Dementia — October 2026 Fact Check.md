### Study 1: Blood Test for Alzheimer's Speeds Up Diagnosis Without Adding Distress, Trial Finds
**PMID:** 42832237 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Planned vs. actual management changes** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Early disclosure also led to more people starting Alzheimer's medication, fewer repeat neuropsychological tests, and more discharges back to primary care"
- **Abstract says:** "more frequent initiation of symptomatic AD treatment (16/112 [14.3%] vs 5/108 [4.6%]) ... fewer planned follow-up neuropsychological reassessments for diagnostic clarification ... and more planned discharge from the memory clinic to primary care"
- **Problem:** Two of the three secondary outcomes were clinician-documented *plans* at the study visit, not tests avoided or discharges that actually occurred; the digest (and the second story angle, 'earlier biomarker results reduced repeat testing and moved stable patients back to primary care sooner') reads as completed care events.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'fewer planned repeat neuropsychological assessments and more planned discharges back to primary care.'"

### Study 3: Home Health Aides Report Racism, Violence, and Burnout While Caring for People With Dementia
**PMID:** 42527830 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline narrows a general older-adult survey to dementia care** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Home Health Aides Report Racism, Violence, and Burnout While Caring for People With Dementia"
- **Abstract says:** "Workplace Challenges and Well-being Among Home Health Aides Caring for Older Adults in a Metropolitan Health System ... This study aimed to evaluate HHAs' workplace experiences and perceptions regarding caring for older adults, including data on dementia care needs."
- **Problem:** The survey covered care of older adults generally; dementia was one component (82.3% had ever cared for someone with dementia). The racism, violence and burnout findings were not measured specifically in dementia care encounters.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to '...While Caring for Older Adults, Most of Them With Dementia Experience' or similar, e.g. 'Home Health Aides Report Racism, Violence, and Burnout Caring for Older Adults — Most Have Cared for Someone With Dementia.'"

### Study 4: Palliative Care Program in Nursing Homes Nearly Eliminated End-of-Life Hospital Transfers
**PMID:** 42580497 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal/hype headline for an uncontrolled before-after quality project** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Palliative Care Program in Nursing Homes Nearly Eliminated End-of-Life Hospital Transfers"
- **Abstract says:** "An organization-wide palliative care pathway in LTCFs appears feasible and may support end-of-life care in facilities, although findings require cautious interpretation."
- **Problem:** 'Nearly eliminated' attributes the drop in hospitalizations to the program, which the authors explicitly decline to do; the digest's own caveat says the drop 'cannot be firmly attributed to the program,' so the headline contradicts the caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as association/observation, e.g. 'After a Nursing Home Palliative Care Pathway, Almost All Residents Died in the Facility Rather Than Hospital.'"

### Study 5: Long Waits and Poor Explanations Drive Bad ER Experiences for Older Patients
**PMID:** 42762629 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Overstated interpretation of association** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It identifies which older adults cycle repeatedly through emergency departments and shows that what most sours the experience is communication, not clinical acuity — something hospitals can change."
- **Abstract says:** "negative ED experience was associated with long waits (≥60 minutes; OR 1.70 ...), complications (OR 1.63 ...), and poor communication or involvement in decisions, with insufficient treatment information showing the strongest association (OR 5.17 ...)"
- **Problem:** 'Shows' plus the 'not clinical acuity' contrast goes beyond an association; complications — a clinical factor — were also independently associated with negative experience, and the headline verb 'Drive' is causal for a retrospective survey-linked analysis.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'was most strongly associated with' and note that complications were also linked to negative experiences; soften 'Drive' to 'Track With' in the headline."

### Study 13: Romance and Sexual Satisfaction Tracked With Slower Memory Decline in Early Dementia
**PMID:** 42648931 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported comparative claim in story angle** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "protecting closeness with a partner may be worth as much attention as puzzles and supplements"
- **Abstract says:** "Intimacy-related dimensions-particularly sexual satisfaction and romance-were associated with attenuated cognitive decline in early-stage NCD. These findings highlighted the potential role of relational and psychosocial factors in cognitive resilience and suggested relevant targets for non-pharmacological interventions."
- **Problem:** The study never compared intimacy with cognitive-training activities or supplements, so ranking it alongside them implies a head-to-head comparison that was not made.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like 'intimacy is worth raising as a health topic in its own right, alongside the other things people try for brain health.'"

### Study 15: Stronger legs, faster walk: physical fitness may buffer age-related slips in mental flexibility
**PMID:** 42833623 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Covariate described as 'income'** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "even after accounting for income, mood, body mass, smoking and cardiovascular conditions"
- **Abstract says:** "adjusting for sex, socioeconomic status, depressive symptoms, cardiometabolic conditions, BMI and smoking"

### Study 20: Who diagnoses your dementia may shape the care you get
**PMID:** 42832713 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Anti-amyloid prescribing framed as a ranking across all three groups** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "yet were the most likely group to prescribe anti-amyloid therapy"
- **Abstract says:** "Specialists were more likely to focus on screening and managing behavioral symptoms, whereas advanced practice providers were more likely to prescribe anti-amyloid therapy."
- **Problem:** The abstract's comparisons are made against primary care physicians as the reference group; it supports APPs being more likely than primary care physicians to prescribe anti-amyloid therapy, not that they outprescribe specialists as well.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'yet were more likely than primary care physicians to prescribe anti-amyloid therapy.'"

### Study 21: Dementia before 65: heavier caregiver burden, less help from long-term care
**PMID:** 42594757 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported explanation for the service gap** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Families facing dementia in midlife are carrying more strain with fewer supports, often because services are designed around frail, much older clients and don't fit a 58-year-old's needs or a working spouse's schedule."
- **Abstract says:** "This discrepancy suggests potential unmet needs and highlights the importance of flexible, age-appropriate LTC strategies for YOD populations."
- **Problem:** The abstract documents higher burden with lower service use and infers unmet need, but it does not identify why services went unused. Stating the reason as fact ("often because services are designed around...") goes beyond the data.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword as a possibility, e.g., "the authors suggest this gap may reflect unmet needs and services that are not designed for younger people with dementia.""

**Issue B. Predictive wording for an association** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "living apart from the family caregiver and more severe agitation predicted service use"
- **Abstract says:** "non-coresidence with family caregivers and greater agitation severity were associated with a higher likelihood of LTC service use"
- **Problem:** "Predicted" implies a forecasting/causal relationship in what is a cross-sectional registry association at first annual follow-up.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "were associated with a higher likelihood of using long-term care services.""

### Study 22: Memantine held most patients steady for six months — but caregiver strain still rose
**PMID:** 42838860 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline attributes stability to memantine** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Memantine held most patients steady for six months"
- **Abstract says:** "Multicenter, prospective, single-arm observational study ... At Week 24, 86.6% of 339 effectiveness-set patients remained within their baseline Clinical Dementia Rating category"
- **Problem:** With no control arm, the study cannot show that memantine "held" patients steady — only that most patients remained in the same CDR category while taking it. The Caveats acknowledge this, but the headline states it as drug effect.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Most patients on memantine stayed in the same dementia stage for six months — but caregiver strain still rose.""

### Study 24: When people in their 90s with dementia are hospitalized, delirium and malnutrition dominate
**PMID:** 42840854 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Drive most of these admissions" overstates frequency** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "urinary tract infections and pneumonia drive most of these admissions"
- **Abstract says:** "The most frequent diagnoses were urinary tract infection and pneumonia."
- **Problem:** "Most frequent" means the top two diagnoses, not that they account for a majority of admissions; the abstract gives no percentages for them.
- **Suggested fix:** ""urinary tract infections and pneumonia were the two most common reasons for admission""

**Issue B. Home care plan presented as the expected norm** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "expect a home care plan to be part of discharge"
- **Abstract says:** "45.51% continued management through a home care plan, and 34.83% completed the planned hospital care."
- **Problem:** Fewer than half of patients continued with a home care plan, so telling readers to "expect" it overstates how routine it was in this cohort.
- **Suggested fix:** ""ask whether a home care plan is appropriate — about 45% of patients in this study were discharged with one.""
