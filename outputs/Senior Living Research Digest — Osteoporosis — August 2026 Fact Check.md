## Study 6: Secondary Fracture Prevention Incentives Linked to Fewer Future Fractures—But Continuity Is Poor

**PMID:** 42579041 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Continuity statistic slightly imprecise** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "two-thirds had no follow-up SFP claims after discharge"
- **Abstract says:** "66.0% of recipients had no subsequent incentive claims"
- **Problem:** The digest says "two-thirds" which equals ~66.7%, while the abstract states exactly 66.0%. This is a minor rounding difference that does not materially change meaning.
- **Suggested fix:** "66% had no follow-up SFP claims after discharge" (or retain "two-thirds" as acceptable approximation)

All other facts (N=11,147, aged 60+, Japan, 15% lower fracture risk, 36.1% received acute-care incentive, 12-month follow-up) are accurate.

## Study 7: Diuretics, Polypharmacy, and Antipsychotics Tied to Injurious Falls in Older Adults

**PMID:** 42489786 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Hazard ratio for diuretics slightly rounded** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Diuretic use more than doubled fall risk (hazard ratio 2.98)"
- **Abstract says:** "diuretic use (aSHR = 2.98, 95% CI: 1.81-4.92)"
- **Problem:** A hazard ratio of 2.98 is nearly triple, not "more than doubled." The phrasing understates the magnitude.
- **Suggested fix:** "Diuretic use nearly tripled fall risk (hazard ratio 2.98)"

**Issue B. Polypharmacy HR description** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "polypharmacy (5+ medications) nearly tripled it (HR 2.62)"
- **Abstract says:** "Polypharmacy (aSHR = 2.62, 95% CI: 1.23-5.59)"
- **Problem:** HR 2.62 is more accurately described as "more than doubled" rather than "nearly tripled." The digest's description of diuretics and polypharmacy uses inconsistent language relative to the actual HRs.
- **Suggested fix:** "polypharmacy (5+ medications) more than doubled it (HR 2.62)"

All other facts (N=952, aged 65+, median 8 years follow-up, Australian cohort, PAF 23% for diuretics) are accurate.
