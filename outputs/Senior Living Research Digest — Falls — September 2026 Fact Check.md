**Primary audience:** older adults and seniors | **Secondary audience:** families, caregivers, and senior living professionals

### Study 1: Worried about falling? That fear predicts falls even when your walking test looks fine
**PMID:** 42708074 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Hazard ratio for concerns about falling misstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Both a simple stated concern about falling (56% higher hazard) and slow TUG performance (46% higher hazard) independently predicted a first fall"
- **Abstract says:** "Single-item concerns (adjusted HR 1.60, 95% CI 1.23-2.09) and slow TUG performance (HR 1.46, 95% CI 1.10-1.92) were independently associated with incident falls."
- **Problem:** HR 1.60 corresponds to a 60% higher hazard, not 56%. The TUG figure is correct.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change "56% higher hazard" to "60% higher hazard"."

**Issue B. Reference group for the combined-risk estimate** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "people who had both had nearly double the risk"
- **Abstract says:** "Using the slow-TUG/no-concern group as the direct reference, the fast-TUG/concern group did not differ significantly (HR 1.08, 95% CI 0.72-1.63), whereas the slow-TUG/concern group had a higher hazard (HR 1.96, 95% CI 1.29-2.97)."
- **Problem:** The HR of 1.96 compares the slow-TUG/concern group with people who walked slowly but had no concerns — not with people free of both risk factors, which is how readers will read "had both." The comparison group is materially different.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "people who were both slow on the TUG and concerned about falling had nearly twice the hazard of those who were slow but unconcerned (HR 1.96).""

### Study 2: Feeling worn out easily may signal a higher chance of falling
**PMID:** 42605068 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Attenuation of mental fatigability not mentioned** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "higher mental fatigability with 41% greater odds, after adjusting for demographics, clinical factors, and prior falls. Physical fatigability remained a significant predictor regardless of whether participants had MS."
- **Abstract says:** "Mental fatigability was attenuated after inclusion of group status."
- **Problem:** The digest reports the 41% figure without noting that the mental-fatigability association weakened and lost significance once MS status was accounted for, which is a relevant qualifier alongside the statement about physical fatigability.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a clause: "the mental fatigability association weakened once MS status was taken into account.""

**Issue B. Study location detail** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "single research group in one metropolitan area"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract lists author affiliations in Bronx, New York, but does not state where participants were recruited."
- **Problem:** The single-metropolitan-area characterization cannot be confirmed from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Flag as unverifiable from abstract only, or soften to "single research group.""

### Study 3: Ambulance calls in the year before a disabling fall may be missed warning signs
**PMID:** 42714914 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Omitted author disclosure** — Severity: Minor

- **Abstract says:** "Conflict of Interest Disclosures: Dr Shah reported contract work with Beckmen Coulter and Sysmex American Inc for device validation outside the submitted work. No other disclosures were reported."

- **Suggested fix:** "Add that one author reported contract work with Beckman Coulter and Sysmex America outside the submitted work."

**Issue B. Methods description not in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers linked EMS records with subsequent injury diagnoses to characterize what kinds of contacts with paramedics preceded these life-altering falls."
- **Abstract says:** "This cohort study characterizes emergency medical services (EMS) encounters in the year prior to diagnosis of a fall-related injury that limits activities of daily living among adults aged 60 years and older."
- **Problem:** The record-linkage method is plausible but is not stated in the available abstract text.
- **Suggested fix:** "Mark as unverifiable from abstract only, or rephrase to mirror the plain-language summary."

### Study 5: Older adults would wear fall-detection tech — but only if it is discreet, affordable and respects privacy
**PMID:** 42677268 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported market claim and causal ranking** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Billions are being invested in fall-detection wearables and sensors for aging in place, and this study shows adoption will hinge less on technical accuracy than on discretion, cost, and trust."
- **Abstract says:** "Stakeholders expressed interest in gait-monitoring technologies that are affordable, discreet, easy to use, and respectful of autonomy... may improve adoption and support safe aging in place."
- **Problem:** The investment figure is not in the abstract, and the study did not weigh technical accuracy against discretion/cost/trust — the abstract only says aligning design with these preferences "may improve adoption."
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the investment claim or source it, and rewrite as "this study suggests adoption will depend heavily on discretion, cost, and trust, not just device performance.""

**Issue B. Overstated paraphrase of provider views** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "providers in this study said continuous data is worthless without someone able to act on it"
- **Abstract says:** "Providers valued continuous data but cited cost, patient tech-readiness, and actionable output as critical."
- **Problem:** "Worthless" is stronger than the abstract's statement that actionable output was considered critical.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "providers said actionable output was critical — continuous data only helps if someone can act on it.""

### Study 6: Brain white matter changes help explain why uneven ground trips up some older walkers more than others
**PMID:** 42679689 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Brain–behavior interaction applied to the wrong walking condition** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with the strongest link between brain changes and unsteady walking in the lower-functioning group"
- **Abstract says:** "A significant group-by-behavior interaction showed that higher FW was more strongly associated with greater baseline gait variability in lower-functioning than higher-functioning older adults, particularly in the body of corpus callosum."
- **Problem:** The headline and body imply the brain measures explained who struggled on uneven terrain, but the reported interaction was with baseline (flat) gait variability; the abstract notes free water and tissue measures had distinct associations with baseline versus uneven-terrain walking.
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify that the strongest brain–behavior link was with baseline gait variability, and note that free water and tissue measures related differently to flat versus uneven-terrain walking."

### Study 7: Piling Up Chronic Conditions in Your 50s and 60s Predicts Falls Six Years Later
**PMID:** 42288171 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Multiple-falls odds ratio denominator** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "roughly 14-15% higher odds of falling and of falling repeatedly"
- **Abstract says:** "greater risk of falling and among fallers having multiple falls (odds ratio (OR): 1.14 and 1.15 per unit increase, respectively)"
- **Problem:** The 1.15 estimate applies to having multiple falls among those who fell, not to repeat falling in the whole cohort.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "14% higher odds of falling, and among those who fell, 15% higher odds of falling more than once.""

**Issue B. Deterministic framing of an observational finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this study shows the trajectory is already set in middle age"
- **Abstract says:** "Our study underscores the importance of identifying multimorbidity as a risk factor for physical function decline and fall risk in middle-age."
- **Problem:** "The trajectory is already set" implies an inevitable course; the cohort showed associations between baseline multimorbidity and later decline and falls, not a fixed trajectory.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to "this study shows risk is already building in middle age.""

### Study 9: Wobble-Board Strength Training Improved Walking-While-Thinking Mobility; Adding Mindfulness Did Not Help
**PMID:** 42677025 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Null secondary outcomes omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Dual-task mobility (moving while performing a cognitive task) improved more in the exercise-only group, and adding mindfulness produced no extra benefit."
- **Abstract says:** "Dual-task cost did not change significantly... Balance confidence improved significantly over time in both groups (mean increase ≈14 points; p < .001), whereas concern about falling remained unchanged."
- **Problem:** The digest reports the dual-task TUG interaction but not that dual-task cost was unchanged, nor that concern about falling (FES-I) did not improve — both qualify the "improved walking-while-thinking" framing.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that dual-task cost and concern about falling did not change significantly."

**Issue B. "Wobble-board" label for the intervention** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Wobble-Board Strength Training"
- **Abstract says:** "instability resistance training (IRT)"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not specify wobble boards; the intervention is described only as instability resistance training.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "strength training on unstable surfaces" or "instability resistance training" in the headline."

### Study 10: Chronic Pain Plus Slower Mental Processing Tripled the Odds of a Fall
**PMID:** 42687648 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Single-exposure groups not reported in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Neither condition alone showed a significant association."
- **Abstract says:** "The participants were categorized into four groups... the coexistence of chronic pain and slower processing speed was significantly associated with falls compared to that with neither condition (odds ratio [OR]=3.203, 95% confidence interval [CI]=1.036-9.901, p=0.043)."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports only the combined-exposure estimate; results for pain alone and slow processing alone are not given, so this statement is unverifiable from the abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the abstract reports only the combined group's association, or flag the single-condition results as unverifiable from the abstract."

### Study 13: A Five-Question Survey Predicted Hip Fracture Recovery Better Than Grip Strength
**PMID:** 42696807 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Odds ratio attached to the inverse outcome** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "those flagged by it were far less likely to get moving in the first days after surgery (odds ratio 7.56 by day 3)"
- **Abstract says:** "Only SARC-F was consistently associated with impaired early mobilization (odds ratio [OR], 1.99; 95% CI, 1.14-3.45 for day 1; OR, 4.90; 95% CI, 2.84-8.44 for day 2; OR, 7.56; 95% CI, 3.61-15.81 for day 3)."
- **Problem:** The OR of 7.56 is the odds of impaired mobilization, not the odds of getting moving; placing it directly after 'far less likely to get moving' invites readers to read it as an odds ratio for the positive outcome.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword as 'had about 7.6 times the odds of impaired mobilization by day 3'."

### Study 19: Nearly Half of Popular Fall-Prevention Videos Online Were Poor Quality — And Likes Didn't Tell You Which
**PMID:** 42531750 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. 'Poor quality' recasts 'did not reach high quality'** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Nearly Half of Popular Fall-Prevention Videos Online Were Poor Quality"
- **Abstract says:** "Mean mDISCERN score was 17.61 (SD 2.87), with 48.1% achieving high quality."
- **Problem:** The study reports the share achieving high quality (48.1%); the remaining 51.9% were simply below that threshold, not necessarily 'poor quality', and 'nearly half' actually describes the high-quality group.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as 'Fewer Than Half of Fall-Prevention Videos Online Met a High-Quality Standard'."

**Issue B. 'Popular' implies popularity-based sampling** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Popular Fall-Prevention Videos Online"
- **Abstract says:** "We conducted a cross-sectional analysis of 216 fall prevention videos from five platforms (Douyin, Kuaishou, Bilibili, Xiaohongshu, Xigua Video) during October-November 2025."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not state that videos were selected for popularity; 30.1% had zero comments, suggesting many were not widely viewed.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop 'Popular' or say 'fall-prevention videos on Chinese short-video platforms'."

### Study 21: Why Seniors With Memory Problems Fall May Differ From Why Others Do
**PMID:** 42666693 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Direction of self-rated health effect not reported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "poorer self-rated health was the only predictor of number of falls among those with cognitive impairment"
- **Abstract says:** "Simple linear regression revealed that self-rated health was the only predictor of falls among individuals with cognitive impairment, whereas the number of medications predicted falls in those without impairment."
- **Problem:** The abstract names self-rated health as a predictor but does not state the direction of the association; "poorer" is a plausible inference but unverifiable from the abstract only. The abstract also specifies simple (unadjusted) linear regression, which the digest describes only as "regression analysis."
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "self-rated health was the only predictor of number of falls among those with cognitive impairment (in unadjusted simple regression)" or note that the direction was not specified in the abstract."

### Study 22: Treating Depression and Anxiety Before Surgery Paid Off Three Months Later — Most Clearly for Cancer Patients

### Study 23: Older Canadians living at home fall more often than those in care facilities — and researchers now know which warning signs matter most
**PMID:** 42497575 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "People who walked fine" misstates the subgroup definition** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Notably, people who walked fine still fell, so feeling steady is not the same as being low risk."
- **Abstract says:** "This model was then applied to a cohort with no serious mobility impairment. Mobility impairment included being completely dependent with ambulation, using a wheelchair or a scooter, or being bedbound."
- **Problem:** The subgroup excluded only severe mobility impairment (fully dependent, wheelchair/scooter, bedbound); those remaining could still have substantial gait or balance problems, so "walked fine" overstates their function.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "people without severe mobility impairment — those not bedbound, wheelchair-dependent or fully dependent for walking — still fell.""

**Issue B. Headline overclaims certainty about risk factors** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and researchers now know which warning signs matter most"
- **Abstract says:** "key fall risk factors identified in this study provide a foundation for risk adjustment in global comparisons of falls in older adults"
- **Problem:** The abstract frames the variables as candidate risk-adjustment factors for a quality indicator, not a definitive ranking of which warning signs matter most.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "and researchers have identified a short list of risk markers worth checking.""

### Study 25: A hospital told staff to get patients moving 'unless contraindicated' — and falls dropped by a third
**PMID:** 42689465 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal phrasing stronger than abstract's hedge** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "structured mobility reduced both falls and fall injuries at low cost"
- **Abstract says:** "This low-cost, scalable intervention suggests that standardizing mobility assessment, communication, and execution may reduce inpatient falls."
- **Problem:** This was an uncontrolled quality improvement project; the abstract says the intervention "may reduce" falls. The digest's flat "reduced" is firmer than the source and than the digest's own caveat about the lack of a control group.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "structured mobility was associated with fewer falls and fall injuries at low cost.""

### Study 26: Virtual reality walking games improved gait in people with early memory loss — but not more than simpler training
**PMID:** 42475977 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. 6-month durability applied to dual-task cost** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and reduced the 'dual-task cost' of walking while thinking — gains still present at 6 months"
- **Abstract says:** "the CDTT group showed within-group improvements in gait speed, step length, step time, and double-support time, which were maintained at 6-month follow-up. DTCs in these same parameters were reduced postintervention in the CDTT group."
- **Problem:** Maintenance at 6 months is reported for the single-task gait parameters; the abstract reports the dual-task cost reductions only at postintervention, so extending durability to DTC is not supported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Separate the claims: "single-task gait gains were still present at 6 months, and dual-task cost was reduced immediately after the program.""
