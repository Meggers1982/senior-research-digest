### Study 3: Review of 61 Studies: Hearing Aids Appear to Support Brain Function, Not Just Hearing
**PMID:** 42447643 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsourced delay statistic** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "If you've been putting off getting hearing aids — the average person waits years —"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract reports only the database search (2005–2025), the 61 included papers, and the conclusion that hearing aid use is useful for neurocognitive function and auditory processing; it contains no data on how long people delay seeking hearing aids."
- **Problem:** A commonly cited figure about help-seeking delay is presented as if it came from this review. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the aside or attribute it separately (e.g., "other research suggests people often wait years")."

### Study 6: More Than Half Failed a Hearing Screening at Free Community Health Events
**PMID:** 42446280 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported subgroup inference** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "most people in this study who suspected a problem did in fact have one"
- **Abstract says:** ""Among the 305 participants, 57% failed the hearing screening. A majority (64.4%) reported suspected hearing loss.""
- **Problem:** The abstract gives the overall failure rate and the overall suspicion rate separately; it never reports the failure rate among those who suspected hearing loss, so the digest's cross-tabulated claim cannot be verified and could overstate how predictive self-suspicion was.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "more than half of everyone screened failed, and about two-thirds already suspected a problem.""

**Issue B. Omits authors' caution about uptake** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is a template worth copying: embedding hearing screening in existing outreach reaches people traditional audiology clinics miss"
- **Abstract says:** ""Embedding hearing screenings within community health outreach initiatives can improve access among underserved populations, though uptake remains low.""
- **Problem:** The digest presents the model as a straightforward template while leaving out the authors' explicit caveat that uptake remained low.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the authors' note that uptake at these events remained low."

**Issue C. "Free" events not stated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "More Than Half Failed a Hearing Screening at Free Community Health Events"
- **Abstract says:** ""305 participants (274 Hispanics) who underwent hearing screenings at six mobile health events""
- **Problem:** The abstract describes mobile health outreach events but does not state that they were free of charge.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "community mobile health events" in the headline."

### Study 7: Osteoporosis Linked to Higher Hearing Loss Risk — and Two Common Bone Drugs to Lower Risk
**PMID:** 42574660 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Treatment sample size understated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "In a second analysis of nearly 65,000 patients matched three ways"
- **Abstract says:** "zoledronic acid (n = 21,811), denosumab (n = 21,811), or without treatment (n = 21,811)"
- **Problem:** The three matched arms total 65,433, which is just over — not "nearly" — 65,000.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "about 65,400 patients matched 1:1:1" or "more than 65,000.""

### Study 8: How Hard You Feel You're Working to Listen Tracks With How Well You Actually Hear
**PMID:** 42348455 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Sub-test sample sizes implied to be uniform** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers asked 635 participants in a longitudinal cohort ... to rate their performance, effort, and frustration on a modified NASA Task Load Index right after standard speech-recognition tests (NU-6, SPIN, and the Staggered Spondaic Word test)."
- **Abstract says:** ""...following the Northwestern University Auditory Test No. 6 (NU-6; n = 635), Speech Perception in Noise test (SPIN; n = 440), and Staggered Spondaic Word test (SSW; n = 455)""
- **Problem:** Only the NU-6 was completed by all 635 participants; the SPIN and SSW analyses used smaller subsamples.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that 635 completed the NU-6, with 440 and 455 completing the SPIN and SSW respectively."

**Issue B. "Consistently" firms up a hedged result** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "lower self-reported listening workload was consistently associated with better speech recognition scores"
- **Abstract says:** ""In general, lower levels of self-reported workload were associated with better speech recognition scores""
- **Problem:** The abstract's "in general" allows for exceptions; "consistently" implies the association held across every measure.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "generally associated" to match the abstract."

### Study 11: Treating Hearing Loss Travels With Better Health Habits, Tasmanian Survey Finds
**PMID:** 42400121 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Comparison group for cholesterol and diabetes ORs** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "they were about 28% less likely to report high cholesterol, 26% less likely to report diabetes, and considerably more likely to be in the low-risk drinking group than those with uncorrected hearing loss"
- **Abstract says:** "The HL-corrected group was more likely to be in the low-risk alcohol group (OR = 0.567, 95% CI: 0.468-0.687), 28% less likely for high cholesterol (OR = 0.722, 95% CI: 0.571-0.913) and 26% less likely for diabetes (OR = 0.743, 95% CI: 0.582-0.948). They also had lower odds than the HL-uncorrected group for alcohol risk (OR = 0.652, 95% CI: 0.508-0.837)."
- **Problem:** The abstract explicitly names the HL-uncorrected group only for the additional alcohol comparison; the reference group for the cholesterol and diabetes odds ratios is not stated and appears to be a different comparator. The digest presents all three as head-to-head comparisons with the uncorrected group.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the corrected group had lower odds of high cholesterol (28%) and diabetes (26%) relative to the study's reference category, and note separately that they also had lower alcohol-risk odds than the uncorrected group."

### Study 16: Two Cochlear Implants Beat One — and Age Doesn't Change That
**PMID:** 42244467 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Non-significant interaction presented as equal effect** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "and it was just as large in patients aged 50 to 70 as in those aged 20 to 50"
- **Abstract says:** "Age group (20 to 50 versus 50 to 70) did not significantly modify the BiCI effect, and combining age groups was required to detect statistical significance, consistent with an age-independent bilateral benefit."
- **Problem:** Absence of a significant interaction is not evidence that the benefit was identical in size, especially since the abstract notes the age groups had to be combined to reach significance — implying the subgroups were underpowered.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write that age group did not significantly change the size of the bilateral advantage, and note that the subgroups were too small to test separately."

**Issue B. "Single strongest predictor" ranking** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "daily wear time was the single strongest predictor of improvement in this cohort"
- **Abstract says:** "Cumulative auditory experience strongly predicted improvement (both spline terms p < 0.001)"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports cumulative auditory experience (daily use × months since activation) as a strong predictor but does not rank predictors or single it out as the strongest, and it is not the same as daily wear time alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say cumulative device experience — daily wear time accumulated over months — strongly predicted improvement."
