## Fact-Check Report

### Study 1: Multidomain intervention improves physical function and may prevent sarcopenia progression in nursing home residents
**PMID:** 42594504 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Sarcopenia prevalence change framing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Sarcopenia prevalence dropped from 37% to 15% in the intervention group while remaining stable in controls."
- **Abstract says:** "Sarcopenia prevalence decreased in the intervention group (37.1% to 15.4%) and remained stable in controls (28.4% to 29.9%), with a borderline group-by-time interaction (p = 0.109)."
- **Problem:** The digest omits that this finding had a borderline, non-significant p-value (0.109). While mentioned in Caveats as "some outcomes showed borderline significance," the body text presents this as a definitive finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "Sarcopenia prevalence dropped from 37% to 15% in the intervention group while remaining stable in controls, though this difference did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.109)."

**Issue B. Participant age threshold** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "90 long-term care facility residents aged 70 and older"
- **Abstract says:** "Residents aged ≥70 years from two Italian LTCFs were screened, and 90 participants were enrolled"
- **Problem:** This is accurate. No issue.

No other factual errors found.

### Study 2: Resistance exercise stands alone as proven method to improve muscle-specific strength in older adults
**PMID:** 42570706 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline uses "proven"** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Resistance exercise stands alone as proven method to improve muscle-specific strength in older adults"
- **Abstract says:** "Resistance exercise is the most effective intervention for improving muscle-specific strength in older adults."
- **Problem:** "Proven" is hype language. The abstract uses "most effective" and the conclusion notes "Further well-designed RCTs...are needed to confirm these findings." The high heterogeneity (I² = 81%) also limits certainty.
- **Suggested fix:** "Resistance exercise emerges as most effective method to improve muscle-specific strength in older adults"

**Issue B. Story angle clinical recommendation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Physical therapists and fitness professionals working with older clients should emphasize resistance training as the primary intervention for improving muscle quality."
- **Abstract says:** Study concludes effects of other interventions "remain inconclusive" and "further well-designed RCTs...are needed."
- **Problem:** Making clinical recommendations from a meta-analysis with high heterogeneity and acknowledged need for confirmation is slightly premature, though resistance exercise benefit is well-established elsewhere.
- **Suggested fix:** "Physical therapists and fitness professionals working with older clients may consider emphasizing resistance training as a primary intervention for improving muscle quality, though optimal protocols remain under investigation."

### Study 9: Vitamin D deficiency amplifies diabetes-related muscle damage in older adults
**PMID:** 42229217 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Sample sizes** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "7,520 older adults from two large cohorts (US Health and Retirement Study and UK English Longitudinal Study of Ageing)"
- **Abstract says:** "7,520 older adults from two nationally representative cohorts...HRS wave 13, n = 3,246; ELSA wave 6, n = 4,274"

**Issue B. Effect size reporting** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "having both low vitamin D and diabetes increased sarcopenia odds by 66%"
- **Abstract says:** "the joint exposure to low 25(OH)D and diabetes was associated with increased sarcopenia odds (OR = 1.66, 95% CI 1.06-2.61)"
- **Problem:** OR=1.66 represents 66% higher odds, which is correctly stated. Accurate.

### Study 14: Exergaming shows promise for preserving muscle mass in low-income older adults living alone
**PMID:** 42497405 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Intervention duration** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "16-week intervention"
- **Abstract says:** Study 1 was "12-week single-arm pilot study" and Study 2 was "16-week intervention"
- **Problem:** The digest only mentions 16 weeks, omitting that the pilot was 12 weeks.
- **Suggested fix:** "The pilot study lasted 12 weeks; the RCT intervention was 16 weeks..."

**Issue B. Statistical significance clarity** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "showed preliminary signals for preserving muscle mass — the intervention group maintained their muscle while the control group declined"
- **Abstract says:** "nominally significant group × time interaction was observed for ASM (P=.04, FDR-adjusted P=.11)...No findings from study 2 survived FDR correction."
- **Problem:** The digest appropriately notes in Caveats that findings "did not survive statistical correction," but the body text could be clearer that the ASM finding was not statistically significant after correction.
- **Suggested fix:** "showed preliminary, though not statistically significant after correction, signals for preserving muscle mass"

**Issue C. Conflict of interest** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** Not mentioned in Caveats
- **Abstract says:** "SC and SHL work at the MIDAS Health & Technology Co., which developed the Brain Step system. SS, SCH, JK, and SC are co-inventors on a patent related to the Brain Step technology."
- **Problem:** Authors developed and hold patents on the intervention being tested. This is a significant conflict that should be disclosed.
- **Suggested fix:** Add to Caveats: "Several authors work for the company that developed the exergaming system and hold related patents."

### Study 17: CT scans reveal thigh muscle shrinkage predicts strength and mobility decline over 5 years
**PMID:** 42287341 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Statistical findings framing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "In women, shrinkage of thigh muscles was strongly associated with declining grip strength and slower 'Timed Up and Go' test performance."
- **Abstract says:** "Among females, each decrease (per cm2) of anterior compartment of thigh muscle size was associated with a 101% decrease of HGS (95% confidence interval [CI], 1.10-3.67; P = 0.02) and an 121% longer TUG (95%CI, 1.16-4.24; P = 0.02)."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a "101% decrease" which appears to be odds ratio phrasing (OR approximately 2.01 and 2.21). The digest simplifies appropriately but "strongly associated" may slightly overstate given moderate effect sizes.

**Issue B. Men's findings** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "In men, lower back muscle density appeared more predictive of grip strength changes."
- **Abstract says:** "Among males, each decrease (per HU) of psoas major muscle density (OR, 0.37; P = 0.04) would decrease the risk of decreased HGS."
- **Problem:** Accurate representation.

No factual errors found.
