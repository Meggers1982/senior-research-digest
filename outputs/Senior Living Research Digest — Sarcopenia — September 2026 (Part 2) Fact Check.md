### Study 1: Frailty, Not Muscle Loss Alone, Best Predicts Who Loses Independence
**PMID:** 42600484 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Competing interests not mentioned** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**Caveats:** Observational cohort — cannot establish causation; single national cohort of Korean older adults, so findings may not generalize to other populations; disability outcomes assessed biennially, which can miss transient changes."
- **Abstract says:** "Conflict of interest statement: Declaration of competing interest The authors declare the following financial interests/personal relationships which may be considered as potential competing interests:"
- **Problem:** The abstract states that the authors declare potential competing interests (details are truncated and so are unverifiable from the abstract alone), but the digest's caveats say nothing about disclosures.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a caveat noting that the authors declared potential financial/personal competing interests (details not available in the abstract)."

**Issue B. Slightly causal phrasing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Physical frailty — alone or together with sarcopenia — raised the risk of IADL disability by roughly 60%-70%"
- **Abstract says:** "IADL disability risk was higher in both frailty only (sub-distribution hazard ratio [sHR] 1.70, 95% CI 1.20-2.42) and coexistent frailty and sarcopenia (sHR 1.58, 95% CI 1.06-2.36)."
- **Problem:** "Raised the risk" reads as causal for what is an observational association; the combined group's 58% increase is also at the bottom edge of the stated "60%-70%" range.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "was associated with a roughly 60%-70% higher risk of IADL disability" (or "about 60% higher" for the combined group)."

### Study 3: Weak Muscles — Not Extra Weight — Tracked With Low Mood and Memory Problems
**PMID:** 42741804 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Odds ratios are handgrip-based only** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Those with low muscle function plus obesity had roughly four times the odds of depressive symptoms and 2.6 times the odds of cognitive impairment compared with the normal group"
- **Abstract says:** "Relative to the normal group, the combined HGS-based group was associated with depressive symptoms (odds ratio 4.18, 95% confidence interval 2.29-7.62) and cognitive impairment (2.58, 1.53-4.37); 30-s STS-based estimates were 2.99 and 1.89."
- **Problem:** The digest presents one set of numbers as if it applied to the whole "low muscle function" definition, but these are the handgrip-based estimates; the sit-to-stand-based estimates were lower (2.99 and 1.89).
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify that the 4.18 and 2.58 figures come from the grip-strength definition, and note the sit-to-stand estimates were 2.99 and 1.89."

**Issue B. "Memory problems" for cognitive impairment** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Tracked With Low Mood and Memory Problems"
- **Abstract says:** "...in relation to depressive symptoms and cognitive impairment in Korean older adults"
- **Problem:** The outcome was screening-defined cognitive impairment, which covers more than memory; renaming it "memory problems" (repeated as "memory difficulties" in the story angle) misstates the outcome variable.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "cognitive impairment" or "thinking and memory problems on a screening test" in the headline and story angle."

### Study 4: Before Major Cancer Surgery, Grip Strength Predicted Who Survived the Year
**PMID:** 42710621 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Incomplete adjustment set** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "each independently predicted death within one year after adjustment for age, sex, BMI, and muscle mass"
- **Abstract says:** "On multivariable logistic regression adjusted for age, sex, body mass index, SMI, and sarcopenia, both low HGS (OR 4.4, 95% CI 1.6-12.6; p = 0.004) and GLIM-defined malnutrition (OR 3.99, 95% CI 1.01-15.87; p = 0.049) remained independently associated with one-year mortality."
- **Problem:** The model also adjusted for sarcopenia status, which the digest omits; this strengthens rather than weakens the finding but the adjustment set as written is inaccurate.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "and sarcopenia status" to the list of adjustment variables."

### Study 6: Not all muscle loss is the same: blood amino acid patterns differ sharply between thin and obese sarcopenia
**PMID:** 42727175 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Thin" for non-obese sarcopenia** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "blood amino acid patterns differ sharply between thin and obese sarcopenia"
- **Abstract says:** "Whether these AAs differentially associate with sarcopenia without obesity (sarcopenia-only) versus sarcopenic obesity (SO) is unknown."
- **Problem:** The comparison group was sarcopenia without obesity, which includes normal-weight and overweight people; calling them "thin" implies underweight.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "non-obese" or "sarcopenia without obesity" in the headline."

### Study 7: A pre-surgery "aging burden" score predicted delirium, complications and 3-year survival after cancer surgery
**PMID:** 42600483 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Effect size unit not stated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Patients with higher scores had nearly twice the odds of postoperative delirium"
- **Abstract says:** "Higher baseline PABI was independently associated with postoperative delirium (adjusted OR per 1-SD increase, 1.88; 95% CI, 1.43-2.47)"
- **Problem:** The 1.88 odds ratio is per 1-standard-deviation increase in the index, not a comparison of "higher" versus "lower" scoring patients; as written a reader may assume a group-versus-group doubling.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "each one-standard-deviation increase in the score was associated with nearly twice the odds of postoperative delirium.""

### Study 9: Diabetes doubled the odds of weak grip in patients waiting for a liver transplant
**PMID:** 42692360 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline understates effect size** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Diabetes doubled the odds of weak grip in patients waiting for a liver transplant"
- **Abstract says:** "Regression analysis identified DM as a predictor of reduced handgrip strength (OR 2.72, 95% CI 1.15-6.43; p = 0.022), independently from age."
- **Problem:** An odds ratio of 2.72 is closer to a near-tripling than a doubling; the headline also states the association more definitively than a wide confidence interval (1.15-6.43) supports.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "nearly tripled the odds" or "was associated with about 2.7 times the odds of weak grip.""

**Issue B. Protein intake finding omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "even though total calorie intake was similar between groups"
- **Abstract says:** "Interestingly, while total daily energy intake was similar between both groups, patients with DM consumed a higher relative percentage of dietary protein."
- **Problem:** The digest mentions only equal calories; the abstract also reports that the diabetes group ate proportionally more protein, which is relevant to the story angle about protein intake.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that the diabetes group actually consumed a higher relative percentage of protein despite similar total energy intake."

### Study 10: How Much Exercise Protects Your Muscles Depends on How Frail You Already Are
**PMID:** 42537596 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Age range not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers followed 10,871 adults (average age 61, range roughly 45-85, 50.7% women)"
- **Abstract says:** "Participants (N = 10871, 50.7% females, age: 61.3 ± 9.3 years) without sarcopenia at baseline from the Canadian Longitudinal Study on Aging were included."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports only a mean and standard deviation; the 45-85 range comes from general knowledge of the CLSA and is unverifiable from the abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the range or state "average age 61 (SD 9)" as reported in the abstract."

### Study 19: New expert guidance: don't let weight loss in later life cost you muscle
**PMID:** 42689016 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Weight-loss drug framing not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "With powerful weight-loss drugs now widely used, older adults face a real risk of shedding muscle along with fat"
- **Abstract says:** "Selection of weight-loss medication should be guided by evidence supporting its benefit for obesity-related complications or comorbidities, with close monitoring of adverse reactions."
- **Problem:** The abstract says nothing about widespread use of potent weight-loss drugs or about drug-induced muscle loss; this is editorial context presented as the study's rationale.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the drug context to background knowledge, e.g. "The consensus urges that medication choice be evidence-based and closely monitored; separately, clinicians have raised concern that rapid drug-induced weight loss can cost muscle.""

### Study 20: Home-Based Digital Exercise Programs Build Strength in Older Adults With Sarcopenia
**PMID:** 42551769 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Intervention modalities not specified in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "assigned to exercise delivered through digital tools — apps, video sessions, telerehabilitation, or connected devices"
- **Abstract says:** "randomized controlled trials involving adults aged ≥60 with sarcopenia receiving digital exercise interventions"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not itemize the digital delivery formats. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "exercise delivered through digital health tools" without listing specific formats."

**Issue B. Headline states effect more firmly than certainty allows** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Home-Based Digital Exercise Programs Build Strength in Older Adults With Sarcopenia"
- **Abstract says:** "Digital exercise interventions show potential for managing sarcopenia in older adults, though the very low to moderate certainty of evidence indicates that true effects may differ substantially from observed estimates."
- **Problem:** The headline asserts a definite effect where the authors describe only "potential" with low certainty; also the trials were not all home-based per the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "Digital Exercise Programs Show Promise for Strength in Older Adults With Sarcopenia — But Evidence Is Weak.""

### Study 22: Drink Too Much Water Before a Body Scan and It May Skew Your Results
**PMID:** 42668016 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Single-center claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Small sample (N=46) split across four groups of 11–12; single-center; participants were not specifically older adults"
- **Abstract says:** "In a randomized controlled trial, 46 participants (n = 26 males, n = 20 females) were assigned to control (no water, n = 11), 250 mL (n = 12), 500 mL (n = 11), or 1000 mL (n = 12) of AWI."
- **Problem:** The abstract never states that recruitment or scanning happened at a single site (author affiliations span two institutions), so the single-center caveat is unverifiable from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "single-center" or rephrase as "conducted in a research/clinical laboratory setting (site details not reported in the abstract)"."

### Study 23: Early-Stage Muscle Weakness Did Not Clearly Predict Later Memory Decline in a Large Chinese Cohort
**PMID:** 42675832 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Outcome was cognitive function, not dementia** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It pushes back on the growing assumption that low grip strength is a reliable early warning sign of future dementia"
- **Abstract says:** "Possible sarcopenia had a negative but imprecise adjusted total association with later cognition (TE = -0.112, 95% CI -0.242 to 0.019; p = 0.094)... compatible with bidirectional or shared aging processes rather than a validated early marker"
- **Problem:** The study modeled a continuous cognitive function score, not dementia incidence or memory decline; framing it as evidence against grip strength predicting dementia overstates the outcome measured.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the study found no precise association with later cognitive function scores, and note that dementia incidence was not assessed."

**Issue B. Definition of "possible sarcopenia" not given in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "measuring "possible sarcopenia" (low grip strength or slow gait) in 2011"
- **Abstract says:** "a primary three-stage sequence: possible sarcopenia, physical activity and covariates in 2011"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not specify the operational criteria for possible sarcopenia; the parenthetical definition, while conventional, is unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the definition generically (e.g., "a screening-level definition based on muscle strength/performance") or note that criteria were not specified in the abstract."

### Study 24: People Whose Muscles Are Aging Faster Show Different Brain Wiring Patterns
**PMID:** 42684633 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline emphasizes the wrong group** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "People Whose Muscles Are Aging Faster Show Different Brain Wiring Patterns"
- **Abstract says:** "decelerated MA subjects exhibit significantly lower MSCoh compared to the normal and accelerated groups in the Alpha 1, Alpha 2, and Beta 1 bands. Furthermore... the decelerated MA group possessed lower values in the right frontal area"
- **Problem:** The group that stood out on most measures was the decelerated (slower-aging muscle) group, not the accelerated group, so the headline implies the opposite emphasis from the results — even though the body text describes it correctly.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "Muscle aging pace tracks with differences in brain connectivity" or name the decelerated group explicitly."

**Issue B. "Single region" sampling claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "sample of 101 healthy older adults from a single region"
- **Abstract says:** "eyes-closed resting-state electroencephalography (EEG) was recorded from 101 healthy, neurologically intact older adults"
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no recruitment location; author affiliations span Rome and Sassari, so "a single region" cannot be confirmed.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "a single-cohort Italian sample (recruitment sites not specified in the abstract)"."
