### Study 1: Nearly Half of Postmenopausal Women Studied Had Both Muscle Loss and Thinning Bones — and a Normal Weight Didn't Protect Them
**PMID:** 42928822 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Headline claims overlap not reported** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Nearly Half of Postmenopausal Women Studied Had Both Muscle Loss and Thinning Bones"
- **Abstract says:** "Sarcopenia and sarcopenic obesity were identified in 43.7% and 27% of participants, respectively, while 48% had osteoporosis at any site."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports sarcopenia and osteoporosis prevalence separately and gives no figure for how many women had both conditions. The headline presents a co-occurrence statistic that is not in the data.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to reflect the separate prevalences, e.g. 'Nearly Half of Postmenopausal Women Studied Had Thinning Bones, and More Than Four in Ten Had Muscle Loss.'"

**Issue B. Unsupported link between muscle and bone loss** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Muscle loss and bone loss travel together after menopause, and the bathroom scale and BMI chart can miss both — leaving women reassured by a normal weight while their fracture and fall risk quietly rises."
- **Abstract says:** "The study aimed to assess the prevalence of sarcopenia and sarcopenic obesity... It also examined predictors of these conditions and their association with bone fragility. [Results report prevalences and BMI/fat-mass predictors only.]"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not report an association between sarcopenia and bone fragility, nor any fall or fracture outcome, so the 'travel together' and 'fracture and fall risk rises' framing goes beyond the reported results.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that both conditions were common in this sample and that the study did not measure falls or fractures."

### Study 2: Osteoporosis Triples the Risk of Broken Bone Around an Elbow Replacement, Study Finds
**PMID:** 42733408 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for retrospective cohort** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Osteoporosis Triples the Risk of Broken Bone Around an Elbow Replacement, Study Finds"
- **Abstract says:** "Osteoporosis is associated with increased risk of periprosthetic fracture and implant loosening following TEA."
- **Problem:** 'Triples the risk' states causation for a matched retrospective cohort; the authors frame the result as an association. The digest's Caveats correctly note this, but the headline does not.
- **Suggested fix:** "'Osteoporosis Linked to Triple the Rate of Fracture Around an Elbow Replacement.'"

**Issue B. Most likely structural failure** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "since fracture around the hardware is the most likely structural failure"
- **Abstract says:** "Patients with osteoporosis had higher two-year rates of periprosthetic fracture (4.4% vs. 1.4%, p<0.001) and implant loosening (5.0% vs. 2.3%, p=0.007)."
- **Problem:** Implant loosening was actually the more common structural complication in osteoporosis patients (5.0% vs 4.4%), so calling periprosthetic fracture the most likely structural failure is not supported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say that fracture around the hardware and implant loosening were both more common in osteoporosis patients."

### Study 4: What Drives Repeat ER Visits Among Older Adults — and What Makes the Experience Feel Bad
**PMID:** 42762629 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Cohort description imprecise** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Australian researchers followed 336,283 emergency department visits by adults aged 65 and older in New South Wales for five years"
- **Abstract says:** "Individuals with an index ED presentation in 2018 who survived the ascertainment period were followed for five years... There were 336,283 index presentations in 2018"
- **Problem:** The 336,283 figure is index presentations in 2018, and it was the individuals (not the visits) who were followed for five years; frequent-use status was defined over the remainder of 2018.
- **Suggested fix:** "'tracked 336,283 older adults who had an emergency department visit in 2018 and followed them for five years.'"

**Issue B. Causal language for associations** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Negative experiences were driven most strongly by not getting enough information about treatment (5.2 times the odds of a negative rating)... the single biggest determinant of whether older patients felt well cared for"
- **Abstract says:** "negative ED experience was associated with long waits... complications... and poor communication or involvement in decisions, with insufficient treatment information showing the strongest association (OR 5.17; 95%CI 3.48-7.69)"
- **Problem:** 'Driven by' and 'determinant' imply causation from a cross-sectional survey analysis within an observational cohort.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'most strongly associated with' and 'the strongest correlate of a negative rating'."

### Study 5: Women With Epilepsy Face Earlier Menopause and Higher Bone-Loss Risk — New Expert Guidance Addresses a Research Blind Spot
**PMID:** 42810186 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Antiseizure medication bone claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Long-term use of certain antiseizure medications is an under-recognized osteoporosis risk factor"
- **Abstract says:** "Women with epilepsy may be at increased risk of early menopause and are more likely to develop cardiovascular disease, osteoporosis, and cognitive impairment than women without epilepsy."
- **Problem:** The abstract attributes elevated osteoporosis risk to women with epilepsy as a group and discusses drug interactions with MHT, but does not state that antiseizure medications themselves are an osteoporosis risk factor. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the elevated osteoporosis risk to women with epilepsy generally, or drop the medication-specific causal claim."

**Issue B. Headline states earlier menopause as fact** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Women With Epilepsy Face Earlier Menopause and Higher Bone-Loss Risk"
- **Abstract says:** "Women with epilepsy may be at increased risk of early menopause"
- **Problem:** The guidance hedges with 'may be at increased risk'; the headline presents it as established.
- **Suggested fix:** "'Women With Epilepsy May Face Earlier Menopause and Higher Bone-Loss Risk'."

### Study 6: Reaching for the Salt Shaker Linked to Higher Osteoporosis Risk in Nearly Half a Million Adults
**PMID:** 42700476 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Reference group for HR 2.68 unclear** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Among participants who also carried a high genetic risk score for osteoporosis, always adding salt was associated with nearly triple the risk (HR 2.68)."
- **Abstract says:** "In joint analyses, participants with high genetic risk who always added salt had the highest risk (HR 2.68, 95% CI 2.41-2.98)."
- **Problem:** This was a joint analysis comparing the high-genetic-risk/always-salting group with the lowest-risk reference group, not a within-high-genetic-risk comparison of salters vs non-salters, as the digest wording implies.
- **Suggested fix:** "'People with both a high genetic risk score and an always-add-salt habit had the highest risk of all — about 2.7 times that of the lowest-risk group.'"

### Study 9: Routine X-Rays After Hip Fracture Surgery Changed Nothing for 1,300 Patients
**PMID:** 42732263 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline omits the asymptomatic qualifier** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Routine X-Rays After Hip Fracture Surgery Changed Nothing for 1,300 Patients"
- **Abstract says:** "Routine postoperative radiographs did not result in any change in management or revision surgery rates in asymptomatic patients... Seventy reoperations occurred during the follow-up period; all these patients presented with symptoms through the emergency department prior to intervention."
- **Problem:** The null finding applies specifically to asymptomatic patients with satisfactory intraoperative imaging; 70 of the 1,303 did go on to reoperation after presenting with symptoms. The headline reads as if nothing happened to anyone.
- **Suggested fix:** "'Routine Follow-Up X-Rays Changed Nothing for Symptom-Free Patients After Hip Fracture Surgery'."

### Study 13: Five-year trial: high-dose vitamin D did not prevent fractures in older adults who already had enough
**PMID:** 42704968 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Fracture counts listed in reversed order** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the 1,600 IU group recorded more fractures than placebo (49 vs. 73 during supplementation; HR 1.52)"
- **Abstract says:** "During the mean 4.1-year supplementation period, there were 49, 73 and 52 fractures in the placebo, 1600 IU/day ... and 3200 IU/day ... arms"
- **Problem:** By convention the first number belongs to the first-named group, so "49 vs. 73" reads as though the 1,600 IU arm had 49 fractures and placebo 73 — the opposite of the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "(73 vs. 49 for placebo during supplementation; HR 1.52)"."

**Issue B. Interpretation attributed to authors; persistence of finding omitted** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "a result the authors do not interpret as a true harm but which offers no support for high-dose use"
- **Abstract says:** "the 1600 IU/day arm had HR = 1.52 (95% CI 1.06-2.18) during supplementation and HR = 1.40 (95% CI 1.08-1.80) over the extended 7.3-year follow-up; the conclusion states only that supplementation did not reduce fracture risk"
- **Problem:** The abstract contains no author statement dismissing the excess fractures, and the statistically significant excess in the 1,600 IU arm persisted into extended follow-up — context the digest omits while labeling it a likely chance finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the abstract does not comment on whether this represents harm, and note the excess persisted over 7.3 years (HR 1.40, 95% CI 1.08-1.80) with no dose-response at 3,200 IU."

**Issue C. Blood-level results came from a sub-cohort** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Supplementation clearly raised blood vitamin D levels — from about 75 nmol/L at baseline to roughly 100 and 120 nmol/L in the two dose groups"
- **Abstract says:** "In the sub-cohort of 550 participants, the mean ± SD baseline serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D concentration was 74.8 ± 18.2 nmol/L. After 12 months, the concentrations were 73.0 ± 17.8, 99.7 ± 21.1 and 120.4 ± 21.8 nmol/L"
- **Problem:** The blood measurements were taken in a 550-person sub-cohort at 12 months, not in all 2,495 participants; the digest implies trial-wide measurement.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "in a 550-person sub-cohort measured at 12 months"."

### Study 14: Nearly one in six adults over 40 has a spinal fracture they don't know about
**PMID:** 42624233 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline generalizes beyond the Spanish sample** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Nearly one in six adults over 40 has a spinal fracture they don't know about"
- **Abstract says:** "MVFs affected nearly one in six Spanish adults aged ≥40 years"
- **Problem:** The abstract's one-in-six figure is specific to a Spanish primary care sample; the headline presents it as a general population rate. The body also mixes morphometric-only, traumatic and clinical fragility fractures under "don't know about."
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "Spanish" or "in a Spanish screening study" to the headline."

**Issue B. Scan duration not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "it adds only minutes and very little radiation"
- **Abstract says:** "Vertebral Fracture Assessment (VFA) by Dual-energy X-ray Absorptiometry (DXA) enables low-radiation detection of morphometric vertebral fractures"
- **Problem:** The low-radiation point is supported, but the abstract says nothing about how long the scan takes.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "only minutes" or attribute it to general practice rather than this study."

### Study 15: Older adults scored under 30% on a basic osteoporosis knowledge test — and men did worst
**PMID:** 42600719 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Borderline association presented as established** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "as were older respondents, those with less education, and those with no family history of osteoporosis"
- **Abstract says:** "age (OR: 1.02, CI: 1.00-1.03), lower education level (OR: 1.38, CI: 1.00-1.89), being male (OR: 2.40, CI: 1.87-3.09) and those without a family history of osteoporosis (OR: 1.79, CI: 1.30-2.45)"
- **Problem:** The education and age confidence intervals touch 1.00, so those associations are borderline; the digest lists them alongside the robust male and family-history findings without qualification.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the education and age associations were borderline (confidence intervals reaching 1.00)."

### Study 18: Exercise does build bone — but how much, and which kind, depends on where in your skeleton you look
**PMID:** 42575297 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Largest synthesis to date" / "confirming"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "This is the largest synthesis to date confirming that structured exercise measurably improves bone density in adults of all ages"
- **Abstract says:** "Exercise is associated with small-to-moderate, site-specific improvements in BMD across adulthood ... Pairwise GRADE certainty ranged from high (femoral neck) to very low (total hip)"
- **Problem:** The abstract makes no claim to being the largest synthesis, and uses associational language with certainty ranging down to very low, so "confirming" overstates it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the superlative and use "one of the largest syntheses to date, finding that structured exercise is associated with..."."

**Issue B. Ranking claim not specified in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "combining aerobic work with resistance training, which ranked well for spine and hip"
- **Abstract says:** "NMA suggested site-specific ranking patterns, but credible intervals were often wide ... agent-specific optima, especially AT+RT at 1800 MET-min/week, were boundary-sensitive and exploratory"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports an exploratory dose optimum for aerobic-plus-resistance training but does not state that this modality ranked well at spine and hip specifically.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the abstract reports an exploratory dose optimum for combined aerobic plus resistance training without giving modality rankings by site."

### Study 21: When weak bones and weak muscles travel together, the risks multiply
**PMID:** 42631701 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Comparative risk claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "yet in older adults they decline together and the combination is far more dangerous than either alone"
- **Abstract says:** "Osteosarcopenia is associated with a higher risk of adverse outcomes, such as falls, fractures, depression, and frailty, and consequently with mortality."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports elevated risk associated with osteosarcopenia but does not make a head-to-head comparison showing the combination is worse than osteoporosis or sarcopenia alone. The digest's "far more dangerous than either alone" asserts a magnitude and comparison the abstract does not support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "and having both is linked to higher risks of falls, fractures, depression, frailty and death" or note that the review does not quantify the comparison with either condition alone."
