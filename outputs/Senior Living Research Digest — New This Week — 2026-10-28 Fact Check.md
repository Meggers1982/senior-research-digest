### Study 1: Fewer restraints in the ICU didn't hurt older sepsis patients — and got them off the ventilator sooner
**PMID:** 42944623 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unverified safety-event example** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with no increase in sedative use, deaths at 28 days, or safety events such as self-extubation"
- **Abstract says:** "No significant between-group differences were observed in delirium incidence, sedative exposure, 28-day mortality, or safety outcomes."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports "safety outcomes" generically and never names self-extubation; the specific example is unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "such as self-extubation" or attribute it as a typical example rather than a reported outcome."

### Study 3: How fast you finish a tablet-based memory test may say more about Alzheimer's-type brain shrinkage than about blood-vessel damage
**PMID:** 42948203 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Secondary analysis presented as the headline comparison** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Completion time — not accuracy — predicted the Alzheimer's-type atrophy pattern, and did so roughly twice as strongly as it predicted vascular burden."
- **Abstract says:** "CADi2 features improved prediction of ADLS (ΔR = +0.054...) but not WML (ΔR = -0.006)... Because predictive headroom differed, we repeated the comparison in residual space. There, the increment correlated about twice as strongly with ADLS as with WML (r = 0.160 vs. 0.080; p = 0.044), although both remained significant."
- **Problem:** The "twice as strongly" figure comes from a secondary residual-space re-analysis with borderline significance (p = 0.044), and in that analysis the vascular association was also significant — nuances the digest omits.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that the two-fold difference came from a secondary analysis with a borderline p value and that the link to vascular burden was present too, just weaker."

### Study 4: Slower thinking is the earliest casualty of small-vessel brain disease — memory loss comes later
**PMID:** 42946888 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Effect size slightly upgraded** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Processing speed showed the earliest and strongest deficits, detectable even at low lesion burden and reaching large effects at the most severe grade."
- **Abstract says:** "Processing speed showed the earliest and strongest deficits, with small effects at low WMH burden and medium-to-large effects by high WHM burden, Fazekas 3 (Fazekas 3 pooled g = - 1.02...)"
- **Problem:** The authors describe the Fazekas 3 processing-speed effect as medium-to-large; the digest calls it "large."
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "reaching medium-to-large effects at the most severe grade.""

**Issue B. Headline sequencing overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Slower thinking is the earliest casualty of small-vessel brain disease — memory loss comes later"
- **Abstract says:** "verbal and visual memory showed small effects at low burden that reached large by Fazekas 3"
- **Problem:** Memory deficits were already detectable (small effects) at low lesion burden, so "comes later" overstates the separation from processing speed; the difference is in magnitude, not purely onset.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like "slower thinking leads the way — memory deficits grow larger as lesions worsen.""

### Study 6: A third of patients lose significant muscle within days of abdominal surgery — and they fare worse
**PMID:** 42946212 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Prevalence denominator imprecise** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A systematic review pooled 55 studies covering 7,196 adults who had repeat measurements of muscle mass or strength taken in the days after intra-abdominal surgery. About 35% met criteria for acute muscle wasting in the early postoperative period"
- **Abstract says:** "Fifty-five studies with 7,196 patients were included. A cut-off for muscle wasting was found in 13 observational studies, with a pooled proportion prevalence of acute muscle wasting of 0.35 (95% confidence interval 0.30-0.41, I2=89.2%)."
- **Problem:** The 35% prevalence was pooled from only the 13 studies that reported a muscle-wasting cut-off, not from all 55 studies or all 7,196 patients as the sentence order implies.
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify that the 35% figure came from the 13 studies that defined a cut-off for muscle wasting."

### Study 11: Seven health systems nudged senior vaccination rates up while the rest of the country slid backward
**PMID:** 42947551 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Comparative effectiveness not established** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the most effective approach here was stacking overlapping interventions — fixing data capture, training staff, and embedding prompts in existing visits — rather than running a standalone campaign"
- **Abstract says:** "describes how health systems: (1) implemented multiple strategies to increase vaccination rates; (2) tailored strategies to the needs of their system; and (3) layered in overlapping interventions strategies and interventions... a coordinated system-level approach... is important to increase vaccination rates"
- **Problem:** The report describes layering of strategies but does not compare approaches head to head, so calling layering 'the most effective approach here' implies a comparison that was not made.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'the approach these systems used was stacking overlapping interventions...'"

**Issue B. 'Recurred everywhere' not stated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The report details which barriers recurred everywhere: poor vaccination data, vaccine hesitancy and fatigue, and difficulty building vaccination into routine clinical visits."
- **Abstract says:** "The Council of Medical Specialty Societies along with the participating societies identified several challenges to vaccination, including data collection, vaccine hesitancy and fatigue, and integration into clinical workflows."
- **Problem:** The abstract lists challenges identified collectively; it does not say each barrier appeared in every participating system.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'The report lists the challenges identified across the initiative:...'"

### Study 12: A common cholesterol drug may build bone in women after menopause — and scientists think they've found the reason
**PMID:** 42946859 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Animal experiments not specified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "In animal and cell experiments, atorvastatin promoted new bone formation specifically under estrogen-deficient conditions"
- **Abstract says:** "atorvastatin promotes bone anabolism specifically under estrogen-deficient conditions by inducing osteogenic differentiation of leptin receptor-expressing (LEPR+) bone marrow stromal cells, which include skeletal stem/progenitor cells"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not state that in vivo animal models were used (nor which species). Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'in laboratory experiments' unless the full text confirms animal models."

### Study 15: Alzheimer's blood tests held steady on repeat draws — with one notable exception
**PMID:** 42947225 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Cut-point approach mislabeled** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Using a validated three-tier cut-point system for p-tau217, 92% of people landed in the same category"
- **Abstract says:** "Using a validated dual cut-point approach for plasma p-tau217, most individuals (92%) remained within the same diagnostic category (high/indeterminate/low)"
- **Problem:** The method is a dual cut-point approach producing three categories; calling it a 'three-tier cut-point system' misnames the method, though the resulting categories are described correctly.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write 'a validated two-cut-point scheme that sorts results into high, indeterminate or low probability'."

### Study 16: Isolation and illness feed each other: 16 years of Korean data trace a two-way loop
**PMID:** 42945538 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal language for observational path analysis** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "It reframes isolation as both a cause and a consequence of declining health"
- **Abstract says:** "Significant longitudinal and bidirectional associations emerged among social isolation, chronic diseases, and depressive symptoms over time."
- **Problem:** The study reports bidirectional associations in a longitudinal path model, not causation; this sentence also contradicts the digest's own caveat that the modeling is 'not proof that isolation causes disease.'
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'It frames isolation as both a predictor and a consequence of declining health.'"

**Issue B. Study span not stated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "16 years of Korean data trace a two-way loop"
- **Abstract says:** "Using data from three waves of the Korean Longitudinal Study of Aging, longitudinal path analysis was conducted on 5,585 participants."
- **Problem:** The abstract specifies three waves but gives no calendar span; the 16-year figure is unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'three waves of Korean national data' in the headline, or confirm the span from the full text."

### Study 17: 41,000 dogs tracked: purebreds died younger than mixed breeds, and where a dog lived mattered as much as for people
**PMID:** 42945030 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. 'Single biggest predictor' not established** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "size is the single biggest predictor of how many years you'll have together"
- **Abstract says:** "This initial DAP survival analysis confirms strong demographic effects on canine longevity... Median lifespan ranged from 14 years in small dogs to less than 10 years in giant AKC-recognized breed dogs."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports strong size effects but does not rank size against all other predictors as the largest.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'size was the strongest of the demographic factors examined'."

**Issue B. Dog-human geographic correlation overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "where a dog lived mattered as much as for people"
- **Abstract says:** "there was significant regional and state-level variation, which correlated positively with human mortality patterns (r = 0.43, ρ = 0.63, p = 0.0017)"
- **Problem:** A moderate positive ecological correlation between canine and human regional mortality is not the same as geography mattering 'as much' for dogs as for people.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as 'regional dog death rates tracked human death rates in the same places'."

### Study 20: Living with a rare muscle disease that starts at 60: survey finds patients satisfied with hospitals but stranded on information
**PMID:** 42934973 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Response count phrasing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "58 of 89 members' responses were analyzed"
- **Abstract says:** "A nationwide survey was conducted from April to September 2025 among 89 members of a Japanese IBM patient association. Of 59 responses received, 58 were included in the analysis."
- **Problem:** The survey went to 89 members and produced 59 responses, 58 of which were analyzed; the digest's phrasing blurs the distinction between members surveyed and responses received.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write '89 members were surveyed, 59 responded, and 58 responses were analyzed'."

**Issue B. Misdiagnosis claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "IBM typically begins in a person's sixties and is often mistaken for ordinary aging or treatment-resistant polymyositis"
- **Abstract says:** "Inclusion body myositis (IBM) is a progressive inflammatory myopathy characterized by weakness of the quadriceps and finger flexors, and often accompanied by dysphagia, resulting in substantial functional impairment."
- **Problem:** The abstract says nothing about misdiagnosis as ageing or polymyositis. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the misdiagnosis point to general background or drop it."
