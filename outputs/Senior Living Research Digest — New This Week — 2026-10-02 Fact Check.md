### Study 1: Which combinations of chronic illness matter most? A 10-year study of 16,000 older adults finds the answer differs for men and women
**PMID:** 42821655 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Female high-risk cluster composition misstated** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "while gout combined with kidney and metabolic problems was especially lethal in women (96% higher risk)"
- **Abstract says:** "Higher mortality was also observed for the metabolic, gout & kidney disease cluster in males (aHR 1.63 [1.47-1.81]) and the gout, anemia & kidney disease cluster in females (aHR 1.96 [1.74-2.21])."
- **Problem:** The 96% figure belongs to a female cluster defined by gout, anemia and kidney disease; the metabolic/gout/kidney combination was the male cluster (63% higher risk). The digest swaps a component condition between sexes.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as: "while a cluster of gout, anemia and kidney disease was especially lethal in women (96% higher risk), and a metabolic, gout and kidney disease cluster carried 63% higher risk in men.""

### Study 9: For older patients with atrial fibrillation who have already had a stroke, early ablation tracked with better outcomes
**PMID:** 42821842 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Age range of cohort** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "For older patients with atrial fibrillation who have already had a stroke, early ablation tracked with better outcomes"
- **Abstract says:** "We retrospectively included patients aged 18-85 years with AF and a history of ischemic stroke from 6 Chinese centers ... A total of 431 patients were included (median age, 70 years; 37% female)"
- **Problem:** The study enrolled adults aged 18–85, not only older adults; the median age was 70, so the "older patients" framing overstates how age-specific the cohort was.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note in the body that the cohort spanned ages 18–85 with a median of 70, or soften the headline to "patients with atrial fibrillation (median age 70).""

### Study 13: Common age-related blood mutations come with biologically 'older' cells, pooled analysis finds
**PMID:** 42463064 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Pooled estimate attributed to the full sample** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers pooled five studies covering 7,483 adults aged 55-79 ... People with CHIP were biologically 'older' than their chronological age by roughly 1.2 to 2.8 years depending on the clock used"
- **Abstract says:** "Five studies comprising 7483 individuals (ages 55-79, 67.1% female) ... were included ... Meta-analysis of three cross-sectional studies (n = 6946) showed that CHIP had higher EAA versus no-CHIP for Horvath1Age IEAA (MD=2.84 years ...), HannumAge EEAA (MD=2.31 ...), PhenoAge (MD=1.84 ...), and GrimAge (MD=1.20 ...)"
- **Problem:** The quantitative 1.2–2.8-year range derives only from the three pooled cross-sectional studies (6,946 people), so attaching it to the full five-study, 7,483-person set slightly overstates the evidence base behind the numbers.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a clause such as "in a pooled analysis of three of those studies (6,946 people), CHIP carriers were biologically older by roughly 1.2 to 2.8 years.""

### Study 19: Frail patients with atrial fibrillation are least likely to get stroke-prevention drugs — and in Asia the gap is widest
**PMID:** 41345290 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal attribution in "Why it matters"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the ones most often left off protective therapy, apparently because frailty itself discourages prescribing"
- **Abstract says:** "After adjustments, being frail was associated with lower OAC prescription, with Asians less likely prescribed than Europeans (OR 0.34, 95% CI 0.25-0.45 vs. OR 0.47, 95% CI 0.40-0.55, pint = 0.037)"
- **Problem:** The registry data show an association between frailty and non-prescription, not that frailty is the reason clinicians withhold anticoagulation; "apparently because" asserts a mechanism the design cannot test and sits awkwardly with the digest's own caveat about unmeasured clinical judgment and bleeding risk.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "are also the ones least likely to be prescribed protective therapy — a pattern associated with frailty, though the reasons cannot be determined from these registries.""

### Study 20: A free AI chatbot eased depression in dementia caregivers across 43 countries
**PMID:** 41644333 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Free" not supported by the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A free AI chatbot eased depression in dementia caregivers across 43 countries"
- **Abstract says:** "Available 24/7, PDC30 is a self-guided, automated intervention consisting of a Guidebook, an AI-powered counseling chatbot, and interactive applications for cognitive-behavioral techniques, relaxation, and caregiver-recipient bonding."
- **Problem:** The abstract never states that PDC30 is free of charge (unverifiable from abstract only), and reducing a multi-component web platform to "an AI chatbot" overstates the role of the chatbot component, even though the body text describes the platform correctly.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "A self-guided online program with an AI counseling chatbot eased depression in dementia caregivers across 43 countries.""

**Issue B. Null anxiety result omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the intervention group showed significant improvements in depression (d = -0.37), caregiving burden (d = -0.34), and positive gains from caregiving (d = 0.42)"
- **Abstract says:** "Intent-to-treat analysis using mixed-effects regression showed treatment x time2 effects on all outcomes except anxiety."
- **Problem:** Anxiety was a prespecified secondary outcome that did not improve; omitting it makes the benefit profile look broader than reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "anxiety, however, did not improve" after the list of significant outcomes."

### Study 21: Early Lewy body dementia declines far more slowly than Alzheimer's — but with bigger ups and downs
**PMID:** 41249712 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Conflict of interest attributed to wrong author** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the senior author reports trial coordination roles and honoraria from Eisai, Roche, Biogen and Novo Nordisk"
- **Abstract says:** "F.B. was the national coordinator for France for the Eisai Delphia (E2027), Axovant Headway-DLB and Roche Graduate therapeutic trials; he had received honoraria from Roche, Eisai and Biogen for oral presentations and from Eisai and Novo Nordisk for a board."
- **Problem:** The declared relationships belong to F. Blanc, the first and corresponding author, not the senior (last) author, who is Sauleau E. The substance of the disclosure is correct but the attribution is not.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "the first/corresponding author reports trial coordination roles and honoraria from Eisai, Roche, Biogen and Novo Nordisk.""

### Study 22: Chronic, unremitting low mood — not occasional bad spells — tracked with faster physical frailty in 58,000 older adults
**PMID:** 42822543 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Direction of education effect inferred** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "lower education consistently predicted belonging to a worse-trajectory group"
- **Abstract says:** "education was a directionally consistent predictor of phenotype membership across the four cohorts (OR 0.32-0.80)"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports only that education was a directionally consistent predictor with ORs below 1; it does not state which direction (i.e., that lower education predicts worse phenotypes), even though that is the likely reading.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "education was consistently related to which trajectory group people fell into (odds ratios 0.32-0.80), with the pattern suggesting less education aligned with worse trajectories.""

**Issue B. Frailty outcome described generically** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The persistent high burden group went on to accumulate frailty faster in every cohort analyzable with a landmark design"
- **Abstract says:** "The persistent high burden phenotype (P5) consistently predicted faster non-cognitive frailty accumulation in the three cohorts amenable to landmark analysis"
- **Problem:** The outcome was specifically non-cognitive frailty index accumulation; the digest drops that qualifier (though the headline's "physical frailty" is broadly consistent).
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the qualifier: "accumulated non-cognitive (physical) frailty faster in all three cohorts amenable to the landmark design.""

- **As written:** "100 people with Parkinson's disease (mean age 60, range extending into the older-adult years)"
