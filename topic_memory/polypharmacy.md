# Topic Memory: polypharmacy
_Last updated: 2026-10-06_

## Established findings
- Pharmacist-led medication review reduces medication burden and inappropriate prescribing without harming quality of life, across hospital, pharmacy and oncology settings; now with an economic case (~€181 net/patient) and no identifiable subgroup that benefits more (PMIDs 42472621, 42570168 Aug 2026; 42731364, 42467355 Oct 2026)
- Specific drug classes — benzodiazepines/psycholeptics, anticholinergics, antipsychotics, opioids, antiepileptics, diuretics, NSAIDs — drive harm more reliably than raw pill counts (PMIDs 42584792, 42285234, 42489786 Aug 2026; 42669283, 42594508, 42503320 Oct 2026)
- Deprescribing is achievable but outcome evidence remains thin: most reviews report prescribing metrics, not falls, readmissions or quality of life (PMIDs 42607278 Aug 2026; 42799555, 42313336, 42731364 Oct 2026)
- Frailty and long medication lists are associated with under-prescribing of guideline treatments (heart failure drugs, AF anticoagulants) in the patients at highest risk (PMIDs 42446811 Aug 2026; 41345290 Oct 2026)
- Raw medication count still works as a trigger, even if it is a weak fall predictor: tied to PIM exposure, ED admission and severe fear of falling (PMIDs 42594508, 42701177, 42628444 Oct 2026)
- Opioids in older adults need start-low/go-slow dosing, a reassessment date, and upfront bowel and fall planning (PMID 42627454, Aug–Sep 2026)

## Emerging threads
- End-of-life/serious-illness deprescribing: patients and caregivers far more willing than clinicians assume; bottleneck is the conversation and team workflow, not refusal (PMIDs 42126766, 42533730, 42519959, 42799555; Oct 2026)
- Standard polypharmacy counts undercount reality — patches, creams, drops, OTC products and supplements are invisible in records (PMIDs 42536289, 42783349; Oct 2026)
- Assisted living as a high-medication setting with no defined clinical owner of the medication list; evidence base dominated by one Finnish program (PMID 42645013, Aug 2026)
- Hospitalization as a missed review checkpoint: drug burden unchanged in 80% of older patients on CNS-active drugs; orthogeriatric co-management changes prescribing and is linked to lower in-hospital mortality (PMIDs 42530030, 42806170, 42821180; Sep–Oct 2026)
- Long-term PPI drift in older adults — 56% long-term users, half also on 10+ other drugs (PMID 42814388, Sep 2026)
- Non-systemic alternatives that avoid adding to the list (e.g., botulinum toxin A for depression) — small trials, not elder-specific (PMID 42669382, Dec 2026)
