### Study 1: The most rigorous global count yet: dementia strikes harder in poorer countries — and in women
**PMID:** 42862582 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "First" claim overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "This is the first harmonized, clinically diagnosed benchmark of dementia incidence spanning rich and poor countries"
- **Abstract says:** "The harmonized estimates presented in this study provide a comparable benchmark for global dementia incidence and inform planning for prevention, diagnosis, and memory-care services, particularly in underrepresented low- and middle-income country settings."
- **Problem:** The authors describe their work as providing "a comparable benchmark," not the first such benchmark; priority claims are not supported by the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "This is a harmonized, clinically diagnosed benchmark of dementia incidence spanning rich and poor countries.""

**Issue B. Headline hype** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The most rigorous global count yet"
- **Abstract says:** "Individual participant-level data from 22 population-based longitudinal cohorts (N = 38 418) spanning 18 countries or territories across 6 continents were included."
- **Problem:** "Most rigorous yet" is an editorial superlative not made in the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use something like "The largest harmonized global count of new dementia cases.""

### Study 2: Staying socially connected tracks with better physical and mental capacity — but it didn't slow decline
**PMID:** 42863480 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported claim about what does track with trajectory** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "pair social programming with the things that do track with trajectory — mobility, hearing and vision care, and chronic disease management"
- **Abstract says:** "Baseline social connection was not associated with differential rates of intrinsic capacity change over follow-up (pooled slope difference β=0·0024 SD per year; 95% CI -0·0004 to 0·0053; I2=26·3%)."
- **Problem:** The study tested only social connection against trajectory; it provides no evidence that mobility, sensory care or chronic disease management track with rate of decline.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to note the study did not identify what does predict trajectory, e.g. "...and pair social programming with other established components of healthy ageing care, which this study did not test.""

**Issue B. Locomotion domain omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "had higher "intrinsic capacity" at baseline, most clearly in the psychological and cognitive domains"
- **Abstract says:** "Locomotion showed a smaller association (β=0·02 SD; 95% CI 0·00-0·04) that remained significant after FDR correction. By contrast, sensory and vitality showed weaker associations that did not remain significant after FDR correction."
- **Problem:** The headline promises "physical" capacity; the body does not mention that locomotion was also significant (though smaller) while sensory and vitality were not.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that locomotion showed a smaller but still significant association, while sensory and vitality did not survive correction."

### Study 3: A non-hormonal menopause drug improved sleep at four weeks — but the advantage had faded by week 12
**PMID:** 42520241 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Primary/prespecified endpoint not stated in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The favorable across-the-period findings came from post hoc analyses, not the prespecified comparison, and the primary objective measure showed no group difference at week 12."
- **Abstract says:** "Elinzanetant was estimated to reduce PSG WASO at Week 4 versus placebo (least square [LS]-mean difference = -22.50 minutes, 90% CI: -35.47, -9.52). Minimal group differences between treatment arms were estimated at Week 12 for PSG WASO (LS-mean difference = -0.94 minutes, 90% CI: -13.96, 12.09)."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports both Week 4 and Week 12 PSG WASO without labelling either as the primary or prespecified endpoint, so calling Week 12 "the primary objective measure" is unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "the post hoc modelling across the full period favoured the drug, while the Week 12 PSG WASO comparison showed essentially no difference" without asserting which endpoint was primary."

**Issue B. "Proven" CBT-I comparison** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "whether proven approaches like cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia have been tried first"
- **Abstract says:** "NIRVANA explored elinzanetant's effect on multimodal measures of objective and subjective sleep disturbance in postmenopausal women."
- **Problem:** The comparison to CBT-I and the word "proven" come from outside this study and cannot be checked against the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "whether behavioural approaches such as cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia have been considered," noting this trial did not compare them."

### Study 4: How steadily you sleep — not just how long — tracks with sharper thinking in your 60s and 70s
**PMID:** 42436643 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Caveat overstates loss of significance** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the longitudinal associations lost statistical significance after adjustment for multiple comparisons"
- **Abstract says:** "In the longitudinal analysis, actigraphy based sleep continuity and variability were associated with cognitive decline, although significance decreased after multiple comparison adjustment."
- **Problem:** The abstract says significance "decreased," not that it was lost; the body text ("weakened") is accurate but the caveat goes further.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change the caveat to "the longitudinal associations weakened after adjustment for multiple comparisons.""

**Issue B. Sleep duration null not stated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "it was sleep continuity and night-to-night variability — not total sleep duration — that tracked with cognitive decline"
- **Abstract says:** "In the longitudinal analysis, actigraphy based sleep continuity and variability were associated with cognitive decline, although significance decreased after multiple comparison adjustment."
- **Problem:** The abstract names the composites that were associated but never reports a null result for total sleep duration specifically; the contrast is inferred.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say that continuity and variability — rather than any duration-based composite reported — were the measures linked to decline."

### Study 5: After a stroke, blood pressure and cholesterol drugs may not protect your memory, review finds
**PMID:** 42863385 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Certainty-of-evidence nuance omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the certainty of evidence was mostly low"
- **Abstract says:** "Certainty of evidence was mostly low, with a few exceptions of moderate to high certainty."
- **Problem:** The digest drops the qualifier that some outcomes had moderate-to-high certainty evidence.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "with a few exceptions rated moderate to high.""

**Issue B. "Proven" claim outside the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "keep taking your prescribed medications — they are proven to reduce the risk of another stroke"
- **Abstract says:** "Current evidence does not support pharmacological interventions after a cerebrovascular event for reducing risk of cognitive decline, MCI, or dementia."
- **Problem:** This review assessed cognitive outcomes only; the stroke-prevention efficacy claim cannot be verified from this abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the secondary-prevention benefit elsewhere, e.g. "these drugs are prescribed to lower the risk of another stroke — this review looked only at cognitive outcomes.""

### Study 6: Taking a break from osteoporosis drugs: new real-world data suggests which one you were on matters
**PMID:** 42862289 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. ZOL null result timeframe** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Among zoledronate patients (20 on holiday, 47 continuing), there were no significant differences in bone density or turnover markers."
- **Abstract says:** "there were no significant differences in BMD (1-year LS: -0.47%... TH: -1.07%...) and BTM changes between ZOL groups at 1- and 2-year follow-up."
- **Problem:** The abstract limits the null finding to 1- and 2-year follow-up; the digest states it without a time window.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "at one and two years of follow-up.""

**Issue B. Causal phrasing in a retrospective cohort** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "stopping led to modest bone density losses within a year"
- **Abstract says:** "Compared with ongoing ALN therapy, initiating drug holiday resulted in moderate declines in BMD at LS (-2.23%...)"
- **Problem:** Although the abstract itself uses "resulted in/caused," the digest's own caveat says causation cannot be established, so the body text should match that hedge.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "was associated with modest bone density losses within a year.""

### Study 8: A blood test may flag which sleep apnea patients face future memory loss
**PMID:** 42862512 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal phrasing about dementia risk** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Sleep apnea is extremely common in older adults and raises dementia risk"
- **Abstract says:** "Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) is associated with cognitive decline, but biomarkers for identifying cognitively unimpaired patients at increased future risk remain limited."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes an association with cognitive decline, not a demonstrated causal increase in dementia risk.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "is linked to cognitive decline.""

**Issue B. Outcome named as memory loss** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "face future memory loss"
- **Abstract says:** "286 cognitively unimpaired adults with polysomnography-confirmed OSA... were followed for incident cognitive impairment"
- **Problem:** The outcome was incident cognitive impairment (and MoCA decline), which is broader than memory loss.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "future cognitive impairment" in the headline."

### Study 9: After a stroke, it's depression — not anxiety — that tracks with declining thinking skills
**PMID:** 42863797 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies trajectory finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "it's depression — not anxiety — that tracks with declining thinking skills"
- **Abstract says:** "Exploratory associations between 6-month affect and cognitive recovery did not survive multiple imputation and were inconclusive."
- **Problem:** The robust result was an association between depression and executive/visuospatial performance, not with cognitive decline over time; the prognostic analyses were inconclusive.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "...that tracks with weaker executive and visuospatial function" and note the recovery analyses were inconclusive."

**Issue B. Inconclusive prognostic analysis not reported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Depression was independently associated with poorer executive and visuospatial function, while anxiety showed no independent cognitive association once depression was accounted for."
- **Abstract says:** "Exploratory associations between 6-month affect and cognitive recovery did not survive multiple imputation and were inconclusive."
- **Problem:** The body omits that the attempt to link 6-month mood to later cognitive recovery failed, which limits the forward-looking framing in "Why it matters."
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a sentence noting that exploratory analyses of whether 6-month mood predicted later cognitive recovery were inconclusive."

### Study 10: When a loved one doesn't realize they can't hear well, caregivers may pay an emotional price
**PMID:** 42861992 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Odds ratio reported as likelihood** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "those supporting someone with unrecognized hearing loss were roughly twice as likely to report depressive symptoms"
- **Abstract says:** "caregivers of those with unrecognized HL were more likely to report depressive symptoms, including little interest or pleasure in doing things (OR 1.85; 95% CI: 1.19-2.87) and feeling down or depressed (OR 2.07; 95% CI: 1.31-3.25)."
- **Problem:** These are odds ratios (1.85 and 2.07), which are not identical to "twice as likely"; the lower estimate is closer to 1.8 and both have wide confidence intervals.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "had roughly 1.8- to 2-fold higher odds of reporting depressive symptoms.""

**Issue B. Authors' own framing of effect size omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "appears to register in the mood of the person doing the caring, even when it doesn't show up on standard burden measures"
- **Abstract says:** "Despite expectations otherwise, associations between HL recognition and caregiver well-being were modest, suggesting current measures may not fully capture the communication challenges shaping caregiving experiences."
- **Problem:** The authors characterize the associations as modest overall; the digest's framing is somewhat stronger than the conclusion.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the authors themselves called the associations modest."

### Study 11: Not all sleep interruptions are equal: one type tracks with Alzheimer's genetic risk and memory decline

### Study 15: A blood test for Alzheimer's reads differently across racial and ethnic groups — but still tracks the disease
**PMID:** 42862590 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Cutoff framing not directly tested** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is among the largest looks at whether a single cutoff value works equally well for everyone"
- **Abstract says:** "To characterize racial and ethnic differences in %p-tau217 and determine whether associations between %p-tau217, cognition, and amyloid positron emission tomography (PET) differ across groups."

### Study 16: The first emergency hospital stay in pancreatic cancer signals how little time is left — and most patients hadn't seen palliative care
**PMID:** 42863588 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Stronger than the authors' claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a concrete, easy-to-spot moment — the first unplanned hospital admission — that reliably marks a short remaining life expectancy and should trigger a palliative care conversation"
- **Abstract says:** "This event may serve as a pragmatic safety net for palliative care integration, complementing earlier needs- and prognosis-based referral strategies."

### Study 17: Biological 'aging clock' doesn't cause worn-out spines — but weight and inflammation do
**PMID:** 42863602 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal certainty for BMI and CRP** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Biological 'aging clock' doesn't cause worn-out spines — but weight and inflammation do"
- **Abstract says:** "In contrast, genetically proxied BMI and CRP were both associated with spinal stenosis (BMI OR=1.55, p<0.001; CRP OR=1.49, p=0.002) and multiple other degenerative spine outcomes."
- **Problem:** The abstract explicitly rules out a causal effect for telomere length but describes BMI and CRP only as "associated"; Mendelian randomization supports causal inference but rests on assumptions the digest itself flags in Caveats.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change the headline to "...but genetic evidence points to weight and inflammation" and the body to "were associated, in genetic analyses consistent with a causal effect, with spinal stenosis.""

### Study 19: Being transferred to another hospital nearly doubles older patients' risk of dying there
**PMID:** 42860638 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for claims data** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Being transferred to another hospital nearly doubles older patients' risk of dying there"
- **Abstract says:** "IHT was associated with... increased risk of in-hospital mortality (AOR 1.91, 95% CI: 1.81-2.02)... Further research is needed to characterize the underlying drivers of these findings"
- **Problem:** "Nearly doubles" asserts that the transfer itself raises risk, while the study is an adjusted observational analysis of billing data in which sicker patients are the ones transferred; it also converts adjusted odds into "risk."
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "Transferred older patients were nearly twice as likely to die in hospital, study finds" and note the odds-ratio basis."

**Issue B. Sensitivity analysis not labeled** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with 30-day readmission odds 29% higher"
- **Abstract says:** "In a sensitivity analysis, odds of readmission within 30 days were 29% higher for older adults who had undergone IHT"
- **Problem:** The 29% figure comes from a sensitivity analysis rather than the main readmission outcome (AOR 1.09), which the digest presents side by side without that distinction.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "in a sensitivity analysis" before the 30-day figure."

### Study 20: Exercise alone in midlife didn't prevent cognitive decline — but exercise plus three other habits did
**PMID:** 42865774 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Significance claimed for the intermediate strategy** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "but exercise plus three other habits did"
- **Abstract says:** "the estimated risk of cognitive impairment was lower under the strategy combining physical activity with healthy diet and no smoking, and with normal body mass index, compared with the natural course. Also, comprehensive strategies targeting physical activity, lifestyle factors, and all vascular risk factors achieved significant risk reduction."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a lower estimated risk for the diet/no-smoking/BMI combination but explicitly attributes significant risk reduction to the comprehensive strategy including vascular factors; the headline (and "Risk dropped meaningfully") implies the intermediate bundle was statistically significant.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "…but exercise combined with other healthy habits looked better — and only the full package including vascular risk control reached significance.""

**Issue B. Outcome name** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "didn't prevent cognitive decline"
- **Abstract says:** "The primary outcome was cognitive impairment based on cognitive tests."
- **Problem:** The outcome was incident cognitive impairment on testing, not rate of cognitive decline.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "cognitive impairment" in the headline, as the Caveats already do."

### Study 21: Going home after being displaced by war isn't a return to normal, older evacuees say
**PMID:** 42860049 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Qualitative finding stated as a ranked comparison** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Communal infrastructure — reopened clubs, transportation, familiar staff, neighbor check-ins — was the strongest differentiator between older adults who reorganized and those who deteriorated."
- **Abstract says:** "The study highlights social infrastructure as critical for post-return adaptation, underscoring the need for policies recognizing the return process as a significant transition requiring continued communal support."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes social infrastructure as critical, but a thematic analysis of 12 interviews cannot rank it as the "strongest differentiator" between trajectories.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as "the authors highlight social infrastructure — reopened clubs, transportation, familiar staff, neighbour check-ins — as critical to whether older adults reorganized or continued to struggle.""

**Issue B. Specific services detail not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a community whose routines and services had not come back"
- **Abstract says:** "(1) the unsettled return, involving altered security, unfamiliarity within the home, and disrupted community life"
- **Problem:** The abstract refers generally to "disrupted community life"; the specific claim that services had not returned is not verifiable from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use the abstract's wording — "disrupted community life" — or mark the detail as drawn from the full text."

### Study 22: A billion people will have osteoarthritis by 2050 — and a Lancet panel says calling it 'just aging' is the core problem
**PMID:** 42860133 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline rounds projection upward** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A billion people will have osteoarthritis by 2050"
- **Abstract says:** "By 2050, almost 1 billion people are expected to live with osteoarthritis"
- **Problem:** The abstract says "almost 1 billion" and frames it as an expectation; the headline states a flat billion as a certainty. The body text ("projected to approach 1 billion") is accurate, so the headline is the outlier.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Nearly a billion people are projected to have osteoarthritis by 2050…""

**Issue B. Named treatments not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Ask specifically about supervised exercise therapy, weight management support, and pain self-management programs — these are first-line treatments, not consolation prizes while you wait for surgery."
- **Abstract says:** "The roadmap refers to an identity problem that "undermines access to effective interventions" and to scaling up "high-value, person-centred, equitable care"; it names prevention targets (excess bodyweight, joint injury) but does not list specific first-line treatments."
- **Problem:** The specific therapies named, and the claim that they are first-line, cannot be verified from the abstract — this is outside background knowledge brought into a direct reader recommendation.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the treatment list to clinical guidelines rather than to this roadmap, or note "Unverifiable from abstract only" and soften to "ask what non-surgical care your health system offers.""
