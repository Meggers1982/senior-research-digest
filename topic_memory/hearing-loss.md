# Topic Memory: hearing loss
_Last updated: 2026-09-06_

## Established findings
- Hearing loss is consistently tied to worse memory/cognition and to brain structural changes; treating it appears protective (PMIDs 42675511, 42645764, 42447643, Sep 2026; 42568626, 42609411, 42591333, Aug 2026)
- Hearing loss sits inside a cluster of modifiable risks (dementia, frailty, depression, metabolic) rather than standing alone (PMIDs 42645764, 42400121, 42223330, Sep 2026; 42437564, 42423142, 42119796, Aug 2026)
- Device type (hearing aid vs cochlear implant vs bimodal) does not itself predict cognitive performance (PMID 42621042; replicated Sep 2026)
- Osteoporosis/fragility fracture associated with higher hearing loss risk; zoledronic acid and denosumab with lower risk (PMID 42574660; replicated Sep 2026)
- Dietary associations with hearing are inconsistent: flavonoids and midlife protein look favorable, seafood/omega-3s show no signal (PMIDs 42693926, 42697173, Sep 2026; 42235109, Aug 2026)

## Emerging threads
- Delivery and access, not technology, is the bottleneck: unfound hospital amplifiers, static Medicaid coverage, unmade implant referrals (PMIDs 42677253, 42172530, 42685269, 42446280)
- Self-report is an unreliable diagnostic but a useful alarm — 70% wrong about ear asymmetry; felt listening effort carries information beyond the audiogram (PMIDs 42467662, 42348455)
- Consumer device choice as its own research area: OTC preset preference over prescribed fittings, built-in step tracking, bilateral implant benefit that does not fade with age (PMIDs 42168802, 42138544, 42244467)
- Occupational noise in older workers, especially aging farmers, is largely unaddressed (PMID 42247643)
- Tinnitus appears separable from hearing loss in cognitive/brain associations (PMID 42675511) — watch for replication
- Audiometric practice refinements (dropping routine 250-Hz bone conduction) may cut unnecessary ENT referrals (PMID 42229406)
