### Study 2: How long does Medicaid home care actually last? A decade of data offers an answer
**PMID:** 42834564 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Overstated duration claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "these numbers show most older adults who start them use them for years"
- **Abstract says:** "Among older adults with 5 years of follow-up (n = 591), 25.2% ended HCBS with community discharge, 16.2% with nursing home entry, and 26.2% with death. The remaining 32.3% still used HCBS... Extended duration of HCBS use was common across our sample"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports no median duration; only 32.3% of the five-year subgroup were still enrolled at five years, so 'most ... use them for years' is an inference the abstract does not support numerically.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as 'extended use was common — about a third of those followed five years were still receiving services.'"

**Issue B. 'Norm rather than the exception'** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "know that long enrollments are the norm rather than the exception"
- **Abstract says:** "Extended duration of HCBS use was common across our sample, which may affect Medicaid's ability to fund HCBS programs."
- **Problem:** 'Common' in the abstract is softened language; 'the norm rather than the exception' implies a majority figure the abstract does not provide.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'long enrollments were common in this cohort.'"

### Study 3: Spinal fluid tests show Alzheimer's-type damage hiding inside Lewy body dementia
**PMID:** 42841041 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unquantified prevalence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "People with DLB frequently showed abnormal amyloid and tau levels"
- **Abstract says:** "Compared to controls, amyloid and tau levels were abnormal in DLB, but not in PD."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports group-level abnormality, not how many individuals were biomarker-positive; 'frequently' and later 'a substantial share' imply a proportion that is not given.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'As a group, people with DLB showed abnormal amyloid and tau levels compared with controls (the abstract does not give the share who were positive).'"

**Issue B. Causal wording for cross-sectional association** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "a substantial share of people diagnosed with Lewy body dementia also carry Alzheimer's pathology that is dragging their thinking down"
- **Abstract says:** "Amyloid and tau positivity in DLB were associated with worse global cognitive outcomes, but not with motor performance."
- **Problem:** 'Dragging their thinking down' asserts causation from an association measured in a cross-sectional biomarker comparison.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'is associated with worse global cognition.'"

**Issue C. Implied longitudinal decline** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "mixed pathology is common and tracks with faster cognitive decline rather than motor decline"
- **Abstract says:** "Amyloid and tau positivity in DLB were associated with worse global cognitive outcomes, but not with motor performance."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports worse cognitive outcomes at assessment, not a faster rate of decline over time; no longitudinal follow-up is described.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'tracks with worse cognition rather than worse motor function.'"

### Study 7: Bed alarms don't prevent falls in hospitals — and experts are now trying to phase them out
**PMID:** 42833806 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline asserts a conclusion not in the abstract** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Bed alarms don't prevent falls in hospitals — and experts are now trying to phase them out"
- **Abstract says:** "The review covers the evidence for fall prevention alarms and other fall prevention practices, factors that contribute to overuse of fall prevention alarms, and strategies that are being tested to de-implement fall prevention alarms."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not state that alarms fail to prevent falls; it only says the review examines the evidence and de-implementation strategies. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to 'Review questions the evidence behind hospital bed alarms — and looks at how to phase them out.'"

**Issue B. Unsourced harms list** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "they can increase noise, disrupt sleep, add to staff alarm fatigue and discourage patients from moving — while the evidence that they actually prevent falls is thin"
- **Abstract says:** "The review covers the evidence for fall prevention alarms and other fall prevention practices, factors that contribute to overuse... and an overview of low-value care and de-implementation science."
- **Problem:** None of these specific harms, nor the characterization of the evidence as 'thin', appear in the abstract. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute these points to the review's full text or state that the abstract does not detail specific harms."

**Issue C. Alternatives presented as evidence-supported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Ask instead about the things that do have support: a medication review, mobility assistance, toileting schedules and safe footwear"
- **Abstract says:** "The review covers the evidence for fall prevention alarms and other fall prevention practices..."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not name any specific alternative practices or say which have supporting evidence.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the review discusses 'other fall prevention practices' without listing them in the abstract."

### Study 14: Where It Hurts May Predict Where Your Mood Goes Next
**PMID:** 42749164 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Predictive language for associational finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Pain at particular body sites predicted the worse paths — finger, wrist, arm, head, and neck pain for the worsening group"
- **Abstract says:** "In fully adjusted models, the strongest associations with the Moderate-Increasing trajectory were observed for finger, wrist, arm, head, and neck pain"
- **Problem:** The multinomial regression tested associations with trajectory membership, not prospective prediction; 'predicted' implies more than the design supports.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'Pain at particular body sites was associated with the worse paths'."

### Study 15: Six Weeks of Squats Improved Balance — Brain Stimulation Added Nothing
**PMID:** 42753671 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Stimulation-alone claim not stated in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and stimulation alone did not improve balance"
- **Abstract says:** "The sham tDCS + ST group significantly improved in SOT composite scores and US with eyes closed (ECL) compared to the a-tDCS and sham tDCS at T2 and T3, with significant within-group gains from baseline."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports that the training groups outperformed the stimulation-only and sham-only groups, but never states that the a-tDCS-alone group failed to improve from baseline; this is an inference.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'the stimulation-only group did not match the training groups' improvements' rather than asserting no improvement."

**Issue B. Participant age not reported in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "participants were healthy adults, not frail or older adults, so results may not generalize to the senior population"
- **Abstract says:** "Forty-eight participants were randomized into four groups ... in healthy adults"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes the sample only as 'healthy adults' and gives no ages, so the assertion that they were not older adults is unverifiable from abstract only (though the generalizability caution itself is reasonable).
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'participants were described only as healthy adults, with no ages reported, so applicability to frail or older people is unknown'."

### Study 16: Family Doctors Get a Roadmap for Diagnosing and Treating Low Testosterone in Men
**PMID:** 42647144 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Panelist conflict characterization unverifiable** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "several panelists work in men's health clinics and industry-adjacent roles"
- **Abstract says:** "An international panel of experts- including GPs, endocrinologists, urologists, andrologists, and clinical chemists- convened in Amsterdam in November 2025 ... International multidisciplinary consensus initiative sponsored by the Androgen Society."
- **Problem:** Affiliations show men's health clinic practice for some panelists, but there is no conflict-of-interest statement in the abstract supporting 'industry-adjacent roles'. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Limit to what is visible in the affiliations ('several panelists practice in men's health clinics') or note that no conflict-of-interest statement accompanies the abstract."

### Study 17: Visual Hallucinations From Sight Loss May Be an Overlooked Cause of Falls
**PMID:** 42833804 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Added mechanism not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "it also contributes directly to falls, as people react to or navigate around hallucinated images"
- **Abstract says:** "recent work has highlighted that CBS may also lead to physical outcomes by causing and increasing susceptibility to falls"
- **Problem:** The abstract states a causal/susceptibility link but offers no mechanism; the 'react to or navigate around hallucinated images' explanation is the digest's own addition. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the mechanism clause or attribute it explicitly as a plausible explanation not detailed in the abstract."

**Issue B. Unsourced background claim in 'Why it matters'** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "are often frightened into silence, fearing a dementia diagnosis"
- **Abstract says:** "Charles Bonnet Syndrome (CBS) refers to visual hallucinations associated with a decline in vision ... awareness of CBS among sight loss and falls prevention specialists is low"
- **Problem:** Under-reporting due to fear of a dementia label is widely described in the CBS literature but is not stated in this abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute to general CBS literature or soften to 'awareness is low among specialists, so the condition is often missed'."

### Study 19: Calorie Formulas Used in Nursing Homes Are Often Wrong — and Usually Too High
**PMID:** 42759166 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. 'Pooled' implies meta-analysis** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Only four studies with 332 participants total pooled"
- **Abstract says:** "A systematic review was conducted in accordance with the PRISMA guidelines and registered in PROSPERO ... Four studies (n == 332) were included."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes a systematic review with QUADAS-2 and GRADE appraisal; no quantitative pooling/meta-analysis is reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'Only four studies with 332 participants in total were included, with no quantitative pooling'."

### Study 20: Older Adults Keep Their Eyes Glued to the Next Step — and Fall Risk May Show Up in Where They Look
**PMID:** 42753670 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Specific gaze target added** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "older adults fixated more often and for longer on immediately relevant spots like the next foot placement"
- **Abstract says:** "more and longer fixations at immediately relevant locations and less time engaging in exploratory gaze behaviors"
- **Problem:** The abstract says 'immediately relevant locations' without specifying foot placement; the headline ('Eyes Glued to the Next Step') builds on this added detail.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'immediately relevant locations such as the walking path ahead' or drop the specific example and adjust the headline accordingly."
