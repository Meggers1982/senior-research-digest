### Study 1: A blood-pressure cuff on the arm, twice a day: early trial tests a low-tech idea for protecting the aging brain
**PMID:** 42921033 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Non-significant comfort difference presented as established** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "note that participants in this study found once-daily sessions far more comfortable than twice-daily"
- **Abstract says:** "Pain/discomfort ratings were numerically higher with twice-daily RIC (median rating/10: 4.23, IQR.0.98-4.95 vs. once-daily: 1.63, IQR:1.00-3.36, P = 0.60)."
- **Problem:** The discomfort difference was numerical only and clearly non-significant; the discussion states there were "no significant differences in safety or tolerability outcomes." Presenting it as a firm finding misleads readers choosing between schedules.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to: "discomfort ratings were numerically but not statistically higher with twice-daily sessions, and focus-group participants generally favoured the once-daily schedule.""

### Study 2: When a hospital asks a court to take over your decisions, it almost always wins
**PMID:** 42929333 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Priority claim not supported by abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is among the first studies to show that who loses the most autonomy tracks with income and education rather than purely with medical need"
- **Abstract says:** "Social determinants of health (SDOH) may influence guardianship outcomes, yet data are limited."
- **Problem:** The abstract notes limited existing data but makes no claim of being among the first such studies. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "one of a small number of studies examining how social factors relate to guardianship outcomes.""

**Issue B. Comparison with medical need not measured** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "tracks with income and education rather than purely with medical need"
- **Abstract says:** "SDOH, including socioeconomic status and educational attainment, correlated with guardianship outcomes."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports correlations with Medicaid status and education; it does not report any measure of medical need or capacity severity against which these were compared, so the "rather than" contrast is an inference.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the contrast: "shows that guardianship scope and guardian type correlate with socioeconomic status and education.""

### Study 3: Metabolic syndrome looks protective for men's bones and harmful for postmenopausal women's — a sex split that complicates fracture risk
**PMID:** 42927503 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal-leaning headline for cross-sectional evidence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Metabolic syndrome looks protective for men's bones and harmful for postmenopausal women's"
- **Abstract says:** "MetS is tentatively associated with sex- and site-specific differences in BMD based on observational studies... Further research is warranted to clarify these associations"
- **Problem:** The authors describe the association as tentative and based solely on cross-sectional data; "protective" and "harmful" imply an effect on bone that the design cannot support (the digest's own caveat says no causation).
- **Suggested fix:** ""Metabolic syndrome is linked to higher bone density in men and lower density in postmenopausal women.""

**Issue B. Skeletal site slightly imprecise** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "those without metabolic syndrome had higher bone density at both the hip and spine than those with it"
- **Abstract says:** "postmenopausal women without MetS had significantly higher FN and LS BMD compared with those with MetS (FN: Z = 2.14, p = 0.03; LS: Z = 2.02, p = 0.04)"
- **Problem:** The significant result was at the femoral neck, not total hip; total hip was listed as an outcome but no significant postmenopausal total-hip result is reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "at the femoral neck and lumbar spine.""

### Study 4: Frailty, not HIV, drives bladder trouble in midlife and older adults
**PMID:** 42929288 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal verb in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Frailty, not HIV, drives bladder trouble in midlife and older adults"
- **Abstract says:** "Regression models stratified by sex evaluated the cross-sectional associations of HIV and frailty with LUTS severity... LUTS are common in people with and at-risk for HIV and are more severe among those who are frail."
- **Problem:** "Drives" implies causation from a cross-sectional design; the digest's caveat correctly says direction of effect cannot be established, so the headline contradicts it.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Frailty, not HIV, tracks with bladder trouble in midlife and older adults.""

### Study 5: Cushing's syndrome raises hip fracture risk — and getting the disease under control doesn't appear to erase it
**PMID:** 42911249 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline firmer and more causal than the abstract** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Cushing's syndrome raises hip fracture risk — and getting the disease under control doesn't appear to erase it"
- **Abstract says:** "Endogenous Cushing's syndrome may be associated with an increased hip fracture risk... (HR 1.57, 95% CI 1.0-2.5; p = 0.05)"
- **Problem:** The authors hedge with "may be associated" and a modest, borderline estimate whose confidence interval touches 1.0; "raises" asserts both causation and statistical certainty.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Cushing's syndrome is linked to a modestly higher hip fracture risk — and remission doesn't appear to erase it.""

**Issue B. Remission comparison was underpowered, not a demonstrated equivalence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "fracture rates were essentially the same in patients whose disease went into remission (3.6%) and those who didn't (4.3%)"
- **Abstract says:** "no significant difference between groups (HR 1.14, 95% CI 0.4-3.0)"
- **Problem:** The confidence interval is wide enough to include a substantial difference in either direction; "essentially the same" reads as a positive finding of no difference rather than an inconclusive one.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that the comparison was statistically inconclusive (HR 1.14, 95% CI 0.4-3.0) rather than showing equivalence."

### Study 11: Heart-health scores taken on day one predict who survives — and who walks again — after hip fracture surgery
**PMID:** 42928905 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal phrasing for prognostic cohort data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It suggests that the everyday habits and the mental health you carry into a hip fracture meaningfully shape whether you survive the year and walk again"
- **Abstract says:** "In multivariable analyses, increase in both LE8 and LC9 related to significant mortality risk reduction and improved functional recovery, independent of age, comorbidity burden, and surgical factors."
- **Problem:** "Shape whether you survive" implies the scores cause the outcomes, whereas the study reports association and discrimination only; the digest's own caveat acknowledges this.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "are associated with whether you survive the year and walk again.""

**Issue B. Self-report caveat not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "several score components rely on self-report"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract states only that LE8 and LC9 were calculated on the first day after admission; it does not describe how components were ascertained."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute to general knowledge of the LE8/LC9 instruments or drop."

### Study 12: Family carers describe dementia as 'totalising' — and say stigma, not just symptoms, shapes when they ask for help
**PMID:** 42923468 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Delayed help-seeking claim beyond abstract** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "It reframes delayed help-seeking and reluctance to consider residential care as a response to stigma and social judgment rather than as denial or poor planning"
- **Abstract says:** "Disclosure, reasoning with symptoms, and care-setting decisions were shaped by cultural, moral, and structural factors, balancing practical needs, autonomy, public responses, and emotional wellbeing."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports that decisions were shaped by cultural/moral/structural factors; it does not report findings about delayed help-seeking or contrast stigma with denial or poor planning.
- **Suggested fix:** "Limit to what the abstract supports: disclosure and care-setting decisions were shaped by cultural, moral and structural pressures alongside practical needs."

**Issue B. Unsupported weighting against clinical need** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "were driven as much by cultural and moral pressure and anticipated public reaction as by clinical need"
- **Abstract says:** "...were shaped by cultural, moral, and structural factors, balancing practical needs, autonomy, public responses, and emotional wellbeing."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes a balance of influences but never quantifies them relative to clinical need.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say these decisions were shaped by cultural, moral and structural factors alongside practical needs."

**Issue C. Specific carer behavior not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "carers in this study described hiding the diagnosis to protect the person's dignity and their family's standing"
- **Abstract says:** "Disclosure... shaped by cultural, moral, and structural factors, balancing practical needs, autonomy, public responses, and emotional wellbeing."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only — the abstract does not describe hiding the diagnosis or the stated motives.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "carers described decisions about whether to disclose the diagnosis as shaped by how others might react.""

### Study 13: In late-stage pancreatic cancer, the direction of change in weight and inflammation predicts survival better than any single snapshot
**PMID:** 42918737 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Comparative claim not tested** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "the direction of change in weight and inflammation predicts survival better than any single snapshot"
- **Abstract says:** "High ΔCAR and body weight loss were associated with shorter overall survival... ECOG PS, ΔCAR, and body weight loss were independent prognostic factors."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports that longitudinal changes are associated with survival but never compares their predictive performance against single time-point measurements.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase the headline to "changes over time in weight and inflammation track with survival" without the head-to-head comparison."

**Issue B. "Median survival" and single-institution design unverified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "had a median survival of 1.38 months, compared with 4.67 months for those with neither pattern... Retrospective, single-institution design with 120 patients"
- **Abstract says:** "those with neither had the longest survival (1.38 vs. 4.67 months)"; the abstract states only "We retrospectively analyzed 120 patients.""
- **Problem:** The abstract does not label the 1.38/4.67 figures as medians, nor does it state the study was single-institution.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "survival of 1.38 versus 4.67 months" and describe the design as retrospective without specifying the number of sites, or note it as unverifiable."

### Study 15: Medicare Advantage home visits are diagnosing more heart conditions — but far fewer patients get treated afterward
**PMID:** 42896184 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Payment claim stated as fact** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "These home visits generate higher payments to insurers"
- **Abstract says:** "Critics argue this practice increases risk-adjusted payments without improving quality."
- **Problem:** The abstract presents this as a criticism, not an established finding of the study.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "Critics argue these visits raise risk-adjusted payments to insurers.""

**Issue B. Overstated 'no treatment' framing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the conditions they turn up often don't lead to actual care — meaning a patient can be labeled with heart failure or diabetes and still walk away with no treatment"
- **Abstract says:** "Treatment rates were substantially lower after HRA-based diagnoses across all conditions."
- **Problem:** Lower treatment rates are not the same as no treatment, and the abstract notes severity could not be compared across settings.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say treatment rates after home-visit diagnoses were substantially lower than after office or hospital diagnoses."

### Study 17: Who your main caregiver is shapes end-of-life planning — but having no caregiver at all is what keeps people out of hospice
**PMID:** 42897085 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for observational survey data** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "having no caregiver at all is what keeps people out of hospice"
- **Abstract says:** "Relative to spouse/partner primary caregiving, hospice use was lower among decedents with no identified caregiver (OR, 0.46) and those receiving formal care only (OR, 0.62)."
- **Problem:** "Keeps people out" asserts causation from a cross-sectional association in survey data; the digest's caveats acknowledge causation cannot be established.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to "having no caregiver at all is linked to much lower hospice use.""

**Issue B. Reference group omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "people with no identified caregiver were far less likely to use hospice (odds ratio 0.46) as were those receiving only formal paid care (0.62)"
- **Abstract says:** "Relative to spouse/partner primary caregiving, hospice use was lower among decedents with no identified caregiver (OR, 0.46)..."
- **Problem:** The odds ratios are relative to spouse/partner caregiving, which the digest does not state, so readers may read them as comparisons with everyone else.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "compared with people whose primary caregiver was a spouse or partner.""

**Issue C. Isolation and advocacy claims beyond the data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "making social isolation, not family structure, the key risk factor for missing out on comfort-focused care... having a paid caregiver does not substitute for the advocacy an informal one provides"
- **Abstract says:** "The study categorized primary caregiver relationship type; it concludes that "caregiver availability and broader caregiving circumstances may be important to consider alongside relationship type.""
- **Problem:** Social isolation was not measured, and no mechanism involving caregiver advocacy was tested.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute to caregiver availability as the authors do, and drop the advocacy mechanism or label it as speculation."

### Study 18: Older adults get better at repeat brain tests just from practice — and how much better may itself be a clue
**PMID:** 42896150 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Improvement on all three tasks overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Scores improved measurably across all three tasks"
- **Abstract says:** "PEs were observed on all tasks, with small effects on CS (d=0.43) and GNG (d=0.32) and a below-threshold effect on SRT (d=0.16)."
- **Problem:** The simple reaction time task showed a below-threshold effect, so "improved measurably" overstates it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that gains were small on the two harder tasks and below threshold on the simple reaction time task."
