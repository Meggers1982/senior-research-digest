### Study 2: Older adults may stick with daily smartphone brain tests better than younger people do
**PMID:** 42719037 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Overstated comparison with clinic testing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Short, repeated tests on a personal phone can capture cognitive change at least as well as a long clinic visit"
- **Abstract says:** "A composite of the remote tests slightly outperformed a traditional composite at separating mutation carriers from non-carriers."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports discrimination between carriers and non-carriers at baseline, not the capture of cognitive change over time; the study had only one week of baseline testing.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'distinguished gene carriers from non-carriers slightly better than traditional in-clinic testing did' rather than 'capture cognitive change'."

**Issue B. Adherence comparison stated more firmly than abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "older participants have historically stuck with these brief daily check-ins better than this young cohort did"
- **Abstract says:** "Average adherence to the study protocol was 42%, lower that observed in previous studies of older adults."
- **Problem:** The abstract makes a single passing comparison to prior studies without quantifying it; the digest's story angle presents it as an established historical pattern. The Caveats do note this, so the issue is mainly one of emphasis.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to 'the authors note adherence here was lower than in prior studies that enrolled older adults'."

### Study 5: First randomized trial of brain stimulation for a rare Parkinson's-like disease shows real movement gains
**PMID:** 42716220 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "First randomized trial" claim unverifiable** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "First randomized trial of brain stimulation for a rare Parkinson's-like disease shows real movement gains"
- **Abstract says:** "We conducted a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of tACS in MSA-C. ... No disease-modifying treatments exist for the cerebellar subtype of multiple system atrophy (MSA-C)."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not claim this is the first randomized trial of brain stimulation in MSA-C. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop 'First' or attribute it ('the authors report no disease-modifying treatments currently exist')."

**Issue B. Single-center attribution** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Single-center trial in China"
- **Abstract says:** "Author affiliations are all at The First Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University and related Fujian institutions; the abstract itself does not state the number of sites."
- **Problem:** Single-center status is inferred from affiliations, not stated in the abstract. Reasonable but not verifiable from the abstract text.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note it as 'conducted at a Chinese university hospital' or mark as inferred."

### Study 6: The more social roles you hold, the lower your inflammation — and the pattern is a slope, not a switch
**PMID:** 42716327 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Claim that effect was strongest at the isolated end** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "the effect appeared strongest at the isolated end where a single new connection counts most"
- **Abstract says:** "compared to the most isolated group, increasingly integrated groups showed progressively stronger inverse effect sizes with inflammation (b=-.04, p=.017)"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes progressively stronger inverse associations as integration increases — i.e., strongest at the most integrated end relative to the most isolated reference group — which is the opposite of the digest's claim that the effect was strongest at the isolated end.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite to 'associations grew progressively stronger the more integrated the group, compared with the most isolated group' and delete the claim about a single connection counting most."

### Study 7: Statins linked to slower frailty accumulation in people aging with HIV
**PMID:** 42717432 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline wording vs. reported direction** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Statins linked to slower frailty accumulation in people aging with HIV"
- **Abstract says:** "statin use was associated with a reduction in FI over time (statin*time interaction β=-2.562, 95%-CI -3.176,-1.987)"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a reduction in frailty index over time, which the digest body correctly describes as 'a decline in frailty index scores'; the headline's 'slower accumulation' understates (or mischaracterizes) an actual decline. Minor, since 'people aging with HIV' here have a median age of 44.7.
- **Suggested fix:** "Consider 'Statins linked to improving frailty scores in people with HIV' and note the cohort's median age in the headline area."

### Study 10: Ear acupuncture cut knee pain and lifted mood in older women, sham-controlled trial finds
**PMID:** 42711151 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Nurse-delivered protocol not stated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a weekly, 10-week nurse-delivered protocol with no reported adverse events"
- **Abstract says:** "100 women aged ≥60 years with chronic knee pain were randomized to receive AA (n = 50) or sham auricular stimulation at a neutral point, once weekly for 10 weeks."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not say who delivered the intervention (the journal is a nursing journal, but that is not evidence of delivery personnel). Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop 'nurse-delivered' or replace with 'a weekly, 10-week clinician-delivered protocol'."

**Issue B. Population described as osteoarthritis pain** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "100 women aged 60 and older with chronic knee osteoarthritis pain"
- **Abstract says:** "100 women aged ≥60 years with chronic knee pain were randomized ... older women with knee osteoarthritis"
- **Problem:** The methods specify eligibility by chronic knee pain, while the title and purpose refer to knee osteoarthritis; the digest merges the two. A small precision issue rather than an error.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'women aged 60 and older with chronic knee pain attributed to osteoarthritis'."

### Study 16: In Saudi Arabia, dementia is being missed — stigma and low awareness delay diagnosis while families shoulder the care
**PMID:** 42706511 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Extrapolation beyond the study population contradicts caveat** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "a pattern that echoes in immigrant and minority communities in Western countries too"
- **Abstract says:** "To synthesize evidence on dementia epidemiology, risk factors, awareness, comorbidities, caregiving challenges, and research trends in Saudi Arabia."
- **Problem:** The review covers only Saudi populations and offers no data on immigrant or minority communities elsewhere; the digest's own Caveats state findings "should not be extrapolated to other populations," so the framing contradicts the stated limitation.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the claim or mark it explicitly as a hypothesis, e.g. "a dynamic that clinicians say may be worth exploring in other family-centred care cultures, though this review did not study them.""

**Issue B. Research output figure not supported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Research output more than doubled after 2020 but remained methodologically inconsistent with little rural representation."
- **Abstract says:** "Research activity increased after 2020, with over half of the included studies published between 2020 and 2024; however, methodological inconsistencies and limited rural representation remain."
- **Problem:** "More than half of studies published 2020–2024" is not the same as output "more than doubling"; the abstract gives no growth multiple.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "More than half of the included studies were published between 2020 and 2024, though the work remained methodologically inconsistent...""

**Issue C. "Almost entirely" family-based** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "caregiving was almost entirely family-based and linked to substantial burden"
- **Abstract says:** "Caregiving was family-based and associated with significant burden."
- **Problem:** The abstract characterizes caregiving as family-based without quantifying it; "almost entirely" adds a degree of certainty not present.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "caregiving was family-based and linked to substantial burden.""

### Study 22: Mindfulness helps anxiety and mood in Parkinson's — but the balance and falls evidence isn't there
**PMID:** 42704301 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Pooled" implies meta-analysis** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Reviewers pooled findings from 14 studies — nine randomized controlled trials and five before-and-after studies — on mindfulness meditation for Parkinson's disease."
- **Abstract says:** "This systematic review examined mindfulness meditation effects on PD motor, cognitive, falls-related ... Fourteen studies met inclusion criteria: nine randomized controlled trials and five pre-post design studies."
- **Problem:** The review was narrative with no quantitative pooling; the body's "pooled" wording conflicts with the digest's own caveat that it was a "systematic review without meta-analysis."
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Reviewers synthesized findings from 14 studies ... " or "reviewed, without pooling, 14 studies.""

### Study 24: Half of urinary tract infections diagnosed in the ER weren't infections at all — and a hospital stay for simple cystitis predicted a move to long-term care
**PMID:** 42731025 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline overstates culture-negative as "not an infection"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Half of urinary tract infections diagnosed in the ER weren't infections at all"
- **Abstract says:** "Of 658 ED-diagnosed UTIs, only 354 (54%) had significant bacterial growth in urine cultures. ... In ED UTIs are frequently overdiagnosed in older adults"

### Study 26: Education and mentally demanding work predicted better everyday functioning — in people whose thinking tested completely normal
**PMID:** 42730663 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline attributes effect to occupational complexity** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Education and mentally demanding work predicted better everyday functioning"
- **Abstract says:** "Among CR domains, education showed the most consistent association with lower impairment across all ADL domains and was indirectly linked to better QoL through improved function."
- **Problem:** The abstract singles out education; it does not report that occupational complexity independently predicted better function, so naming "mentally demanding work" in the headline gives it unearned billing.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "A cognitive reserve score — led by education — predicted better everyday functioning.""

**Issue B. Caveat details not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Volunteer online cohort that is heavily female (81%), likely well-educated and Canadian, so results may not generalize. Functional ability was assessed by self-report"
- **Abstract says:** "Baseline data from 1,571 CU participants (mean age: 64.7±7.6; 81.3% female) from the CAN-PROTECT study were analyzed. ... Functional ability was measured using the Standard Assessment of Global Everyday Activities (SAGEA) scale"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not state that the cohort was recruited online/by volunteers or that SAGEA was self-reported. Unverifiable from abstract only (the sex split and Canadian setting are supported).
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute these design details to the full paper or drop the "online volunteer" and "self-report" descriptors."

### Study 27: What wears dementia caregivers down most isn't memory loss — it's rigidity and apathy
**PMID:** 42731983 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Effect sizes are small** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "those were the top drivers of burden"
- **Abstract says:** "Behavioural rigidity (standardised β = 0.15, 95% CI [0.03, 0.28], p = 0.015) and apathy (standardised β = 0.14, 95% CI [0.02, 0.26], p = 0.020) showed the strongest associations with carer burden overall."
- **Problem:** "Strongest associations" in the abstract are modest standardized betas around 0.15; calling them "top drivers" implies a larger causal contribution than the data show.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "those showed the strongest, though modest, statistical links to burden.""

**Issue B. Single-center description unverifiable** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Single specialist center in Sydney; only 172 participants had follow-up data over roughly one year."
- **Abstract says:** "Author affiliations span the Brain and Mind Centre, The University of Sydney, Macquarie University Health, Gosford Hospital, and Royal Prince Alfred Hospital; ethical approval from South Eastern Sydney Local Health District, UNSW and University of Sydney."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not state that recruitment came from a single center, and multiple Sydney-area sites are listed. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "specialist clinic cohort in Sydney, Australia" unless single-site recruitment is confirmed in the full text."

### Study 28: Amino acid supplements for muscle loss may backfire in older adults carrying extra weight
**PMID:** 42727175 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies supplements were tested** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Amino acid supplements for muscle loss may backfire in older adults carrying extra weight"
- **Abstract says:** "Serum BCAAs and AAAs were quantified at baseline. ... These distinct profiles, alongside dietary correlates, caution against uniform AA supplementation and suggest phenotype-specific nutritional strategies in older adults."
- **Problem:** The study measured circulating amino acid levels, not supplementation; "supplements may backfire" goes further than the authors' caution against uniform supplementation, though the digest's caveat does note no supplement was tested.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "Blood amino acid levels point in opposite directions for muscle loss depending on body fat — authors caution against blanket supplementation.""
