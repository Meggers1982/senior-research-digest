**Primary audience:** older adults and seniors | **Secondary audience:** families, caregivers, and senior living professionals

### Study 1: Poor Sleep Before Heart Valve Surgery Doubles the Odds of Post-Op Confusion
**PMID:** 42665464 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies causation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Poor Sleep Before Heart Valve Surgery Doubles the Odds of Post-Op Confusion"
- **Abstract says:** "each 10% decrease in presurgical sleep efficiency was associated with more than double the odds of delirium (OR 2.16, 95% CI [1.10, 3.71])"
- **Problem:** The headline states a flat causal-sounding effect ("Doubles the Odds") for an observational association that was measured per 10% decrease in sleep efficiency, not for "poor sleep" as a category. The body text is correctly hedged, but the headline is not.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to e.g. "Poor Sleep Before Heart Valve Surgery Linked to Doubled Odds of Post-Op Confusion.""

**Issue B. Unsupported timing detail** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "measurable sleep problems in the weeks before an operation may flag who is most vulnerable"
- **Abstract says:** "Before surgery, we assessed sleep using self-report, ambulatory polysomnography, and actigraphy."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not state how far in advance of surgery sleep was measured, so "in the weeks before" is unverifiable from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "sleep problems measured before an operation" without specifying a time window."

### Study 3: Acupuncture and Massage Both Eased Insomnia in Advanced Cancer — With No Clear Winner
**PMID:** 42471129 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Timepoint attribution for pain-to-sleep association** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "people whose pain improved saw bigger sleep gains, and people whose sleep improved saw bigger pain reductions at both 10 and 26 weeks"
- **Abstract says:** "Pain responders had greater improvements in insomnia at week 10 (Δ = -2.97; P = 0.0003), although this association was attenuated by week 26. Insomnia responders showed greater reductions in pain at weeks 10 and 26 (both P < 0.0001)."
- **Problem:** The sentence can be read as applying "at both 10 and 26 weeks" to both directions, but the pain-responder-to-sleep association weakened by week 26.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the qualifier: "...people whose pain improved saw bigger sleep gains at week 10 (an association that faded by week 26), and people whose sleep improved saw bigger pain reductions at both 10 and 26 weeks.""

**Issue B. Why-it-matters goes beyond tested comparison** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and that treating pain and sleep together may be more effective than tackling either alone"
- **Abstract says:** "Sleep and pain showed a bidirectional relationship, supporting integrative approaches targeting symptom clusters."
- **Problem:** The trial did not compare combined versus single-symptom treatment; it observed correlated improvement. The comparative-effectiveness claim is not tested.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "and that pain and sleep improve together, which the authors say supports addressing them as a symptom cluster.""

### Study 4: Non-Hormonal Hot Flash Drugs Also Improved Sleep in Menopausal Women, Review Finds
**PMID:** 42837121 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Drug-manufacturer link not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "One author's institution receives research funding from Bayer, which manufactures elinzanetant"
- **Abstract says:** "A.M.K.'s employer, The University of Florida College of Medicine, Jacksonville, receives research funding from Bayer. His institution also received past research funding from Mylan. He has an ongoing financial relationship with Estetra."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not state that Bayer makes elinzanetant; that connection is unverifiable from the abstract only (though the funding disclosure itself is correctly reported).
- **Suggested fix:** "Either drop the manufacturer clause or attribute it to an outside source."

**Issue B. Population vs stated audience** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**To older adults and seniors:** If night sweats are wrecking your sleep and hormone therapy isn't an option for you, ask your clinician about neurokinin receptor antagonists."
- **Abstract says:** "Eligible studies enrolled perimenopausal or postmenopausal women with moderate to severe VMS"
- **Problem:** The pooled trials enrolled perimenopausal and postmenopausal women, many of whom are in midlife rather than older adulthood; the caveats note symptom severity but not that the evidence base is not specific to older adults.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add to Caveats that participants were perimenopausal/postmenopausal women with moderate-to-severe VMS and that age-specific data for older women are not reported."

### Study 5: Sedative Prescriptions After Hip Fracture Have Plummeted in Ontario — But Opioids Haven't
**PMID:** 42852585 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Survivorship inclusion criterion misstated** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "the cohort excluded people who died within roughly three months, which may understate risk"
- **Abstract says:** "The cohort included individuals aged 66 years and older ... admitted for an acute hip fracture between April 1, 2008, and March 31, 2024, and who survived for at least 275 days in the following year."
- **Problem:** Patients had to survive at least 275 days (about nine months) of the following year, so anyone dying before then was excluded — a much larger survivorship restriction than "roughly three months," and the digest therefore understates the selection bias it is trying to flag.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "the cohort was limited to people who survived at least 275 days (about nine months) after the fracture, excluding the sickest patients.""

### Study 9: Opening a Window: An Everyday Habit Linked to Better Mood in Older Adults
**PMID:** 42860785 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Sleep duration described as sleep quality** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this large study found more frequent window ventilation went along with better reported sleep and fewer depressive symptoms"
- **Abstract says:** "Serial mediation models were applied to investigate if self-reported total sleep duration and psychological resilience mediated the association between window ventilation frequency and depressive symptoms."
- **Problem:** The study measured self-reported total sleep duration, not sleep quality; "better reported sleep" implies a quality measure that was not assessed.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "went along with longer self-reported sleep duration and fewer depressive symptoms.""

### Study 12: Less REM sleep linked to faster buildup of Alzheimer's-related protein in healthy older adults
**PMID:** 42851166 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Exploratory subgroup presented as a main result** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and the link was strongest among those already amyloid-positive"
- **Abstract says:** "In exploratory analyses, the association between low REM sleep percentage and faster decline in Aβ42/40 ratio was stronger in the amyloid-positive group."
- **Problem:** The digest reports the amyloid-positive subgroup finding as settled without noting the authors flagged it as exploratory; the caveats section also omits this.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add 'in exploratory analyses' to the body sentence or note it among the caveats."

**Issue B. Null longitudinal SWA result not stated in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Slow-wave activity in frontal regions tracked with amyloid levels at a single point in time but not with change over time."
- **Abstract says:** "Frontal slow-wave activity (SWA) was inversely associated with plasma Aβ42/40 at cross-section ... In comparison, low rapid eye movement (REM) sleep percentage was associated with a large longitudinal decline in annualized blood-based Aβ42/40."
- **Problem:** The abstract contrasts cross-sectional SWA with longitudinal REM but never explicitly reports a null longitudinal SWA result; this is an inference. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to 'frontal slow-wave activity was related to amyloid levels at a single point in time, while REM percentage was what predicted change over time.'"

### Study 14: Telephone counseling by trained lay volunteers eased loneliness — and improved sleep — in older adults a year later
**PMID:** 42850158 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. 'Volunteers' not supported by abstract** — Severity: Minor

### Study 15: In people with depression late in life, poor sleep and the APOE4 gene together track with more Alzheimer's plaque
**PMID:** 42857391 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Exploratory analysis not labelled** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this study also found longer periods of untreated depression tracked with more amyloid in ε4 carriers"
- **Abstract says:** "Exploratory analyses demonstrated APOE ɛ4 also moderated the effect of lifetime duration of untreated depression on increased Aβ."
- **Problem:** The digest presents an explicitly exploratory secondary finding alongside the main results without noting its status, in a story angle aimed directly at patients.

### Study 16: Too Little or Too Much Sleep Both Track With Weaker Thinking Skills in Older Couples
**PMID:** 42755049 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal phrasing for an observational association** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this study found that a person's own sleep, not their spouse's, drove the cognitive association"
- **Abstract says:** "Own nocturnal sleep duration shows a robust, symmetric inverted U-shaped association with cognitive function in both spouses. The possible husband-to-wife partner effect did not survive correction for multiple testing..."
- **Problem:** 'Drove' implies causation in a longitudinal observational dyadic analysis; the abstract describes associations only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as 'a person's own sleep, not their spouse's, was what tracked with cognitive scores.'"

### Study 17: The More Hours You Use CPAP After a Stroke, the Better Your Mood May Be
**PMID:** 42800142 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. 'Well above' the adherence threshold overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with a meaningful difference emerging at roughly 31 hours of use per week — well above the conventional four-hours-a-night threshold"
- **Abstract says:** "Model-based predictions indicated that the estimated change in depressive symptoms became statistically different from no change at approximately 31 h of CPAP use per week ... suggesting benefits beyond a conventional CPAP adherence threshold."
- **Problem:** 31 hours per week is only modestly above 4 hours every night (28 hours); 'well above' exaggerates the gap, though the abstract does say 'beyond a conventional threshold.'
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'above the conventional four-hours-a-night threshold.'"

**Issue B. Disclosures attributed to 'several authors'** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "several authors report extensive pharmaceutical industry consulting and equipment support (Takeda, Jazz, Eisai, Zoll Itamar and others)"
- **Abstract says:** "Dr. Boulos reports consulting fees and honoraria from Sentrex, Axsome, Takeda, Jazz Pharmaceuticals, Paladin Labs, Eisai, Precision AQ, Lundbeck; and receipt of sleep equipment from Zoll Itamar and CGX ... All other authors, declare that they have no known competing financial interests."
- **Problem:** Only the senior author reports industry relationships; all other authors declare none.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'the senior author reports extensive industry consulting and equipment support.'"

### Study 18: Mood and Sleep Problems Cluster Together in Parkinson's — and Affect Nearly Half of Patients
**PMID:** 42579898 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Odds ratios described as 'times more likely'** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "restless legs was four times more likely, sleep apnea more than twice as likely"
- **Abstract says:** "sleep apnea (OR 2.58, 95% CI 1.47-4.92), and restless leg syndrome (RLS; OR 4.12, 95% CI 1.81-12.1) were more common in patients with Parkinson's than in controls"
- **Problem:** These are odds ratios, which overstate relative risk, and the RLS estimate has a very wide confidence interval (1.81-12.1) that the digest does not convey.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'about four times the odds (though with a wide confidence interval)' and 'roughly 2.5 times the odds.'"

### Study 19: Experimental Pill Dramatically Restored Wakefulness in Narcolepsy Trial
**PMID:** 42636845 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Hype in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Experimental Pill Dramatically Restored Wakefulness in Narcolepsy Trial"
- **Abstract says:** "In this phase 2 trial, once-daily oral alixorexton provided clinically meaningful improvements at 6 weeks for participants with narcolepsy type 1, including in wakefulness, excessive daytime sleepiness, and cataplexy."
- **Problem:** 'Dramatically restored' is stronger than the abstract's 'clinically meaningful improvements' for a six-week phase 2 trial, though the effect size is large.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to 'Experimental Pill Sharply Improved Wakefulness in Early Narcolepsy Trial.'"

**Issue B. 'Near the test's ceiling' not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "participants on the drug stayed awake 22 to 26 minutes longer than placebo — near the test's ceiling"
- **Abstract says:** "the observed MSL on the MWT was 2·3 min (SD 2·7) for placebo, 24·0 min (8·7) for alixorexton 4 mg, 25·9 min (9·4) for 6 mg, and 28·2 min (11·4) for 8 mg"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not report the MWT protocol's maximum duration, so the claim that results approached the ceiling cannot be checked. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the ceiling phrase or state the observed mean sleep latencies (24-28 minutes versus 2.3 on placebo)."

### Study 20: What Actually Works for Hot Flashes — and Which Treatments Also Improve Sleep
**PMID:** 42832800 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Comparative-effectiveness claim not supported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "hormone therapy remains the most effective for symptom severity, but several non-hormonal drugs improved sleep quality too"
- **Abstract says:** "Compared with placebo, estrogens with or without progestogens, conjugated estrogen with bazedoxifene (CE/BZA), and oxybutynin reduced VMS frequency and severity ... Little comparative information exists."
- **Problem:** The review was placebo-controlled with little head-to-head data, and oxybutynin and CE/BZA also reduced severity, so ranking estrogens as 'the most effective' is not established by this evidence.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as 'hormone therapy was among the few options that reduced symptom severity as well as frequency, though the review found little direct head-to-head comparison.'"

### Study 21: Older adults who sleep 8 hours move more — and grip harder
**PMID:** 42821756 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "per night" vs "per day"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Those sleeping 8 or more hours a night logged significantly more moderate-intensity, vigorous-intensity, and combined moderate-to-vigorous activity than those sleeping less, in both men and women."
- **Abstract says:** "...were significantly higher among participants with ≥8 hours of sleep per day than among those with <8 hours."
- **Problem:** The abstract's exposure is total sleep per day (which in KNHANES can include napping), not nighttime sleep alone; the digest narrows it to "a night."
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Those sleeping 8 or more hours a day..." and keep the headline framing as daily sleep duration."

**Issue B. Causal speculation from cross-sectional data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "so a short night may be both a cause and a symptom of a less active day"
- **Abstract says:** "Cross-sectional analysis of a single survey wave; results are reported as significant differences, odds ratios, and a correlation (r = .177), with no temporal or causal claim."
- **Problem:** Introduces cause-and-effect speculation that the single-survey design cannot support, even though it is hedged and the caveat flags the limitation.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "so short sleep and a less active day tend to occur together, though this study cannot say which comes first.""
