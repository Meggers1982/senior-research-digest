## Study 1: Eating More Flavonoid-Rich Foods Linked to Lower Risk of Hearing Loss
**PMID:** 42235109 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. Framing is consistent with the abstract. The 16% lower risk (HR 0.84), sample size (55,859), follow-up period (~11 years), UK Biobank population, and CRP mediation are all accurately reported. Appropriate observational caveats are included.

## Study 2: Hearing Aids May Weaken the Link Between Hearing Loss and Frailty
**PMID:** 42423142 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Frailty increase percentage** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "non–hearing aid users showed a nearly 18% increase in frailty for every 10-decibel worsening in hearing."
- **Abstract says:** "For non-hearing aid users, each 10 dB increase in hearing threshold corresponded to a 17.9% (95% CI: 1.9, 36.5%) higher frailty index score."
- **Problem:** "Nearly 18%" is acceptable rounding of 17.9%, but the abstract says "frailty index score" increase, not "increase in frailty" generally. This is a subtle but acceptable simplification.
- **Suggested fix:** No revision required; the simplification is reasonable for lay audiences.

No other issues found. The "essentially disappeared" framing for hearing aid users is supported by the -4.2% null result.

## Study 22: Hearing Decline Predicts Cognitive Trajectory and Brain Changes in Healthy Older Men
**PMID:** 42591333 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Industry employment disclosure** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** Caveats state "one author employed by hearing device company"
- **Abstract says:** "Authors RN and EN were employed by Oticon A/S"
- **Problem:** Two authors (not one) were employed by the hearing device company.
- **Suggested fix:** Change to "two authors employed by hearing device company (Oticon A/S)"

All other details (N=51, male cohort, Danish birth cohort, age 70-71, temporal lobe/cerebellum findings) are accurate.

## Study 25: Hearing Loss Is a Modifiable Risk Factor in High-Risk Parkinson's Disease Combinations
**PMID:** 42581680 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. PAF percentage phrasing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Removing these modifiable exposures could theoretically reduce Parkinson's incidence by over 30%."
- **Abstract says:** "removal of modifiable risk factors accounted for an estimated 34.4% reduction in LOPD incidence"
- **Problem:** The digest says "over 30%" which is technically accurate but less precise than the actual 34.4% figure.
- **Suggested fix:** No revision required; "over 30%" is accurate and appropriately cautious for lay audiences.

All other details (311,000+ participants, UK Biobank, 15-year follow-up, 18 risk factors) are accurate.
