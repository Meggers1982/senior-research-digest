# Fact-Check Report

### Study 1: Taking Multiple Medications Linked to Higher Fall Injury Risk — But Not All Drugs Are Equal
**PMID:** 42343007 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Incomplete reporting of FRID classes** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "...such as opioids, antidepressants, anticonvulsants, antihistamines, and muscle relaxants..."
- **Abstract says:** The abstract mentions opioids and antidepressants specifically with risk data but does not enumerate the full list of FRIDs included.
- **Problem:** The digest lists anticonvulsants, antihistamines, and muscle relaxants as examples, but these specific classes are not mentioned in the abstract. While these are commonly included in FRID definitions, they cannot be verified from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "...fall-risk-increasing drugs (FRIDs), including opioids and antidepressants..." or flag as drawn from standard FRID definitions rather than the abstract.

**Issue B. Missing key finding on exposure timing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** Digest does not mention exposure duration/recency findings.
- **Abstract says:** "Prolonged fall-risk-increasing drug exposure (>9 months; aOR 1.69) and current exposure (within 30 days before hospitalisation; aOR 1.78) showed higher odds."
- **Problem:** The abstract emphasizes that duration and recency of FRID exposure significantly affected risk, which is clinically relevant information omitted from the digest.
- **Suggested fix:** Add to the study description: "Risk was particularly elevated with prolonged use (>9 months) and current exposure (within 30 days of hospitalization)."

**Issue C. Antihypertensive finding not directly supported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "...when researchers separated blood pressure medications from other FRIDs, they found antihypertensives alone showed no significant link to fall injuries..."
- **Abstract says:** The abstract does not mention antihypertensives or blood pressure medications at all.
- **Problem:** This finding cannot be verified from the abstract provided.
- **Suggested fix:** Flag as "Unverifiable from abstract only" or remove this claim unless full text confirms.

### Study 5: Half of Older Adults Worldwide Take Five or More Medications — And the Rate Is Rising
**PMID:** 42412515 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Dose-response statistics slightly simplified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "5% increased mortality risk, 2% increased emergency visits, and 4% increased hospitalizations per medication added"
- **Abstract says:** "mortality (HR: 1.05, 95%CI: 1.03-1.06), emergency (HR: 1.02, 95%CI: 1.02-1.03), and hospital admission (HR: 1.04, 95%CI: 1.02-1.07)"
- **Problem:** The digest presents these as percentages, which is a reasonable interpretation of hazard ratios, but technically HR 1.05 means 5% higher hazard, not exactly "5% increased risk." This is a minor simplification acceptable for a lay audience.
- **Suggested fix:** No change needed; interpretation is reasonable for the stated audience.

No other issues found. Study count (545), participant count (16.6 million), country count (56), and prevalence (50.4%) all match.

### Study 10: New Guidelines Needed: Current Advice for Polypharmacy Falls Short on Implementation
**PMID:** 41679655 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Reporting percentages need clarification** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "guidelines scored well on stating their purpose (82%) and presenting recommendations clearly (61%), but poorly on methodological rigor (17%) and practical applicability (8%)"
- **Abstract says:** "Scope and Purpose (81.9 %) and Clarity of Presentation (61.1 %) demonstrated the highest median scores, whereas Rigor of Development (16.7 %) and Applicability (8.3 %) scored the lowest"
- **Problem:** Minor rounding differences (81.9% vs 82%, 16.7% vs 17%, 8.3% vs 8%). Acceptable.

**Issue B. Reporting completeness figure** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Only 43% of recommended content was properly reported"
- **Abstract says:** "overall reporting completeness was 43.2%"
- **Problem:** Accurate.

**Issue C. Patient preferences percentage** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "only 14% of guidelines fully addressed patient preferences and long-term monitoring"
- **Abstract says:** "far fewer incorporated patient preferences (n = 9, 42.9%) or monitoring strategies (n = 9, 42.9%)" and "Only three guidelines (14.3%) fully adhered to all five steps of Ariadne principles"
- **Problem:** The 14% refers to Ariadne principle adherence, not specifically "patient preferences and long-term monitoring." This conflates two different findings.
- **Suggested fix:** "Only 14% of guidelines fully adhered to all five recommended steps for managing polypharmacy, and fewer than half addressed patient preferences or monitoring strategies."

### Study 15: Cardiovascular Polypharmacy Linked to Kidney Problems in Primary Care Patients
**PMID:** 42056610 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Age comparison finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Adults 80+ had over 8 times higher odds of cardiovascular polypharmacy compared to younger patients"
- **Abstract says:** "Older adults (≥80 years) had higher odds of CVD-related polypharmacy (OR 8.17, 95% CI 5.47-12.21)"
- **Problem:** The abstract doesn't specify "compared to younger patients" as the reference group, though this is implied. Acceptable interpretation.

**Issue B. Kidney dysfunction OR** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "45% higher odds of kidney dysfunction"
- **Abstract says:** "CVD-related polypharmacy was significantly associated with kidney dysfunction (OR 1.45, 95% CI 1.30-1.62)"
- **Problem:** Accurate representation of OR 1.45 as "45% higher odds."

All other statistics (N=13,568, median age 63, 54% male, one-third with CV polypharmacy) match.
