### Study 2: When Older Patients Say Their Thinking Is Off After Surgery, They're Often Right — But Not Always
**PMID:** 42855369 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Single-center" not stated in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Single-center study of elective noncardiac surgery patients; findings may not generalize to emergency or cardiac surgery."
- **Abstract says:** "Prospective cohort study. PARTICIPANTS: Patients aged ≥65 years undergoing elective noncardiac surgery. (Authors affiliated with UCSF, Virginia Tech, Rosalind Franklin University; no study-site count given.)"
- **Problem:** The abstract never states the study was conducted at a single center; this is inferred from author affiliations. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "conducted by a single academic perioperative research group" or drop the single-center descriptor unless confirmed in the full text."

**Issue B. Specificity slightly overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with high specificity but low sensitivity"
- **Abstract says:** "Discrimination was poor to moderate (AUC 0.52-0.66), with generally higher specificity but low sensitivity."
- **Problem:** "High specificity" is firmer than the abstract's "generally higher specificity," which is a relative, hedged statement.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "with generally higher specificity but low sensitivity.""

### Study 3: Hammertoes Don't Just Hurt — They Cut Push-Off Power Nearly in Half
**PMID:** 42766945 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline verb for cross-sectional data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Hammertoes Don't Just Hurt — They Cut Push-Off Power Nearly in Half"
- **Abstract says:** "In this cross-sectional observational study... Hammertoe deformity was associated with a distal-to-proximal redistribution of mechanical work during gait"
- **Problem:** "They Cut" asserts causation from a cross-sectional between-group comparison; the abstract consistently uses "associated with." The body and caveats are appropriately hedged, so this is confined to the headline. The abstract also does not report pain, so "Don't Just Hurt" is outside the data.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like "Hammertoes Linked to Sharply Lower Push-Off Power"."

**Issue B. "Nearly in half" understates the figure** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "They Cut Push-Off Power Nearly in Half"
- **Abstract says:** "a 58.7% reduction in ankle push-off power"
- **Problem:** A 58.7% reduction is more than half, not "nearly" half.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "by nearly 60%" or "by more than half.""

### Study 4: Palliative Care Consults in the Hospital Cut Costs and Boosted Follow-Up Care for Medicare Advantage Members
**PMID:** 42773642 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "A further 147% increase" misreads the referral data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "drove a 650% jump in referrals to community-based palliative care in 2022 and a further 147% increase in 2023 over 2021 levels"
- **Abstract says:** "referrals to community-based palliative care dramatically increased, with a 650% rise in 2022 and a 147% increase in 2023 compared to 2021."
- **Problem:** Both percentages are measured against the same 2021 baseline; "a further" implies the 2023 rise was additional to the 650%, when in fact 2023 referrals were lower relative to baseline than 2022.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "a 650% rise in 2022 and a 147% rise in 2023, both compared with 2021.""

**Issue B. Causal headline for retrospective matched analysis** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Palliative Care Consults in the Hospital Cut Costs and Boosted Follow-Up Care"
- **Abstract says:** "Retrospective cohort analysis... Patients were propensity score matched... These findings suggest that incentive-based programs... can effectively align financial incentives with improved palliative care delivery."
- **Problem:** "Cut" and "Boosted" assert causation from a retrospective propensity-matched claims analysis; the abstract uses "suggest." The caveats do flag non-causality, so the issue is limited to headline framing.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "Palliative Care Consults Linked to Lower Costs and More Community Referrals.""

### Study 6: Repeated Mild Head Injuries Appear to Speed Up Brain Aging in Veterans
**PMID:** 42832957 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Adjustment for comorbidities not confirmed in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Crucially, the analysis separated mild TBI effects from common co-occurring conditions such as PTSD, mood disorders, and alcohol misuse."
- **Abstract says:** "These studies provide important insights, yet were limited in their ability to differentiate mTBI effects from those of common comorbid conditions... Associations between age, mTBI burden, and WM integrity... were analyzed using multivariate regression of whole-tract means and along-tract effect clustering"
- **Problem:** The abstract frames separating mTBI from comorbid conditions as a gap in prior work and does not state which covariates were included in this analysis. The digest presents the separation as a demonstrated feature of the results.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "The study set out to distinguish mild TBI effects from common co-occurring conditions such as PTSD, mood disorders and alcohol misuse" — or mark as unverifiable from the abstract."

### Study 10: Why Older Walkers Stride Differently Isn't About Speed, Height, or Weight
**PMID:** 42759222 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Age × cadence interaction omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Even after matching on body size and walking speed, age group still shaped stride-length variability"
- **Abstract says:** "Model fitting identified cross-level interactions between age group and cadence and between walking direction (clockwise or counterclockwise) and side (left or right leg)... Only the age group main effect influenced stride length LRCs."
- **Problem:** The paper's titular finding — that cadence and age interact to determine long-range correlations in stride intervals — is not mentioned, leaving readers with an incomplete picture of what the model found.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a clause noting that for stride-interval variability, age interacted with cadence (step rate), while stride-length variability showed only an age effect."

**Issue B. "Proven" overstates outside evidence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The practical takeaway remains the proven one — balance and strength training, not just speed, is what protects you from falls."
- **Abstract says:** "(No intervention, no fall outcomes; the study examined long-range correlations in stride intervals and stride lengths in healthy adults.)"
- **Problem:** "Proven" is hype language and the claim is not supported by this study at all; it is imported external context presented as a takeaway from the research.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as "the practical advice is unchanged: balance and strength training, supported by separate trial evidence, is what reduces fall risk.""

### Study 11: Big-data model sharpens survival estimates for men with lymph node-positive prostate cancer
**PMID:** 42690326 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Model accuracy presented as sharper than reported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a Random Survival Forest model performed best, separating patients into clearly distinct risk groups"
- **Abstract says:** "Random Survival Forest (RSF) outperformed all models, achieving the highest C-index (0.697 internal; 0.740 temporal validation). RSF-stratified risk groups showed significant survival differences (P < 0.001)."
- **Problem:** The headline ("sharpens survival estimates") and the "Why it matters" promise of "a more individualized number" imply strong predictive accuracy, but a C-index of 0.697-0.740 is moderate discrimination. Readers are not given the number to judge for themselves.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the C-index (0.70 internal, 0.74 in temporal validation) and describe the accuracy as moderate rather than implying precise individual prediction."

**Issue B. Unmentioned sensitivity analysis** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the apparent survival benefit of prostatectomy may partly reflect that healthier men with less advanced disease get surgery in the first place, not the surgery itself"
- **Abstract says:** "IPTW confirmed survival benefits of aggressive local control."
- **Problem:** The caveat is a legitimate caution, but the abstract reports an inverse-probability-weighted analysis intended to address exactly this confounding; omitting it slightly understates what the authors did.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the authors ran an IPTW sensitivity analysis that supported the benefit of aggressive local control, while residual confounding in registry data remains possible."

### Study 12: Brain scans of 36- to 80-plus-year-olds suggest thicker sensory and motor cortex tracks with sharper thinking — but mainly in midlife
**PMID:** 42784930 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Sample composition claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the 80+ group is the smallest and hardest to recruit"
- **Abstract says:** "We analyzed cross-sectional data from cognitively healthy adults in the Human Connectome Project-Aging/Aging Adult Brain Connectome, spanning middle (36-59 years), early older (60-79), and late older (80 +) adulthood."
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no group sizes, so the claim that the 80+ cell is the smallest is unverifiable from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Either drop the claim or attribute it ("cohorts of this kind typically have the fewest participants aged 80+")."

### Study 13: Six weeks of heavy squats improved balance — the brain-stimulation add-on did nothing
**PMID:** 42753671 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Recruitment setting inferred** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "participants were healthy adults recruited through a sports university"
- **Abstract says:** "Forty-eight participants were randomized into four groups: a-tDCS + ST, sham tDCS + ST, a-tDCS, and a sham tDCS. ... To determine whether 6 weeks of squat training (ST) combined with anodal tDCS (a-tDCS) results in synergistic benefits to postural control in healthy adults."
- **Problem:** The abstract says nothing about where participants were recruited; the detail appears to be inferred from the authors' affiliation (Beijing Sport University). Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "healthy adults; recruitment setting and age range not reported in the abstract" instead of asserting a sports-university sample."

**Issue B. Fall-risk framing beyond the studied population** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Balance is the hinge on which fall risk turns, and this trial reinforces that progressive lower-body strength training — not a neurotech gadget — is what moves the needle."
- **Abstract says:** "Six weeks of squat training significantly improved dynamic and static postural control. Adjunctive a-tDCS applied over M1 prior to training failed to augment these benefits under the current parameters."
- **Problem:** The trial measured posturography in healthy adults of unreported age and did not assess falls or older adults; the body framing presents it as fall-prevention evidence for seniors, which sits in tension with the digest's own caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "heavy squat training improved laboratory measures of postural control in healthy adults; whether that translates to fewer falls in older adults was not tested.""

### Study 14: What Autopsies of 22 People Who Lived Past 100 Reveal About the Aging Brain
**PMID:** 42777343 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Cause-of-death denominator omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Pneumonia was the most common cause of death, followed by cardiac amyloidosis."
- **Abstract says:** "Of those who underwent a complete autopsy, the most common cause of death was pneumonia in 10 individuals followed by cardiac amyloidosis in 5."
- **Problem:** Cause of death was determined only in the 15 who had full autopsies (7 had brain-only examinations), so the statement applies to a subset, not all 22. The caregiver angle then builds a prevention message on it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the qualifier: "Among the 15 who underwent a full autopsy, pneumonia was the most common cause of death (10 cases), followed by cardiac amyloidosis (5).""

**Issue B. Headline age framing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "What Autopsies of 22 People Who Lived Past 100 Reveal About the Aging Brain"
- **Abstract says:** "we examined pathologic findings in 15 centenarians (99-109 years) and 7 supercentenarians (111-115 years)"
- **Problem:** The centenarian group starts at age 99, so not every one of the 22 had passed 100; a small slippage the body text itself corrects.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Autopsies of 22 people who reached their late 90s and beyond" or "aged 99 to 115.""

### Study 16: A Community Health Worker at Your Side: Early Pilot Improves Symptoms in Pancreatic Cancer Patients
**PMID:** 42681826 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for an uncontrolled pilot** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Early Pilot Improves Symptoms in Pancreatic Cancer Patients"
- **Abstract says:** "A single-center prospective single-arm pilot study. ... Patients' physical QoL and symptom burden improved from baseline to 12 weeks ... Prospective clinical trials are needed to assess the intervention's ability to address the QOL needs of PDAC patients."
- **Problem:** With no control group, before-after change cannot be attributed to the program; the headline asserts the intervention improved symptoms, which the digest's own caveat says cannot be concluded.
