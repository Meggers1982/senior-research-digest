### Study 2: Denosumab still works in the oldest patients — but spine gains may be slightly smaller with age
**PMID:** 42661953 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Absolute benefit inferred** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this analysis suggests denosumab continues to build bone at advanced ages, with only a modest, site-specific age effect at the spine"
- **Abstract says:** "Older baseline age was associated with a modestly smaller lumbar spine BMD gain in treatment-naive patients, whereas no statistically significant age-related association was observed at the total hip."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports only how age modified the size of BMD gain; it gives no absolute BMD gain figures, so the claim that the drug "continues to build bone at advanced ages" is an inference rather than a reported result.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to: "age was linked to only modestly smaller spine gains and made no difference at the hip, so age alone appears to be a weak predictor of response.""

**Issue B. Rebound-risk claim not from this study** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "remember that stopping denosumab abruptly carries rebound fracture risk"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract addresses only the association between baseline age and percent BMD gain over 3 years; it does not mention discontinuation or rebound fractures."
- **Problem:** A clinical caution is presented alongside the study's findings although it is unverifiable from this abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the rebound-risk point to general denosumab guidance rather than to this study, e.g. "separately, established guidance warns that…""

### Study 3: Weak bones raise the odds of implant problems after elbow replacement
**PMID:** 42733408 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Study source described as a database** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Retrospective, observational database study"
- **Abstract says:** "A retrospective analysis was performed including 1,410 adult patients who underwent total elbow arthroplasty (TEA) with a minimum follow-up of two years."
- **Problem:** The abstract calls this a retrospective cohort study and never identifies an administrative or claims database; the design detail is unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "retrospective, observational cohort study" unless the database source can be confirmed from the full text."

### Study 4: A 77-year-old's broken shoulder revealed a genetic bone disease missed for a lifetime
**PMID:** 42741772 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Confirmed" overstates the genetic result** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "genetic testing confirmed hereditary hypophosphatemic rickets with hypercalciuria, a condition usually diagnosed in childhood"
- **Abstract says:** "Genetic analysis identified a homozygous synonymous variant in SLC34A3 (c.942G > C, p.Ala314=) in the context of a characteristic biochemical phenotype consistent with HHRH."
- **Problem:** The author frames the diagnosis as a synonymous variant interpreted together with a characteristic biochemical picture — "consistent with" rather than confirmatory proof.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "genetic testing identified an SLC34A3 variant that, with his lab pattern, pointed to hereditary hypophosphatemic rickets with hypercalciuria.""

### Study 10: Men at High Risk for Osteoporosis Almost Never Get a Bone Density Test, U.S. Data Show
**PMID:** 42493193 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Eligibility age bands simplified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "men 70 and older, women 65 and older, and adults 50+ with risk factors such as very low body weight, smoking, alcohol use disorder, or steroid therapy"
- **Abstract says:** "We included men aged≥70 years and women aged≥65 years. Also, men aged 50-69 years and women aged 50-64 years with additional risk factors, such as body mass index<18.5 kg/m2, tobacco use, alcohol use disorder, or glucocorticoid therapy."
- **Problem:** The risk-factor arm applied to specific age bands (men 50–69, women 50–64), not to all adults 50 and over; the abstract also notes that visits by patients already diagnosed with osteoporosis were excluded.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "men 70+, women 65+, and men 50–69 or women 50–64 with risk factors…" and note that patients already diagnosed with osteoporosis were excluded."

**Issue B. Hip-fracture/mortality claim not in this abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Men account for a substantial share of hip fractures and have higher post-fracture mortality than women"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract reports only DXA ordering patterns and concludes that screening "appears markedly low among men at risk for osteoporosis, representing a missed opportunity for early detection and fracture prevention.""
- **Problem:** The epidemiological framing is not verifiable from this abstract (it comes from the separate men's bone health review in item 1).
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the mortality/hip-fracture context to the Maturitas review cited in item 1, or drop it."

### Study 18: Steroid Pills Raise Fracture Risk by 70% — Equally, Whether or Not You Have Diabetes
**PMID:** 42722898 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline vs. observational design** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Steroid Pills Raise Fracture Risk by 70% — Equally, Whether or Not You Have Diabetes"
- **Abstract says:** "After weighting, GC treatment was associated with an IRR of 1.70 [1.55-1.85], with no evidence of effect modification by T2D... We performed a nationwide registry-based cohort study"
- **Problem:** "Raise" asserts causation from an observational registry analysis, and the digest's own caveat says the study "cannot prove causation" — the headline contradicts the caveat (the abstract title does use "increases," so this is mild).
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "Steroid Pills Linked to 70% Higher Fracture Risk — Equally, With or Without Diabetes.""

**Issue B. Short-course inference** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "because the median treatment period here was only about 35 days, risk shows up even with short courses, not just chronic use"
- **Abstract says:** "Median follow-up time was 35 days (with a mean of 212 days) during GC treatment... when allowing the effects of GC treatment to vary smoothly as a function of time-since-initiation, when accounting for differences in daily dosage with different durations of treatment"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a short median exposure and duration-related sensitivity analyses but does not report a specific risk estimate for short courses, so "risk shows up even with short courses" is an inference rather than a stated finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say that most treatment episodes in the study were short (median 35 days), so the overall risk estimate largely reflects short courses, without asserting a separate short-course finding."

**Issue C. Funding statement not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "though the analysis itself reports no specific industry funding"
- **Abstract says:** "(No funding statement appears in the abstract; only conflict-of-interest declarations are listed.)"
- **Problem:** Absence of industry funding cannot be confirmed from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the clause or mark it as "no funding source stated in the abstract." Unverifiable from abstract only."

### Study 19: Losing Weight in Later Life Costs You Bone — Here's What the Evidence Says Protects It

**Issue A. Publication date** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**Journal:** *Curr Osteoporos Rep* / **Published:** 2026 Sep 8"
- **Abstract says:** "Curr Osteoporos Rep. 2026 Sep 9;24(1):38."
- **Problem:** The digest and citation table list 8 September; the record shows 9 September.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change the date to 2026 Sep 9 in both the entry and the citation table."

**Issue B. "Strongest countermeasures" ranking** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the strongest countermeasures identified were adequate calcium and protein intake plus exercise"
- **Abstract says:** "Current evidence emphasizes the importance of adequate nutrient intake (e.g., calcium, protein, etc.) and exercise as countermeasures to attenuate bone loss."

- **Suggested fix:** "Use "the countermeasures the authors emphasize were adequate calcium and protein intake plus exercise.""

### Study 20: A Powerful Bone-Building Drug Boosts Spine and Hip Scores — But Wrist and Shin Bone Didn't Follow
**PMID:** 42711379 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Femoral neck result omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Spine density rose nearly 10% and total hip 4.4%"
- **Abstract says:** "At T1, aBMD was significantly higher than at T0 at the lumbar spine (+9.6% [IQR = 6.6,15.7], p < 0.001) and total hip (+4.4 ± 5.8%, p = 0.001) but not femoral neck."
- **Problem:** The digest reports the two significant sites but omits that femoral neck aBMD did not increase significantly, which slightly overstates the "hip" gains implied by the headline.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "while femoral neck density did not change significantly.""

**Issue B. Disclosure misattributed** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "two authors report consulting or lecture relationships with Amgen and UCB, the manufacturers of romosozumab"
- **Abstract says:** "B. van Rietbergen is a consultant for SCANCO Medical AG and Amolyt pharma. JvdB is a consultant for Amgen, UCB, and Porous GmbH and received lecture fees from Amgen and UCB."
- **Problem:** Only one author (van den Bergh) discloses Amgen/UCB ties; the second author's disclosed relationships are with SCANCO and Amolyt, not the romosozumab manufacturers.
- **Suggested fix:** "State that one author consults for and receives lecture fees from Amgen and UCB (romosozumab's manufacturers), and another consults for medical-device and pharmaceutical firms (SCANCO, Amolyt)."

### Study 21: AI Reading Routine Chest X-Rays Could Catch Osteoporosis Early — If Anyone Follows Up
**PMID:** 42732841 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Undisclosed author employment by AI developer** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "the AI tool's developer (Promedius Inc.) provided financial support to the lead author, and multiple authors report extensive consulting and speaking relationships with osteoporosis drug and device companies"
- **Abstract says:** "Jung J — author affiliation: 'Promedius Inc., Seoul, Republic of Korea'; 'Jean-Yves Reginster reports financial support was provided by Promedius Inc.' and 'Jean-Yves Reginster reports a relationship with Promedius that includes: consulting or advisory.'"
- **Problem:** The caveat mentions funding to the lead author but omits that one of the co-authors is directly affiliated with (employed by) Promedius Inc., the company whose AI product the model evaluates, and that the lead author is also a paid Promedius advisor — a stronger commercial tie than 'provided financial support' conveys.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add: 'one co-author is affiliated with Promedius Inc., the company that makes the AI tool, and the lead author also reports a consulting/advisory relationship with Promedius.'"

**Issue B. Editor-in-Chief conflict not noted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "multiple authors report extensive consulting and speaking relationships with osteoporosis drug and device companies"
- **Abstract says:** "Peter Ebeling is Editor-in-Chief of Bone"
- **Problem:** A co-author is Editor-in-Chief of the journal that published the paper — a disclosure the digest flags for other Bone papers (studies 7 and 16) but omits here.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add 'a co-author is Editor-in-Chief of Bone, the publishing journal' to the caveats."

### Study 22: CBD and Bone Health: Intriguing Lab Results, Almost No Human Evidence Yet
**PMID:** 42709344 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Direction of the single clinical finding softened** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a medical cannabis product altered bone turnover markers in healthy adults"
- **Abstract says:** "The first clinical study in this area reported that a medical cannabis product reduced markers of bone turnover in healthy adults."
- **Problem:** The abstract specifies the direction (reduced); the digest's 'altered' removes information readers could use, though it is not factually wrong.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'reduced markers of bone turnover in healthy adults.'"

**Issue B. 'Small' human study not supported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Narrative review dominated by in vitro and rodent data with only a single small human study"
- **Abstract says:** "The first clinical study in this area reported that a medical cannabis product reduced markers of bone turnover in healthy adults."
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no sample size or indication that the clinical study was small. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop 'small' or say 'only a single clinical study, whose size and design are not described in the abstract.'"

**Issue C. Drug-interaction advice not from this paper** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "do tell your doctor and pharmacist what you're taking, since these products can interact with other medications"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract discusses phytocannabinoid effects on bone homeostasis and the endocannabinoid system; it makes no statement about drug–drug interactions."
- **Problem:** The interaction claim is general background rather than a finding of this review; it is presented as flowing from the study.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute it as general safety advice, e.g. 'as a general precaution (not something this review examined), tell your doctor and pharmacist what you're taking.'"
