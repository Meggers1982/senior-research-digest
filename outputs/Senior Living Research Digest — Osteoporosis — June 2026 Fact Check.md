## Fact-Check Report

### Study 1: Low Grip Strength Predicts Future Osteoporosis in Older Adults
**PMID:** 42167463 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. Framing is consistent with the abstract. Sample size (5,921), follow-up period (median 12.5 years), population (50+ from ELSA), HR of 1.413 (reported as 41% higher), and the finding that the association was particularly pronounced among women are all accurately reported. Caveats appropriately note self-reported diagnosis and observational design.

### Study 2: Weak Grip Plus Depression or Slow Walking Speed Dramatically Increases Fracture Risk
**PMID:** 42067696 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. Sample size (16,357), population (Australians aged 70+), and the hazard ratios are accurately reported: slow gait + depressive symptoms = 46% higher (abstract: sHR=1.46), weak grip + depression = 47% higher (abstract: sHR=1.47), slow gait + weak grip = 23% higher (abstract: sHR=1.23). The finding that effects were stronger among males and those with diabetes is confirmed. Observational limitation appropriately noted.

### Study 3: Three Common Osteoporosis Drugs Equally Effective in Men
**PMID:** 42096654 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. Sample size (390 men), study design (randomized, open-label), 12-month follow-up, and bone density gains are accurately reported. Abstract states spine BMD increased 4.83%, 4.32%, 5.18% (digest says "4-5%") and hip increased 2.75%, 2.50%, 2.83% (digest says "2.5-3%")—both reasonable summaries. ### Study 4: Anti-Osteoporosis Medications Don't Delay Fracture Healing
**PMID:** 42229491 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Teriparatide healing time reduction** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Teriparatide (a bone-building medication) actually reduced healing time by about 3 weeks."
- **Abstract says:** "Pooled analysis showed a significant reduction in time to union with use of teriparatide compared with a control (mean difference -3·03, 95% CI -3·61 to -2·45)"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a mean difference of -3.03 without specifying units. The digest assumes this is weeks, but the abstract does not explicitly state the unit. While weeks is plausible for fracture healing, this cannot be definitively confirmed from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Teriparatide (a bone-building medication) actually reduced healing time significantly (mean difference of approximately 3 units on the time-to-union measure)." OR add caveat: "reduced healing time by about 3 weeks (based on the mean difference reported)."

### Study 8: Prolonged Bed Rest After Spinal Fracture Dramatically Increases Risk of Vertebral Collapse
**PMID:** 42165006 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Bed rest risk magnitude** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Bed rest exceeding 2 weeks increased this risk nearly 10-fold"
- **Abstract says:** "prolonged bed rest >2 weeks (OR 9.69, 95% CI 3.92-23.93)"
- **Problem:** OR 9.69 is accurately characterized as "nearly 10-fold." No error here.

**Issue B. Diabetes risk magnitude** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "diabetes increased it 40-fold"
- **Abstract says:** "Diabetes mellitus (OR 40.61, 95% CI 7.80-211.34)"
- **Problem:** The digest says "40-fold" which is consistent with OR 40.61. However, the extremely wide confidence interval (7.80-211.34) suggests substantial uncertainty that could be mentioned.
- **Suggested fix:** Consider adding to caveats: "wide confidence intervals for diabetes association indicate uncertainty in the precise magnitude."

**Issue C. Prediction tool accuracy** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A prediction tool combining these factors achieved 94% accuracy"
- **Abstract says:** "The nomogram achieved an area under the curve of 0.938 (95% CI 0.906-0.970) in the training cohort"
- **Problem:** AUC of 0.938 is not the same as "94% accuracy." AUC measures discrimination ability, not accuracy.
- **Suggested fix:** "A prediction tool combining these factors achieved an AUC of 0.94 in the training cohort, indicating excellent discrimination ability."

### Study 12: Hospital Frailty Screening Alert System Successfully Identifies Hidden Geriatric Conditions
**PMID:** 41865380 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Dementia identification rate** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The team identified previously unrecognized dementia in 87% of cognitive impairment cases"
- **Abstract says:** "MFIT further identified syndromes which may be neglected during acute admission such as underlying dementia (87 %, N = 83)"
- **Problem:** The abstract states 87% with N=83, but the phrasing is ambiguous—it may mean 87% of total patients had underlying dementia identified (not 87% of cognitive impairment cases). The digest's interpretation is reasonable but not definitively supported.
- **Suggested fix:** "The team identified underlying dementia in 87% of cases screened (N=83)" or note this interpretation is inferred.

The other statistics (81% with at least one geriatric syndrome, 69% with multiple, 13% osteoporosis, 10% incontinence, 79% discharge recommendation adherence) are accurately reported.

### Study 14: Biological Aging Markers Predict Osteoporosis and Multiple Chronic Diseases
**PMID:** 42160766 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Risk signature percentages** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "low-risk (58%), medium-risk (9%), and high-risk (32%)"
- **Abstract says:** "Low (58.5%), medium (9.2%) and high-risk (32.3%)"
- **Problem:** Minor rounding differences. Not consequential but slightly imprecise.
- **Suggested fix:** Use "58.5%," "9.2%," and "32.3%" or note these are approximate.

**Issue B. Disease cluster naming** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "osteoporosis/hypothyroid/respiratory conditions"
- **Abstract says:** "hypothyroid/osteoporosis/respiratory"
- **Problem:** Order differs but content is the same. Not an error.

All other statistics (89% higher 12-year mortality, 46-149% higher 8-year risk) match the abstract (HR 1.89, OR range 1.46-2.49). Caveats appropriately note biomarkers not yet clinically available and validation cohort composition.

### Study 18: CT-Based Spine Imaging Outperforms Standard Bone Density for Fracture Prediction
**PMID:** 42199482 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Accuracy comparison framing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "predicted vertebral fractures with 82-84% accuracy, outperforming standard bone mineral density measurements (75% accuracy)"
- **Abstract says:** "The combined cortical and cancellous radscore model (vertebral model) attained an AUC of 0.825 and 0.840 for OVF prediction, outperforming the vBMD model (AUC: 0.752/0.735)"
- **Problem:** The digest describes AUC values as "accuracy," which is technically imprecise. AUC measures discriminative ability, not accuracy in the traditional sense.
- **Suggested fix:** "achieved AUC values of 82-84%, outperforming standard bone mineral density measurements (AUC 73-75%)" or clarify these are discrimination metrics.
