### Study 1: Telling your doctor about a fall could change your osteoporosis treatment
**PMID:** 42689245 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. One-sided presentation of reclassification** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Most people (72%) came out with a higher 10-year fracture risk under FRAXplus (average increase of 4.8 percentage points), and 16% of major osteoporotic fracture estimates were reclassified upward"
- **Abstract says:** "Most individuals (72%) had higher probabilities with FRAXplus (mean absolute increase: +4.8%), while 27% had lower probabilities (mean absolute decrease: -1.0%). ... led to an upward reclassification in 16% of MOF estimates and a downward reclassification in 1%."
- **Problem:** The digest omits that 27% of individuals had lower probabilities with FRAXplus and that 1% were reclassified downward, which slightly overstates the one-directional effect of the tool.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a clause such as 'while 27% had slightly lower estimates and 1% were reclassified downward.'"

### Study 3: Uneven ground trips up some older walkers far more than others — and brain scans hint at why
**PMID:** 42679689 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Free water attributed to the lower-functioning group** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Their brain scans showed more extracellular free water — a marker of white matter deterioration — which was more strongly tied to unsteady walking in this group"
- **Abstract says:** "Older adults showed widespread higher FW and lower tissue integrity than young adults... A significant group-by-behavior interaction showed that higher FW was more strongly associated with greater baseline gait variability in lower-functioning than higher-functioning older adults, particularly in the body of corpus callosum."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports higher free water in older adults versus young adults, not in lower- versus higher-functioning older adults; only the strength of the free water–gait variability association differed between the two older groups (the reported lower- vs higher-functioning difference was in axial diffusivity).
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to: 'Older adults overall showed more extracellular free water than young adults, and in the lower-functioning group that free water was more strongly tied to unsteady walking.'"

**Issue B. Association was with baseline (flat) gait variability** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "which was more strongly tied to unsteady walking in this group"
- **Abstract says:** "higher FW was more strongly associated with greater baseline gait variability in lower-functioning than higher-functioning older adults"
- **Problem:** The interaction applied specifically to baseline (flat-walking) variability, not to variability on uneven terrain, which is the headline theme.
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify 'more strongly tied to their step variability even on flat ground.'"

### Study 4: In the Oldest Old, Slow Walking Predicts Falls — and for Men, Memory Problems Do Too
**PMID:** 42734897 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal phrasing for an observational cohort** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "In women, slow gait alone raised fall rates by about a third, while cognitive impairment alone did not."
- **Abstract says:** "In women, 'slow gait' (IRR = 1.33, 95% CI = 1.03-1.72) and 'both' (IRR = 1.35, 95% CI = 1.06-1.73) were significantly associated with an elevated risk of future falls, whereas 'impaired cognition' was not"
- **Problem:** 'Raised fall rates' implies causation; the study reports associations from an observational cohort (as the Caveats acknowledge).
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'was associated with about a third higher fall rate.'"

**Issue B. Omits that 'both' was significant in women; combined-screen claim overstated for women** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "these two simple checks together give a much clearer read on your fall risk than either alone"
- **Abstract says:** "In women, 'slow gait' (IRR = 1.33) and 'both' (IRR = 1.35) were significantly associated ... in men, 'slow gait' (IRR = 1.70), 'impaired cognition' (IRR = 1.75), and 'both' (IRR = 2.34)"
- **Problem:** In women the combined category (IRR 1.35) was barely different from slow gait alone (IRR 1.33), so the combination added little; the digest also omits that 'both' was statistically significant in women.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the combination added substantial risk information mainly in men, and mention that 'both' was also significant in women (IRR 1.35)."

### Study 10: A Short Safety Talk at the Right Moment Cut Falls Nearly in Half After Stroke
**PMID:** 42731691 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for a retrospective pre-post study** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "A Short Safety Talk at the Right Moment Cut Falls Nearly in Half After Stroke"
- **Abstract says:** "Standardized fall-prevention education delivered upon achieving independent walking was associated with a lower subsequent fall risk among patients with subacute stroke."
- **Problem:** The study is a non-randomized retrospective pre-post comparison; 'Cut falls nearly in half' asserts a causal effect the design cannot establish (the authors use 'associated with' throughout).
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'A Short Safety Talk at the Right Moment Was Linked to Far Fewer Falls After Stroke.'"

**Issue B. 'Fall risk spikes' claim not supported by the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "that milestone is when fall risk spikes"
- **Abstract says:** "The time to first fall after achieving independent walking was analyzed... Among 1,437 patients, 217 experienced at least one fall after achieving independent walking."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports falls occurring after independent walking was achieved but does not compare risk before and after that milestone, so a 'spike' at that point is unverifiable from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'falls commonly occur in the period after independent walking is achieved' instead of claiming risk spikes."

**Issue C. 'Short' session length not specified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a standardized fall-prevention education session"
- **Abstract says:** "Standardized fall-prevention education delivered immediately after achieving independent walking versus usual care without a standardized education program."
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no duration, so describing it as a 'short'/'brief' talk in the headline and Why it matters is unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the length descriptor or attribute it to the full text."

### Study 11: How Tired Activities Make You Feel May Predict Your Next Fall
**PMID:** 42605068 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Comparison with performance testing not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Fatigability is a simple questionnaire measure that may flag fall risk earlier than physical performance testing"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract reports only that physical (OR 1.54) and mental (OR 1.41) fatigability predicted falls after covariate adjustment; no comparison against physical performance tests was made."
- **Problem:** Implies a head-to-head advantage over objective performance testing that the study did not evaluate. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to: 'Fatigability is a simple questionnaire measure that was prospectively linked to falls and is potentially modifiable through conditioning and activity pacing.'"

### Study 12: Falls, Depression and Untreated Hearing Loss Track With Dementia — and the Burden Falls Unequally
**PMID:** 42731172 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Outcome scope narrowed** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Advanced age, low education, stroke, falls, and depression or anxiety were all associated with higher odds of probable dementia"
- **Abstract says:** ""Advanced age, low education, stroke, falls, and depression/anxiety were associated with higher Odds of possible and probable dementia (Prb-D)""
- **Problem:** The abstract reports higher odds for both possible and probable dementia; the digest mentions only probable dementia.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add 'possible and' before 'probable dementia'."

**Issue B. Claim about Black older adults** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "This study also underscores that standardized programs may serve Hispanic and Black older adults poorly"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract's specific disparity findings concern Hispanic adults (higher predicted probability at 85+, larger education effect, chewing/swallowing, hearing aid use); NH Black hearing aid users showed lower probability, similar to NH White users. The conclusion refers generally to 'minority older adults.'"
- **Problem:** Naming Black older adults specifically goes beyond the reported results, where the sharp disparities shown were for Hispanic adults.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'may serve Hispanic and other minority older adults poorly, with the clearest disparities here among Hispanic adults'."

### Study 13: Falls in Australian nursing homes held steady for eight years — even as sedative prescribing dropped sharply
**PMID:** 42786600 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Number of medication quality indicators** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "tracking quarterly rates of falls and four medication-related quality indicators"
- **Abstract says:** ""quarterly prevalence estimates of four QIs were evaluated" — the medication-related QIs named are high sedative load, antipsychotic use, and anti-anxiety or hypnotic medications (three), with falls as the remaining indicator."
- **Problem:** The four QIs include falls; only three are medication-related.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'falls and three medication-related quality indicators'."

**Issue B. 'Dropped sharply' overstates sedative load trend** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "even as sedative prescribing dropped sharply"
- **Abstract says:** "High sedative load fell by only 0.17% per quarter (28.1%–32.1%), while antipsychotics fell 2.14% and anti-anxiety/hypnotics 1.63% per quarter."
- **Problem:** The broadest sedative measure barely moved; 'sharply' applies only to antipsychotics and anxiolytics/hypnotics.
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify 'even as antipsychotic and anti-anxiety prescribing dropped sharply' and note that high sedative load changed only marginally."

**Issue C. Ecological inference about deprescribing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this eight-year record suggests deprescribing alone does not move the needle on falls"
- **Abstract says:** "The study reports parallel trends only and concludes 'Medication-related QIs decreased during the study period, especially after national reforms'; it does not test whether deprescribing affected falls."
- **Problem:** Draws a conclusion about the effect of deprescribing from parallel population trends, though the caveat does note causation cannot be established.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to 'falling medication use over these eight years was not accompanied by falling fall rates, though the study cannot tell us why'."

### Study 15: Chronic pain plus a slower-thinking brain tripled fall odds in older adults
**PMID:** 42687648 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal verb in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Chronic pain plus a slower-thinking brain tripled fall odds in older adults"
- **Abstract says:** ""This cross-sectional study... the coexistence of chronic pain and slower processing speed was significantly associated with falls compared to that with neither condition (odds ratio [OR]=3.203...)""
- **Problem:** 'Tripled' implies the combination caused the increase, but this is a cross-sectional association with falls already reported over the past year.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'was linked to roughly triple the odds of a fall'."

### Study 16: Tai chi, Baduanjin and yoga improved balance in frail and pre-frail older adults, review finds
**PMID:** 42775218 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Chair-modified claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "these are low-intensity, low-cost activities that frail older adults can realistically do — including in a chair-modified form in many programs"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract describes Tai Chi, Baduanjin and Yoga as 'low-intensity interventions' but says nothing about chair-modified delivery or cost."
- **Problem:** Adds a practical detail about seated adaptations that the review does not report. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Delete the chair-modified clause or attribute it to general practice rather than this review."

**Issue B. Heterogeneity and participant-pool caveats unverifiable** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Only 10 trials with a modest total participant pool; substantial heterogeneity across studies with wide confidence intervals"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract reports 10 RCTs with 12 comparisons and pooled SMDs with CIs, but gives no total sample size and no heterogeneity statistics."
- **Problem:** Total participants and 'substantial heterogeneity' are not stated in the abstract, though the wide CI for static balance is visible.
- **Suggested fix:** "Limit the caveat to what is shown: 'only 10 trials, with a wide confidence interval around the static-balance estimate; total participant numbers are not given in the abstract.'"

### Study 18: A Five-Question Checklist Beats Grip Strength at Predicting Who Recovers After a Hip Fracture
**PMID:** 42696807 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Plateau claim broader than the measured outcome** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the four-month mark appears to be the point beyond which further gains are unlikely"
- **Abstract says:** ""No significant improvement in BI was observed in either group between 4 and 12 months.""
- **Problem:** The plateau was observed specifically on the Barthel Index of daily function, not on recovery generally; walking mobility was only followed to 4 months.
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify 'daily-function scores did not improve further between 4 and 12 months in either group'."

**Issue B. Absolute statement about grip and calf measures** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the physical measurements — grip and calf size — did not reliably predict anything"
- **Abstract says:** ""Handgrip strength or calf circumference was not consistently associated with the studied outcomes.""
- **Problem:** 'Did not predict anything' is stronger than 'not consistently associated' with the outcomes studied.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'were not consistently associated with the outcomes measured'."

### Study 22: Dedicated Hospital Delirium Units Show Promise — With Less Restraint Use and Better Function
**PMID:** 42776711 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Associations described as benefits** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The review found the most consistent benefits in shorter delirium duration, better discharge outcomes, lower use of physical restraints, and improved functional recovery"
- **Abstract says:** "The narrative synthesis suggested that specialized delirium care environments were most consistently associated with shorter delirium duration, more favorable discharge outcomes, lower physical restraint use, and better functional recovery"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports associations from a narrative synthesis of studies all judged at high or critical risk of bias; calling them "benefits" that the review "found" implies a demonstrated effect the authors explicitly decline to claim.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to: "The narrative synthesis found these settings were most consistently associated with shorter delirium duration, better discharge outcomes, lower physical restraint use, and improved functional recovery.""
