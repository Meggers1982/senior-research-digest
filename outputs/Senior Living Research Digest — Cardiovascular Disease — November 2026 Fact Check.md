### Study 6: Inflammation plus insulin resistance: a two-marker combination flags doubled cardiovascular risk in midlife and older adults
**PMID:** 42994151 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Attributing the strongest signal to insulin resistance alone** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Insulin resistance in particular carried the strongest signal here, and it is modifiable through weight, activity, and medication."
- **Abstract says:** "Three phenotypes were identified: low CTI-high eGDR (n = 2344), high CTI-intermediate eGDR (n = 1351), and intermediate CTI-low eGDR (n = 1188)... Compared with the first phenotype, the latter two were associated with higher CVD risk after multivariable adjustment (HR 1.25, 95% CI 1.08-1.46; and HR 2.12, 95% CI 1.86-2.42)."
- **Problem:** The study tested joint CTI–eGDR phenotypes, not the independent contribution of insulin sensitivity; the abstract does not isolate insulin resistance as the dominant driver, nor does it address modifiability.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to: 'The combination of low insulin sensitivity with intermediate inflammation carried the highest risk in this analysis, though the study cannot separate the contribution of each component.'"

### Study 8: Depression raises heart and death risk in people with type 2 diabetes — and blood sugar and cholesterol don't explain it
**PMID:** 42624218 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for observational data** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Depression raises heart and death risk in people with type 2 diabetes — and blood sugar and cholesterol don't explain it"
- **Abstract says:** "Depression is associated with greater risks for incident cardiovascular disease and all-cause death in people with type 2 diabetes."
- **Problem:** 'Raises' asserts causation from a retrospective population database analysis; the body text and caveats correctly use associational language, so the headline overstates relative to the study.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'Depression is linked to higher heart and death risk in people with type 2 diabetes — and blood sugar and cholesterol don't explain it.'"

**Issue B. Behavioral mediators not tested** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this suggests untreated depression is a heart risk in its own right — not simply a cause of poorer diet, exercise or medication adherence"
- **Abstract says:** "We examined the mediating effects of eight cardiometabolic risk factors in these risk associations... Mediation analyses revealed minor contributions from metabolic risk factors on these associations"
- **Problem:** Only eight cardiometabolic risk factors were tested as mediators; diet, exercise and adherence were not measured, so the digest rules out pathways the study never examined.
- **Suggested fix:** "Limit the claim to what was tested: 'conventional cardiometabolic measures explained little of the excess risk, though behavioural pathways such as diet, activity and adherence were not assessed.'"

**Issue C. Baseline characteristics may describe the depression subgroup** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "571,129 Chinese adults in Hong Kong with type 2 diabetes (mean age 60.4 years, 66% women)"
- **Abstract says:** "Among 571,129 people with type 2 diabetes, 7.5% had depression (mean age 60.4 years, 66.1% women, mean HbA1c 7.2%)."
- **Problem:** In the abstract the age/sex figures sit inside the parenthesis following the depression subgroup, so it is ambiguous whether they describe the whole cohort or only those with depression; the digest assigns them to the full cohort.
- **Suggested fix:** "Either attribute the figures to the depression subgroup or note the ambiguity, e.g. 'those with depression had a mean age of 60.4 years and 66% were women'."

### Study 10: Financial strain and discrimination show up in the blood — but mainly in older Black adults who already have heart disease
**PMID:** 42843435 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Tiny heart-disease subgroup not disclosed** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the links appeared only in combination with cardiovascular burden — for example, discrimination interacted with heart disease across 20 proteins and financial burden across 11"
- **Abstract says:** "Cardiovascular burden included BMI (mean = 30.6, SD = 6.4 kg/m2), hypertension (80.1%), type 2 diabetes (25.1%), and heart disease (6.8%)."
- **Problem:** Only about 44 of the 642 participants had heart disease, so the headline interaction rests on a very small subgroup; the digest reports hypertension and diabetes prevalence but not this figure, and the caveats don't mention it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that only 6.8% of participants had diagnosed heart disease, making the interaction findings exploratory and based on a small subgroup."

**Issue B. Screening recommendation from exploratory cross-sectional data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the findings support screening residents with cardiac conditions for financial strain and experiences of discrimination"
- **Abstract says:** "The effects of psychosocial stress-inflammation associations are complex and may depend on cardiovascular burden. Replication is needed to establish temporality and clinical relevance."
- **Problem:** The authors explicitly state that replication is needed to establish clinical relevance, so framing the results as supporting a screening practice goes a step beyond the evidence.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to: 'the findings add to the rationale for asking residents with cardiac conditions about financial strain and discrimination, though the clinical relevance is not yet established.'"

### Study 11: Texting and Portal Messages Nudged Thousands Back onto Their Statins
**PMID:** 42667613 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline overstates magnitude** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Texting and Portal Messages Nudged Thousands Back onto Their Statins"
- **Abstract says:** "initial digital outreach increased 14-day statin refill vs usual communication (14.4% vs 12.0%; adjusted risk difference, 2.3 percentage points [95% CI, 1.3-3.4])"
- **Problem:** A 2.3-percentage-point absolute difference across roughly 15,000 participants assigned to digital outreach corresponds to a few hundred extra refills attributable to the intervention, not "thousands." The body text and caveats are accurate, but the headline reads as a much larger effect.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to reflect the modest absolute gain, e.g. "Texting and Portal Messages Nudged a Few More Patients Back onto Their Statins — and a Second Nudge Helped More.""

**Issue B. Second-stage arm described as "stop outreach"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "non-responders were randomized a second time to repeat, switch, or stop outreach"
- **Abstract says:** "Initial nonresponders within 14 days underwent prespecified second-stage randomization to repeat the same outreach, switch communication modality, or receive usual communication."
- **Problem:** The third second-stage arm was usual communication, which is not strictly the same as stopping all outreach.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change "stop outreach" to "revert to usual communication.""

### Study 16: Your heart risk score may also say something about your brain
**PMID:** 42898366 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Missing nuance on amyloid/tau associations** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Higher 10-year heart risk was linked to more amyloid and tau signal, thinner cortex, smaller hippocampus, more white matter damage, and lower scores in memory, executive function, processing speed, and language — even after accounting for APOE ε4 genetic status and amyloid burden."
- **Abstract says:** "Associations between ten-year ASCVD risk and structural and vascular injury markers were observed in both amyloid-positive and amyloid-negative participants, whereas associations with amyloid and MTL tau PET were modest and statistically significant only among amyloid-negative participants."
- **Problem:** The amyloid and tau associations were modest and held only in the amyloid-negative subgroup, a qualification the digest omits; also, the "even after accounting for ... amyloid burden" adjustment applied to the cognitive outcomes, not to the amyloid imaging outcome itself.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that the amyloid and tau links were modest and significant only in amyloid-negative participants, and attach the adjustment clause to the cognitive findings."

### Study 17: A five-minute artery stiffness test predicted who died over the next 13 years
**PMID:** 42988562 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Borderline significance not noted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "those with the stiffest arteries (CAVI ≥ 9.0) had a 38% higher risk of death from any cause compared with those below 8.0"
- **Abstract says:** "the CAVI 3 group showed increased risks of all-cause (hazard ratio: 1.38, 95% confidence interval: 1.00-1.90)"
- **Problem:** The all-cause mortality result sits exactly at the significance boundary (CI lower limit 1.00); the caveats flag the CVD and non-CVD CIs but not this one, so readers may take the headline finding as firmer than it is.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note the confidence interval (1.00-1.90) in the body text or extend the caveat to mention that the all-cause result was borderline."

**Issue B. Confusing phrasing of the risk comparison** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Among the 295 people who died, those with the stiffest arteries (CAVI ≥ 9.0) had a 38% higher risk of death from any cause"
- **Abstract says:** "During follow-up, 295 deaths occurred. Compared with the CAVI 1 group, the CAVI 3 group showed increased risks of all-cause (hazard ratio: 1.38 ...)"
- **Problem:** The hazard ratio was calculated across the whole cohort of 5,994, not within the 295 decedents; as written it implies the comparison was made among those who died.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as: "There were 295 deaths during follow-up; participants with the stiffest arteries (CAVI ≥ 9.0) had a 38% higher risk of death from any cause than those below 8.0.""

**Issue C. "Five-minute" test duration** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A five-minute artery stiffness test predicted who died over the next 13 years"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract describes CAVI as "a blood pressure-independent measure of arterial stiffness" but gives no information on how long the test takes."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the specific duration or attribute it to general descriptions of the test rather than this study."

### Study 18: Blood sugar swings that standard tests miss were tied to higher heart risk in people without diabetes
**PMID:** 42752803 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Odds figure attributed to two measures** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Higher glucose variability and more time above 140 mg/dL were associated with higher estimated cardiovascular risk and 21–26% higher odds of hypertension and dyslipidemia"
- **Abstract says:** "1 SD higher % time >140 mg/dL (15.4%) was associated with 2% higher 10-year CVD risk estimates and 21-26% higher odds of hypertension and dyslipidemia, even after adjusting for FPG"
- **Problem:** The 21-26% figure is given in the abstract specifically for percent time above 140 mg/dL, not for glucose variability as well.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the 21-26% odds specifically to time above 140 mg/dL, e.g. "each standard-deviation increase in time above 140 mg/dL was associated with 21-26% higher odds ...""

**Issue B. Direction of discordance** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Notably, many people flagged as higher-risk by their CGM patterns were not flagged by conventional fasting glucose or HbA1c testing."
- **Abstract says:** "Glycemic profiles representing higher CGM dysglycemia revealed discordance with traditionally defined glycemic status (FPG and HbA1c). Although 48.1-84.2% of individuals with traditionally defined prediabetes were included in CGM profiles representing higher dysglycemia ..."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports discordance in both directions without quantifying how many CGM-flagged people were missed by FPG/HbA1c; the digest asserts one specific direction.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say CGM profiles "did not line up neatly with fasting glucose and HbA1c classifications" rather than specifying the direction."

### Study 20: Why men die earlier — and what the data say is actually driving it
**PMID:** 42956774 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. CVD ranked as the single largest contributor** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The largest single contributor to men's shorter lives is cardiovascular disease, a condition that is substantially preventable with screening and treatment that many men avoid or delay."
- **Abstract says:** "Although cardiovascular disease and cancer make the largest contributions to men's higher mortality rates ..."
- **Problem:** The abstract pairs cardiovascular disease and cancer as joint largest contributors and does not rank one above the other.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "cardiovascular disease and cancer are the largest contributors to men's higher mortality" rather than naming CVD as the single largest."

**Issue B. Individual screening recommendation from population modeling** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Booking the blood pressure, cholesterol, and cancer screenings you have been putting off is the single most evidence-aligned response."
- **Abstract says:** "the pattern of many diseases contributing to health differences reflects the intersection of health behaviours, shaped by patriarchy, ideas of masculinity, and structural drivers (including commercial determinants of health); many of these are preventable."
- **Problem:** This is an analytical overview of modeled burden-of-disease estimates; it does not evaluate screening uptake or its effect on outcomes, so calling screening "the single most evidence-aligned response" to this paper overstates what the analysis shows.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to something like "the conditions driving the gap are ones that routine blood pressure, cholesterol and cancer checks can help catch early," without claiming the paper establishes screening as the best response."

### Study 22: A Blood Marker of Inflammation Flagged Future Heart Failure Years Before Symptoms — and Weight Loss Lowered It
**PMID:** 42687462 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Per-SD effect described without the log transformation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "higher IL-6 was tied to a 43% higher risk of new heart failure per standard-deviation increase"
- **Abstract says:** "Higher IL-6 was significantly associated with higher risk of incident HF in the overall cohort (HR: 1.43; 95% CI: 1.16-1.75 per 1-SD higher log[IL-6])"
- **Problem:** The hazard ratio is per 1-SD higher log-transformed IL-6, not per SD of raw IL-6; the shorthand slightly misstates the scale of the exposure.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "per standard-deviation increase in log IL-6 (HR 1.43, 95% CI 1.16-1.75)"."

**Issue B. "hs-CRP predicted nothing" overstates the null result** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "hs-CRP predicted nothing, but higher IL-6 was tied to a 43% higher risk"
- **Abstract says:** "After adjusting for demographics, CVD risk factors, and statin use, hs-CRP was not significantly associated with the risk of ASCVD or incident HF."
- **Problem:** The null applies to adjusted models for two specific outcomes; "predicted nothing" reads as a blanket statement about the marker.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "after adjustment, hs-CRP was not significantly associated with atherosclerotic events or new heart failure"."

**Issue C. Implied chain from lowering IL-6 to lower heart failure risk** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "it measurably cooled the inflammation linked to future heart failure"
- **Abstract says:** "Higher IL-6 was significantly associated with higher risk of incident HF... An ILI targeting weight loss reduced IL-6 over 1-year of follow-up."
- **Problem:** The trial showed the lifestyle arm lowered IL-6 and, separately, that IL-6 was associated with heart failure; it did not show that lowering IL-6 reduced heart failure events. The Caveats flag the observational link, but the story angle still joins the two.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that the trial did not test whether reducing IL-6 translates into fewer heart failure cases."

**Issue D. "Years Before Symptoms" not supported in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A Blood Marker of Inflammation Flagged Future Heart Failure Years Before Symptoms"
- **Abstract says:** "Associations of inflammatory biomarkers with risk of ASCVD and incident HF were evaluated using adjusted Cox models. (no follow-up duration reported)"
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no follow-up length for the incident heart failure analysis, so the "years before" timeframe is unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the timeframe from the headline or note that follow-up duration is not reported in the abstract."

### Study 23: People Over 80 Are Dying Less of Heart Disease — and Living Longer With Disability
**PMID:** 42594758 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline asserts an individual-level conclusion the authors disclaim** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "People Over 80 Are Dying Less of Heart Disease — and Living Longer With Disability"
- **Abstract says:** "The findings support greater emphasis on function-preserving geriatric care and long-term care, but do not by themselves establish individual-level expansion or compression of morbidity."
- **Problem:** "Living Longer With Disability" is precisely the individual-level expansion-of-morbidity interpretation the authors say the data cannot support; the headline contradicts the digest's own caveat. The heart-disease element also rests on a declining share of burden rather than a measured drop in cardiovascular death rates.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as population-level, e.g. "Among the oldest-old, fatal burden is falling while the disabling share of health loss rises — and heart disease makes up less of the total"."

**Issue B. YLL rates described as "death rates"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Death rates fell in all 10 countries (median -31.3%), while disability rates rose in six"
- **Abstract says:** "YLL ASRs declined in all 10 countries (median change, -31.3%), whereas YLD ASRs increased in six (median change, +1.9%)."
- **Problem:** Years of life lost (age-standardized) is not the same as a death rate; the -31.3% refers to YLL ASRs.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "age-standardized years of life lost fell in all 10 countries (median -31.3%)"."

**Issue C. "Median" and "mean" qualifiers dropped from the share figures** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "pushing the disabling share of total health burden from 19.0% to 25.9%. Cardiovascular disease's share of the burden dropped sharply from 42.3% to 28.6%"
- **Abstract says:** "Median DBR increased from 19.0% to 25.9%... Mean cardiovascular-disease share decreased from 42.3% to 28.6%"
- **Problem:** These are median and mean values across the 10 selected countries, not a single pooled figure; omitting the qualifier implies a uniform trend.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "median" and "mean across the 10 countries" to the respective figures."
