## Study 4: Healthy Dietary Patterns Consistently Linked to Slower Frailty Progression in Chinese Elders
**PMID:** 41830663 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Follow-up period duration** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Following 1,374 community-dwelling older Chinese adults over 7 years"
- **Abstract says:** "A total of 1,374 community-dwelling older adults from the China Health and Nutrition Survey (CHNS), 2004-2011"
- **Problem:** The abstract indicates data from 2004-2011, which spans 7 years of data collection, but the digest presents this as "7 years" of follow-up. This is technically accurate but could be more precise about the longitudinal design.
- **Suggested fix:** "Analyzing repeated measures from 1,374 community-dwelling older Chinese adults across the 2004-2011 waves of the CHNS"

**Issue B. Number of significant dietary patterns** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Higher adherence to six of these patterns was significantly associated with slower frailty progression"
- **Abstract says:** "Higher adherence to AHEI, DASH, aMED, hPDI, LCD, and CHEI showed significant inverse dose-response associations with frailty progression"
- **Problem:** The count is correct (6 patterns), and the statement is accurate.

No significant issues. The minor issue above doesn't change meaning.

## Study 7: Oral Health Program Reduces Frailty Risk Markers in Japanese Community Dwellers
**PMID:** 42128471 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Age criterion for enrollment** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "enrolled 518 Japanese adults (median age 72, 75% women)"
- **Abstract says:** "Community-dwelling adults aged ≥50 years were recruited" and "518 (median age 72 years, 74.5% women)"
- **Problem:** The digest says 75% women, abstract says 74.5%. Minor rounding difference. Also, the enrollment was 651 with 518 completing paired data.
- **Suggested fix:** "The CAMCAM-P program enrolled 651 Japanese adults aged 50+ in a six-month intervention... Of these, 518 (median age 72, 74.5% women) with complete paired data were analyzed."

**Issue B. Mediation percentage** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "oral health improvements accounting for about 30% of the reduction in care dependency risk"
- **Abstract says:** "changes in OF scores accounted for 30.2% of the total effect"
- **Problem:** This is accurate (30% vs 30.2% is appropriate rounding).

## Study 11: Multicomponent Exercise Plus Nutrition Support Most Effective for Strength and Mobility
**PMID:** 42241794 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Effect size values** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "about 6.4 kg better grip strength and nearly 2 seconds faster mobility times"
- **Abstract says:** "For Hand Grip strength, the largest improvement was observed in multicomponent exercise combined with nutritional support versus control (effect size [ES]: 6.39; 95% CI: 0.48 to 12.30)" and "the greatest reduction in TUG completion time was found in multicomponent plus interventions versus control (ES: -1.88; 95% CI: -2.73 to -1.02)"
- **Problem:** The digest rounds appropriately (6.39 to "about 6.4 kg" and 1.88 to "nearly 2 seconds"). However, noting that the 95% CI for grip strength is quite wide (0.48-12.30) might be relevant but is acceptable to omit.

No significant factual errors found.

- **Abstract says:** "each 10-unit increase in the FI was associated with a 54% higher risk of CVD mortality (HR = 1.54, 95% CI: 1.24-1.91; P < 0.001) and a 55% higher risk of all-cause mortality (HR = 1.55; 95% CI: 1.38-1.73, P < 0.0001)"
- **Problem:** The digest reversed the percentages (says 55% all-cause, 54% CVD; abstract says 55% all-cause, 54% CVD). Wait—actually the digest says "55% higher all-cause mortality and 54% higher cardiovascular mortality" which matches the abstract (55% all-cause, 54% CVD). This is accurate.

No factual errors found.
