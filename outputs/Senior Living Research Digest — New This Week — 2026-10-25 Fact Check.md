### Study 2: People living with HIV show about 3.4 years of extra biological aging — but almost nobody has studied the oldest patients
**PMID:** 42921758 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Pooled estimate was clock-specific** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Investigators reviewed 55 studies applying DNA-methylation "epigenetic clocks" to people living with HIV across the lifespan, and pooled 14 of them in a meta-analysis."
- **Abstract says:** "A meta-analysis of HorvathAge EAA across 14 studies found a mean difference of 3.41 years (95%CI 2.22, 4.60) in PWH relative to controls."
- **Problem:** The 3.41-year estimate comes only from studies using the HorvathAge clock, not from epigenetic clocks in general; readers may assume the figure is clock-independent.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the pooled 3.41-year figure comes from 14 studies using one specific clock (HorvathAge)."

### Study 3: A brain scan touted for Parkinson's shows a real group difference — but can't yet diagnose an individual
**PMID:** 42911266 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Number of reports pooled** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers pooled 65 reports on neuromelanin-sensitive MRI of the locus coeruleus"
- **Abstract says:** "Sixty-five reports were included, of which 17 contributed to at least one main pooled outcome."
- **Problem:** Sixty-five reports were included in the review, but only 17 contributed data to any pooled analysis; saying all 65 were pooled overstates the meta-analytic base.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say researchers reviewed 65 reports and pooled data from the 17 that contributed to the main analyses."

**Issue B. Heterogeneity and prediction intervals generalized to all analyses** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "However, variation between studies was substantial and the prediction interval crossed zero in every analysis"
- **Abstract says:** "Between-study heterogeneity was substantial (I2 = 88.4%), and the 95% prediction interval crossed zero (- 2.28 to 0.38). The LC-volume analysis included five independent cohorts ... (g = - 0.87, 95% CI - 1.13 to - 0.62; P = 0.00071; I2 = 0%). The negative mean LC-signal effect was preserved across sensitivity analyses, but every specification-level prediction interval crossed zero."
- **Problem:** The substantial heterogeneity and zero-crossing prediction intervals apply to the LC-signal analyses; the volume analysis had I² = 0% and no prediction interval is reported for it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Limit the heterogeneity/prediction-interval statement to the LC-signal analyses, noting the smaller volume analysis showed no heterogeneity."

### Study 4: Low mood plus a slipping memory: the combination that doubles the risk of losing independence
**PMID:** 42925897 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Interaction was sub-additive** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Mood and memory are usually screened and treated separately, but this very large international analysis suggests they travel together as a package that predicts loss of independence years ahead"
- **Abstract says:** "joint ADL associations were below multiplicative and additive expectations (interaction ratio 0.756; relative excess risk due to interaction [RERI] -0.240)"
- **Problem:** The combined risk was lower than what the two conditions would predict if their effects simply added, so framing them as a synergistic 'package' slightly oversells the joint effect.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that although dual-domain risk was highest, the combined association was actually less than the sum of the individual associations."

### Study 5: Adding a second brain target to deep brain stimulation doesn't fix Parkinson's freezing of gait, review finds
**PMID:** 42909654 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Interpretation of stimulation-off comparisons stated too firmly** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Apparent gains seen when comparing stimulation on versus off simply reflect the benefit of DBS itself, not the extra target."
- **Abstract says:** "Secondary baseline comparisons favored co-stimulation for UPDRS-III (MD - 13.55) and FoG (Hedges' g - 0.60), but did not isolate an incremental SNr effect. ... Improvements versus stimulation-off or baseline cannot establish an incremental SNr effect."
- **Problem:** The abstract says these comparisons cannot isolate an added SNr effect, which is weaker than asserting the gains are 'simply' the benefit of DBS itself.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'these on-versus-off comparisons cannot separate any added benefit of the second target from the effect of stimulation itself.'"

**Issue B. Setting and duration not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "findings come from short-term specialist-center studies that may not reflect routine care"
- **Abstract says:** "No information on study duration or care setting is given in the abstract."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the descriptor or attribute it to the full text."

### Study 8: Even modest walking is linked to far fewer pneumonia deaths in people with dementia
**PMID:** 42925461 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline omits mild cognitive impairment** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Even modest walking is linked to far fewer pneumonia deaths in people with dementia"
- **Abstract says:** "116,563 individuals with clinically confirmed mild cognitive impairment or dementia in the Korean Dementia Relief Center registry"
- **Problem:** The cohort included people with mild cognitive impairment as well as dementia; the headline applies the finding only to dementia.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'people with cognitive impairment or dementia' in the headline, as the body text does."

**Issue B. Cause-specific comparison described as a clear contrast** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The strongest signals were not for heart disease but for infection"
- **Abstract says:** "These point estimates were numerically lower than for ischemic heart disease and cerebrovascular disease (sHRs, 0.613 and 0.630)."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes the pneumonia/COVID-19 estimates as only numerically lower than the cardiovascular estimates, with no formal test that the causes differ.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note the infection estimates were numerically — not statistically demonstrably — larger than those for heart and cerebrovascular disease."

### Study 9: The 'use it or lose it' theory of brain reserve takes a hit — and bilingualism is the crack in it
**PMID:** 42926022 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Hearing aids and brain-training not examined in the review** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Before you buy another brain-training subscription ... so money spent on hearing aids, blood pressure control, and staying active may be better placed."
- **Abstract says:** "We argue that these findings are compatible with multiple pathways involving selection, correlated socioeconomic and health factors, domain-specific knowledge and expertise, processing efficiency, and effects on brain and physical health, as well as possible changes in cognitive capacity."
- **Problem:** The review discusses education, occupational complexity, cognitively stimulating activities and bilingualism; commercial brain training and hearing aids are not evaluated, so the spending advice is the digest's extrapolation.
- **Suggested fix:** "Present the hearing-aid/blood-pressure suggestion as general prevention advice from elsewhere, not as a finding of this review."

### Study 10: A simple diabetes-free measure of insulin resistance flags which postmenopausal women accumulate multiple chronic diseases
**PMID:** 42926119 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. eGDR components not stated in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The estimated glucose disposal rate (eGDR), a score built from waist size, blood pressure status, and HbA1c"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract names eGDR as one of eleven insulin resistance surrogate indices but does not define its components."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only (though consistent with the standard eGDR formula).
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the formula to the standard definition of eGDR rather than to this paper."

**Issue B. Comparison with fasting glucose not supported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "since this analysis found that combination tracked better with future chronic disease accumulation than fasting glucose alone"
- **Abstract says:** "eGDR showed the highest discrimination among the evaluated indices in NHANES (AUC = 0.809) and in the CHARLS cumulative analysis (AUC = 0.634)."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports eGDR outperforming the other insulin-resistance surrogate indices; it does not report a head-to-head comparison against fasting glucose alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say eGDR outperformed the other ten insulin-resistance indices tested."

**Issue C. Ambiguous 'diabetes-free' in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A simple diabetes-free measure of insulin resistance"
- **Abstract says:** "Eleven IR surrogate indices were evaluated ... estimated glucose disposal rate (eGDR) showed a strong inverse association"
- **Problem:** The abstract offers no basis for calling eGDR 'diabetes-free'; the score incorporates HbA1c and the study did not restrict to women without diabetes, so the phrase may mislead.
- **Suggested fix:** "Replace with something like 'a measure built from routine clinic numbers' or 'a no-extra-test measure of insulin resistance.'"

### Study 11: Loneliness plus inactivity beat out blood pressure and diabetes as the riskiest dementia combination in a quarter-million adults
**PMID:** 42911078 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Cholesterol not a studied risk factor** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this study suggests that combination deserves as much attention as your cholesterol numbers"
- **Abstract says:** "Ten modifiable risk factors: obesity, hypertension, diabetes, hearing loss, visual loss, physical inactivity, excessive alcohol consumption, smoking, loneliness, and depression, were used to derive clusters"
- **Problem:** Cholesterol was not one of the ten factors analysed, so the study cannot speak to how the psychosocial/inactive cluster compares with lipid levels.
- **Suggested fix:** "Compare instead to factors actually studied, e.g. "deserves as much attention as your blood pressure or blood sugar.""

**Issue B. Reference group for hazard ratios unstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the cluster combining psychosocial problems (loneliness, depression) with physical inactivity carried the highest dementia risk (hazard ratio 1.23)"
- **Abstract says:** "After adjustment for sociodemographic factors, the psychosocial and physically inactive cluster was associated with the highest hazard of dementia (hazard ratio (HR), 1.23; 95% CI, 1.16-1.32)"
- **Problem:** The digest does not say the hazard ratios are relative to the lower-risk cluster, and omits that adjustment was limited to sociodemographic factors.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "compared with the lower-risk cluster, after adjustment for sociodemographic factors.""

### Study 12: Hospital staff admit they're more anxious caring for Parkinson's patients who become confused — and say the family often knows more than they do
**PMID:** 42924868 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Relatives were often right" not stated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "staff in this study described tension when relatives corrected them — but also acknowledged the relatives were often right about Parkinson's specifics"
- **Abstract says:** "relationship tensions occurred with juxtaposition of staff delirium expertise and patient/carer lived experience of PD"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes a juxtaposition of expertise, not staff conceding that relatives were frequently correct; the digest attributes an acknowledgement the abstract does not report.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to "staff described tension between their delirium expertise and the lived PD expertise of patients and carers.""

**Issue B. "Time-critical" medication detail** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "difficulty with time-critical Parkinson's medication"
- **Abstract says:** "PD specific challenges were PD medications and a reciprocal relationship between diagnostic overshadowing and identifying the cause of delirium"
- **Problem:** The abstract names PD medications as a challenge but does not specify timing as the issue.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "difficulty managing Parkinson's medications" unless the full text supports the timing detail."

### Study 13: Hip fracture outcomes are no worse for people with Parkinson's — but twice as many need rehab instead of going home
**PMID:** 42911098 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Propensity methods described as matching** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Using propensity score methods to match groups on comorbidities and injury characteristics"
- **Abstract says:** "Inverse-probability weighting with estimated propensity scores determined the association of Parkinson's with outcomes, using a logistic regression model that included covariates and injury characteristics"
- **Problem:** The analysis used inverse-probability weighting, not matching; the two are different adjustment strategies.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "using inverse-probability weighting based on propensity scores.""

**Issue B. Caveat not supported by abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Parkinson's status and severity not clinically verified"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract reports only that 208 of 5,553 registry patients "had PD" and does not describe how Parkinson's was ascertained or whether severity was recorded"
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "the abstract does not describe how Parkinson's diagnosis or severity was ascertained.""

### Study 14: A common gene variant may help explain why some Parkinson's patients develop gambling and compulsive shopping problems
**PMID:** 42908235 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. PPMI called US-based** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with impulse control disorders in the US-based cohort (odds ratio 1.56)"
- **Abstract says:** "an independent Parkinson's Progression Markers Initiative (PPMI) cohort (N = 433)"
- **Problem:** PPMI is an international multi-site study, as the digest itself states a sentence earlier; calling it "US-based" is internally inconsistent and inaccurate.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "in the PPMI cohort.""

**Issue B. In silico work described as laboratory** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Laboratory annotation linked the risk version of the gene to lower KCNJ6 expression"
- **Abstract says:** "in silico functional annotation ... expression, chromatin accessibility, and methylation quantitative trait loci (QTL) data linked risk alleles to reduced KCNJ6 expression"
- **Problem:** The annotation was computational (in silico, using existing QTL databases), not new laboratory experiments.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Computational analysis of existing genomic databases linked the risk alleles to lower KCNJ6 expression.""

**Issue C. Ancestry claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "findings come from predominantly European-ancestry populations and require independent validation"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract reports a Seville cohort and the PPMI cohort but does not describe participant ancestry"
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the ancestry claim or attribute it to the cohorts' known composition rather than this abstract."

### Study 17: Lifelong high cholesterol appears to age the immune system about six years early — and statin users looked better off
**PMID:** 42900678 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Midlife level" misdescribes the comparison** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "reached the typical midlife level of healthy controls roughly six years earlier"
- **Abstract says:** "FH patients reached the median CD4+ RTE percentage of HCs ∼6 years earlier (males: 19.12 vs 25.51 years; females: 19.18 vs 25.58 years)"
- **Problem:** The crossing ages are roughly 19 versus 25 years — young adulthood, not midlife. Calling it a "midlife level" misstates what was measured and makes the finding sound more relevant to older readers than it is, partly contradicting the digest's own caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "reached the median thymic-output level of healthy controls about six years earlier (around age 19 versus 25).""

**Issue B. Causal wording for observational data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It suggests cholesterol damages more than arteries, accelerating immune aging"
- **Abstract says:** "Hypercholesterolaemia was associated with reduced thymic output, while statin-treated patients showed partially preserved circulating CD4+ RTE levels"
- **Problem:** "Damages" and "accelerating" assert causation from a cross-sectional comparison, conflicting with the digest's own caveat that the design cannot establish causation.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as "It suggests high cholesterol is associated with signs of earlier immune aging, not just arterial disease.""

**Issue C. Repertoire diversity description** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with less diverse T-cell repertoires"
- **Abstract says:** "FH patients showed skewing towards high-frequency T-cell receptor beta clones"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports clonal skewing rather than explicitly reporting reduced diversity; the digest states a conclusion the abstract only implies.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "with T-cell repertoires skewed toward a few expanded clones.""

### Study 18: Lupus turns out to be most common in people over 85, New Zealand primary care records show
**PMID:** 42904345 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Prevalence denominator/case definition** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "They found 68 cases, yielding a crude prevalence of 63.3 per 100,000 in 2024"
- **Abstract says:** "Sixty-eight SLE cases were identified (55 definite, 6 probable, 7 possible) ... Prevalence was based on definite and probable cases. The crude prevalence of SLE estimated using primary care records was 63.3 per 100,000 people in 2024"
- **Problem:** The 63.3 per 100,000 figure was calculated from the 61 definite and probable cases, not all 68 identified cases, so the sentence links the rate to the wrong count.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "They identified 68 possible cases; the 61 classed as definite or probable yielded a crude prevalence of 63.3 per 100,000 in 2024.""
