### Study 3: When agitation lands someone with dementia in a psychiatric hospital, 30% never go home
**PMID:** 42414168 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Permanence not established** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A psychiatric hospitalization for dementia-related agitation is frequently a turning point that ends home care permanently"
- **Abstract says:** "Median length of stay was 14 days (IQR, 9-26); 54 (30%) resulted in discharge to a higher-level residential setting."
- **Problem:** The study reports discharge destination at the end of the hospitalization, not whether the move to assisted living or a nursing home was permanent; the headline's 'never go home' carries the same implication.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say that 30% of hospitalizations ended in discharge to assisted living or a nursing home rather than home, without asserting the change is permanent."

**Issue B. Causal inference about the crisis vs. treatment** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "suggesting the crisis itself, not the treatment received, is what changes where a person lives"
- **Abstract says:** "Most hospitalizations resulted in no change in the number of scheduled CNS-active medications ... while escalation occurred in 19%-28%, depending on medication class."
- **Problem:** This is a causal interpretation drawn from a retrospective descriptive study with no comparison of treatment approaches; medication counts do not capture treatment content.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as an observation: hospitalizations often ended without a change in the number of scheduled psychiatric medications, and the study cannot tell us what drove the placement change."

### Study 5: Hospice professionals are largely open to a new, controversial approach to feeding in advanced dementia
**PMID:** 42331265 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Polling participation number** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Very small convenience sample (51 attendees, 42 polled, 22 completed the survey)"
- **Abstract says:** "Fifty-one people attended the presentation. Forty-five attendees (88%) participated in audience polling, and 22 (43%) participated in both polling and the survey."
- **Problem:** 45 attendees took part in the audience polling; 42 is the denominator for the specific attitude items, not the number polled.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to '51 attendees, 45 participated in polling (42 answered the attitude items), 22 completed the survey.'"

**Issue B. Characterization of the presentation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "self-selected audience already attending a presentation advocating the approach"
- **Abstract says:** "During a presentation on MCF's definition and ethical basis, attendees engaged in audience response polling and an optional electronic survey."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes an educational presentation on definition and ethical basis, not one explicitly advocating MCF; 'advocating' adds an editorial characterization.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'a presentation on MCF's definition and ethical basis' while still noting the audience was self-selected."

### Study 6: Moving a Parent Into Memory Care Doesn't End Caregiving — It Rewrites It
**PMID:** 42720084 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Study design label** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "qualitative descriptive design with no control group"
- **Abstract says:** "This descriptive phenomenological study explored the lived experiences of 9 informal family caregivers"
- **Problem:** The abstract specifies a descriptive phenomenological design analyzed with Colaizzi's method, which is a specific qualitative methodology rather than generic qualitative description.
- **Suggested fix:** "Describe it as a descriptive phenomenological (qualitative) study using Colaizzi's method."

**Issue B. Recruitment setting** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "participants were recruited from a limited setting"
- **Abstract says:** "(no recruitment setting is described in the abstract)"
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only — the abstract does not state where or how participants were recruited.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the recruitment claim or state that recruitment details are not reported in the abstract."

**Issue C. Strength of claim from N=9** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The common assumption that placement 'relieves' family burden appears to be wrong"
- **Abstract says:** "Placement of a relative with Alzheimer's disease and related dementias (ADRD) in residential care is often assumed to reduce caregiver burden ... the findings suggest that caregiving continues in sustained and multidimensional ways after residential placement."
- **Problem:** A nine-person qualitative study can illustrate that caregiving continues, but 'appears to be wrong' overstates what this sample can establish about the general assumption.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'these nine families describe caregiving continuing in a changed form rather than ending, which challenges the assumption that placement simply relieves burden.'"

### Study 11: Diabetes Plus Depression: Together They Multiply 15-Year Dementia Risk More Than Fourfold
**PMID:** 42556583 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal/interaction framing in headline** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Diabetes Plus Depression: Together They Multiply 15-Year Dementia Risk More Than Fourfold"
- **Abstract says:** "Individuals with T2DM + MDD comorbidity had poorer cognitive performance, and a higher 15-year dementia incidence (HR = 4.44, 95% CI = [3.23,6.11])."
- **Problem:** "Multiply" implies the two conditions causally act together to raise risk; this is a retrospective observational comparison of a comorbid group against controls, with no test of interaction or multiplicative effect reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to association language, e.g. "Diabetes Plus Depression: Comorbid Group Had More Than Fourfold Higher 15-Year Dementia Rate"."

**Issue B. "Not simply additive" unsupported** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "this analysis indicates that having both is not simply additive but marks a substantially higher-risk group for cognitive decline"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract reports the dementia HR only for the comorbid group versus controls; it does not report dementia HRs for MDD-only or T2DM-only, and reports no additivity/interaction test. Only for proteins does it note 4 markers higher in the comorbid group than in either condition alone."
- **Problem:** Whether risk is additive or supra-additive cannot be judged from the abstract, which gives no single-condition dementia risk estimates.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the comorbid group showed a markedly elevated dementia rate compared with controls, and note that direct comparison with each condition alone is not reported in the abstract."

### Study 12: Delirium on Admission and Social Disadvantage Predict Three-Week-Plus Hospital Stays in Dementia
**PMID:** 42686639 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Delirium described as one of the strongest signals** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "confusion at admission is one of the strongest signals that a stay may drag on"
- **Abstract says:** "Social vulnerability (OR: 1.74, 95% CI: 1.16, 2.61), higher Nosos chronic disease burden (OR: 1.06, 95% CI: 1.01, 1.10), and presence of delirium at admission (OR: 1.55, 95% CI: 1.07, 2.26) were associated with higher odds of prolonged hospitalization."
- **Problem:** Delirium's odds ratio (1.55) was smaller than that for social vulnerability (1.74), and both confidence intervals are wide; calling it one of the strongest signals overstates its rank among the identified factors.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say delirium at admission was one of several day-one factors associated with higher odds of a prolonged stay, alongside social vulnerability and chronic disease burden."

### Study 13: Sharing Memories Works Better When Caregivers Are Partners, Not Bystanders
**PMID:** 42720117 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Comparative effectiveness claim from a scoping review** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Sharing Memories Works Better When Caregivers Are Partners, Not Bystanders"
- **Abstract says:** "This scoping review mapped literature regarding dyadic home-based reminiscence interventions... Caregiver involvement formed a "continuum of engagement"... Caregivers should be recognised as active partners capable of shaping and enhancing intervention outcomes."
- **Problem:** The review maps how caregivers were involved and what outcomes were reported; it does not compare outcomes across engagement levels or show that higher involvement works better. The digest's own caveat says exactly this, so the headline contradicts it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe descriptively, e.g. "Review Maps How Caregivers Are Involved in At-Home Reminiscence — From Bystanders to Facilitators"."

**Issue B. Benefit location claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "shared creation appears to be where the relational benefits show up"
- **Abstract says:** "Key outcomes included quality of life, dyadic relational experience, and PWD neuropsychiatric symptoms."
- **Problem:** The abstract lists outcome domains studied across all engagement types; it does not attribute relational benefits specifically to the co-creator role.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that relationship quality was one of the outcomes commonly measured, without attributing it to one engagement category."

### Study 16: Severe Memory and Function Problems Are Common in Older Adults With Bipolar Disorder
**PMID:** 42456815 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Population framed as older adults** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Severe Memory and Function Problems Are Common in Older Adults With Bipolar Disorder"
- **Abstract says:** "The study included 108 individuals with BD, of whom 32 were euthymic, while the remainder were symptomatic and 39 with major neurocognitive disorder."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports no age range or mean age for the bipolar sample and does not describe participants as older adults; the digest (and its story angles about "years" of illness) generalizes to a population the abstract does not define.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "adults with bipolar disorder" and note that participant ages are not reported in the abstract."

**Issue B. Comparison group for predictors** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "while younger age, absence of hypertension, and fewer depressive symptoms were associated with better cognitive profiles"
- **Abstract says:** "Multinomial logistic regression analyses indicated that younger age, lower rates of hypertension, and fewer depressive symptoms were associated with greater odds of belonging to the BD-P and BD-M groups compared with the Dementia."
- **Problem:** These factors distinguished the preserved/mild bipolar groups from the dementia comparison group, not simply "better cognitive profiles" within bipolar disorder.
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify that these factors predicted membership in the preserved or mild bipolar groups relative to the dementia group."

**Issue C. Mood state not noted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Cross-sectional design — cannot show that bipolar disorder or hypertension causes cognitive decline, or that these individuals will progress to dementia."
- **Abstract says:** "of whom 32 were euthymic, while the remainder were symptomatic"
- **Problem:** Most bipolar participants were symptomatic at testing, which can depress cognitive and functional scores; this limitation is absent from the caveats.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add to Caveats that only 32 of 108 bipolar participants were euthymic, so current mood symptoms may have influenced test performance."

### Study 17: A Simple Worksheet Helped Hospice Teams and Families Talk About Dementia at the End of Life
**PMID:** 42713768 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies unmeasured benefit** — Severity: Minor

- **Abstract says:** "This study provides preliminary evidence for implementation outcomes... Findings suggest the intervention can be implemented in a hospice setting, with moderate worksheet uptake and perceived value among FCPs and clinicians."

### Study 19: Phone-only telehealth is a lifeline for dementia patients in rural and underserved communities
**PMID:** 42705121 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Hype and inference beyond descriptive data** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Phone-only telehealth is a lifeline for dementia patients in rural and underserved communities... this suggests that dropping payment for plain phone visits would disproportionately cut off the dementia patients who have the fewest alternatives"
- **Abstract says:** "higher adjusted probabilities of exclusive audio-only use were observed among Black individuals (+0.4 percentage points; P < .01) and rural beneficiaries (+0.8 percentage points; P < .01)... Continued coverage for audio-only services could be a potential approach to promote digital health equity for older adults with ADRD."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports very small absolute differences and offers a hedged policy suggestion ("could be a potential approach"); "lifeline" and the prediction that patients "would be cut off" assert an outcome the descriptive claims analysis did not measure. The digest's caveat about sub-1-point differences contradicts this framing.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to match the authors, e.g. "Phone-only telehealth remains a steady option for dementia patients — and underserved groups rely on it most" and describe the policy point as the authors' suggestion."

### Study 20: Sleep problems look different in Alzheimer's versus Parkinson's dementia — and the differences are clinically useful
**PMID:** 42468248 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Magnitude of sleep-quality difference** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Parkinson's dementia patients reported notably worse subjective sleep quality"
- **Abstract says:** "PDD remained independently associated with poorer sleep quality (B = 0.355, 95% CI: 0.092-0.619, p = 0.008)"
- **Problem:** The adjusted difference on the first PSQI component (a 0–3 item) is about a third of a point — statistically significant but modest; "notably" oversells it, especially since global PSQI scores did not differ.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say PDD patients reported modestly but significantly worse scores on the subjective sleep-quality item, while overall PSQI scores were similar."

### Study 21: People with Alzheimer's can still learn new physical routines, study finds
**PMID:** 42709508 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Task description not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers tested motor sequence learning — the ability to acquire a new pattern of button-press movements — in 28 people with Alzheimer's dementia"
- **Abstract says:** "performed a computerized task that assessed general motor performance and motor sequence learning abilities"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not specify that the task involved button presses; this is a plausible but unverifiable detail from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Describe it as "a computerized task measuring general motor performance and the learning of new movement sequences," or attribute the button-press detail to the full paper."

**Issue B. Headline generalizes lab task to "physical routines"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "People with Alzheimer's can still learn new physical routines, study finds"
- **Abstract says:** "individuals with aMCI and AD dementia showed relatively preserved motor sequence learning abilities, despite some decrements in the initial rate of learning"
- **Problem:** The study measured a computerized motor sequence task, not everyday physical routines; the headline also drops the abstract's qualifier "relatively preserved" and the slower initial learning rate.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like "People with Alzheimer's can still learn new movement sequences in a lab task — though more slowly at first.""

**Issue C. Story angle extends findings to untested real-world skills** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "hands-on routines like a new exercise sequence, a walker technique, or a kitchen workaround may still be learnable"
- **Abstract says:** "Future studies should use more complex sequential movements and further explore the value of integrating motor measures in AD/aMCI assessment and monitoring."
- **Problem:** The abstract explicitly notes that more complex sequential movements remain to be studied, so applying the result to walker technique or kitchen tasks goes beyond the evidence; the Caveats flag this, but the angle states it without hedging.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a hedge in the angle itself, e.g. "the authors call for studies of more complex movements before assuming this transfers to skills like walker use.""

**Issue D. Cross-sectional design claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Cross-sectional design with no follow-up, and the biomarker prediction findings are exploratory."
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract describes group comparisons and biomarker prediction models but does not state the design as cross-sectional or the analyses as exploratory."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only, though consistent with the described methods.
- **Suggested fix:** "Retain but note the design characterization is inferred, or confirm against the full text."
