### Study 1: Two to three hours of brisk walking a week is the sweet spot for aging bones
**PMID:** 42716561 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Site-specific results simplified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Brisk walking or jogging came out on top for hip and total hip density, while combined aerobic-plus-resistance training helped the lumbar spine, and mind-body exercise (such as tai chi or yoga) improved femoral neck density"
- **Abstract says:** "For lumbar spine BMD, low certainty evidence showed that brisk walking or jogging ... and low to moderate certainty evidence showed that combined aerobic-resistance exercises ... probably result in improvements ... For femoral neck BMD, brisk walking or jogging ... and mind-body exercise ... may provide the greatest benefit. For total hip BMD, only brisk walking or jogging ... may provide the greatest benefit."
- **Problem:** The digest implies a clean division of labor by site. In fact brisk walking/jogging also improved lumbar spine BMD, and it shared top billing with mind-body exercise at the femoral neck; the phrase "hip and total hip" is ambiguous.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as: "Brisk walking or jogging improved density at all three sites and was the only clear winner at the total hip; combined aerobic-resistance training also helped the lumbar spine, and mind-body exercise helped the femoral neck.""

**Issue B. Dose threshold described as a peak** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "benefits peaked around 400-600 MET-minutes per week"
- **Abstract says:** "Minimal clinically important difference might be reached at about 400 metabolic equivalents of task in minutes per week (METs-min/week) for lumbar spine and around 600 METs-min/week for femoral neck and total hip BMD."
- **Problem:** 400-600 METs-min/week is the dose at which a minimal clinically important difference might be reached, not the point at which benefit peaks.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say benefits became clinically meaningful at roughly 400-600 MET-minutes per week, with an inverted-U shape suggesting little added gain beyond that."

### Study 2: One in three common prescriptions for older adults may be treating a side effect of another drug
**PMID:** 42727946 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Headline misstates the 37% figure** — Severity: Major

- **As written:** "One in three common prescriptions for older adults may be treating a side effect of another drug"
- **Abstract says:** "Twenty four prescribing cascades (37%) met all three prioritisation criteria."
- **Problem:** The 37% refers to the share of the 65 expert-identified candidate cascades that met the study's prioritisation thresholds — not to the share of prescriptions written for older adults. Readers could conclude a third of their medications are treating drug side effects, which the study does not show (the individual cascade incidences were around 10-12%).
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe, e.g. "Two dozen common drug pairs may mean one prescription is treating the side effect of another" or "24 of 65 suspect prescribing chains confirmed in 2.3 million older Ontarians.""

### Study 6: International drug pricing rules could cut Medicare spending by more than $6 billion a year — if manufacturers don't push back
**PMID:** 42732769 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Denominator for the 73% figure** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "for 73% of drugs, the projected Medicare savings would exceed the manufacturer's entire annual sales in the reference country setting the price"
- **Abstract says:** "For 101 (73·2%) of 138 drugs, estimated reductions in Medicare spending would exceed annual sales in the binding referenced country."

### Study 7: Frailty roughly doubles the risk of dying with cancer — across every cancer type and treatment
**PMID:** 42731927 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Across every cancer type" not supported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "across every cancer type and treatment"
- **Abstract says:** "Subgroup and meta-regression analyses did not identify significant association of age, sex, body mass index, cancer stage, performance status, or treatment modalities with frailty and mortality."
- **Problem:** The abstract lists age, sex, BMI, stage, performance status and treatment modality as subgroup variables; cancer type is not among the reported moderators, so consistency across every tumor type cannot be claimed from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "regardless of stage, performance status or treatment" and drop "every cancer type.""

**Issue B. Causal verb in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Frailty roughly doubles the risk of dying with cancer"
- **Abstract says:** "Frailty was independently associated with higher all-cause mortality (n = 162; adjusted HR, 1.98; 95% CI, 1.85-2.09)"
- **Problem:** "Doubles" reads causally for a pooled analysis of observational studies, although the digest's caveat correctly notes frailty is a marker of risk.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Frailty is linked to roughly double the risk of dying...""

### Study 8: Big Parkinson's study finds levodopa isn't to blame for the freezing that stops people mid-step
**PMID:** 42715510 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Null result stated as exoneration** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the association disappeared in both cohorts"
- **Abstract says:** "levodopa status showed a nonsignificant effect size on FOG (NS-Park odds ratio [OR] 1.84, 95% CI 0.94-3.61, p = 0.074; PPMI OR 0.76, 95% CI 0.38-1.51, p = 0.430) ... with the effect estimates significantly attenuated despite residual uncertainty"
- **Problem:** In NS-Park the adjusted odds ratio remained 1.84 with a borderline p value (0.074); the association was substantially attenuated and no longer statistically significant rather than absent, and the authors flag residual uncertainty.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the association was strongly attenuated and no longer statistically significant in either cohort, with the NS-Park estimate still borderline."

### Study 9: A clinic built for the last stage of Parkinson's got 69% of patients into hospice before they died
**PMID:** 42727035 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal framing of an uncontrolled chart review** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "this shows a longitudinal team model can hold onto them and change how they die"
- **Abstract says:** "This clinic highlights the feasibility and value of a longitudinal, outpatient interdisciplinary movement-neuropalliative care model ... hospice utilization at the time of death substantially exceeded rates reported in usual care."
- **Problem:** A single-center retrospective series with no comparison group cannot show that the model changed outcomes; the abstract claims feasibility and value, not effect. This also sits uneasily with the digest's own caveat about the lack of a control group.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "suggests a longitudinal team model is feasible for a group often lost to follow-up, with hospice enrollment at death well above published usual-care rates.""

**Issue B. Headline denominator and verb** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "got 69% of patients into hospice before they died"
- **Abstract says:** "Among 58 deceased patients, 40 (69.0%) were enrolled in hospice at the time of death."
- **Problem:** The 69% applies only to the 58 patients who died, not to all 175, and "got ... into" attributes the result to the clinic.
- **Suggested fix:** ""69% of the clinic's patients who died were enrolled in hospice at the time of death.""

### Study 11: Pharmacist-led medication cleanups cut risky prescriptions in older cancer patients — but nobody has measured whether people feel better
**PMID:** 42731364 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Safety not assessed** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this review shows a formal review can safely prune that list"
- **Abstract says:** "Deprescribing interventions in older adults with cancer appear feasible and are associated with reduced inappropriate medication use. However, confidence in this finding is limited by the small number of studies with serious risk of bias."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports feasibility and reduced PIM use, not safety; adverse-outcome data were among the missing patient-centred outcomes.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "a formal review can reduce that list, though safety and patient outcomes were not adequately measured.""

**Issue B. Source of 62.5–73% range** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Between 62.5% and 73% of flagged inappropriate medications were successfully stopped"
- **Abstract says:** "Four studies (n = 365) reported PIM deprescribing rates, ranging from 62.5% to 73%."
- **Problem:** The range comes from only four of the eight studies (365 of 1,018 participants), which the digest sentence does not convey.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "in the four studies (365 patients) that reported deprescribing rates.""

**Issue C. Strength of the 'nobody measured' claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "almost no study measured whether patients ended up with fewer falls, fewer hospital stays, or better quality of life"
- **Abstract says:** "Evidence for broader patient-centred outcomes was limited and primarily narrative."
- **Problem:** The abstract says such evidence was limited and narrative, not that it was essentially absent.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "evidence on falls, hospital stays and quality of life was limited and reported only narratively.""

### Study 13: Steroid pills raise fracture risk by 70% — and diabetes doesn't make it worse
**PMID:** 42722898 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Median duration vs median follow-up** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "median treatment duration in this cohort was just 35 days"
- **Abstract says:** "Median follow-up time was 35 days (with a mean of 212 days) during GC treatment and 731 days in the pre-treatment period."
- **Problem:** The 35 days is median follow-up time during treatment, which the abstract does not equate with treatment duration; the mean was 212 days.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "median follow-up during treatment was 35 days (mean 212 days).""

**Issue B. Speed of risk onset** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "since elevated fracture risk appeared quickly after treatment began"
- **Abstract says:** "These results were similar across subgroup and sensitivity analyses... when allowing the effects of GC treatment to vary smoothly as a function of time-since-initiation"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports that results were robust to time-since-initiation modelling but gives no estimate showing risk rises quickly. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the timing claim or attribute it as not reported in the abstract."

**Issue C. Named drug** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "at least one prescription for a systemic glucocorticoid such as prednisolone"
- **Abstract says:** "Individuals with or without T2D receiving systemic GC treatment (≥ 1 redeemed prescription) between 2000 and 2018"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not name prednisolone or any specific agent. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "a systemic glucocorticoid" without naming a specific drug, or verify from the full text."

### Study 14: How long your parents lived shows up in your blood at 70 — including a marker of Alzheimer's
**PMID:** 42726198 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Parental longevity was three groups, not two** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "comparing people whose parents both lived to 85 (17.2%) with those whose parents did not"
- **Abstract says:** "PL groups were categorised as high if both parents survived to age 85 (17.2%); medium if one parent had survived (43.3%), and low if neither parent had survived to age 85 (39.4%)."
- **Problem:** The study used a three-level exposure; the digest collapses medium and low into a single comparison group.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note the three categories (both parents, one parent, neither reaching 85)."

**Issue B. Selective list of associations** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Long-lived parents were associated with less hypertension, higher MMSE cognitive scores, lower BMI, less smoking, and lower levels of inflammatory markers (IL-6, CRP) and homocysteine."
- **Abstract says:** "higher PL was associated with higher MMSE score, total cholesterol, HDL-cholesterol (HDL-c) and LDL-cholesterol (LDL-c), lower BMI, homocysteine and inflammatory markers (IL-6, CRP) levels, and less smoking"
- **Problem:** The digest omits that high parental longevity was also associated with higher total and LDL cholesterol, which cuts against the uniformly favourable picture presented.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that total and LDL cholesterol were higher, not lower, in the high-parental-longevity group."

**Issue C. 'Outperformed genetic scores'** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a simple two-question family history — did each parent reach 85? — outperformed genetic scores as a marker of broad biological aging in this cohort"
- **Abstract says:** "PGLSs were mainly related to socioeconomic and cardiovascular factors, while individuals with long-lived parents, in addition, had several other characteristics of longevity"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes a broader set of associations for parental longevity but reports no head-to-head statistical comparison establishing that it 'outperformed' the polygenic scores.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "parental longevity was associated with a wider range of ageing markers than the polygenic scores in this cohort.""

### Study 18: Nine years of data show hip fracture leaves a lasting mark — and the 70s may be the riskiest decade
**PMID:** 42721595 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal language for observational data** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Hip fracture caused an immediate jump in everyday-activity difficulties"
- **Abstract says:** "Hip fracture was associated with an immediate increase of 0.580 ADL difficulties (P < .001), followed by gradual annual recovery."
- **Problem:** The body text asserts causation from a prospective observational cohort, directly contradicting the digest's own caveat that the study "cannot prove causation."
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Hip fracture was associated with an immediate increase of about 0.58 ADL difficulties.""

**Issue B. Wrong age band in story angle** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "while residents 85+ appear to keep declining after the fracture"
- **Abstract says:** "whereas those aged ≥80 years exhibited increasing ADL disability during recovery"
- **Problem:** The continued-decline group was aged 80 and older, not 85 and older; the angle would misdirect who gets ongoing maintenance therapy.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change "85+" to "80 and older," matching the body text."

**Issue C. Group mean read as individual outcome** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this long-term data suggests most people never fully return to their prefracture level of independence"
- **Abstract says:** "Difficulties remained 25% above prefracture levels at 6 years after fracture."
- **Problem:** The 25% figure is a group-level average difference; the abstract does not report the proportion of individuals who fully recovered.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "on average, difficulty levels remained about 25% above prefracture levels six years on.""

**Issue D. Participant age range** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "25,873 participants aged 45 and older in the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study"
- **Abstract says:** "including 25,873 participants (719 with incident hip fracture)"
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no age range, only "Chinese older adults." Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the age range or source it from the full text."

### Study 19: A simple height-weight calculation flagged older adults at nearly triple the risk of losing independence
**PMID:** 42715815 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Weight change not studied** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Unintentional weight loss and shrinking body size in later life are not harmless; if your weight has been drifting down, raise it with your clinician"
- **Abstract says:** "BMR was estimated using the revised Harris-Benedict equation and categorized into quartiles... Participants in the lowest estimated BMR quartile had a higher risk of incident disability than those in the highest quartile (hazard ratio, 2.8; 95% CI, 2.4-3.1)."
- **Problem:** The study assessed baseline estimated BMR, not change in weight over time, so it cannot speak to weight that is 'drifting down.'
- **Suggested fix:** "Frame the advice around small body size/low estimated BMR as a risk marker, and note that weight change over time was not examined."

### Study 20: Folate and homocysteine levels tracked how fast older adults' bodies aged — over four and a half years
**PMID:** 42720848 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Which ageing measure the year estimates apply to** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Higher folate was linked to slower biological aging (each doubling associated with roughly 1.1 years slower), while higher homocysteine was linked to faster aging (each doubling associated with roughly 3.2 years faster)."
- **Abstract says:** "Longitudinally, each doubling in homocysteine was associated with ~ 3.2 years faster Phenotypic Age acceleration, whereas each doubling in folate was associated with ~ 1.1 years slower aging."
- **Problem:** These year figures come specifically from Phenotypic Age acceleration (the clinical-lab measure), not from biological ageing measured across both approaches.
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify "on the clinical-lab Phenotypic Age measure" when quoting the 1.1- and 3.2-year figures."

### Study 23: A sleeping-pill swap that actually works — and older adults did best
**PMID:** 42717137 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline asserts efficacy from an uncontrolled design** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A sleeping-pill swap that actually works — and older adults did best"
- **Abstract says:** "DORA treatment for at least six months may support BZ/BZRA dose reduction among long-term users, particularly in older patients."
- **Problem:** 'Actually works' states efficacy, while the study is a mirror-image claims analysis with no comparison group and the authors use 'may support'. The digest's own caveat concedes causation can't be established, so the headline pulls against it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like 'A sleeping-pill swap linked to lower benzodiazepine doses — and older adults did best'."

**Issue B. 'Roughly doubled' understates the odds ratios** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "being 65 or older roughly doubled the odds of both outcomes (OR 2.49 for halving, OR 2.64 for stopping)"
- **Abstract says:** "age ≥ 65 years was independently associated with both ≥ 50% reduction (odds ratio [OR] 2.485; 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.190-5.192; p = 0.015) and discontinuation (OR 2.636; 95% CI 1.158-6.000; p = 0.021)"
- **Problem:** ORs of ~2.5 and ~2.6 are closer to two-and-a-half times the odds than 'roughly doubled'; the wide confidence intervals also aren't conveyed.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'about two and a half times the odds' and note the wide confidence intervals."

### Study 25: Men caring for a spouse or parent with dementia want practical help — not a support-group circle
**PMID:** 42716444 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline overstates rejection of emotional-support formats** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Men caring for a spouse or parent with dementia want practical help — not a support-group circle"
- **Abstract says:** "Results highlight male caregivers expression for support aligned with their coping styles, and interventions should prioritise practical and educational components."
- **Problem:** The abstract says men wanted support matched to their coping styles and that practical/educational components should be prioritised; it does not report that men reject support groups. 'Selective help-seeking' was a theme, not refusal of group support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as 'Men caring for someone with dementia want support that matches how they cope — practical and educational first'."

**Issue B. Unsupported 'growing share' claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Sons, husbands and male partners make up a large and growing share of unpaid dementia caregivers"

- **Problem:** The abstract supports 'a significant proportion' but says nothing about the share growing over time. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop 'and growing' or cite a separate source for the trend."
