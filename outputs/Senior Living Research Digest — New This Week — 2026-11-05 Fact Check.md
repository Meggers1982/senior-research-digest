### Study 1: Dialysis or no dialysis? A landmark trial finds no clear winner for frail older adults with kidney failure
**PMID:** 42982076 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Non-significance presented as equivalence** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "for this group, choosing not to dialyze does not appear to cost quality-adjusted survival."
- **Abstract says:** "mean QALYs were 1·354 ... and 1·472 ... (adjusted mean difference -0·147 [95% CI -0·350 to 0·056] p=0·16) ... we found no evidence that either preparing for responsive management or preparing for renal dialysis was superior in terms of QALYs"
- **Problem:** This was a superiority trial; a non-significant result with a confidence interval extending to -0.35 QALYs does not demonstrate equivalence. The abstract also reports 157 (69%) deaths in the responsive management group versus 136 (63%) in the dialysis group, which the digest does not mention.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "the trial found no evidence that either path was superior on quality-adjusted life-years, though the comparison cannot rule out a modest advantage for dialysis.""

**Issue B. "First randomized comparison" claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "This is the first randomized comparison of dialysis versus conservative kidney care in older, frailer patients"
- **Abstract says:** "The comparative effectiveness of conservative kidney management and dialysis in older people ... is unknown ... This trial provides novel comparative information for clinical decision making."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes the information as novel but never claims the trial is the first of its kind. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "This is the first trial to provide direct randomized comparative evidence in this group, according to the authors.""

**Issue C. Harms outcomes not prespecified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Hospital admissions were similar, while complications from dialysis access procedures were substantially lower in the non-dialysis group."
- **Abstract says:** "Specific harms outcomes were not prespecified, but hospital admission rates were similar in the two groups and dialysis access complications were significantly lower in the prepare for responsive management group (adjusted incident rate ratio 0·41 ...), based on 74 events in 36 participants"
- **Problem:** The digest reports these secondary harms without noting they were not prespecified and rest on only 74 events in 36 participants.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that harms outcomes were not prespecified and were based on a small number of events."

### Study 2: Elder abuse doesn't just harm in the moment — it predicts hospitalization, disability and earlier death
**PMID:** 42981484 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Screening recommendation beyond abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It positions mistreatment of older adults as a measurable medical risk factor — on par with other conditions clinicians screen for — not solely a social or legal problem, strengthening the case for routine screening in primary care and long-term care settings."
- **Abstract says:** "More attention should be paid to recognizing the underestimated phenomenon of MOA, which represents not only a human rights violation but also an additional risk factor for the health of the older population."
- **Problem:** The abstract calls for greater recognition but makes no comparison with other screened conditions and does not recommend routine screening programs; the comparison "on par with other conditions clinicians screen for" is not supported by any reported effect-size benchmarking.
- **Suggested fix:** "Trim to "it supports treating mistreatment as a health risk factor and paying closer clinical attention to it," dropping the screening-program comparison."

### Study 4: Quercetin, cocoa flavonoids, green tea extract: the evidence for "muscle-protecting" polyphenol supplements is thinner than the marketing
**PMID:** 42983146 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Trial count wording** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "fewer than a half-dozen human trials exist in aging muscle"
- **Abstract says:** "Six randomized controlled trials were identified; five had reported outcome data and were included in the synthesis"
- **Problem:** Six trials were identified, which is exactly a half-dozen, not fewer; five had usable results. The digest body correctly says six earlier, so the "Why it matters" line is internally inconsistent.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "just six human trials exist, only five with reported results.""

**Issue B. Sarcopenia status of participants** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "most participants did not have confirmed sarcopenia"
- **Abstract says:** "Larger, standardized randomized trials in populations with confirmed sarcopenia, incorporating direct neuromuscular assessment, are required before clinical translation."
- **Problem:** The abstract calls for future trials in confirmed sarcopenia but does not state what proportion of current participants had it. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "the authors call for future trials in populations with confirmed sarcopenia.""

### Study 5: A lower-cost denosumab alternative matches the original in a 514-woman osteoporosis trial
**PMID:** 42980777 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Switch analysis covered safety, not bone density** — Severity: Minor

### Study 7: Feeling younger than your age predicts better sleep four years later — and vice versa
**PMID:** 42986003 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Bidirectional claim in headline** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Feeling younger than your age predicts better sleep four years later — and vice versa"
- **Abstract says:** "In longitudinal analysis, subjective age discrepancy was a predictor of sleep quality 4 years later, and subjective age discrepancy predicted sleep quality both cross-sectionally and longitudinally."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports only one longitudinal direction — subjective age discrepancy predicting later sleep quality. It does not report sleep quality predicting later subjective age, so "and vice versa" asserts an unreported reverse direction.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "and vice versa," or replace with "and the two were linked cross-sectionally as well.""

### Study 9: A treatable cause of unsteady walking in older adults may be detectable with a smartphone
**PMID:** 41604771 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Story angle contradicts caveat on readiness** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "objective gait tracking tools are becoming accessible enough for routine use"
- **Abstract says:** "Portable accelerometry offers an accessible, objective tool for diagnosis, monitoring, and prognostication in iNPH, with growing potential for routine clinical integration."
- **Problem:** The digest's own Caveats state these tools are "not yet validated for routine screening," while the story angle tells senior living staff they are accessible enough for routine use — an internal contradiction, and the abstract describes only "growing potential" for routine integration.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "objective gait tracking tools are moving toward clinical use but are not yet validated for routine screening.""

**Issue B. Overstated reversibility of iNPH** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a condition causing gait disturbance, cognitive decline and urinary incontinence that can often be reversed with shunt surgery"
- **Abstract says:** "iNPH is a reversible cause of gait disturbance, cognitive decline, and urinary incontinence in older adults. Early detection with shunt surgery can significantly improve symptoms."
- **Problem:** The abstract says shunt surgery can significantly improve symptoms; "often be reversed" implies a frequency and completeness of reversal the abstract does not quantify.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "a condition whose symptoms can significantly improve with shunt surgery when detected early.""

### Study 11: Statin users were 20% less likely to develop depression in a large study of adults over 70
**PMID:** 42982348 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Existing-depression subgroup nuance omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "but statins showed no benefit for people who already had depression"
- **Abstract says:** "No significant association was observed in existing depression cohort; while protective association was observed in higher PGS (RR 0.84, 95% CI 0.73-0.95)."
- **Problem:** The blanket "no benefit" statement overlooks the significant protective association reported in the high-genetic-risk subgroup of those with existing depression.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add: "...with the exception of those at highest genetic risk, where a modest protective association persisted (RR 0.84).""

**Issue B. Outcome is symptom scale, not diagnosed depression** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Statin users were 20% less likely to develop depression in a large study of adults over 70"
- **Abstract says:** "Statin use was associated with a lower risk of depression (RR, 0.80; 95% CI, 0.76-0.84)... a lower risk of depressive symptom occurrence (prevention)"
- **Problem:** The headline says "develop depression" while the measured outcome was depressive symptom occurrence; the digest's own caveat acknowledges this, but the headline does not.
- **Suggested fix:** "Headline: "Statin users were 20% less likely to develop depressive symptoms...""

### Study 12: Frailty can be reversed after heart pump surgery — and those who improve live markedly longer
**PMID:** 42981223 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mechanistic claim beyond the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this analysis suggests frailty driven by failing circulation can reverse once the heart is supported"
- **Abstract says:** "Frailty, however, proved substantially reversible after LVAD support, and its improvement was associated with markedly better survival."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports that frailty improved after implantation but does not establish that the frailty was driven by failing circulation; this is the digest's causal interpretation.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "frailty measured before implantation often improved after the device was in place, suggesting it is not necessarily permanent.""

**Issue B. Unadjusted results omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Obesity (40% of patients) was linked after adjustment to higher mortality, heart failure hospitalization, infection and arrhythmia"
- **Abstract says:** "Unadjusted 2-year outcomes were similar between groups (freedom from the primary endpoint: 75.1% vs 77.1%, log-rank P = .34; survival: 79.9% vs 81.2%, P = .57). After covariate adjustment, obesity was independently associated with a higher risk..."
- **Problem:** The digest reports only the adjusted result; readers are not told that unadjusted two-year survival was essentially identical between obese and non-obese recipients, which tempers the obesity signal.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a clause: "unadjusted two-year survival was similar between obese and non-obese recipients, but after adjustment obesity was linked to...""

### Study 13: A blood test for Parkinson's fall risk? Not yet — but an inflammation clue emerges
**PMID:** 42543049 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Direction of CCL11 difference not stated in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "CCL11 levels differed significantly between patients with and without symptomatic drops in blood pressure on standing.""

### Study 19: An AI that reads ordinary health questionnaires spotted Parkinson's with near-perfect accuracy
**PMID:** 42983008 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Missing-data tolerance not demonstrated in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Because it encodes question meaning, it tolerates missing answers that typically degrade conventional screening models."
- **Abstract says:** "Conventional models are built for each questionnaire, and missing responses degrade their performance. Quest2Dx uses language modeling to encode the meaning of each question and answer, allowing it to transfer across questionnaires while enhancing interpretability."
- **Problem:** The abstract states that missing responses degrade conventional models and that Quest2Dx transfers across questionnaires, but it does not report a result showing Quest2Dx tolerates missing answers.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "the authors position this question-meaning approach as a way around the missing-answer problem that degrades conventional models.""

**Issue B. "Near-perfect accuracy" overstates an AUROC** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "spotted Parkinson's with near-perfect accuracy"
- **Abstract says:** "Quest2Dx achieved areas under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROCs) of 0.977 and 0.974, respectively, outperforming existing methods."
- **Problem:** AUROC is a discrimination metric, not accuracy, and the values come from retrospective cohorts of already-diagnosed participants; "near-perfect accuracy" reads as hype for a non-prospective model.
- **Suggested fix:** "Headline: "An AI that reads ordinary health questionnaires separated Parkinson's patients from others with high accuracy in research datasets.""
