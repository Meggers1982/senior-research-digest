### Study 3: Grip strength or memory: which fades first may depend on your age and daily functioning
**PMID:** 42950710 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal phrasing for an observational association** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "diabetes raised the risk of crossing into low reserve in both domains"
- **Abstract says:** "Diabetes was associated with higher intensities in both domains, but its pathway contrast remained uncertain (0.751, 95% CI 0.554-1.019; P=0.058)."
- **Problem:** "Raised the risk" states causation; the abstract reports an association from pooled observational cohorts and explicitly says the findings do not establish a biological sequence.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "diabetes was associated with faster transitions into low reserve in both domains.""

### Study 4: Rural cancer patients can get good palliative care close to home — but the evidence for how to deliver it is thin
**PMID:** 42951088 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Caveat overstates absence of evidence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "no comparative or economic evaluation available"
- **Abstract says:** "Comparative and economic evaluation of scalable rural models remains limited."
- **Problem:** The abstract says such evaluation is limited, not absent; the digest body correctly says "barely exist" but the caveat says none exists.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change caveat to "comparative and economic evaluation remains very limited.""

**Issue B. Population framed as older adults** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Older adults living in rural areas with advanced cancer are the group most likely to be forced to travel long distances or go without specialist end-of-life care"
- **Abstract says:** "Systematic review of rural palliative care models for malignant disease... Access to palliative care for people with advanced cancer in rural settings remains inequitable due to workforce shortages, geographic isolation, and limited formalised care structures."
- **Problem:** The review covered people with advanced cancer in rural settings generally, not an older-adult population; the relevance claim about older adults specifically is not supported by the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the review covered rural patients with advanced cancer of all ages, many of whom are older adults."

**Issue C. Headline slightly stronger than conclusion** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Rural cancer patients can get good palliative care close to home"
- **Abstract says:** "Rural palliative care models may support symptom management, access and care closer to home"
- **Problem:** "Can get good palliative care" asserts more certainty than the hedged "may support" conclusion drawn from mostly observational, heterogeneous studies.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "Rural cancer patients may be able to get palliative care closer to home.""

### Study 5: A 12-week chatbot "brain workout" nudged memory scores — and brain wiring — in healthy older adults
**PMID:** 42950244 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Single-site claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Small sample (N=72), single trial site, short 12-week follow-up"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract reports a single-blind randomized controlled trial of 72 participants but does not state the number of study sites; author affiliations list both Ewha Womans University Mokdong Hospital and Ewha Womans University Seoul Hospital."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only, and affiliations suggest more than one hospital may have been involved.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "single trial site" or change to "conducted at a single Korean university hospital system.""

### Study 6: In a brain inflammation emergency, delay is costliest for the patients who don't look textbook — and for those over 65
**PMID:** 42951877 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Age odds ratio presented without subgroup context** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Being 65 or older roughly quintupled the odds of a poor outcome"
- **Abstract says:** "Ab+ outcomes were dictated by failure to achieve early functional remission (discharge mRS: OR = 3.74; CASE: OR = 1.56; both p < 0.01) and age ≥ 65 years (OR = 5.78, p = 0.040). Conversely, Ab- outcomes were... driven by baseline demographic and phenotypic features: age ≥ 65 and atypical presentation."
- **Problem:** The OR of 5.78 comes from the antibody-positive cohort specifically; the digest presents it as applying to the whole 498-patient sample. Age ≥65 mattered in both serostatus groups, but only the antibody-positive effect size is quantified.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the context: "among antibody-positive patients, being 65 or older roughly quintupled the odds of a poor outcome (OR 5.78), and age 65+ also predicted poor outcomes in antibody-negative patients.""

### Study 8: Half of nursing home residents on opioids are also taking drugs that amplify them
**PMID:** 42951674 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Predicted" in a cross-sectional study** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Pain and anxiety predicted this combination"
- **Abstract says:** "Pain and other comorbid conditions (e.g., anxiety) were significantly associated with such uses (p < 0.001). In this cross-sectional study design..."
- **Problem:** "Predicted" implies a temporal sequence; the study is cross-sectional and reports associations only. The digest's own caveat flags the cross-sectional design, so this is an internal inconsistency.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Pain and anxiety were associated with this combination.""

### Study 9: Weight-loss drugs in older adults: specialists spell out when the risks outweigh the pounds
**PMID:** 42952307 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Hedged evidence stated as fact** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "They highlight that a substantial share of the weight lost on these drugs in major trials is lean muscle mass"
- **Abstract says:** "evidence from major clinical trials suggests that a substantial proportion of medication-associated weight loss agents may result in loss of lean mass, potentially posing risks for this population"
- **Problem:** The abstract says trials "suggest" weight loss "may" include lean mass loss; the digest presents it as established.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "They highlight evidence suggesting that a substantial proportion of the weight lost on these drugs may be lean mass.""

**Issue B. Underrepresentation claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "These drugs are being prescribed rapidly to people in their 70s and 80s who were largely underrepresented in the trials"
- **Abstract says:** "These therapies are increasingly prescribed... We also discuss identifying priority areas for future research, including age-stratified clinical trials and the routine inclusion of body composition and physical function outcomes."
- **Problem:** The abstract notes a need for age-stratified trials but does not state that people in their 70s and 80s were underrepresented in existing trials, nor characterize prescribing in that age band. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to "the authors call for age-stratified trials, which do not yet exist for this population.""

### Study 10: Low levels of one omega-3 fat predicted who became frail over the next decade
**PMID:** 42950709 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Effect size reported without its comparison unit** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Higher plasma DHA was associated with an 18% lower risk of developing frailty after adjusting for demographics and medical history"
- **Abstract says:** "The incidence of frailty was inversely associated with plasma level of DHA (hazard ratio [HR] per inter-quintile range: 0.82, 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.69-0.97; p=0.019)"
- **Problem:** The 18% reduction corresponds to a specific contrast — per inter-quintile range — not to "higher" DHA generally; readers may assume it applies to any increase.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the unit: "an 18% lower risk comparing the top to the bottom of the DHA distribution (HR 0.82 per inter-quintile range).""

**Issue B. "Strongest for" vs significant only for** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the signal was specific to DHA, and strongest for the low-physical-activity and exhaustion criteria"
- **Abstract says:** "Plasma level of DHA was inversely associated with the incidence of two frailty criteria-low physical activity and exhaustion."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports that DHA was associated with two of the five criteria, not that the association was "strongest" for them relative to the others; ranking of effect sizes is not given.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "and present only for two of the five criteria — low physical activity and exhaustion.""

### Study 11: Caregivers are going broke, and almost nobody has studied whether help with money helps
**PMID:** 41371759 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Population narrowed to older adults** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Lost wages and out-of-pocket costs are among the heaviest burdens families carry when caring for a seriously ill older relative"
- **Abstract says:** "Financial challenges are a significant issue navigated by family caregivers of individuals across the lifespan due to employment challenges and costs of caregiving"
- **Problem:** The review covered caregivers of care recipients across the lifespan and across diverse health conditions, not specifically caregivers of seriously ill older adults.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the review covered family caregivers across the lifespan and that its relevance to older-adult caregiving is an extrapolation."

**Issue B. Headline asserts financial hardship the review did not measure** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Caregivers are going broke, and almost nobody has studied whether help with money helps"
- **Abstract says:** "Direct measurement of income or perceived financial well-being was not assessed as an outcome."
- **Problem:** The review explicitly found no study measured income or financial well-being, so the digest's own headline asserts a degree of financial ruin the evidence base did not quantify.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to something like 'Caregivers face real financial strain — and almost no studies have tested whether financial help works.'"

**Issue C. Unsupported claim about proactive offering** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "since this review suggests such supports are rarely offered proactively"
- **Abstract says:** "Financial interventions ranged from embedded supports in larger programs (n=8) to stand-alone initiatives (n=3) across diverse health conditions and economic contexts."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes where financial supports sat within interventions; it says nothing about whether real-world services offer them proactively.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to 'this review found stand-alone financial supports are rare in the research literature' or drop the claim."

### Study 12: A single nurse making follow-up phone calls can free up dozens of hospital beds — but the payoff depends heavily on the hospital
**PMID:** 42946540 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Bed-days reported as beds in headline** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "A single nurse making follow-up phone calls can free up dozens of hospital beds"
- **Abstract says:** "CTraC was estimated (base-case) to reduce non-VA hospital readmission charges by $22,369 and free 55.5 VA bed-days-of-care annually."
- **Problem:** 55.5 bed-days per year is roughly one bed occupied for two months, not dozens of beds; the headline unit error substantially overstates the capacity benefit even though the body text gets it right.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'can free up about 55 hospital bed-days a year' or 'nearly two months of bed capacity'."

**Issue B. 'Pays for itself' not established in abstract** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "can pay for itself, though not equally everywhere"
- **Abstract says:** "Investments in transitional care may reduce readmissions and generate economic benefit, but the magnitude of returns varied across VA hospitals."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports modelled savings and freed bed-days but never states that savings exceeded the cost of the nurse; whether the program is cost-neutral or cost-saving is unverifiable from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the model estimated 'economic benefit that varied by facility' rather than asserting the program pays for itself."

### Study 14: Weak bones plus weak muscles: the combination doubles death risk in older adults
**PMID:** 41818462 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. 'Held across five definitions' not shown in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the pattern held across five different definitions of sarcopenia"
- **Abstract says:** "Five definitions for diagnosis of SP were considered: FNIH1 and FNIH2, EWGSOP2, AWGS, and IWG... (EWGSOP2: MBMDD: HR: 1.64...; NHANES: HR: 1.45...)"
- **Problem:** The abstract states five definitions were examined but reports hazard ratios only for EWGSOP2; consistency of results across all five cannot be confirmed from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'five definitions of sarcopenia were compared; the reported figures use the EWGSOP2 definition' and mark the cross-definition consistency as unverifiable from the abstract."

**Issue B. Causal phrasing in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the combination doubles death risk in older adults"
- **Abstract says:** "osteoporosis, sarcopenia, and OSP were all significantly associated with increased mortality risk"
- **Problem:** 'Doubles death risk' reads as a causal effect, whereas these are adjusted associations in observational cohorts; the body text correctly uses 'linked to'.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'is linked to roughly double the death rate'."

**Issue C. Sarcopenia range drawn from one definition** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "sarcopenia alone to a 45%-64% higher risk"
- **Abstract says:** "sarcopenia (EWGSOP2: MBMDD: HR: 1.64, 95% CI: 1.52-1.78; NHANES: HR: 1.45, 95% CI: 1.30-1.62)"
- **Problem:** The 45%–64% range comes specifically from the EWGSOP2 definition in the two cohorts, not from the full set of definitions as the sentence implies.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add 'using the EWGSOP2 definition' when giving the sarcopenia-alone figures."

### Study 15: A broken hip or spine years ago may raise your fracture risk more than the standard calculator says
**PMID:** 41810867 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline includes hip fracture among underestimated types** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A broken hip or spine years ago may raise your fracture risk more than the standard calculator says"
- **Abstract says:** "observed-to-predicted ratios indicated underestimation of MOF risk for non-recent vertebral (1.32) and multiple fractures (1.34), and underestimation of hip fracture risk for non-recent multiple fractures (1.70)"
- **Problem:** Underestimation was demonstrated for prior vertebral fractures and for multiple prior fractures; a single prior hip fracture is not shown in the abstract to be underestimated, though the body text is correct.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword the headline to 'A spine fracture — or several past breaks — may raise your risk more than the standard calculator says'."

### Study 16: In Parkinson's disease, depression may explain much of the link between advancing disease and memory problems
**PMID:** 42949295 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Parallel-model result reported without instrument caveat** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "When anxiety and depression were analyzed together, only the depression pathway remained significant"
- **Abstract says:** "In the parallel model, the indirect pathway through depression remained significant with MoCA but not with CMMS."
- **Problem:** The digest omits that the surviving depression pathway applied to MoCA scores, not to the CMMS, overstating how consistent the parallel-model finding was.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add 'for MoCA scores, though not for the CMMS'."

**Issue B. Cognition described as memory** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the link between advancing disease and memory problems"
- **Abstract says:** "Cognitive function was evaluated with the Chinese Mini-Mental Status (CMMS) and Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA)."
- **Problem:** Both instruments measure global cognition (orientation, attention, executive function, language as well as recall), so 'memory problems' is narrower than the outcome actually studied.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'thinking and memory scores' or 'cognitive test scores' instead of 'memory problems'."

**Issue C. 'Explain much of' overstates mediated share** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "depression may explain much of the link"
- **Abstract says:** "HAMD accounted for 23.74% of the association... with HAMD accounting for 35.12%"
- **Problem:** A mediated share of roughly a quarter to a third is a substantial but minority portion; 'much of' implies more, and 'explain' nudges toward causality the authors disclaim.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'may account for roughly a quarter to a third of the statistical link'."
