# Fact-Check Report: Senior Living Research Digest — New This Week — September 2026

**Primary audience:** older adults and seniors | **Secondary audience:** families, caregivers, and senior living professionals

### Study 1: How you fill out a questionnaire may hint at dementia years before diagnosis
**PMID:** 42760255 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unstated lead time** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "How you fill out a questionnaire may hint at dementia years before diagnosis"
- **Abstract says:** "Associations with subsequent dementia risk were examined within each cohort using Cox proportional hazards models adjusted for demographic covariates and accounting for death as a competing risk."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports prediction of incident dementia in longitudinal cohorts but never specifies how far in advance the signals appear, so "years before diagnosis" is unverifiable from the abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "may hint at dementia risk before diagnosis" or drop the time frame."

**Issue B. Selective effect sizes** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "several of these 'atypical response' markers predicted later dementia diagnoses, with risk rising roughly 22% to 31% per standard deviation for multivariate outliers and incompatible answer patterns"
- **Abstract says:** "significant associations with incident dementia were observed for item-nonresponse (overall hazard ratio [HR] per SD increase = 1.06), random response errors (HR = 1.25), multivariate outlier responses (HR = 1.31), and incompatible response patterns indexed by Guttman errors and person-fit statistics (HRs = 1.22-1.25)"
- **Problem:** The digest lists skipped items (item-nonresponse) among the predictive markers but quotes only the largest effects; item-nonresponse carried a much smaller HR of 1.06, which readers may not infer.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that skipped items showed a much weaker association (about 6% per SD) than the outlier and incompatibility markers."

### Study 2: For patients in their 70s, a lower-quality donor kidney may offer no survival edge over staying on dialysis
**PMID:** 42759976 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Setting described as five countries** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Using registry data from five European countries"
- **Abstract says:** "European Renal Association Registry data from five countries or regions: Catalonia (Spain), Denmark, France, Norway, and the UK."
- **Problem:** One of the five is a region (Catalonia), not a country; the abstract explicitly says "countries or regions."
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "five European countries or regions (Catalonia, Denmark, France, Norway and the UK).""

**Issue B. Causal phrasing for an emulated trial** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Standard-criteria donor kidneys improved five-year survival compared with staying on dialysis at every recipient age."
- **Abstract says:** "Standard criteria donor kidney transplantation was consistently associated with a survival benefit compared with continued dialysis."
- **Problem:** The abstract consistently uses associational language for this observational target trial emulation; "improved" states a causal effect.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "was associated with higher five-year survival.""

### Study 4: Your own sleep, not your spouse's, tracks with your memory — a large couples study finds
**PMID:** 42755049 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Null partner effect overstated as well-powered** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "this well-powered analysis found no reliable evidence for that spillover"
- **Abstract says:** "The possible husband-to-wife partner effect did not survive correction for multiple testing and warrants adequately powered investigation before informing couple-based clinical recommendations."
- **Problem:** The authors explicitly say the partner effect needs adequately powered study, so describing this analysis as "well-powered" and the spillover question as settled overstates the strength of the null.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the possible husband-to-wife effect did not survive correction for multiple testing and the authors call for better-powered studies before drawing conclusions."

**Issue B. Unsupported premise about prior assumptions** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "it has been assumed one partner's poor sleep drags down the other's brain health"
- **Abstract says:** "Sleep duration has well-established U-shaped associations with cognitive health, but research has largely ignored spousal interdependence."
- **Problem:** The abstract says spousal interdependence has been largely ignored, not that a spillover effect was an established assumption.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to "spousal sleep spillover has rarely been studied.""

### Study 5: Steroid-sparing drugs for giant cell arteritis and polymyalgia rheumatica don't automatically mean fewer infections
**PMID:** 42762860 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Infection risk only fell" overstates result** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "infection risk only fell when the steroid dose in the treatment group was substantially lower than in the comparison group"
- **Abstract says:** "When the active intervention had much lower glucocorticoid dosing than the comparator, the RR for infection was either close to 1 or <1."
- **Problem:** An RR "close to 1" means no difference in risk, not a reduction; the abstract also concludes these therapies were "not consistently associated with lower infection risk."
- **Suggested fix:** "Say infection risk was neutral or lower (RR close to 1 or below 1) only when the steroid dose was substantially lower."

**Issue B. "Pooled" implies meta-analysis** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers at Boston University pooled 10 randomized controlled trials (1,487 patients, average age 50 and above)"
- **Abstract says:** "Infection Risk Associated with Steroid-Sparing Therapies in GCA, PMR, and ANCA-Associated Vasculitis: A Systematic Review... We included 10 RCTs (1,487 patients total, enrolling adults averaged ≥50 years)"
- **Problem:** This was a systematic review that calculated per-trial relative risks, not a pooled meta-analysis; "pooled" suggests a combined effect estimate that the abstract does not report.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "reviewed 10 randomized controlled trials" instead of "pooled.""

### Study 6: Prism glasses plus tablet games beat pencil-and-paper cognitive rehab for stroke survivors — and the gains lasted six months

**Issue B. Prevalence figure not from this abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **Suggested fix:** "Say cognitive impairment is highly prevalent after stroke, or attribute the "over half" figure to the other study."

### Study 8: Three months of online group exercise in midlife lifted mood and changed blood biology — but didn't sharpen memory
**PMID:** 42754613 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. In vitro assay described as reaching the brain** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "suggesting exercise changes circulating factors that reach brain stem cells"
- **Abstract says:** "significant intervention-by-time effects were detected... for the percentage of proliferative cells in vitro (β = -0.23, adjusted p = 0.02), suggesting that exercise remodels the circulating milieu in ways that directly influence hippocampal progenitor biology"
- **Problem:** The assay applied participants' serum to cultured cells; nothing shows these factors reached participants' own brains. "Reach brain stem cells" implies an in vivo pathway not tested.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say blood from exercisers changed how cultured hippocampal progenitor cells behaved, suggesting exercise remodels circulating factors relevant to brain stem cell biology."

**Issue B. Direction of cell effect unstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "altered cell proliferation"
- **Abstract says:** "the percentage of proliferative cells in vitro (β = -0.23, adjusted p = 0.02)"
- **Problem:** The reported coefficient is negative, i.e., a decrease in proliferative cells; "altered" leaves readers to assume an increase, which fits the positive framing of the headline.
- **Suggested fix:** "State the direction: the proportion of proliferating cells decreased in the exercise group."

### Study 9: Older walkers keep their eyes glued to the ground — and that may be part of the fall problem
**PMID:** 42753670 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Ground" overstates "immediately relevant locations"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Older walkers keep their eyes glued to the ground"
- **Abstract says:** "more and longer fixations at immediately relevant locations and less time engaging in exploratory gaze behaviors"
- **Problem:** The review describes fixation on immediately relevant targets across varied tasks (obstacles, stairs, turning), not specifically on the ground.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "older walkers lock their gaze on what's immediately in front of them.""

**Issue B. Practical advice not tested in the review** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Good lighting, clear uncluttered pathways, single-vision distance glasses for walking rather than multifocals on stairs, and an up-to-date eye exam all reduce how much visual work walking demands of you."
- **Abstract says:** "The objective of this review is to evaluate eye tracking metrics that can be collected during walking tasks and eye movements associated with walking in older adults."
- **Problem:** The review evaluated eye-tracking measures and gaze patterns; it tested no environmental or eyewear interventions, so these recommendations are not supported by this study.
- **Suggested fix:** "Flag the advice as general fall-prevention guidance from elsewhere, not a finding of this review."

### Study 10: Why deep brain stimulation transforms some Parkinson's patients and disappoints others — the answer may be in the brain scan taken before surgery
**PMID:** 42753347 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Percentages attributed to patients** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Most patients had movement disorders — primarily Parkinson's disease — while about one in five had psychiatric conditions, mainly major depression."
- **Abstract says:** "Fifty-three studies (n = 1758 patients) were included. Movement disorders comprised 73.6%, primarily Parkinson's disease (PD), while psychiatric disorders comprised 20.8%, mainly major depressive disorder (MDD)."
- **Problem:** The abstract's 73.6% / 20.8% breakdown is not explicitly tied to patients and most plausibly refers to the included studies; stating it as a patient proportion is unverifiable from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "about three-quarters of the included studies covered movement disorders and about one in five psychiatric conditions.""

**Issue B. Thalamus and subthalamic laterality dropped** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the primary motor cortex, thalamus, brainstem and subthalamic nucleus in Parkinson's"
- **Abstract says:** "Internal network analysis identified the primary motor cortex, left thalamus, brainstem, and right subthalamic nucleus as central in PD"
- **Problem:** The abstract specifies left thalamus and right subthalamic nucleus; dropping laterality is a small loss of precision.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the sides, or note that specific hemispheres were implicated."

### Study 11: Genetic testing explained only 7% of Parkinson's cases in a large clinical cohort — but the gene it kept finding matters
**PMID:** 42762359 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Cohort described as "large"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Genetic testing explained only 7% of Parkinson's cases in a large clinical cohort"
- **Abstract says:** "A retrospective analysis was conducted on 137 patients diagnosed with Parkinson's disease according to the Movement Disorder Society (MDS) clinical diagnostic criteria."
- **Problem:** 137 patients from one Turkish center is a modest single-center series, not a large cohort; the caveats correctly call it a single-center cohort, so the headline conflicts with the digest's own framing.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "in a Turkish clinical cohort" or "in 137 patients at one clinic.""

**Issue B. Novel LRRK2 duplication presented without its uncertainty** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a previously undescribed LRRK2 duplication was detected only because the analysis looked for copy number changes that routine testing misses"
- **Abstract says:** "A novel heterozygous duplication involving LRRK2 exons 6-20 was also identified; however, as it likely results in a loss of function, its pathogenicity remains uncertain under dominant gain-of-function models."
- **Problem:** The digest reports the detection but not the authors' explicit statement that the variant's pathogenicity remains uncertain, leaving the impression it is a meaningful finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "though the authors note its clinical significance is still uncertain.""

### Study 13: Heat waves, cold snaps and smoky air send nursing home residents to the ER — even with indoor temperature rules on the books
**PMID:** 42760629 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Federal" regulations not specified in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The increases occurred despite federal regulations that govern indoor temperature in nursing facilities — and there are no comparable federal standards at all for indoor air quality."
- **Abstract says:** "nursing facility regulations address only indoor temperature with no comparable standards for indoor air quality, including PM2.5"
- **Problem:** The abstract refers generically to nursing facility regulations and standards; it does not identify them as federal.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "federal" or attribute it as the digest's own characterization of US nursing facility regulation."

**Issue B. Source of PM2.5 attributed to wildfire/traffic** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the buildings older adults live in are not fully buffering them from outdoor weather and wildfire or traffic smoke"
- **Abstract says:** "elevated fine particulate matter (PM2.5; particles 2.5 μm or less) ... elevated PM2.5 (PM2.5 > 9 μg/m3)"
- **Problem:** The study measured total ambient PM2.5 and did not attribute it to wildfire smoke or traffic; the headline's "smoky air" carries the same implication.
- **Suggested fix:** "Refer to "fine particle air pollution" rather than naming wildfire or traffic sources."

### Study 14: Group physical therapy on a hospital geriatrics ward: safe, popular, and it let therapists see 21% more patients
**PMID:** 42760622 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies causation and overstates acceptability** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Group physical therapy on a hospital geriatrics ward: safe, popular, and it let therapists see 21% more patients"
- **Abstract says:** "Mean therapist productivity was notably higher on group days than on non-group days (1.59 vs. 1.31 patients/h; approximately 21% higher) ... Further work should assess patient acceptability, fully characterize staff time requirements, and assess patient-centered outcomes."
- **Problem:** Productivity was an observed 21% higher patients-per-hour rate on group days in an uncontrolled QI project, not a demonstrated effect of the program; and the authors explicitly state patient acceptability was not yet assessed, so "popular" (based only on a 69% approach-acceptance rate) is a stretch.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "safe, well taken up, and therapist productivity ran about 21% higher on group days.""

### Study 15: What makes 'age-friendly' hospital care actually stick — and where it keeps breaking down
**PMID:** 42760752 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Most likely to be skipped" not supported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this shows the element most directly about the patient's own voice is the one most likely to be skipped"
- **Abstract says:** "Barriers included: challenges meaningfully eliciting and integrating "What Matters" into inpatient care; difficulty identifying short-term patient impact; discipline-specific staffing issues; and lack of AFHS inpatient teams to consult about implementation."
- **Problem:** The qualitative study reports that staff found What Matters hard to elicit and integrate; it does not rank barriers or measure how often any of the 4Ms is omitted.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say staff described What Matters as the hardest of the 4Ms to elicit and act on, rather than the one most likely to be skipped."

**Issue B. Miscount of the 4Ms in story angle** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a short written note on the resident's baseline mobility, usual mental status, medications, and stated priorities, handed over at admission, addresses three of the four Ms directly"
- **Abstract says:** "complete AFHS 4Ms assessments (What Matters, Medications, Mentation, and Mobility)"
- **Problem:** The four items listed (mobility, mentation, medications, priorities/What Matters) map onto all four Ms, not three.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change "three of the four Ms" to "all four Ms.""

### Study 16: AI reading doctors' notes flagged mobility problems in 4 of 5 older adults — and those patients landed in the ER twice as often
**PMID:** 42760637 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline conflates any impairment with 3+ impairments** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "AI reading doctors' notes flagged mobility problems in 4 of 5 older adults — and those patients landed in the ER twice as often"
- **Abstract says:** "79.4% had at least one mobility impairment ... Individuals with 3 or more impairments had an increased adjusted odds of ED visits (Adjusted Odds Ratio (aOR): 2.18 (1.64, 2.90)) and hospitalizations (aOR: 2.35 (1.51, 3.65)) compared to unimpaired individuals"
- **Problem:** The doubled odds applied only to the subgroup with three or more impairments, not to the 4-in-5 with at least one; the headline also converts an odds ratio into a visit frequency. The body text states the qualifier correctly, but the headline read alone misleads.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword the headline, e.g. "…and those with the most impairments had roughly double the odds of an ER visit.""

### Study 17: A group exercise and coaching program cut opioid-type painkiller use — and that's why falls went down
**PMID:** 42755166 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Single-mechanism framing of a multi-mediator finding** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "and that's why falls went down"
- **Abstract says:** "Several factors mediated falls prevention at 12 months, notably a reduction in controlled pain medication in the first 6 months of follow-up."
- **Problem:** Medication reduction was one of several mediators, described as notable but not exclusive; the headline (and the "why it matters" line that the drug reduction did "much of the work") implies it was the reason falls fell. The body's "a key reason" is the accurate version.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "— and that helped explain why falls went down" and soften "doing much of the work" to "one of several mechanisms identified.""

**Issue B. Program length not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a 12-week physical and psychological group program"
- **Abstract says:** "a physical and psychological group intervention (PPGI)"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not state the intervention's duration. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "12-week" or source it explicitly to the parent BOOST trial report."

### Study 18: 1 in 10 older ED patients return four or more times a year — and what upsets them most isn't the wait
**PMID:** 42762629 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal wording for an association** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "but by far the strongest driver was not getting enough information about treatment (OR 5.17)"
- **Abstract says:** "negative ED experience was associated with ... poor communication or involvement in decisions, with insufficient treatment information showing the strongest association (OR 5.17; 95%CI 3.48-7.69)"
- **Problem:** The survey analysis identified the strongest statistical association, not a demonstrated driver, in cross-sectional self-reported experience data.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "the strongest association was with not getting enough information about treatment.""

**Issue B. Odds ratio read as a likelihood multiple** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "patients who didn't get that information were five times more likely to rate the experience negatively"
- **Abstract says:** "insufficient treatment information showing the strongest association (OR 5.17; 95%CI 3.48-7.69)"
- **Problem:** An odds ratio of 5.17 is not the same as being five times more likely, particularly for a common outcome.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the odds of a negative rating were about five times higher."

### Study 19: Returning drained abdominal fluid doesn't extend life in advanced cancer — but it nearly doubles the time between drainage procedures
**PMID:** 42759903 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Halving the frequency" overstates the effect** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "halving the frequency of an uncomfortable procedure is a meaningful quality-of-life gain"
- **Abstract says:** "CART was associated with a lower repeat drainage rate (adjusted hazard ratio, 0.73; 95%CI, 0.72-0.74), consistent with longer observed intervals between drainage procedures (median, 15 vs. 8.3 days)."
- **Problem:** The measured reduction in repeat drainage was about 27% (aHR 0.73); the near-doubled median interval is a different, unadjusted observed measure. "Halving the frequency" is not supported by either figure.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say CART was associated with about a quarter fewer repeat drainage procedures and a longer interval between them."

**Issue B. Causal verbs for an observational comparison** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Returning drained abdominal fluid doesn't extend life in advanced cancer — but it nearly doubles the time between drainage procedures"
- **Abstract says:** "CART was associated with lower rate of repeat drainage but no meaningful survival prolongation compared with paracentesis alone."
- **Problem:** This is a retrospective claims cohort; the headline's "doesn't extend life" and "doubles" state causation. The caveats flag the design, but the headline contradicts that hedging.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "was linked to" / "was associated with nearly double the interval.""

### Study 20: A 100-day survival score for older cancer patients holds up — but the researchers warn against using it to decide on treatment
**PMID:** 42759253 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Conflict-of-interest detail flattened** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "one author reports fees from Ipsen, Johnson & Johnson, MSD and AstraZeneca outside this work"
- **Abstract says:** "Maxime Frélaut reports institutional fees from Ipsen and Johnson & Johnson, personal fees from MSD, all outside the submitted work. He also reports invitations to medical conferences from AstraZeneca, Ipsen, and MSD, outside the submitted work."
- **Problem:** AstraZeneca provided conference invitations, not fees, and the Ipsen/J&J payments were institutional rather than personal.

### Study 21: A tablet-based cognitive test could rule out two-thirds of people before they ever need an Alzheimer's blood test
**PMID:** 42757183 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Device format not stated in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A tablet-based cognitive test could rule out two-thirds of people before they ever need an Alzheimer's blood test"
- **Abstract says:** "We evaluated the Altoida NeuroMarker Platform as a digital cognitive triage tool before plasma p-tau217 testing."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes only a "digital cognitive assessment"/"NeuroMarker Platform" and never states the hardware used. Calling it "tablet-based" is unverifiable from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "A digital cognitive test could rule out two-thirds of people..." or add that the platform is app-based per the manufacturer, outside the abstract."

**Issue B. PPV described as "accuracy"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "raising the modeled accuracy of a positive blood test from 81.7% to 95.3%"
- **Abstract says:** "improving the modeled positive predictive value of plasma p-tau217 from 81.7% to 95.3%"
- **Problem:** "Accuracy" is a distinct statistical term from positive predictive value; readers may take it to mean overall test accuracy rather than the probability a positive result is correct.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "raising the modeled chance that a positive blood test is correct (positive predictive value) from 81.7% to 95.3%.""

**Issue C. Cost claim not supported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a cheap digital screen first could mean fewer people get an alarming and possibly wrong result"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract reports diagnostic performance and a modeled two-step workflow; it makes no statement about cost."
- **Problem:** Describing the digital screen as "cheap" introduces an economic claim the abstract does not support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Replace "cheap" with "non-invasive" or "scalable," matching the abstract's language."
