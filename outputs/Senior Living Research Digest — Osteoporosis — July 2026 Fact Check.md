### Study 1: Dynamic Exercise Outperforms Brief Strength Sessions for Balance and Walking Speed in Women with Bone Loss

**PMID:** 42402085 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. The digest accurately reports the sample size (194 women), age range (65-79), study design (randomized controlled trial), duration (9 months), intervention details (OsteoStrong once weekly 20 min vs. DME twice weekly 60 min), and all key statistical findings (+7.1% vs. +3.2% gait speed, +27.6% vs. +4.4% back endurance, +13% vs. -2% one-leg standing). ### Study 8: Three Osteoporosis Medications Show Similar Bone Benefits in Men

**PMID:** 42096654 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. BMD percentage ranges slightly imprecise** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "All three medications similarly increased bone mineral density at the spine (4.3-5.2%) and hip (2.5-2.8%)"
- **Abstract says:** "4.83 ± 0.89%, 4.32 ± 0.77%, 5.18 ± 0.73% at lumbar spine" and "2.75 ± 0.51%, 2.50 ± 0.61% and 2.83 ± 0.59% at total hip"
- **Problem:** The digest rounds/truncates to 4.3-5.2% when the actual range is 4.32-5.18%; similarly 2.5-2.8% vs. 2.50-2.83%. This is acceptable rounding but slightly loses precision.
- **Suggested fix:** No change required; rounding is reasonable for lay audience.

**Issue B. Country of study not mentioned in body text** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** Body text doesn't mention study location
- **Abstract says:** Study conducted in China (evident from institutions)
- **Problem:** The caveat does note "conducted in China" but general readers may assume this applies to their setting.
- **Suggested fix:** Already addressed in caveats; no change required.

### Study 11: Bisphosphonates Don't Delay Fracture Healing; Teriparatide May Speed It Up

**PMID:** 42229491 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Healing time acceleration slightly imprecise** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Teriparatide (a bone-building medication) was associated with fractures healing about 3 weeks faster on average."
- **Abstract says:** "mean difference -3.03, 95% CI -3.61 to -2.45" (units not specified in abstract excerpt but context suggests weeks)
- **Problem:** The digest says "about 3 weeks" which is accurate given the mean difference of -3.03. No correction needed.
- **Suggested fix:** No change required.

No other factual errors found. The digest accurately reports the study design (systematic review and meta-analysis), number of trials (28), patient count (5,085), and key findings about bisphosphonates showing no significant effect on healing time, delayed union, or non-union rates.

### Study 12: Long-Term Cancer Survivors Face Higher Osteoporosis and Physical Limitation Risks

**PMID:** 42349049 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Risk statistics require clarification** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Recent cancer survivors were 81% more likely to report physical limitations than those without cancer history. Long-term survivors (5+ years) had triple the odds of severe cumulative physical limitations and double the risk of osteoporosis"
- **Abstract says:** "Recent cancer survivors were 1.81 (95% CI: 1.08, 3.06) times more likely to report at least one physical performance limitation" and "long-term survivors exhibited a higher risk of severe cumulative physical performance limitations (OR = 3.05)" and "osteoporosis risk was twice as high in long-term cancer survivors... (RRR 2.26)"
- **Problem:** "81% more likely" is correct interpretation of OR 1.81. "Triple the odds" for OR 3.05 is accurate. "Double the risk" for RRR 2.26 is accurate. No correction needed.
- **Suggested fix:** No change required.

**Issue B. Sample size clarification** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "5,891 Irish adults aged 50 and older"
- **Abstract says:** The total analyzed appears to be 157 + 208 + 5526 = 5,891
- **Problem:** Calculation checks out. No error.

No significant factual errors found.

### Study 18: Survey Reveals Inconsistent Practices for Managing Post-Infusion Reactions to IV Osteoporosis Medications

**PMID:** 42289804 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Paracetamol usage percentage** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "While 88% used paracetamol and 53% used corticosteroids"
- **Abstract says:** "Eighty-eight percent (60) of those using IVBP reported using paracetamol" and "53% (36) used corticosteroids"
- **Problem:** The digest should clarify these are percentages of those who prescribe IVBP (68 respondents), not all 75 respondents. However, this is a minor distinction.
- **Suggested fix:** No change required; the meaning is clear in context.

**Issue B. Corticosteroid benefit rate** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Only half of clinicians using paracetamol found it helpful, compared to 92% who found corticosteroids beneficial"
- **Abstract says:** "Only 50% (30) of clinicians using paracetamol... reported finding these beneficial" and "92% (33) of those using corticosteroids reported observing benefit"
- **Problem:** Accurately reported.

No significant factual errors found.
