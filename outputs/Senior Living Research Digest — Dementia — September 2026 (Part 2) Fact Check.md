### Study 2: Frailty That Worsens Raises Dementia Risk — and Depression May Be a Key Link
**PMID:** 42600788 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal phrasing in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Frailty That Worsens Raises Dementia Risk — and Depression May Be a Key Link"
- **Abstract says:** "Frailty worsening was associated with higher dementia risk, whereas frailty improvement was associated with lower risk in some cohorts."
- **Problem:** "Raises" implies causation from pooled observational cohort data; the body text and caveats correctly hedge, but the headline does not.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Worsening Frailty Is Linked to Higher Dementia Risk — and Depression May Be a Key Link""

**Issue B. Frailty measure described as phenotype** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Ask your clinician about a frailty check-up that looks at things like walking speed, grip strength, exhaustion, and weight loss"
- **Abstract says:** "A harmonized 25-item FI defined frailty (FI ≥ 0.25)."
- **Problem:** The study used a 25-item deficit-accumulation frailty index, not the Fried phenotype criteria (gait speed, grip strength, exhaustion, weight loss) the tip describes, so the suggested assessment doesn't match what was measured.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "a frailty assessment that tallies up health deficits — mobility problems, everyday-task difficulty, mood and other conditions — as this study did.""

### Study 4: A New Way of Reading Tau Brain Scans Catches Alzheimer's Changes Far Earlier
**PMID:** 42766299 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies temporal earliness not measured** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A New Way of Reading Tau Brain Scans Catches Alzheimer's Changes Far Earlier"
- **Abstract says:** "detected more early-stage tau pathology than the regulatory-approved binary approach ... prospective multicenter cross-sectional study"
- **Problem:** The study is cross-sectional with no follow-up; it shows more people classified as having early-stage tau, not that changes are detected "far earlier" in a person's disease course.
- **Suggested fix:** ""A New Way of Reading Tau Brain Scans Flags More Early-Stage Changes""

### Study 6: Lifestyle Programs Can Change Habits in People Worried About Their Memory — If They're Built the Right Way
**PMID:** 42586247 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Reporting category renamed** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "9% recorded socioeconomic status"
- **Abstract says:** "Few studies recorded ethnicity (13%) or sociodemographic status (9%)"
- **Problem:** The abstract's category is "sociodemographic status," which is broader than socioeconomic status.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "9% recorded sociodemographic status.""

**Issue B. Unstated comparison about education-only programs** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "brief education-only workshops underperform, while sustained, socially embedded, resource-supplied programs move the needle"
- **Abstract says:** "Moderate-certainty evidence indicated that interventions providing a socially supportive environment and combining nutritional education and counselling for 6 + months improved diet. Physical activity improved primarily in interventions incorporating education, structured training, and enablement strategies"
- **Problem:** The abstract identifies what worked but never reports a head-to-head finding that brief education-only workshops underperform; this is an inference presented as a review finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "education alone was not enough — the programs that worked added structure, social support and practical tools.""

### Study 12: When Dementia Strikes Before 65, Caregivers Carry More and Get Less Help
**PMID:** 42594757 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Driven by" implies explanation of the group difference** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Caregivers in the young-onset group reported significantly higher burden, driven by greater functional impairment and neuropsychiatric symptoms"
- **Abstract says:** "Caregivers of individuals with YOD reported significantly higher burden than those caring for individuals with LOD. Among individuals with YOD, greater functional impairment and overall neuropsychiatric symptom severity were associated with higher caregiver burden"
- **Problem:** The functional-impairment and neuropsychiatric associations were found within the young-onset group, not shown to explain (drive) the YOD-versus-LOD burden gap.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to: "...reported significantly higher burden; within the young-onset group, greater functional impairment and neuropsychiatric symptoms were associated with higher burden.""

**Issue B. Unsupported claim about program design** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "existing adult day programs and residential options are largely built around people in their 80s, and younger clients may decline them for that reason"
- **Abstract says:** "This discrepancy suggests potential unmet needs and highlights the importance of flexible, age-appropriate LTC strategies for YOD populations."
- **Problem:** The specific assertion that services are built for people in their 80s and are therefore declined is not in the abstract; it is editorial inference presented as established fact. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute as interpretation, e.g. "the authors call for more flexible, age-appropriate long-term care options, suggesting current services may not fit younger clients.""

### Study 18: In Bipolar Disorder, Some Older Adults' Thinking Problems Rival Dementia
**PMID:** 42456815 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Age of study population not stated in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "In Bipolar Disorder, Some Older Adults' Thinking Problems Rival Dementia"
- **Abstract says:** "The study included 108 individuals with BD, of whom 32 were euthymic, while the remainder were symptomatic and 39 with major neurocognitive disorder."
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no age range or mean age for the bipolar sample, so describing them as older adults with "long-standing bipolar disorder" is unverifiable from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "adults with bipolar disorder" in the headline and body, or note that participant ages were not reported in the abstract."

**Issue B. Comparison group for the risk-factor finding not specified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "older age, hypertension and more depressive symptoms were associated with worse profiles"
- **Abstract says:** "younger age, lower rates of hypertension, and fewer depressive symptoms were associated with greater odds of belonging to the BD-P and BD-M groups compared with the Dementia"
- **Problem:** The regression compared the preserved and mild bipolar profiles against the dementia group, not against "worse profiles" generally; the inversion is directionally right but loses the reference group.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the reference group: "compared with the dementia group, younger age, less hypertension and fewer depressive symptoms predicted membership in the better-functioning bipolar profiles.""

### Study 20: Nearly One in Four Hospice Patients With Dementia Left Hospice Alive — and For-Profit Agencies Led the Trend
**PMID:** 42744747 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Dropped "slightly" from widening ownership gap** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "For-profit hospices consistently discharged patients alive at higher rates than nonprofits, and the gap in transfers, moves, and revocations widened."
- **Abstract says:** "For-profit hospices had consistently higher overall disenrollment rates than nonprofits, with slightly widening differences in transfers, moves, and revocations."
- **Problem:** Omitting "slightly" makes the ownership divergence sound larger than the authors describe, which also inflates the headline claim that for-profits "led the trend."
- **Suggested fix:** "Restore the qualifier: "...and the gap in transfers, moves, and revocations widened slightly.""

### Study 22: When a Loved One With Alzheimer's Struggles to Follow Conversation, Behavior Problems Are More Likely
**PMID:** 42770466 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Specific symptoms not reported in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Difficulty following conversation in real-world settings may be an overlooked driver of agitation, anxiety and other distressing behaviors in Alzheimer's"
- **Abstract says:** "Functional hearing difficulty was also associated with greater BPSD severity ... operationalized as the Neuropsychiatric Inventory five-domain total score"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports only a total five-domain NPI severity score; it does not break out agitation or anxiety specifically, and "driver" implies causation from a cross-sectional association.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "may be associated with more severe behavioral and psychological symptoms overall," and avoid "driver" for a cross-sectional finding."

**Issue B. Treatability claim beyond the data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a clean hearing test doesn't rule out a real, treatable communication problem. Ask about listening strategies, quieter settings, and whether hearing devices might help"
- **Abstract says:** "Caregiver-rated functional hearing difficulty was selectively linked to verbal fluency and BPSD severity in patients with AD ... longitudinal studies are needed to clarify temporal relationships"
- **Problem:** No intervention was tested; the study cannot show that functional hearing difficulty is treatable or that devices or strategies change outcomes.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the study only measured associations and did not test whether addressing listening difficulty improves symptoms."

**Issue C. Overreach in professional angle** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this supports treating communication breakdown as a first-line explanation for agitation and distress"
- **Abstract says:** "Exploratory analyses showed a pattern statistically consistent with an indirect association via verbal fluency."
- **Problem:** An exploratory indirect-association pattern in 101 cross-sectional cases does not support elevating communication breakdown to a "first-line explanation" for behavior.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "supports considering communication difficulty as one possible contributor worth checking.""
