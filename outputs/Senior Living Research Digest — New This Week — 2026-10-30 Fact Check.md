### Study 1: Daily low-dose aspirin didn't protect older adults' physical and mental capacity — and women fared slightly worse
**PMID:** 42955660 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Strength of validation correlations** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The index tracked well against chronological age, accumulated health deficits and epigenetic aging clocks"
- **Abstract says:** "IC correlated with chronological age (r = - 0.30, both sexes), a system-wide deficit-accumulation index (r = - 0.56 and - 0.54 ...), and epigenetic clocks"
- **Problem:** "Tracked well" overstates a correlation of r = -0.30 with chronological age, which is weak-to-moderate; the deficit index correlation was stronger.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the index correlated moderately with accumulated health deficits and epigenetic clocks and weakly with chronological age."

**Issue B. Patent ownership detail** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "One co-author holds patents on the epigenetic clocks used and is affiliated with a biotech company."
- **Abstract says:** "The Regents of the University of California are the sole owner of patents and patent applications directed at epigenetic biomarkers ... for which SH is a named inventor. He is a founder and paid consultant of the non-profit Epigenetic Clock Development Foundation that licenses these patents."
- **Problem:** The co-author is a named inventor on university-owned patents and a paid consultant to the foundation licensing them, not the patent holder.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "One co-author is a named inventor on the university-owned epigenetic clock patents, a paid consultant to the foundation that licenses them, and a principal investigator at a biotech company.""

### Study 4: The 'good days and bad days' of Lewy body dementia don't fade in advanced disease — and two standard scales barely agree
**PMID:** 42953268 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Baseline vs longitudinal correlates conflated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "were linked to hallucinations, daytime sleepiness, parkinsonism, autonomic symptoms including orthostatic hypotension, overall symptom burden and medication burden"
- **Abstract says:** "Baseline fluctuations correlated with female sex, symptom burden, cognitive severity, neuropsychiatric symptom burden, hallucinations, sleepiness/alertness, parkinsonism, autonomic symptom burden, and orthostatic hypotension. ... Longitudinal fluctuations correlated with other DLB features, comorbidities, and medication burden."
- **Problem:** Medication burden was reported as a correlate of longitudinal fluctuations, not of baseline fluctuations; the digest merges the two lists.
- **Suggested fix:** "Separate the two: baseline fluctuations correlated with hallucinations, sleepiness, parkinsonism, autonomic symptoms and symptom burden, while fluctuations over time correlated with comorbidities and medication burden."

**Issue B. Setting not stated in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the cohort is drawn from specialty academic centers, which may not represent all people with advanced DLB"
- **Abstract says:** "(no recruitment setting described; author affiliations are University of Florida, University of Michigan and University of Cincinnati)"
- **Problem:** The recruitment setting is unverifiable from abstract only, though author affiliations make it plausible.
- **Suggested fix:** "Mark as inferred from author affiliations, or state simply that the recruitment setting is not described in the abstract."

### Study 5: Three simple office tests can flag which Parkinson's patients will fall in the next year
**PMID:** 42960478 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Relative importance of depression not reported** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "note that depression scores mattered as much as motor severity, so mood is worth raising even if you think of falls as a purely physical issue"
- **Abstract says:** "The incident model, comprising Movement Disorder Society-Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale Part III, Montreal Cognitive Assessment, and Geriatric Depression Scale (GDS) ..."
- **Problem:** The abstract lists the predictors in each model but gives no coefficients or variable-importance ranking, so there is no basis for saying depression mattered as much as motor severity.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say only that a depression score was one of the three predictors in both models, alongside motor and cognitive measures."

**Issue B. Headline firmness** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Three simple office tests can flag which Parkinson's patients will fall in the next year"
- **Abstract says:** "Clinical measures may identify next-year fall risk in PD with moderate discrimination, although performance attenuated in external validation."
- **Problem:** "Can flag which patients will fall" implies better individual-level accuracy than AUROC 0.63–0.64 supports; the authors hedge with "may identify" and "moderate discrimination."
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "may help estimate who is at higher risk of falling in the next year.""

### Study 7: A hospital fracture follow-up service cut repeat breaks by more than half — but only after the first year
**PMID:** 42955080 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal language in headline** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "A hospital fracture follow-up service cut repeat breaks by more than half"
- **Abstract says:** "FLS attendance was associated with a significant reduction in refractures, with less than half the risk at three years"
- **Problem:** "Cut" asserts causation for an observational comparison against a self-selected control group of decliners and non-attenders; the authors use "associated with" throughout.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "linked to less than half the rate of repeat breaks" or "patients in a fracture follow-up service had half the repeat breaks.""

### Study 8: Video and console-based balance training beats doing nothing — but not a regular exercise class
**PMID:** 42954170 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Dropout comparison group** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "dropout rates were no higher than in conventional programs"
- **Abstract says:** "Dropout did not differ between technology-based and control arms (odds ratio 1.10, 95% CI 0.87-1.40; P=.41)."
- **Problem:** The comparison was technology arms versus all control arms (which include no-treatment as well as conventional exercise), not specifically versus conventional exercise classes.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "dropout did not differ between technology-based and control arms.""

### Study 9: Acupuncture after knee replacement eased pain and improved function in older patients, review finds
**PMID:** 42956696 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Participant count attribution** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "pooled 15 randomized controlled trials covering 1,037 patients who received acupuncture or electroacupuncture after total knee replacement"
- **Abstract says:** "Fifteen RCTs involving 1037 participants were included."
- **Problem:** The 1,037 is the total across both intervention and control arms, not the number who received acupuncture.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "15 randomized trials involving 1,037 patients, who received either acupuncture or electroacupuncture or control care after total knee replacement.""

**Issue B. Geographic origin of trials** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "most trials appear to originate from a single research region, limiting generalizability"
- **Abstract says:** "(no information on the country or region of the included trials)"
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only; the abstract gives no breakdown of trial locations.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the claim or state that the abstract does not report where the included trials were conducted."

### Study 10: Older drivers aren't reckless — they slow down, brake earlier and crash less in simulator tests
**PMID:** 42958638 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Braking outcome mislabeled** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "they slow down, brake earlier and crash less in simulator tests"
- **Abstract says:** "lower mean speeds, fewer speeding violations, longer braking maneuvers, reduced variation in throttle control, and a lower incidence of simulated accidents"
- **Problem:** The measure was longer braking maneuvers, not earlier braking onset; the body text gets this right but the headline does not.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change the headline to "they slow down, brake more gradually and crash less in simulator tests.""

**Issue B. Size of mediation effect** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Steering variability and self-reported risk-taking explained much of the age difference in accidents."
- **Abstract says:** "Mediation analyses showed that steering variance in the simulator and self-reported health/safety risk-taking significantly mediated the relationship between age group and number of accidents."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports statistically significant mediation but gives no proportion mediated, so "much of" is not supported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the two measures "significantly mediated" the age difference in accidents, without quantifying how much."

### Study 11: Retiring makes people more helpful — European study tracks the same individuals before and after
**PMID:** 42956395 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for observational data** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Retiring makes people more helpful — European study tracks the same individuals before and after"
- **Abstract says:** "the transition from employment to retirement was associated with an increase in instrumental support provision outside the household... These findings indicate that retirement may promote activity and facilitate engagement in helping others."
- **Problem:** "Makes" asserts causation from a fixed-effects analysis of observational survey data, and directly contradicts the digest's own caveat that the study "cannot establish that retirement causes increased helping."
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like "Retirement is linked to more helping — European study tracks the same individuals before and after.""

**Issue B. Overgeneralization in reader advice** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the evidence suggests most people naturally shift their energy toward helping family, friends and neighbors"
- **Abstract says:** "the transition from employment to retirement was associated with an increase in instrumental support provision outside the household"
- **Problem:** The study reports an average within-person increase in support provision, not that a majority of individuals shift toward helping; "most people" is not supported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "on average, people provide more practical help to others after retiring.""

**Issue C. Interpretation of the social-spending finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "suggests that generous public services free retirees up to help others rather than replacing informal help"
- **Abstract says:** "We also found suggestive evidence that this association was stronger in countries with higher social expenditures."
- **Problem:** The "crowding in rather than crowding out" interpretation is the digest's own inference; the abstract reports only a suggestive country-level moderation without explaining a mechanism.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute as interpretation, e.g. "may be consistent with public services complementing rather than replacing informal help, though the authors call this evidence only suggestive.""

### Study 12: Getting frail seniors online may not lift their mood, large British study finds
**PMID:** 42954453 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Stratified result not reported in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "But how often someone used the internet showed no significant association with depression, in either group, once frailty was accounted for."
- **Abstract says:** "Internet use was not significantly associated with depression, whereas frailty status was significantly associated with depression."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports an overall null association; it does not state that separate frail and non-frail analyses were run or that both were null. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "in either group" or note that the abstract reports the overall model result."

**Issue B. Intensifier not in source** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Frail participants reported markedly higher depression scores than their non-frail peers."
- **Abstract says:** "Frail older people reported higher depression scores than non-frail individuals."
- **Problem:** "Markedly" adds a magnitude claim; no effect size is given in the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "significantly higher" or simply "higher.""

### Study 16: Hearing loss plus memory trouble: brain scans show who is most likely to progress to dementia

### Study 21: Which MRI scan your clinic uses could change whether you qualify for a new Alzheimer's drug
**PMID:** 42959393 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Claim about drug-label imaging basis not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the thresholds in the drug labels were based largely on older GRE imaging"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract states only that "Prospective studies are needed to establish SWI-based safety thresholds for anti-amyloid therapies" and does not describe what imaging the existing label thresholds were based on."
- **Problem:** The assertion about the imaging basis of regulatory label thresholds is presented as established fact but is not supported anywhere in the abstract. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to something the abstract supports, e.g. "the authors note that SWI-based safety thresholds have not yet been established," or attribute the label claim to an external source."

**Issue B. Consensus step omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Three neuroradiologists independently counted tiny brain microbleeds"
- **Abstract says:** ""Three neuroradiologists independently identified and then reached consensus on mH detection.""
- **Problem:** The digest describes only the independent reading and omits that the final counts came from consensus, which slightly misdescribes the method.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "and then reached consensus" after "independently counted.""

### Study 22: Falling blood pressure in later life may signal frailty — and frailty may lead to financially devastating medical bills
**PMID:** 42950708 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Population described as older adults only** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Using 3,996 participants from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study, researchers mapped systolic blood pressure patterns from 2011 to 2015"
- **Abstract says:** "The study analyzed 3996 CHARLS participants and repeatedly describes the sample as "middle-aged and older adults.""
- **Problem:** The digest, including the headline's "in later life," presents the findings as applying to older adults, but the cohort also includes middle-aged participants and the abstract does not report age-stratified results.
- **Suggested fix:** "State that the cohort was middle-aged and older Chinese adults, and note that results are not specific to those 65 and over."

**Issue B. Causal wording for a mediation analysis** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this analysis suggests that decline, not the blood pressure itself, is what drives families toward financial catastrophe"
- **Abstract says:** ""Frailty may be one pathway linking the High-decreasing SBP trajectory with subsequent household CHE... Further prospective studies are needed to clarify the temporal and causal relationships.""
- **Problem:** "Drives" implies causation from an observational mediation model, which the authors explicitly decline to claim; the body text sits uneasily with the digest's own caveat that causation cannot be established.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as "suggests frailty may be the pathway linking a falling blood pressure pattern to later catastrophic household health costs.""
