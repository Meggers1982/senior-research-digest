### Study 1: Hospital Delirium Linked to Five-Fold Higher Dementia Risk in Later Years
**PMID:** 42970282 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Cohort demographics not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The cohort is from one largely white Midwestern US county and may not generalize."
- **Abstract says:** "The MCSA is a prospective, population-based longitudinal study of cognitive aging among residents of Olmsted County, Minnesota."
- **Problem:** The abstract identifies the county but says nothing about the racial makeup of the cohort; the "largely white" characterization is unverifiable from abstract only (though it is consistent with the known MCSA population).
- **Suggested fix:** "Either drop "largely white" or attribute it ("the cohort is drawn from a single Midwestern US county, Olmsted County, Minnesota, and may not generalize")."

### Study 2: Sleep Problems Tied to Higher Suicide Risk in People Living With Dementia
**PMID:** 42790647 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Relative vs absolute risk wording** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The absolute risk increase was modest (adjusted hazard ratio 1.12)."
- **Abstract says:** "Sleep disturbances were associated with increased suicide risk after adjustment for sociodemographic and clinical covariates (aHR, 1.12; 95% CI, 1.02-1.23)."
- **Problem:** A hazard ratio is a relative measure, not an absolute risk increase; the absolute difference in the abstract is the incidence gap (868.3 vs 729.7 per 100,000 person-years).
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "The increase in relative risk was modest (adjusted hazard ratio 1.12), corresponding to roughly 139 extra suicide events per 100,000 person-years.""

### Study 5: Cognitive Changes Keep Many ALS Patients Out of End-of-Life Planning Conversations
**PMID:** 42586324 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Timing of the rise attributed to the 2017 law** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "rising from 51.6% to 62% over the period following Italy's 2017 self-determination law"
- **Abstract says:** "Demographic and clinical data from the PARALS Register (2008-2020) were retrospectively examined... The ACP prevalence increased over time from 51.6% to 61.94% (P = 0.025)."
- **Problem:** The increase is reported across the full 2008-2020 study window, most of which predates Law 219/2017; the digest implies the rise occurred after the law took effect.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "rising from 51.6% to 62% across the 2008-2020 study period, which spans Italy's 2017 self-determination law.""

**Issue B. "Single Italian region" not specified in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "conducted in a single Italian region under a specific national legal framework"
- **Abstract says:** "Demographic and clinical data from the PARALS Register (2008-2020)... Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis Center, University of Turin, Turin, Italy."
- **Problem:** The abstract names the PARALS register and Turin-based centers but does not state the geographic coverage; "a single Italian region" is unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "conducted using one Italian population-based ALS register under a specific national legal framework.""

### Study 10: Telehealth Visits With a Nurse Practitioner Eased Burden for Dementia Caregivers
**PMID:** 42631925 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline from a single-arm pilot** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Telehealth Visits With a Nurse Practitioner Eased Burden for Dementia Caregivers"
- **Abstract says:** "The data suggest that a nurse-practitioner-led telehealth program is a feasible and acceptable intervention for caregivers caring for PLWD, with improvement in caregiver burden scores. Future studies are needed to examine its efficacy..."
- **Problem:** "Eased" asserts that the intervention caused the drop in burden, which a single-armed pilot with no control group cannot establish — and which the digest's own Caveats explicitly deny.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "Caregiver Burden Scores Fell During a Nurse Practitioner Telehealth Pilot" or similar non-causal wording."

**Issue B. Hospice-ineligibility of enrolled patients** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "focused on helping family caregivers manage symptoms in people living with dementia who were not eligible for hospice"
- **Abstract says:** "While hospice care is standard for end-of-life symptom management, predicting 6-month survival is challenging, leaving many who could benefit ineligible. The study team conducted a single-armed pilot study implementing a nurse-practitioner-led telehealth program for caregivers at an outpatient clinic, focusing on symptom management for PLWD."
- **Problem:** The abstract gives hospice ineligibility as the rationale for the program but does not state that enrolled patients were themselves hospice-ineligible; as written this is unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the program was designed to fill the gap for people with dementia who do not qualify for hospice, rather than stating that participants were ineligible."

### Study 12: Frailty that worsens raises dementia risk — and depression appears to be part of the pathway
**PMID:** 42600788 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal verb for observational finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Frailty at baseline raised dementia risk (hazard ratios 1.35-2.23) and worsening frailty raised it more (1.43-2.53)"
- **Abstract says:** "Baseline frailty was associated with higher risk (HRs, 1.35-2.23), as was frailty worsening (HRs, 1.43-2.53)."
- **Problem:** "Raised" implies causation in a purely observational cohort analysis, which the digest's own caveat says cannot be established.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Frailty at baseline was associated with higher dementia risk ... and worsening frailty with a higher risk still," and soften the headline to "Worsening frailty linked to higher dementia risk.""

### Study 19: Genetic Evidence Strengthens the Case That Tau Drives Alzheimer's — and Possibly Vascular Dementia Too
**PMID:** 42979808 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Dropped subgroup qualifier on the AD estimate** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Higher spinal fluid p-tau was causally associated with modestly increased Alzheimer's risk (about 7-8% higher odds per unit)"
- **Abstract says:** ""CSF p-tau in individuals with abnormal amyloid and APOE ε4 non-carriers was positively associated with AD risk [IVW: OR = 1.07 ... OR = 1.08 ...]""
- **Problem:** The two AD estimates come from specific strata (abnormal-amyloid and APOE ε4 non-carrier samples); presenting them as a general CSF p-tau–AD association slightly overstates the scope.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the qualifier: "In analyses restricted to people with abnormal amyloid and to APOE ε4 non-carriers, higher spinal fluid p-tau was associated with about 7-8% higher odds of Alzheimer's.""

### Study 24: What Actually Drives Repeat ER Visits for Older Adults — and What Makes the Visit Feel Bad
**PMID:** 42762629 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal language for association** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the single strongest driver of a negative visit was not getting enough information about treatment (OR 5.17)"
- **Abstract says:** "negative ED experience was associated with ... poor communication or involvement in decisions, with insufficient treatment information showing the strongest association (OR 5.17; 95%CI 3.48-7.69)"
- **Problem:** "Driver" (and "dominant factor" in Why it matters) converts a cross-sectional survey association into a causal claim, which sits awkwardly with the digest's own caveat that causation cannot be established.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "the factor most strongly associated with a negative visit was not getting enough information about treatment (OR 5.17)" and "identifies communication as the factor most strongly associated with a poor rating"."

**Issue B. Index presentations vs total presentations** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers analyzed 336,283 emergency department presentations by adults aged 65 and older"
- **Abstract says:** "There were 336,283 index presentations in 2018"
- **Problem:** The 336,283 figure counts index (first-in-2018) presentations per person, not all ED visits analyzed; as written readers may think it is the total visit volume.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "336,283 older adults with an index emergency department visit in 2018"."

**Issue C. Omitted dementia-specific finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "multimorbidity, rural residence, and living in residential aged care predicted frequent use"
- **Abstract says:** "Female, dementia, osteoporosis, frailty, non-urgent presentation, and hospitalisation were associated with lower odds of frequent presentation."
- **Problem:** In a dementia-focused digest, the abstract's directly relevant (and counterintuitive) result that dementia was associated with lower odds of frequent ED presentation is left out entirely.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a sentence noting that dementia, frailty and female sex were associated with lower odds of frequent presentation in this cohort."
