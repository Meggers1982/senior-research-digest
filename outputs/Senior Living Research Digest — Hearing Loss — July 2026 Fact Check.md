# Fact-Check Report: Senior Living Research Digest — Hearing Loss — July 2026
**Checked:** 2026-07-12 | **Studies reviewed:** 30
**Primary audience:** older adults and seniors | **Secondary audience:** families, caregivers, and senior living professionals

## Fact-Check Report

### Study 1: Hearing loss linked to higher depression risk across three countries and cultures
**PMID:** 42119796 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. Framing is consistent with the abstract. The digest correctly reports the odds ratios for fair hearing (9–17% higher, matching ORs of 1.09, 1.11, 1.17) and poor hearing (1.52–1.64 range). The observational caveat is appropriately noted.

---

### Study 2: Flavonoid-rich diets associated with lower risk of developing hearing loss
**PMID:** 42235109 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. Sample size (55,859), follow-up (613,590 person-years), incident cases (1,681), and effect size (16% lower risk, HR 0.84) all match the abstract. The mediation by CRP is correctly described. Caveats appropriately note observational design and dietary recall limitations.

---

### Study 3: Gamified online hearing-cognitive training shows promise for older adults
**PMID:** 42302249 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mean age slightly imprecise** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "mean age 68 years"
- **Abstract says:** "mean age 67.65, SD 4.78 years"
- **Problem:** Minor rounding; 67.65 rounds to 68, but 67 would be equally accurate.
- **Suggested fix:** "mean age approximately 68 years" or "mean age 67.7 years"

**Issue B. Sex ratio description omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** No mention of sex distribution
- **Abstract says:** "45/60, 75% male"
- **Problem:** The sample was predominantly male (75%), which is relevant context but not a factual error in what was stated.
- **Suggested fix:** No change required, but could add "75% male" for completeness.

---

### Study 4: Hearing loss worsening over time linked to increased loneliness
**PMID:** 42065922 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. The digest correctly captures the key finding that within-individual progressive hearing decline (not between-individual differences) was associated with greater loneliness. The hybrid random-effects model interpretation is accurate. Caveats are appropriate.

---

### Study 5: Poorer hearing linked to worse cognitive screening scores and reduced quality of life
**PMID:** 42151840 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. Sample size (60 total: 40 with presbycusis, 20 controls), the correlations between PTA and cognitive scores, the 90% reporting reduced quality of life, and the exploratory nature of duration findings are all correctly reported. The single-center, cross-sectional caveats are appropriately noted.

---

### Study 6: Hearing aids may help reduce frailty risk in older adults
**PMID:** 42423142 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Frailty index score percentage slightly misstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "an 18% increase in frailty index score"
- **Abstract says:** "17.9% (95% CI: 1.9, 36.5%) higher frailty index score"
- **Problem:** 17.9% rounded to 18% is acceptable, but the digest says "18%" while the abstract says "17.9%."
- **Suggested fix:** Either "approximately 18%" or "17.9%" for precision.

No other issues. The 24% higher odds per 10 dB and the null association among hearing aid users are accurately reported.
