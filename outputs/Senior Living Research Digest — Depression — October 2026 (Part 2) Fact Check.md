**Primary audience:** older adults and seniors | **Secondary audience:** families, caregivers, and senior living professionals

### Study 1: Treadmill walking eased back pain — and lifted mood — in an 8-week trial
**PMID:** 42892150 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Single-center claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Small sample (N=45, 15 per group), single-center, short 4-week follow-up"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract reports 45 patients randomized to three groups of 15 but never describes the setting as a single center; authors are affiliated with Gachon University, a convalescent hospital in Seoul and CHA University."
- **Problem:** The number of recruiting sites cannot be confirmed from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "single-center" or write "recruitment setting not described in the abstract.""

**Issue B. Extension to residents unable to do floor exercise** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "in residents who are too painful to tolerate conventional floor exercise"
- **Abstract says:** "Participants were 45 patients with chronic mechanical low back pain; the comparison group performed self-directed core stabilization exercises, and no subgroup of people unable to tolerate floor exercise was studied (ages are not reported)."
- **Problem:** Implies the trial tested treadmill walking in people who cannot tolerate conventional exercise, which it did not; it also assumes an older/frail population the abstract does not describe.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to: "in patients with chronic mechanical low back pain — though participant ages were not reported, so applicability to frail residents is unknown.""

### Study 2: Air pollution and the aging brain: depression and heart disease may be links in the chain
**PMID:** 42632461 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Intervention leap from unconfirmed mediators** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this review strengthens the case for treating resident depression and cardiovascular risk as brain-health interventions, not just standalone problems"
- **Abstract says:** ""Cardio and Cerebro-vascular diseases emerged as a potential key mediator. Mental health outcomes, sleep, and lung function also showed mediating potential, but further research is needed to confirm these findings"; the review calls for "rigorous causal mediation analyses.""
- **Problem:** The review identifies candidate pathways and explicitly says they are unconfirmed; framing depression and cardiovascular treatment as established brain-health interventions goes beyond that, and sits awkwardly with the digest's own caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "this review raises the possibility that treating resident depression and cardiovascular risk could also matter for brain health — a hypothesis the authors say still needs confirming.""

### Study 3: Some brains shrink but keep thinking clearly — and researchers are learning who they belong to
**PMID:** 42731569 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Depression-treatment advice not supported by the direction of the finding** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "keep the focus on what you can act on, including getting depressive symptoms properly evaluated and treated"
- **Abstract says:** ""Sex-disaggregated results highlighted indicators unique to the females, such as higher body mass index (stability) and lower geriatric depression score (stability, vulnerability).""
- **Problem:** Lower depression scores in women predicted both the stable and the vulnerable pathway, so the result does not indicate that treating depressive symptoms protects cognition; the digest body text correctly notes the double link but the angle ignores it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as: "mood scores showed up as a marker in women on more than one pathway, so the link is not a simple one — but depressive symptoms are worth evaluating in their own right.""

### Study 4: When Depression Meets Frailty, Hospital Stays Climb — and Office Visits Drop
**PMID:** 42849853 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal phrasing for an observational association** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "each 5-point rise in depressive symptoms raised the odds of hospital admission by 25% among frail participants … Depression in an older adult who is already frail appears to push care away from routine clinic visits and toward the hospital"
- **Abstract says:** ""For admission, each 5-point increase carried higher odds in frail (adjusted odds ratio, 1.25 [95% CI, 1.18-1.33]) but not robust participants"; depressive symptoms were "associated with" fewer outpatient visits regardless of frailty."
- **Problem:** "Raised the odds" and "push care away" imply causation in a person-interval observational cohort; also, the lower outpatient use applied to everyone, not specifically to frail participants.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "was associated with 25% higher odds" and note that the drop in outpatient visits occurred regardless of frailty."

### Study 5: Weekly Exercise Appears to Blunt the Depression Toll of Smoking and Heavy Drinking
**PMID:** 42721779 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Most valuable substitute" overstates the comparison** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "for those who cannot exercise much, staying socially engaged is the most valuable substitute"
- **Abstract says:** ""Additionally, social activity provided a protective effect in the absence of MVPA.""
- **Problem:** The study only examined four behaviors, so it cannot rank social engagement against unmeasured alternatives as the "most valuable" substitute.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "social activity was the one behavior in this analysis that still showed a protective association when physical activity was low.""

### Study 6: Losing Your Appetite May Forecast Losing Your Mobility
**PMID:** 42861605 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Appetite reported as intake** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "This study suggests reduced intake may precede visible mobility decline by many months"
- **Abstract says:** "Participants reported appetite as "good or poor" at baseline; food intake or meal consumption was not measured."
- **Problem:** The study measured a one-item self-rated appetite question, not how much people actually ate, so attributing the finding to reduced intake misstates the exposure.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "reduced appetite may precede visible mobility decline by many months," keeping the observational framing."

### Study 7: Social Disadvantage in Your 40s Shows Up in the Brain Decades Later
**PMID:** 42574936 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Follow-up length not stated in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Social Disadvantage in Your 40s Shows Up in the Brain Decades Later"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract reports SDOH measured in early midlife (mean age 40.0 ± 3.6) with repeated cognitive and MRI measures and effects expressed "per 5-year interval," but gives no total follow-up duration."
- **Problem:** "Decades later" asserts a follow-up window the abstract does not specify.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "shows up in later brain scans" or state the outcomes were tracked over repeated follow-up of unspecified length."

### Study 8: Dancing in the Nursing Home: 12 Weeks of Weekly Classes Eased Depression and Lifted Quality of Life
**PMID:** 42843433 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for an uncontrolled pilot** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "12 Weeks of Weekly Classes Eased Depression and Lifted Quality of Life"
- **Abstract says:** ""These findings suggest that a group-based dance program is a feasible intervention in nursing home settings and may be associated with … improvements on … depressive symptoms and QoL in this non-controlled pilot study.""
- **Problem:** The headline states the classes caused the improvement, while the authors explicitly hedge because there was no control group — directly at odds with the digest's own caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "Depression Scores Fell and Quality of Life Rose After 12 Weeks of Weekly Classes — in an Uncontrolled Pilot.""

**Issue B. Walking speed not among the measured outcomes** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Balance, walking speed and cognitive test scores did not change"
- **Abstract says:** ""No significant changes were observed in functional outcomes (Berg Balance Scale, Timed Up and Go, Mini Motor Test) or cognitive performance (Mini-Mental State Examination).""
- **Problem:** Timed Up and Go is a mobility/transfer test, not a gait-speed measure; walking speed was not reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Balance, mobility (Timed Up and Go) and cognitive test scores did not change.""

### Study 10: Loneliness, Depression and Stress Linked to Faster Biological Aging in Midlife and Beyond
**PMID:** 42566822 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Strongest" signal not reported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with the strongest and most consistent signals on second-generation clocks (PhenoAge and GrimAge)"
- **Abstract says:** ""Irrespective of the type of psychological adversity, positive associations were consistently observed for second-generation epigenetic clocks (PhenoAge and GrimAge).""

### Study 11: Psilocybin Therapy Worked Just as Well — and Was No Riskier — for Cancer Patients in Active Treatment

### Study 13: Depression in Later Life: Common, Treatable, and Often Left Untreated
**PMID:** 42624700 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Barrier interpretation overstated** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "yet the oldest group is the least likely to get outpatient care — often because they don't see their symptoms as treatable rather than because help is unavailable"
- **Abstract says:** "Among untreated individuals with perceived unmet need, barriers to treatment were attitudinal (86.2%; e.g., thought could handle without treatment), access-related (73.1%), and financial (48.6%)."
- **Problem:** Barriers were reported only by untreated people who perceived an unmet need (a group that was notably small among those 65+), and access-related barriers were reported by nearly three-quarters of them. Saying help was not 'unavailable' contradicts the access figure and attaches whole-sample barrier data to the oldest subgroup.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as: 'among untreated adults who recognised a need, attitudinal barriers were most commonly reported (86.2%), though access (73.1%) and cost (48.6%) barriers were also widespread.'"

**Issue B. Single 'most common reason' claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "'I can handle it myself' was the single most common reason older adults in this survey went untreated"
- **Abstract says:** "barriers to treatment were attitudinal (86.2%; e.g., thought could handle without treatment)"
- **Problem:** 'Thought could handle without treatment' is one example within the broad attitudinal category, not a separately measured single most common reason, and the figures describe untreated people with perceived unmet need rather than all untreated adults.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'attitudinal barriers — such as thinking they could handle it without treatment — were the most commonly cited category among untreated adults who felt they needed care.'"

### Study 14: More Than Half of Nursing Home Residents Showed Signs of Depression During the Pandemic
**PMID:** 42909849 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Protective factors stated too firmly** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Consistent staffing, maintained social connection, and ongoing family communication emerged as protective conditions."
- **Abstract says:** "consistent staffing, social connection and family communication were potential protective conditions."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes these as potential protective conditions identified through narrative synthesis, not established protective effects; the digest's 'Why it matters' further calls them 'three concrete, controllable buffers'.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'were identified as potential protective conditions' and soften 'concrete, controllable buffers' to 'possible buffers worth testing'."

### Study 15: Exercise Eased Depression and Low Mood Through the Menopause Transition
**PMID:** 42868040 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Specific mind-body modality not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "mind-body approaches such as yoga improved psychological quality of life specifically"
- **Abstract says:** "intervention types (aerobic-based [ABI] and mind-body [MBI]) ... whereas MBIs improved psychological QoL (g=0.80, 95% CI [0.43-1.16])"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not identify which mind-body modalities were pooled; naming yoga is unverifiable from the abstract alone and is repeated in the reader-facing story angle.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'mind-body programmes' without naming yoga, or attribute the example to the full text."

### Study 16: Isolation Doesn't Make Older Adults Frail Directly — Depression Appears to Be the Bridge
**PMID:** 42873644 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Loneliness substituted for social isolation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It suggests that the physical decline linked to loneliness may be preventable"
- **Abstract says:** "social isolation is recognized as a prevalent psychosocial risk factor ... Social isolation functions as a distal determinant"
- **Problem:** The exposure measured was social isolation, not loneliness; the two are related but distinct constructs and the abstract never measures loneliness.
- **Suggested fix:** "Replace 'loneliness' with 'social isolation' in the 'Why it matters' sentence."

**Issue B. Prevention implied from mediation model** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "may be preventable if the mood symptoms that follow isolation are caught and treated"
- **Abstract says:** "its impact was exclusively indirect through three distinct pathways ... These findings highlight the need for integrated interventions targeting social engagement, mental health, and cognitive function"
- **Problem:** A cross-wave mediation model cannot show that treating depression prevents frailty; the body text leans further toward an intervention claim than the caveat about causation allows.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as 'points to depression as a pathway worth testing in intervention studies' rather than implying prevention."

### Study 22: A Nurse-Led Symptom Program Eased Pain and Discomfort in Palliative Care — Yet Depression Barely Budged
**PMID:** 42908282 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline wording on depression result** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Yet Depression Barely Budged"
- **Abstract says:** "No statistically significant between-group differences in anxiety, depression, quality of life, or functional status were observed post the 6-week intervention."
- **Problem:** "Barely budged" suggests a small but real measured improvement; the abstract reports no statistically significant between-group difference at all. The body text states this correctly, but the headline blurs it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "Yet Depression Showed No Measurable Benefit" or "Yet Mood Scores Didn't Differ From Usual Care.""

**Issue B. Comparator described as "usual care"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "or usual care (n=75), over six weeks"
- **Abstract says:** "Participants were assigned to an intervention group (n = 75) or a control group (n = 75)."
- **Problem:** The abstract identifies only a "control group" without describing its content; calling it usual care is an assumption. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "or a control group (n=75)" unless the full text confirms usual care."

### Study 23: Is It Depression or Early Alzheimer's? Researchers Map Which Brain Markers Tell Them Apart
**PMID:** 42904492 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Overstates diagnostic clarity** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this review clarifies which tests currently carry diagnostic weight and which don't"
- **Abstract says:** "This scoping review mapped biomarker evidence differentiating depression and AD... Amyloid biomarkers were most consistently associated with clinically diagnosed AD, whereas findings for other biomarkers were more heterogeneous... highlighting the need for longitudinal studies using scalable biomarkers to clarify causal and temporal relationships."
- **Problem:** A scoping review maps the literature and reports association patterns; it did not assess diagnostic accuracy or assign diagnostic weight to specific tests. The headline phrase "tell them apart" carries the same implication.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "maps which biomarkers have been most consistently linked to each condition, while noting that evidence for most markers is mixed.""

**Issue B. Heterogeneity of non-amyloid findings not mentioned** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Amyloid markers were the most consistent signal of Alzheimer's, and neurodegeneration markers such as hippocampal shrinkage also tracked with Alzheimer's."
- **Abstract says:** "Amyloid biomarkers were most consistently associated with clinically diagnosed AD, whereas findings for other biomarkers were more heterogeneous."
- **Problem:** The digest omits the abstract's explicit statement that results for biomarkers other than amyloid were heterogeneous, which tempers the vascular/connectivity contrast described next.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that findings for all non-amyloid biomarkers were heterogeneous across studies."
