**Primary audience:** older adults and seniors | **Secondary audience:** families, caregivers, and senior living professionals

### Study 1: Big surgery-prep trial finds three weeks of supervised exercise didn't keep frail seniors independent
**PMID:** 42743945 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unverifiable superlative** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is the largest and most rigorous test to date"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract describes a multicentre, parallel-group, randomised controlled trial with blinded outcome assessment across 19 hospitals in Germany, but makes no claim about being the largest or most rigorous trial of prehabilitation."
- **Problem:** The ranking claim cannot be verified from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "one of the largest randomised tests of prehabilitation in frail older surgical patients" or drop the superlative."

**Issue B. Intervention description incomplete** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a 3-week personalized prehabilitation program — 30 supervised half-hour sessions covering exercise, nutrition, cognitive or psychological support, medication review and more"
- **Abstract says:** ""a 3-week personalised prehabilitation programme consisting of 30 supervised 30-min sessions and a recommendation for 18 additional unsupervised sessions during this period""
- **Problem:** The digest omits the 18 recommended unsupervised sessions, which slightly understates the dose of the intervention and is relevant to the "highly compliant" framing (adherence was reported only for the 30 supervised sessions).
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that participants were also advised to do 18 additional unsupervised sessions, and note that the 29.5/30 adherence figure refers to supervised sessions."

### Study 2: Clot-busting drug helped stroke patients over 70 — even well past the traditional treatment window
**PMID:** 42749350 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline firmer than authors' conclusion** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Clot-busting drug helped stroke patients over 70"
- **Abstract says:** ""This secondary analysis suggests that the evidence trends towards safety and effectiveness of intravenous alteplase in patients aged over 70 years during 4.5 to 24 hours after stroke onset.""
- **Problem:** The authors deliberately hedge ("trends towards") for a post hoc subgroup analysis; "helped" states the benefit as established.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "Clot-busting drug looked beneficial for stroke patients over 70 in a post hoc analysis" or similar."

### Study 3: Opening a blocked neck artery did not improve thinking in older adults, major trial finds
**PMID:** 42747834 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Population described as older adults** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Opening a blocked neck artery did not improve thinking in older adults, major trial finds"
- **Abstract says:** ""Adults (aged 35-86 years) without dementia with eighth-grade education or higher and unilateral asymptomatic ICA stenosis greater than or equal to 70%"; mean age 71 years."
- **Problem:** Eligibility started at age 35, so the sample was not exclusively older adults, even though the mean age was 71.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note in the body that participants ranged from 35 to 86 years old with a mean age of 71."

**Issue B. Scope of the null result** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "cognitive scores improved modestly in both groups, with no advantage for revascularization"
- **Abstract says:** "The primary analysis found "no difference in cognition by treatment group over 1 year" and a non-significant treatment × hemodynamic status interaction, specifically "among those with baseline reduced cognition.""
- **Problem:** The headline finding applies to the pre-specified subgroup with reduced baseline cognition; the digest presents it as a general result, though the Caveats note the 51-participant basis.
- **Suggested fix:** "Make clear that the primary test — and the null result — was among the 51 participants with reduced baseline cognition."

### Study 4: Vitamin D and fish oil didn't move Alzheimer's blood markers over four years
**PMID:** 42746141 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Subgroup analyses mislabeled** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Exploratory subgroup analyses hinted at possible effects in men and in Black participants"
- **Abstract says:** ""Pre-specified subgroup analyses suggested potential interactions by sex and race"; sex and race "were pre-specified effect modifiers.""
- **Problem:** Calling them exploratory understates their status — they were pre-specified, though uncorrected for multiple testing.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Pre-specified subgroup analyses hinted at possible effects in men and in Black participants, though uncorrected for multiple testing.""

### Study 5: Working caregivers got the most out of an online dementia support program
**PMID:** 42745561 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Between-group vs within-group effect conflated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Caregiver burden fell substantially more in this group: about 7 points at 6 weeks"
- **Abstract says:** "The moderating (extra) reduction was -10.00 at 6 weeks (95% CI -19.70 to -0.31); the absolute reduction within intervention-group carers with WCRC was -7.19 (95% CI -12.72 to -1.67) at 6 weeks."
- **Problem:** The ~7-point figure is the absolute within-group drop, not the amount by which it exceeded carers without work-care conflict (about 10 points), so the sentence pairs the wrong number with "more."
- **Suggested fix:** "Say burden fell about 7 points in intervention-group carers with work-care conflict — roughly 10 points more than in carers without it."

**Issue B. Non-WCRC group characterization** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "while caregivers without work-care conflict showed no comparable benefit"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract reports that the moderating effect "was driven by a robust, absolute reduction in caregiver burden in intervention group participants with WCRC" but does not report an estimate for carers without WCRC."
- **Problem:** A plausible inference, but the abstract does not state that carers without work-care conflict lacked benefit.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the effect to the WCRC group without asserting a null result in the other group."

**Issue C. Unverifiable context statistic** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Roughly a quarter of dementia caregivers juggle paid work with care"
- **Abstract says:** ""Forty-two of 93 carers reported WCRC" — the abstract gives no population prevalence figure."
- **Problem:** The one-in-four figure comes from outside the abstract and sits oddly next to the study's own 45% figure.
- **Suggested fix:** "Cite a source for the prevalence figure or replace it with the trial's own proportion (42 of 93 carers)."

### Study 9: Meditation beat yoga, tai chi and sleep hygiene advice for older adults' sleep in a 45-trial comparison
**PMID:** 42576331 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Head-to-head superiority overstated** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Meditation beat yoga, tai chi and sleep hygiene advice for older adults' sleep in a 45-trial comparison"
- **Abstract says:** "Meditation, yoga, qigong and tai chi all improved sleep quality versus control; only the comparison with sleep hygiene education is reported as superior (MD -2.87, 95% CrI -4.45 to -1.30). Meditation "show[ed] the highest probability of being ranked best in the primary analysis.""
- **Problem:** The abstract reports no statistically significant pairwise advantage of meditation over yoga, qigong or tai chi — only the best ranking probability. The headline presents a ranking as a demonstrated win over the other therapies.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "Meditation ranked best among mind-body options — and beat sleep hygiene advice — for older adults' sleep in a 45-trial comparison.""

**Issue B. Unverifiable caveat and angle detail** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "no long-term follow-up on whether benefits persist"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract reports search methods, 45 studies, 3,568 participants and effect estimates, but says nothing about follow-up duration or whether interventions were taught versus self-directed."
- **Problem:** Both the follow-up caveat and the story-angle claim that "these trials used taught programs rather than self-directed effort" cannot be checked against the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Mark these as not reported in the abstract or remove them."

### Study 10: Apathy — not depression or anxiety — is the strongest warning sign that mild memory problems will progress to Alzheimer's
**PMID:** 42749536 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Missing qualifier on depression subgroup** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "reaching significance only in studies followed for three or more years"
- **Abstract says:** ""a significant association was observed only among studies with follow-up ≥ 3 years (HR = 2.20, 95% CI: 1.08-4.46), although the test for subgroup differences did not reach conventional significance.""
- **Problem:** The digest omits the authors' caveat that the subgroup difference itself was not statistically significant, which weakens the follow-up-duration explanation.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that the test for subgroup differences was not statistically significant."

**Issue B. Deterministic headline wording** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the strongest warning sign that mild memory problems will progress to Alzheimer's"
- **Abstract says:** ""Apathy is a robust and consistent behavioral predictor of progression from MCI to clinical AD dementia"; only apathy, depression and anxiety were compared."
- **Problem:** "Will progress" reads as prediction of certainty, and "strongest" is only relative to the three neuropsychiatric symptoms examined — not to all known predictors.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "the neuropsychiatric symptom most strongly linked to progression from mild cognitive impairment to Alzheimer's.""

### Study 12: A routine liver blood test ratio is tied to faster cognitive decline in people carrying the main Alzheimer's risk gene
**PMID:** 42741023 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies effect only in ε4 carriers** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A routine liver blood test ratio is tied to faster cognitive decline in people carrying the main Alzheimer's risk gene"
- **Abstract says:** "Higher baseline AST/ALT was associated with faster cognitive decline and AD-signature cortical thinning, with larger associations among APOE ε4 carriers (interaction p = .0006-0.040)."
- **Problem:** The association with faster decline was found overall, and was simply larger among ε4 carriers; the headline reads as though it applies only to gene carriers.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to e.g. "...is tied to faster cognitive decline — especially in people carrying the main Alzheimer's risk gene.""

### Study 13: An eye scan tracked brain small-vessel damage and cognitive decline over a year after mild stroke

### Study 15: An anesthetic add-on cut post-surgery confusion in older patients by nearly half across 17 trials

**Issue B. Single-country claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **Abstract says:** "The abstract reports no information on the countries in which included trials were conducted."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Either drop the claim or attribute it to the full text rather than presenting it as an abstract-level fact."

### Study 16: Ashwagandha improved sleep and menopause symptoms in a 12-week trial — but the study was small and short

### Study 18: Doctors' gut-feel frailty check is good at ruling frailty out — but wrong half the time when it says 'frail'
**PMID:** 42750643 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. NPV described as "accuracy"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Eyeballing caught 94% of truly frail patients and ruled frailty out with 98.5% accuracy"
- **Abstract says:** "The eyeballing demonstrated high sensitivity (93.9%) and negative predictive value (NPV) (98.5%), with specificity of 80.8%, PPV of 49.2%, and an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.87."
- **Problem:** 98.5% is the negative predictive value, not diagnostic accuracy; overall accuracy and AUC (0.87) are different measures, so readers may over-read the figure.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "when it said a patient was not frail, that was right 98.5% of the time (negative predictive value).""

### Study 20: Phone-based palliative care didn't ease caregiver strain — but it didn't make things worse either
**PMID:** 42749467 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Null between-group result presented as no benefit in either arm** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "this trial suggests caregivers fare about the same as with in-person specialty care, though neither approach measurably lifted their burden"
- **Abstract says:** "The analysis did not reveal any differences in caregiver strain, QOL, and bereavement between the two care models... The study did not demonstrate that nurse-led telephonic care improved caregiver strain, QOL or bereavement outcomes compared with traditional outpatient palliative care."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports only that the two palliative care models did not differ; with no usual-care arm it cannot show that neither model improved caregiver burden. The claim also contradicts the digest's own caveat that there was no usual-care comparison.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to "caregivers fared about the same under either model — the trial could not test whether either model reduced burden compared with no palliative care.""

**Issue B. Headline implies absence of any benefit** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Phone-based palliative care didn't ease caregiver strain — but it didn't make things worse either"
- **Abstract says:** "The estimated difference in average change in strain scores between groups was -1.6 points (95% CI: -3.4 to 0.3)"
- **Problem:** "Didn't ease caregiver strain" reads as an absolute finding rather than the comparative one actually reported against specialty palliative care.
- **Suggested fix:** "Make the comparison explicit: "Phone-based palliative care left caregivers no better — and no worse — off than in-person specialty care.""

### Study 21: For end-of-life breathlessness, a high-flow nasal tube may beat a tight-fitting mask
**PMID:** 42749468 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline drops the key physiological condition** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "For end-of-life breathlessness, a high-flow nasal tube may beat a tight-fitting mask"
- **Abstract says:** "Respiratory support selection should be guided by respiratory physiology (hypercapnic vs hypoxemic)... NIV remains optimal for hypercapnic failure, whereas HFNT offers superior comfort and preserves communication in hypoxaemic patients."
- **Problem:** The review does not conclude HFNT is generally better than NIV; it concludes each is preferred in a different physiological state (HFNT in low-oxygen failure, NIV when carbon dioxide is retained). The headline implies a blanket winner, though the body text does explain the distinction.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to, e.g., "When oxygen is low at the end of life, a high-flow nasal tube may be better tolerated than a tight-fitting mask"."

### Study 24: In Healthy Adults Over 70, Body Fat Tracked With One Specific Mental Skill — and Education and Mood Explained Much of It
**PMID:** 42747296 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Cross-sectional age difference framed as weight loss over time** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the finding that greater age came with lower adiposity in this cohort is a reminder that weight loss in later life is not automatically good news. Unexplained shrinking body fat in an 85-year-old deserves clinical attention, not congratulations."
- **Abstract says:** "Greater age was associated with lower adiposity and poorer cognitive performance (p < 0.05)."
- **Problem:** This was a cross-sectional comparison across participants of different ages, not a measurement of individuals losing fat over time; the study cannot speak to the meaning of weight loss in a given person. The clinical advice, while sensible geriatrics, does not come from these data.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the age–adiposity pattern is a snapshot across ages, and attribute the advice about unexplained weight loss to general clinical practice rather than to this study."

### Study 25: Sleep-Study Brain Waves Can Signal Which People With Dream-Acting Disorder Are Headed Toward Parkinson's
**PMID:** 42747400 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline misdescribes the EEG recording** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Sleep-Study Brain Waves Can Signal Which People With Dream-Acting Disorder Are Headed Toward Parkinson's"
- **Abstract says:** "Resting-state EEG was recorded from 134 patients with iRBD and 51 healthy controls (HC)."
- **Problem:** The predictive measures came from resting-state EEG, not from a sleep study (polysomnography). Readers — and families asking clinicians about it — could think the prognostic signal comes from the overnight sleep test used to diagnose iRBD. The body text correctly says "resting-state EEG."
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Awake brain-wave recordings can signal..." or "A routine EEG may signal..." to match the body text."

### Study 26: Burned-Out and Depressed Family Caregivers Understand Health Information Less Well — Which May Put Their Relative at Risk
**PMID:** 42745685 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unmeasured downstream harm in headline and angle** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Which May Put Their Relative at Risk"
- **Abstract says:** "In the ordinal logistic regression analysis, higher educational attainment, a greater number of days per week devoted to caregiving, lower levels of burnout, and the absence of depressive symptoms were associated with increased odds of having higher levels of health literacy."
- **Problem:** The study measured only caregiver health literacy and its correlates; it did not assess any outcome for the older adult being cared for, so patient risk (or medication safety, as raised in the second story angle) is speculation layered onto a cross-sectional association.
- **Suggested fix:** "Hedge explicitly that care-recipient outcomes were not measured, e.g., "— a gap researchers worry could affect the person they care for, though this study did not measure that.""

### Study 27: Ireland Tried America's Best-Known Age-Friendly Care Framework — Staff Say It Works, With One Awkward Problem
**PMID:** 42749688 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Intensified wording on barriers** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Barriers included heavy reliance on Americanized materials, risk of tokenistic box-ticking adoption, uneven integration across settings, and almost no measurement of whether outcomes actually improved."
- **Abstract says:** "Barriers to implementation included reliance on Americanized materials, risks of tokenistic adoption, variable integration across settings, and limited outcome evaluation."
- **Problem:** "Heavy" reliance and "almost no measurement" are stronger than the abstract's "reliance" and "limited outcome evaluation," nudging a qualitative theme toward a quantified deficiency the abstract does not claim.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use the abstract's wording: "reliance on Americanized materials... and limited evaluation of whether outcomes improved.""

**Issue B. Headline implies a single barrier** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Staff Say It Works, With One Awkward Problem"
- **Abstract says:** "Barriers to implementation included reliance on Americanized materials, risks of tokenistic adoption, variable integration across settings, and limited outcome evaluation."
- **Problem:** Four distinct barriers were identified, not one; the headline's framing understates the range of implementation problems the body text then lists.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "— Staff Say It Works, But Flag Several Problems"."
