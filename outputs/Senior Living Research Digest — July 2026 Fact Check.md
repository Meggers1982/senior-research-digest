### Study 5: Smartwatches Win Over Older Adults in Bangladesh — With Time and Support
**PMID:** 42378312 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Incorrect baseline comfort percentage** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Comfort ratings improved dramatically — from 26% finding the watch 'very comfortable' initially to 80% at final follow-up."
- **Abstract says:** "with participants rating the smartwatch as very comfortable, increasing from 26% (5/19) at baseline to 80% (16/20) at final follow-up"
- **Problem:** The digest is accurate but the denominators differ slightly (19 at baseline, 20 at follow-up), which the digest omits. This is a very minor simplification and doesn't misrepresent the finding.
- **Suggested fix:** No change required; this is acceptable rounding/simplification.

**Issue B. Claim about education and adaptation rate** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Those with less formal education actually adapted faster than those with more education."
- **Abstract says:** "faster adoption among participants with lower educational backgrounds"
- **Problem:** The abstract states this in the conclusions section as a finding about "faster adoption," which aligns with the digest. Verified.
- **Suggested fix:** None needed.

### Study 11: Depression and Low Activity Together Dramatically Increase Frailty Risk
**PMID:** 42372268 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Statistical claim precision** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Those without depression and with very high physical activity had 99% lower odds of frailty compared to those with depression and very low activity."
- **Abstract says:** "The biggest odds reduction occurred in participants without depression and with very high PA (pre-frailty: OR = 0.016, 95% CI = 0.013, 0.019; frailty: OR = 0.001; 95% CI = 0.000, 0.001)"
- **Problem:** An OR of 0.001 translates to 99.9% lower odds, not 99%. The digest rounds appropriately for a lay audience but slightly understates the magnitude.
- **Suggested fix:** "Those without depression and with very high physical activity had over 99% lower odds of frailty..." (or retain as-is; this is acceptable simplification).
