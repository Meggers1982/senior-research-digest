### Study 4: Making a digital collage of your life lifted confidence in older cancer patients — and eased the strain on their families
**PMID:** 42853972 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Definitive causal framing of a preliminary pilot** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Making a digital collage of your life lifted confidence in older cancer patients — and eased the strain on their families"
- **Abstract says:** "These findings suggest that digital collage activity based on a life review has the potential to improve psychosocial self-efficacy among patients and quality of life among family caregivers."
- **Problem:** The authors frame the results as preliminary potential; the headline asserts the effects as established, despite only 13 of 21 intervention participants completing the program.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to something like 'may lift confidence ... and ease strain on families, a small pilot trial suggests'."

**Issue B. Direction of caregiver burden change inferred** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "their family caregivers reported improved overall quality of life with reduced psychological burden"
- **Abstract says:** "In the family intervention group, significant interactions were observed for the total CQOLC score, and significant changes were noted in the psychological burden subscales."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports 'significant changes' in the psychological burden subscales without stating the direction; the digest asserts reduction.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write 'significant changes in the psychological burden subscales' or note the direction is not specified in the abstract."

**Issue C. Story angle overstates certainty** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is an example of an intervention that measurably reduced caregiver psychological burden, not just patient distress"
- **Abstract says:** "investigate its preliminary effectiveness ... has the potential to improve ... quality of life among family caregivers"
- **Problem:** Presents a preliminary pilot result to professionals as a demonstrated reduction.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add 'in a small preliminary trial' and note the high dropout."

### Study 5: A 4-week trial of '40 Hz' light therapy failed to change brain activity in healthy older adults
**PMID:** 42847333 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported superlative** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "know that the best-designed test so far found no measurable effect on brain activity"
- **Abstract says:** "In this randomized, double-blind, active-controlled pilot trial, 33 cognitively normal older adults were assigned ..."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes a pilot trial and calls for larger randomized trials; it makes no claim about being the best-designed evidence to date. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Replace with 'a randomized, double-blind pilot trial found no measurable effect on brain activity'."

**Issue B. Study setting not stated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Caveats: Pilot trial with only 33 participants (29 analyzed), single center, cognitively normal volunteers"
- **Abstract says:** "Chang Gung University, Taoyuan, Taiwan; Chang Gung Memorial Hospital, Kaohsiung, Taiwan"
- **Problem:** The digest never tells readers the trial was conducted in Taiwan, which is relevant context for generalizability alongside the single-center caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the country to the study description or caveats."

### Study 6: Those white spots on your brain MRI: a review of 5,700 scans ties them to slower thinking

- **Problem:** The abstract states the synthesis was narrative but does not give heterogeneity as the reason. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'narrative synthesis only, with no pooled statistics' and drop the stated reason."

### Study 9: Spinal fusion without the expensive bone-growth protein worked just as well, five-year data suggest
**PMID:** 42854246 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Comparative claim with no comparison group** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Spinal fusion without the expensive bone-growth protein worked just as well, five-year data suggest ... this large single-center series suggests comparable results without it"
- **Abstract says:** "The authors retrospectively reviewed the records of patients who underwent MI-TLIF without BMP at a single academic center ... MI-TLIF without BMP demonstrates high fusion rates, durable clinical improvement, and low reoperation rates, supporting its use as a safe and cost-effective alternative to BMP-augmented fusion."
- **Problem:** The study has no BMP-treated comparison arm, so 'worked just as well' and 'comparable results' assert an equivalence the data cannot test — and the digest's own caveat says exactly that, contradicting the body text.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as 'achieved a 91.9% fusion rate without the bone-growth protein' and note that no BMP comparison group was studied."

### Study 10: A brain growth factor measured a week after stroke tracked with mood and thinking months later — the much-hyped gene variant didn't
**PMID:** 42852574 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies a six-month mood association that was null** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "A brain growth factor measured a week after stroke tracked with mood and thinking months later"
- **Abstract says:** "Baseline serum BDNF was inversely associated with baseline BDI-II score (adjusted Spearman rho = -0.256, p = 0.007) ... No significant association was found with BDI-II at 6 months."
- **Problem:** The mood association was concurrent at day 7 only; there was no significant association with depressive symptoms at six months. The body text gets this right ('at the time of measurement'), so the headline contradicts the article.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as 'tracked with mood at the time and with thinking months later'."

**Issue B. 'Why it matters' overstates risk prediction for depression** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this suggests an early blood marker may flag who is at risk"
- **Abstract says:** "No significant association was found with BDI-II at 6 months ... These findings support the clinical relevance of BDNF as a potential biomarker of mood and cognitive outcomes in stroke survivors"
- **Problem:** For depression specifically, BDNF did not predict later symptoms, so 'flag who is at risk' overstates the prospective value shown here.
- **Suggested fix:** "Limit the forward-looking claim to cognitive outcomes, noting the six-month depression association was null."

### Study 11: Traffic and home-heating pollution each raise dementia risk — and the danger multiplies for those with high genetic risk
**PMID:** 42853264 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal verb in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Traffic and home-heating pollution each raise dementia risk — and the danger multiplies for those with high genetic risk"
- **Abstract says:** "Higher source-specific PM2.5 exposure levels were associated with a higher risk of incident dementia."
- **Problem:** "Raise" and "the danger multiplies" assert causation and an interaction effect from an observational cohort that reports adjusted hazard ratios and a joint-category comparison only.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Traffic and home-heating pollution are each linked to higher dementia risk — and risk is highest in those who also carry high genetic risk.""

**Issue B. Joint-risk magnitude unquantified / framed as amplification** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "People with both high pollution exposure and high genetic susceptibility had dramatically elevated risk compared with those low on both, and a similar amplifying pattern appeared for unhealthy lifestyle combined with high exposure."
- **Abstract says:** "Participants with high levels of source-specific PM2.5 exposure and high genetic susceptibility had a greater risk of incident dementia compared to those with the lowest pollutant exposure and lowest genetic risk (∼236% to 352%). A similar pattern was observed for the joint relationship between source-specific PM2.5 and lifestyle factors."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a joint-category contrast, not a formally demonstrated amplifying (interaction) effect; the digest also omits the reported magnitude range.
- **Suggested fix:** "Give the figure (roughly 2.4- to 4.5-fold higher risk than the low-exposure/low-genetic-risk group) and say the two risks "combined" rather than "amplified.""

**Issue C. Elaboration of 'domestic' source** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "household heating and cooking, road transport, other transport, or industry"
- **Abstract says:** "PM2.5 from domestic, transport, other transport, and industry sources"
- **Problem:** The abstract labels the category only as "domestic"; specifying heating and cooking is an elaboration not verifiable from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "domestic (household) sources" or flag the heating/cooking detail as the usual composition of that category."

### Study 12: Doctors rarely write down why an older gay or lesbian patient's abuse happened — a first look at elder abuse in LGB veterans
**PMID:** 42844572 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsourced background claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Older LGB adults often have smaller family safety nets and histories of discrimination that shape both their risk of abuse and their willingness to report it"
- **Abstract says:** "Older adults who identify as lesbian, gay, or bisexual (LGB) may be at increased risk of elder abuse (EA) due to unique risk factors"
- **Problem:** The specific mechanisms (smaller family networks, discrimination affecting reporting) are stated as established fact but are not in the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute to prior literature or soften to "researchers suggest LGB elders may face unique risk factors.""

**Issue B. Sampling frame omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "researchers reviewed charts of veterans aged 60 and older who identified as lesbian, gay, or bisexual and had a social work visit for possible abuse or neglect"
- **Abstract says:** "We randomly sampled 100 of 201 Veterans aged ≥60 years ... We conducted protocolized chart reviews ... for 23 LGB patients, with EHR documentation confirming EA"
- **Problem:** Readers may think all eligible veterans were reviewed; in fact 100 of 201 were sampled and 23 had confirmed abuse.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that 100 of 201 eligible charts were randomly sampled, of which 23 had confirmed elder abuse."

### Study 15: Being a conscientious person and acting conscientiously in the moment had opposite links to memory performance
**PMID:** 42663588 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Delivery method not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "completed brief smartphone-based cognitive tests"
- **Abstract says:** "We conducted a three-week ecological momentary assessment study of middle-aged and older adults (N = 183)."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not specify that the momentary cognitive tests were smartphone-based. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "brief repeated cognitive tests delivered in daily life (ecological momentary assessment).""

**Issue B. Overstated within- vs between-person claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "your own good days and off days — when you are more focused, more organized, more socially engaged — track with your thinking performance more meaningfully than any trait label does"
- **Abstract says:** "we demonstrate that trait-level associations do not necessarily generalize to state-level ones"
- **Problem:** The study shows the two levels diverge, not that within-person states are a more meaningful predictor for an individual; no comparison of predictive value is reported.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Your day-to-day fluctuations show a different — sometimes opposite — pattern from what broad personality labels would predict.""

### Study 16: Pessimism about aging — not optimism about it — predicted how well patients recovered after hip fracture and stroke
**PMID:** 42625386 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Nuance on 'only negative views mattered'** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Positive views of aging showed no significant benefit; only the negative views mattered."
- **Abstract says:** "Negative views had a significantly stronger effect on RE than positive views, but only for the AAQ subscales."
- **Problem:** The direct superiority of negative over positive views held only for the generalized (AAQ) measure, not for the personal (AARC) measure; the digest presents it as a blanket conclusion.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that negative views were statistically stronger than positive views only on the generalized attitudes measure."

### Study 17: A single inflammation marker in midlife predicted physical decline, diabetes and weaker thinking seven years later
**PMID:** 42841728 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal framing contradicts caveat** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "the evidence that chronic low-grade inflammation is a broad driver of how people age"
- **Abstract says:** "These findings demonstrate that chronic inflammation is associated with multiple dimensions of aging in a lower-middle income setting"
- **Problem:** "Driver" asserts causation from an observational prospective association, and contradicts the digest's own caveat that causation cannot be established.
- **Suggested fix:** ""the evidence that chronic low-grade inflammation tracks with multiple dimensions of how people age.""

**Issue B. Headline says 'a single marker'** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A single inflammation marker in midlife predicted physical decline, diabetes and weaker thinking seven years later"
- **Abstract says:** "two complementary measures of chronic inflammation-plasma C-reactive protein (CRP) and a DNA methylation-based surrogate (DNAm-CRP)"
- **Problem:** Two distinct markers were measured and compared; "a single marker" misstates the design, and "midlife" is loose for a cohort aged 43–75 at follow-up.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Two inflammation markers measured years earlier predicted physical decline, diabetes and weaker thinking in Filipino women.""

### Study 18: The oldest seniors reported the least heat stress — and researchers say that may be the problem
**PMID:** 42841726 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Interpretation attributed to researchers** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "The oldest seniors reported the least heat stress — and researchers say that may be the problem"
- **Abstract says:** "The results reveal that much older adults (aged 80 +) were less likely to experience heat stress compared to their relatively younger counterparts. ... These results underscore the complex interplay of behavioral, socio-demographic, and health-related factors"
- **Problem:** The abstract offers no interpretation that the 80+ finding reflects impaired perception or under-reporting; the digest's own Caveats concede this is the digest's inference, so the headline misattributes it to the study authors.
- **Suggested fix:** ""The oldest seniors reported the least heat stress — a finding worth a second look" and keep the under-reporting explanation clearly labeled as interpretation."

**Issue B. Coping finding upgraded** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "interviews pointed to individual behavioral adjustment as the most effective coping strategy people had found"
- **Abstract says:** "The qualitative results uncovered individual-level behavioural adjustment as an effective coping mechanism during extreme heat events."
- **Problem:** The abstract calls it "an effective" coping mechanism, not the most effective; no ranking of strategies is reported.
- **Suggested fix:** ""interviews pointed to individual behavioral adjustment as an effective coping strategy.""

**Issue C. Physiological-risk leap** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this suggests the people at greatest physiological risk may be the least likely to report feeling stressed by it"
- **Abstract says:** "much older adults (aged 80 +) were less likely to experience heat stress compared to their relatively younger counterparts"
- **Problem:** The study measured reported heat stress only; it did not assess physiological risk or compare perception with physiological vulnerability.
- **Suggested fix:** "Frame as a hypothesis: "if, as other research suggests, the oldest adults are physiologically most vulnerable, this reporting gap would be worrying.""
