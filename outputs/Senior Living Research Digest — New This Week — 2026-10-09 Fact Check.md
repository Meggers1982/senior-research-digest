### Study 1: A popular anti-aging supplement made Parkinson's slightly worse in a large trial
**PMID:** 42848371 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Demographics attributed to randomized rather than analyzed sample** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "410 people with Parkinson disease diagnosed within the previous two years (mean age 65.6, 34% women) at 11 Norwegian centers were randomized"
- **Abstract says:** "Of 537 screened individuals, 410 were randomized ... and 393 were included in the primary analysis. ... Among the 393 participants in the primary analysis, the mean age was 65.6 (SD, 9.4) years and 133 (34%) were female."
- **Problem:** The mean age and sex breakdown describe the 393 participants in the primary analysis, not all 410 randomized; the digest also never mentions the analysis population.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that 410 were randomized and 393 were analyzed, with the age/sex figures describing the analyzed group."

### Study 3: Osteoporosis patients with a stubbornly low lab value may have a missed genetic bone disease
**PMID:** 42844428 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Treatment claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the distinction matters because standard bone-building drugs such as bisphosphonates are generally inappropriate in HPP"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract discusses only the frequency and prevalence of HPP among patients with persistently low ALP and advises clinicians to consider the diagnosis; it says nothing about bisphosphonates or any treatment."
- **Problem:** A specific therapeutic claim is presented as the study's rationale but cannot be verified from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the bisphosphonate point to general clinical guidance rather than to this review, or drop it."

**Issue B. Funding characterization unverifiable** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Industry-funded and authored — several authors are employees of or consultants to Alexion/AstraZeneca Rare Disease"
- **Abstract says:** "The conflict-of-interest statement lists one current Alexion employee (Shona Fang), one former AstraZeneca Rare Disease employee (Liliana-Georgiana Wegmann), and many authors with Alexion consulting/honoraria ties; no funding source is stated."
- **Problem:** Author ties are documented, but the abstract contains no funding statement, so "industry-funded" is unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "industry-affiliated authorship — one current and one former Alexion/AstraZeneca Rare Disease employee and multiple authors with company payments" unless a funding statement is confirmed."

### Study 8: Qigong improved memory and thinking in people with mild cognitive impairment, a review of 21 trials finds
**PMID:** 42847483 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline states efficacy the abstract hedges** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Qigong improved memory and thinking in people with mild cognitive impairment, a review of 21 trials finds"
- **Abstract says:** "Qigong-based exercises may improve cognition in older adults with MCI ... Further trials with long-term follow-up are needed to confirm the efficacy of the intervention."
- **Problem:** The abstract explicitly says "may improve" and calls for confirmatory trials; the headline asserts a demonstrated improvement.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Qigong may improve memory and thinking ...""

**Issue B. Effect-size characterization attributed to the paper** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with effect sizes described as large"
- **Abstract says:** "SMDs of 0.88 (MoCA), 1.03 (MMSE), 1.03 (WMS), 1.01 (AVLT) and -0.93 (Stroop) are reported, with no verbal characterization of their magnitude."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports numbers but never describes the effects as large; the phrasing implies the author did.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "effect sizes in the range conventionally considered large (SMD ~0.9–1.0).""

**Issue C. Origin of included trials unverifiable** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "most included trials come from Chinese research settings"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract lists CNKI, Wanfang, SinoMed and VIP among the databases searched but does not report the countries of origin of the 21 included trials."
- **Problem:** A plausible inference from the database list is presented as a fact about the included studies.
- **Suggested fix:** "Flag as "Unverifiable from abstract only" or rephrase as "Chinese-language databases were searched, suggesting many trials come from Chinese settings.""

### Study 9: Dose matters: higher-dose brexpiprazole eased agitation in Alzheimer's even in severe dementia, lower dose fell short
**PMID:** 42847294 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. CGI-S pattern misdescribed** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Global clinical severity ratings followed the same pattern."
- **Abstract says:** "Differences in CGI-S score were -0.3 (p = 0.0598) and -0.4 (p = 0.0762) in brexpiprazole 1 mg, -0.8 (p < 0.0001) and -0.7 (p = 0.0002) in brexpiprazole 2 mg"
- **Problem:** On CGI-S the 1 mg dose failed to reach significance in BOTH severity groups, not just in severe dementia, so the pattern differs from CMAI, where 1 mg was significant in mild-to-moderate disease.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "Global clinical severity ratings also favored the 2 mg dose in both groups, while the 1 mg dose did not reach significance in either.""

### Study 13: A second, simple question may help doctors spot which patients need palliative care — and it works the same across Europe
**PMID:** 42853125 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unspecified GP characteristics** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "while the GPs' own characteristics (such as experience or country) had no influence on their answers"
- **Abstract says:** "GP characteristics did not influence the answer. ... no associations were found between measured GP characteristics and responses"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not say which GP characteristics were measured; naming experience and country is unverifiable from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "the measured characteristics of the GPs themselves had no influence" without specifying which ones."

**Issue B. Practice recommendation ahead of validation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this supports adding the second question to routine reviews as a low-cost prompt"
- **Abstract says:** "We recommend prospective studies in real-world populations to relate the answers to the Double Surprise Question to actual death to further demonstrate its clinical efficacy."
- **Problem:** The authors explicitly call for real-world validation before clinical efficacy is established; suggesting routine adoption goes a step beyond a hypothetical-vignette study.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "this makes the second question worth watching as a low-cost prompt, pending real-world validation studies the authors call for"."

### Study 19: Who you first see for palliative care predicts how much care you actually get
**PMID:** 42849667 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Nearly a third" overstates** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "nearly a third of eligible decedents had no palliative encounter at all despite being treated at hospitals with established programs"
- **Abstract says:** "We identified 33,677 poor-prognosis cancer decedents attributed to 276 hospitals; 72.8% had at least one PC encounter."
- **Problem:** 27.2% had no encounter, which is closer to a quarter than a third.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "more than a quarter of eligible decedents had no palliative encounter at all"."

**Issue B. Missing comparator for odds ratio** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "first encounters with a team-based physician were associated with nearly three times the odds of having three or more encounters"
- **Abstract says:** "team-based physician first encounters were associated with higher odds of 3+ encounters (Odds Ratio [OR]=2.88, 95% CI=2.70-3.08) vs. independent physician encounters."
- **Problem:** The OR is relative to independent physician first encounters, not to all other clinician types; omitting the comparator makes the effect sound absolute.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "compared with an independent physician" after the odds statement."

### Study 20: Pancreatic cancer patients and caregivers describe delays, financial strain — and widespread confusion about palliative care
**PMID:** 42842360 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Quantified harm not in the data** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "these accounts show that misunderstanding what palliative care is can cost patients months of symptom relief and support they were entitled to from the start"
- **Abstract says:** "End-of-life conversations and palliative care were often misunderstood ... exploratory qualitative study ... Semistructured interviews with 21 adults"
- **Problem:** The study identified misunderstanding as a theme; it did not measure delayed palliative care or quantify lost months of symptom relief. "Show" plus a time quantity asserts more than qualitative interview themes can support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe: "these accounts suggest that confusion about what palliative care is may keep patients from supportive services they could use earlier"."

**Issue B. "Not more information" contradicts abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the participants named compassion and clarity, not more information, as what was missing"
- **Abstract says:** "Patients with advanced pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) and their families encounter challenges that extend beyond disease management, encompassing informational gaps ... Suggestions for health care providers emphasized the need for a holistic approach and practical resources."
- **Problem:** The abstract explicitly names informational gaps and requests for practical resources, so ruling out "more information" as a need misstates the findings.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "participants asked for clarity and compassion alongside practical resources and information"."

### Study 22: Stroke survivors with aphasia face nearly three times the risk of developing epilepsy
**PMID:** 42848724 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. 'Nearly three times' is an unadjusted comparison** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Stroke survivors with aphasia face nearly three times the risk of developing epilepsy"
- **Abstract says:** "the incidence of PSE was higher in patients with aphasia than in those without aphasia (13.02 vs. 4.56 per 1,000 person-years; aRD 2.96, 95% confidence interval [CI] 2.60-3.37)"
- **Problem:** The ~2.9-fold figure comes from the crude incidence rates; the abstract reports only an adjusted rate difference (about 3 extra cases per 1,000 person-years), not an adjusted ratio. The headline implies an adjusted relative risk the abstract does not give.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'roughly three times the crude rate of epilepsy' or lead with the adjusted excess of about 3 cases per 1,000 person-years."

**Issue B. Median follow-up rounded across two cohorts** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "for a median of roughly 8 years"
- **Abstract says:** "With a median follow-up of 7.56 years for the PSA group (N=23,431) and 8.67 years for the non-aphasia group (N=130,058)"
- **Problem:** A single '8 years' figure blends two different medians; the aphasia group was followed for a shorter median time.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write 'for a median of 7.6 years (aphasia group) and 8.7 years (no aphasia).'"

### Study 23: Glasses are costing older Americans the most — and seniors are the likeliest to pay the whole bill themselves
**PMID:** 42848386 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Age-group estimates come from the 2018–2023 adjusted period** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Adults aged 65 to 74 were 12.3 percentage points more likely to have lens spending than people under 21, spent $129 more on average, and were 18.8 percentage points more likely to cover the whole cost themselves."
- **Abstract says:** "On an adjusted basis in the 2018 to 2023 period, spending outcomes increased with age; 65- to 74-year olds were 12.27 percentage points ... more likely to have any spending ... higher mean spending by $129.34 ... and were 18.78 percentage points ... more likely to finance all spending out-of-pocket, compared with respondents who were 20 years and younger."
- **Problem:** The digest presents these as general findings without noting they are adjusted estimates restricted to the 2018–2023 window.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add 'in adjusted analyses covering 2018 through 2023' before the age-group figures."

**Issue B. 'Likeliest to pay the whole bill' overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "seniors are the likeliest to pay the whole bill themselves"
- **Abstract says:** "The uninsured were 34.6 percentage points (95% CI, 31.52-37.70) more likely to be 100% out-of-pocket compared with those with private health insurance."
- **Problem:** The abstract's largest out-of-pocket disparity is by insurance status, not age; the 65–74 figure is relative only to people aged 20 and under.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as 'adults 65 to 74 are far more likely than young people to pay the entire bill themselves' and note the uninsured gap."

### Study 24: Pain relief from platelet injections for knee arthritis faded within months, small study finds
**PMID:** 42847270 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Timing of fading benefit not stated in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Pain relief from platelet injections for knee arthritis faded within months"
- **Abstract says:** "WOMAC and NRS scores decreased during the early follow-up period, reaching their lowest values at T2, followed by progressive increases at T3 and T4."
- **Problem:** The abstract labels assessments only as T0–T4 and never specifies the interval in months, so 'within months' cannot be verified from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'faded at later follow-up assessments' unless the month intervals can be sourced from the full text."

**Issue B. TUG finding omitted from functional summary** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Six-minute walk distance and objectively measured gait did not improve significantly at any point."
- **Abstract says:** "TUG showed a significant overall change over time, although no consecutive comparison remained significant after correction for multiple comparisons. No significant changes over time were observed in 6MWT or gait-related outcomes."
- **Problem:** The sensor-measured timed-up-and-go did show a significant overall change over time (though not between consecutive time points after correction), so a blanket 'no objective improvement' slightly oversimplifies.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that the timed-up-and-go test changed significantly overall but no individual comparison survived correction for multiple testing."
