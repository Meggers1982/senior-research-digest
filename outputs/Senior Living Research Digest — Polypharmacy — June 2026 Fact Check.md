## Study 2: Certain fall-risk medications—but not blood pressure drugs—drive hospitalization risk in older adults
**PMID:** 42343007 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Slight overstatement of antihypertensive finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Notably, antihypertensive medications alone were not linked to increased fall-hospitalizations."
- **Abstract says:** The abstract focuses on "fall-risk-increasing drugs" as a category and does not specifically report results for antihypertensives alone. The abstract's FRID definition appears to exclude antihypertensives based on the digest's framing, but the abstract states the study examined "fall-risk-increasing drug exposure... by drug class."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not explicitly state that antihypertensives were analyzed separately and found to have no association. This claim cannot be fully verified from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "The study focused on fall-risk-increasing drugs excluding antihypertensives, finding opioids and antidepressants showed the strongest associations with fall-related hospitalization."

**Note:** The core findings (51% higher risk, opioids and antidepressants as highest risk) are accurately reported. Sample size (16,118 cases and controls), mean age (79.76 rounded to 80), and country (Italy) are correct.

## Study 9: Cardiovascular polypharmacy linked to kidney dysfunction in older patients on cholesterol medications
**PMID:** 42056610 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Age reporting** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "median age 63"
- **Abstract says:** "median age 63 years, interquartile range [IQR] 54-72"
- **Problem:** Accurate, but the headline mentions "older patients" while median age is 63. The study included adults of various ages on lipid-lowering therapy.
- **Suggested fix:** Consider noting in the study description that participants ranged in age, with older adults (≥80 years) showing higher odds of CVD-related polypharmacy.

**Issue B. Effect size reporting** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "45% higher odds of kidney dysfunction"
- **Abstract says:** "OR 1.45, 95% CI 1.30-1.62"
- **Problem:** Accurate conversion of OR to percentage.

Overall findings are accurately reported. The cross-sectional caveat is appropriately noted.

**PMID:** 42288945 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. The digest correctly reports:
- Duration (six months)
- Sample size (40 participants)
- Population (African American older adults 65+, predominantly female, Houston TX)
- Inclusion criteria (5+ medications, smart device ownership)
- Interventions (medication reminder app, two pharmacist-led telehealth MTM sessions)
