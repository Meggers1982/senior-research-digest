# Fact-Check Report

### Study 1: Biological aging measured from blood tests linked to early muscle loss in adults under 40
**PMID:** 42449042 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. Framing is consistent with the abstract. The digest correctly reports the sample size (165,015 with 61,669 having repeat scans), mean age (39.9 rounded to 40), median follow-up (4.1 years), study design, and key findings about biological age acceleration being associated with myopenia and myosteatosis, including in participants <40 years.

### Study 2: New 6-question screening tool catches sarcopenia risk in community settings
**PMID:** 42508218 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. AUC range slightly misreported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "good overall accuracy (AUC 0.80–0.92)"
- **Abstract says:** "the AUC of the screening instrument ranged from 0.798 to 0.822 based on Asian standards, compared to 0.840 to 0.922 based on European standards"
- **Problem:** The digest conflates the two AUC ranges. The 0.80 appears rounded from Asian standards (0.798-0.822), while 0.92 comes from European standards (up to 0.922). This could be clearer.
- **Suggested fix:** "good overall accuracy (AUC 0.80–0.82 against Asian criteria; 0.84–0.92 against European criteria)"

### Study 8: CT scans reveal thigh muscle shrinkage predicts grip strength and mobility decline over 5 years
**PMID:** 42287341 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Outcome measures slightly simplified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "slower walking speed"
- **Abstract says:** "an 121% longer TUG" (Timed Up and Go test)
- **Problem:** The digest says "slower walking speed" but the abstract specifically refers to TUG performance, not gait speed per se. TUG is a mobility measure that includes walking but is not simply walking speed.
- **Suggested fix:** "declining grip strength and mobility (Timed Up and Go performance)"

### Study 16: Sarcopenia nearly doubles cognitive impairment risk in Latin American older adults
**PMID:** 42155950 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Effect size slightly overstated in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "nearly doubles" (headline)
- **Abstract says:** "OR = 2.30; 95% CI: 1.68-3.15"
- **Problem:** An OR of 2.30 is "more than doubles," not "nearly doubles." The body text correctly says "2.3-fold higher odds."
- **Suggested fix:** Change headline to "Sarcopenia more than doubles cognitive impairment risk in Latin American older adults"

**Issue B. EWGSOP2 effect correctly reported** — The "nearly 3-fold" (OR 2.96) for EWGSOP2 criteria is accurate.

### Study 17: Elevated metabolic index linked to new sarcopenia cases over 4 years
**PMID:** 42217315 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Cumulative CHG odds ratio reported instead of HR** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "25% higher odds of new sarcopenia"
- **Abstract says:** "OR = 1.25, 95% CI: 1.02-1.45" (for cumulative CHG analysis)
- **Problem:** The baseline analysis used Cox regression (HR = 1.287 = 29% higher risk), while cumulative analysis used logistic regression (OR = 1.25). The digest conflates "odds" terminology but the numbers are accurate.

Otherwise accurate. Sample size (5,580) and 4-year follow-up correctly reported.

### Study 19: Osteoporosis and sarcopenia share genetic links and may accelerate each other
**PMID:** 42380078 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Genetic correlation rounded** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "25% genetic correlation"
- **Abstract says:** "positive genetic correlation (r = 0.25, p = 1.6 × 10-5)"
- **Problem:** The correlation coefficient is 0.25, which the digest expresses as "25%." While technically a correlation of 0.25 could be described this way, it may confuse readers who expect percentages to represent something different. Consider "genetic correlation of 0.25" for precision.
- **Suggested fix:** "The conditions shared a genetic correlation of 0.25"

Otherwise accurate in describing bidirectional associations and shared factors.
