### Study 2: A common sedative cuts post-surgery confusion in older patients nearly in half — but slows the heart
**PMID:** 42963481 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Heterogeneity overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "moderate heterogeneity"
- **Abstract says:** "dexmedetomidine significantly reduced the incidence of POD (RR = 0.56, 95% CI: 0.47 to 0.66; P < 0.00001; I2 = 24%) and reduced PONV (RR = 0.79; 95% CI = 0.62 to 0.99; P = 0.04; I2 = 0%)"
- **Problem:** I² of 24% for the primary outcome and 0% for PONV is low, not moderate, heterogeneity — the caveat makes the pooled estimate sound shakier than the reported statistics suggest.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'low statistical heterogeneity for the main outcome (I² = 24%), though trials varied in surgery type, dosing and delirium assessment.'"

### Study 3: Nearly 6 in 10 people with a rotator cuff tear also have weakened bone, review finds
**PMID:** 42955077 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Denominator applied to all three estimates** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers pooled 12 studies covering 1,413 patients with rotator cuff tears who had bone density measured. Osteoporosis was present in 17.3%, osteopenia (milder bone loss) in 41.9%, and abnormal bone mass overall in 57.9%."
- **Abstract says:** "Twelve studies involving 1,413 participants ... The osteoporosis analysis comprised 10 studies, with 184 cases among 1,094 participants ... Seven studies contributed to both the osteopenia and exploratory abnormal bone mass analyses, comprising 297 and 404 cases, respectively, among 721 participants."
- **Problem:** Each prevalence figure comes from a different subset of studies and patients, not from the full 1,413; the osteopenia and 57.9% figures rest on only 7 studies and 721 participants, and 57.9% was an exploratory outcome.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the osteoporosis estimate came from 10 studies (1,094 patients) and the osteopenia/abnormal-bone-mass estimates from 7 studies (721 patients), the latter an exploratory combined outcome."

**Issue B. Study-size caveat not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "most included studies were relatively small"
- **Abstract says:** "Twelve studies involving 1,413 participants with rotator cuff tears and available categorical bone-status data were included."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not report individual study sizes, so this characterization cannot be confirmed. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the claim or attribute it explicitly to the full text if verified there."

### Study 4: Tiny brain bleeds visible on routine MRI flag a 10-fold higher risk of a major hemorrhage years later
**PMID:** 42963963 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Odds ratio described as risk** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "flag a 10-fold higher risk of a major hemorrhage years later"
- **Abstract says:** "In adjusted analyses, any CMB (OR 10.96, 95% CI 4.00-30.02) ... In a multivariable model, CMB (OR 10.50,95% CI 3.63-30.42) remained independently associated with ICH"
- **Problem:** These are odds ratios from a case-control design, which cannot be read directly as absolute or relative risk; no incidence rates are reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say microbleeds were associated with roughly 10-fold higher odds of later hemorrhage in this case-control comparison."

**Issue B. Timeframe overstated in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a major hemorrhage years later"
- **Abstract says:** "The median interval between MRI and ICH was 14.6 months (IQR 8.3-29.5)."
- **Problem:** The typical gap was about 14 months, not 'years'; the body text gives the correct median but the headline generalizes beyond it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'about a year or more later' or 'a median of 14 months later' in the headline."

**Issue C. Perivascular spaces lost significance** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with enlarged perivascular spaces and white matter changes also linked"
- **Abstract says:** "In a multivariable model, CMB ... remained independently associated with ICH, whereas PVS > 20 did not reach significance (OR 3.35, 0.95-11.75)."
- **Problem:** Enlarged perivascular spaces were significant only in the adjusted single-marker analysis and dropped out of the multivariable model — presenting it alongside microbleeds overstates its standing.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that perivascular spaces no longer reached significance once all markers were considered together."

### Study 6: A widely used chemo risk calculator predicts side effects in older cancer patients — but only modestly
**PMID:** 42962620 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Odds ratios read as risk multiples** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "patients scored high-risk were nearly three times as likely to have severe toxicity and twice as likely not to complete treatment"
- **Abstract says:** "the high-risk category on the CARG toxicity tool was significantly associated with Grade ≥ 3 toxicity (OR 2.93 (95% CI 1.10-7.79, p = 0.03). High-risk CARG toxicity scores were correlated with treatment incompletion (OR 2.12, CI 1.04-4.30, p = 0.04)"
- **Problem:** With a 70% baseline toxicity rate, an odds ratio of 2.93 corresponds to a much smaller increase in actual probability; 'three times as likely' overstates the practical difference.
- **Suggested fix:** "Phrase as 'had nearly three times the odds of severe toxicity and about twice the odds of not completing treatment.'"

### Study 7: A brain abnormality long linked to Parkinson's turns out to have little to do with movement symptoms
**PMID:** 42960446 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Imaging subgroup size** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "43 followed for two years with dopamine brain imaging"
- **Abstract says:** "(1) baseline and 2-year follow-up (n = 43), with concurrent motor assessment and 18F-DOPA positron emission tomography (PET) imaging (n = 24)"
- **Problem:** Only 24 of the 43 longitudinal patients had the 18F-DOPA PET imaging; the digest implies all 43 did.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write '43 followed for two years, 24 of them with dopamine PET imaging.'"

### Study 8: Grip strength plus a simple blood-and-weight score flags who is most likely to become frail
**PMID:** 42956513 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal-sounding wording** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "while greater handgrip strength relative to body size was strongly protective (hazard ratios 0.57 and 0.36)"
- **Abstract says:** "whereas relative handgrip strength was inversely associated with frailty (Chinese cohort, hazard ratio = 0.57; English cohort, hazard ratio = 0.36)"
- **Problem:** 'Protective' implies a causal effect; the cohorts show only an inverse association, as the Caveats elsewhere acknowledge.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'was associated with substantially lower frailty risk.'"

**Issue B. Earlier-detection claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "together identify at-risk people earlier than either alone"
- **Abstract says:** "Participants with a high triglyceride-cholesterol-body weight index and low relative handgrip strength had the highest frailty risk ... predictive performance using the Harrell C-index, time-dependent area under the curve, and calibration plots."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports that the combination carried the highest risk, not that it identifies people earlier than either measure alone; no timing or head-to-head discrimination result is given.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the combination marked out the highest-risk group, rather than claiming earlier identification."

### Study 11: A memory test built around faces and names may catch early decline that standard screening misses
**PMID:** 42961537 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline reverses the comparative advantage** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "A memory test built around faces and names may catch early decline that standard screening misses"
- **Abstract says:** "ROC analyses demonstrated significant discrimination between groups for both DFR (AUC = 0.711) and the MoCA (AUC = 0.705), with no significant difference between the two ROC curves. While the MoCA showed higher sensitivity, DFR provided greater specificity"
- **Problem:** The face-name test did not detect more cases than the MoCA — the MoCA was more sensitive. The face-name test's edge was fewer false positives, not catching missed cases. The body text states this correctly, but the headline implies the opposite.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like 'A face-and-name memory test matches standard screening — with fewer false alarms' or 'may add information standard screening misses.'"

### Study 12: In cancer care, how well you cope may matter as much as your symptoms for predicting who ages fast
**PMID:** 42962619 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Prediction framing beyond a cross-sectional model** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "routinely asking cancer patients about resilience and coping — not just symptoms — can identify who is at risk of rapid functional decline during treatment, before it shows up as a crisis"
- **Abstract says:** "Outpatients (n = 1340) completed questionnaires prior to their second or third cycle of chemotherapy... Inclusion of a measure of psychological resilience in the model allows for better discrimination of inter-individual variability in clinical aging phenotypes... This approach may assist with identification of patients at increased risk for accelerated aging."
- **Problem:** The study is a single-timepoint latent class analysis; no one was followed to see who declined. 'Identify who is at risk of rapid functional decline during treatment, before it shows up as a crisis' asserts a temporal predictive capability the design cannot demonstrate, even though the Caveats note the absence of follow-up.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to 'may help distinguish patients whose overall profile looks older and frailer, a group the authors suggest could be at higher risk.'"

**Issue B. Age of cohort not stated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers surveyed 1,340 outpatients before their second or third round of chemotherapy"
- **Abstract says:** "Outpatients (n = 1340) completed questionnaires prior to their second or third cycle of chemotherapy."
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no age range or mean age, and reports that being *younger* was associated with the worst clinical aging profiles. The digest's story angles address older adults specifically, which the abstract does not support as the study population. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the cohort was general oncology outpatients of unspecified age, and that younger age was associated with worse clinical aging profiles."

### Study 13: The new Alzheimer's blood test survives real-world handling — a hurdle for rolling it out beyond specialist clinics
**PMID:** 42958309 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Incomplete stability list** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "results held steady for two days at room temperature, a week refrigerated, and 12 weeks frozen, and survived one freeze-thaw cycle"
- **Abstract says:** "analyte stability for all sample types was determined to be 2 days at 25 °C, 7 days at 4 °C, 12 weeks at -20 °C, and 16 weeks at -80°C; stability was also observed after one freeze/thaw cycle"
- **Problem:** The digest reports only the -20 °C figure and omits the 16-week stability at -80 °C, understating the assay's storage window.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add '12 weeks at -20 °C and 16 weeks at -80 °C.'"

**Issue B. Shipping claim outruns the described handling** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "whether a sample drawn at a small primary care office still gives a valid answer after shipping — this suggests it does"
- **Abstract says:** "These were transported under cooled (never frozen) conditions to the measurement site and processed within 6 h of collection."
- **Problem:** Samples were transported cooled and processed within six hours; the study did not test prolonged or uncontrolled shipping from a remote office. The digest's claim generalizes beyond the conditions actually examined.
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify that samples were kept cooled and processed within six hours of collection, so the findings apply to that handling pathway."

### Study 14: Chronic pain and thinking problems in later life may travel through sleep and mood
**PMID:** 42958704 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unquantified 'substantial part'** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a substantial part of the link ran indirectly — through depressive symptoms, and through a chain from poor sleep to depression to cognition"
- **Abstract says:** "TSSEM further identified a significant direct association between chronic pain and cognitive dysfunction, as well as significant indirect pathways through depressive symptoms and through the serial pathway involving sleep disturbances and depressive symptoms."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports that the indirect pathways were statistically significant but gives no effect sizes or proportion mediated, so 'a substantial part' (and the later 'may miss half the picture') is not supported by the available numbers.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the indirect pathways through depression, and through sleep to depression, were statistically significant, without implying a known magnitude."

**Issue B. 'Tightly' interrelated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "All four problems were tightly interrelated."
- **Abstract says:** "Chronic pain, sleep disturbances, depressive symptoms, and cognitive dysfunction were interrelated in older adults."
- **Problem:** 'Tightly' adds a strength qualifier the abstract does not provide.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop 'tightly' and write 'All four problems were interrelated.'"

### Study 15: A simple multimedia e-book cut older patients' MRI anxiety more than pamphlets or a verbal explanation
**PMID:** 42954308 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Post-MRI comparison overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the e-book group was the least anxious both after the preparation and after the scan"
- **Abstract says:** "After the intervention, the e-book group had lower anxiety than both comparators (p < .001); after MRI, it remained lower than the pamphlet group (p = .025)."
- **Problem:** After the scan, the abstract reports only that the e-book group was lower than the pamphlet group; a significant difference versus the standard-care control at that timepoint is not stated.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write that the e-book group was lowest after preparation, and still lower than the pamphlet group after the scan."

### Study 16: Older adults know heat waves and wildfire smoke are dangerous — they just don't think it applies to them
**PMID:** 42954290 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Wildfire smoke not specified in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Older adults know heat waves and wildfire smoke are dangerous — they just don't think it applies to them"
- **Abstract says:** "Most recognized the health risks of extreme climate events but did not identify personal vulnerability."
- **Problem:** The abstract refers generically to 'extreme climate events' and never mentions wildfire smoke; naming it in the headline (and 'heat or smoke event' in a story angle) attributes a specific hazard focus the abstract does not support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'extreme heat and other climate events' in the headline."

**Issue B. Overstated claim about what works** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The review points to messaging delivered by trusted authorities plus concrete removal of barriers — transport to cooling centers, help paying for air conditioning, mobility assistance — as what actually converts knowledge into action."
- **Abstract says:** "The effectiveness of studied interventions was mixed, influenced by intervention type and study design... Improving climate health literacy among older adults requires public health messaging, engagement of trustworthy authorities, and interventions addressing social and structural barriers."
- **Problem:** The authors recommend these approaches, but the review found intervention effectiveness to be mixed. 'What actually converts knowledge into action' presents a recommendation as a demonstrated result, and the specific examples (transport, AC subsidies) are the digest's own, not the abstract's.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as 'the authors recommend trusted-messenger public health messaging alongside interventions that address social and structural barriers, though the review found intervention effectiveness mixed.'"

- **As written:** "Cannabis use is rising fastest among adults over 50"
- **Abstract says:** "(no epidemiological trend data reported; abstract covers only the 47-participant pilot)"

### Study 18: Tau, not amyloid, appears to be what's actually breaking the brain's connections in Alzheimer's
**PMID:** 42960521 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline asserts mechanism from a mediation analysis** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Tau, not amyloid, appears to be what's actually breaking the brain's connections in Alzheimer's"
- **Abstract says:** "Tau pathology showed strong associations with synaptic biomarkers, with a potential mediating role in the association between amyloid pathology and synaptic dysfunction. These findings support a central role of tau-related processes in synaptic dysfunction in AD."
- **Problem:** The authors describe associations and a 'potential' mediating role in a cross-sectional biomarker study. 'What's actually breaking the brain's connections' states a causal mechanism and excludes amyloid more firmly than the data allow, even though the Caveats note that mediation is not cause.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'Tau is more closely tied than amyloid to markers of synapse damage, new biomarker study finds.'"

**Issue B. 'Almost entirely' overstates 'largely'** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Statistically, amyloid's link to synapse loss ran almost entirely through tau."
- **Abstract says:** "The association between amyloid burden and synaptic biomarkers was largely indirect, statistically mediated by CSF p-tau181."
- **Problem:** The abstract says 'largely'; 'almost entirely' implies a higher proportion mediated than is reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'largely ran through tau.'"

**Issue C. Unsupported background claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Synapse loss is the brain change that tracks most closely with memory symptoms"
- **Abstract says:** "(no comparison of synapse loss with other pathologies as a correlate of symptoms; no clinical outcomes reported)"
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only — the study measured no cognitive outcomes and made no such comparison.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute to prior literature or remove the superlative."
